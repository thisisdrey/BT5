# [H] MJR-8 Possible underflow

## Summary
Severity: High

## Details
The vulnerability is an arithmetic underflow that can occur when the protocol records the free balance for a new era. The contract assumes that the free balance reported for the current era will never be lower than the free balance recorded for the previous era. Because there is no explicit check enforcing this monotonicity, a situation may arise where the reported free balance is smaller than the prior value. When the contract later calculates the amount to be bonded or transferred using a subtraction that expects a non‑negative result, the subtraction can underflow, wrapping around to a very large unsigned integer. This can be exploited by an attacker who manipulates the reported free balance (for example by triggering a state transition that yields a lower balance) and then initiates a bonding or withdrawal operation that relies on the faulty subtraction. The impact is that the protocol may credit the attacker with an inflated bonded amount or allow withdrawals that exceed the actual holdings, effectively draining funds or breaking the accounting guarantees of the staking system. The condition occurs at the boundary between eras, specifically at the point where the ledger updates the free balance (see Ledger.sol line 297). Any participant whose balance is tracked by the ledger – including regular stakers, delegators, and the protocol itself – can be affected because the accounting error propagates to subsequent reward calculations and bond management. The issue was discovered during a manual audit of the Lido KSM smart‑contract code, where the auditor noticed that the free‑balance update logic did not enforce a non‑decreasing invariant and that the subsequent bonding calculation performed an unchecked subtraction. The bug can be hard to notice because the underflow only manifests when a rare state transition produces a lower free balance, which may not happen under normal operation, and the resulting huge number may be interpreted as a legitimate value by downstream logic. To fix the problem, the contract should introduce an explicit variable or flag that determines the exact amount to be bonded for the next era and should add a validation step that ensures the new free balance is not less than the previous one, or that uses safe arithmetic (e.g., SafeMath) with proper revert on underflow. In user‑facing terms, a staker might see their balance unexpectedly drop to zero or see a refund that is far larger than expected, contradicting the expectation that their funds are safely accounted for. This class of bug falls under unchecked arithmetic underflow in state‑transition logic, violating the fundamental accounting assumption that balances cannot become negative.

## Recommendation
We recommend to add a variable to control which amount should be bonded on the next era.
