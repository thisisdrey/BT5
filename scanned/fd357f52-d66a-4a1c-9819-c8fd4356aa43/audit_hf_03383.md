## Details
The vulnerability is a configuration omission in the contract initialization routine where three critical parameters are not registered in the initAllowedBaseKeys function. The missing keys are the minimum position size expressed in USD, the maximum profit‑and‑loss factor that can be applied to deposits, and the maximum profit‑and‑loss factor that governs the auto‑deleveraging (ADL) mechanism. Because these keys are absent, the contract falls back to default values, typically zero, which disables the intended safety checks. As a result, traders can open positions of any size, including amounts far below the intended economic threshold, and the system does not cap the PNL factor for deposits, allowing arbitrarily large profit or loss calculations. Likewise, the ADL safeguard that should limit exposure when a position’s loss exceeds a defined factor is ineffective, so extreme losses are not automatically reduced. The exploit can be carried out simply by submitting a trade that would normally be rejected or limited; the contract accepts it and computes payouts based on unbounded formulas, potentially draining user balances or exposing the protocol to systemic risk. The impact is that users may lose funds unexpectedly, the protocol’s risk parameters are bypassed, and the overall financial integrity of the platform is compromised. The condition occurs at deployment or any upgrade that invokes initAllowedBaseKeys without supplying the missing entries, meaning the bug is present from the moment the contract becomes live. All participants—traders, liquidity providers, and the protocol itself—are affected because the missing constraints apply globally to every trade. The issue was discovered during a manual security audit that compared the contract’s documented configuration schema with the actual initialization code and identified the absent entries. It is hard to notice because the contract does not revert or emit explicit errors; the defaults silently disable the checks, and only extreme edge cases reveal the problem through abnormal loss or profit outcomes. To remediate, the initAllowedBaseKeys function should be updated to include the three missing keys with appropriate non‑zero default values that reflect the protocol’s risk policy, thereby restoring the intended minimum position size enforcement, PNL factor caps, and ADL limits. This class of bug falls under missing configuration or parameter validation errors, where critical business‑logic constants are omitted, leading to accounting inconsistencies and violation of the platform’s economic assumptions. From a user’s perspective the symptoms may appear as “my position opened with a tiny amount even though the UI said there is a minimum”, “my profit was huge and my balance exploded”, or “my loss was not reduced by ADL and my funds vanished”. The expectation that the system would enforce size and risk limits is therefore not met, resulting in money disappearing or unexpected zero‑balance outcomes.

## Recommendation
Add the missing keys to initAllowedBaseKeys.
