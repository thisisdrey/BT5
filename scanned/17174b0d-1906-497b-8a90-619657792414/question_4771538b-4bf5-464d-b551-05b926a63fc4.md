Target `RPCFastCallReadOnlyRequestHandler::try_parse_request` field `self.call_read_only_handler.maximum_call_argument_size`: attacker who does hold the correct `authorization` password (this is the one authenticated case) sends `content-length` header lying about the true body size, e.g. declares `content_len` just under `maximum_call_argument_size` but sends a larger raw body afterward, or `content_len` computed from header not matching `body.len()` passed into `parse_read_only_call_body(body, read_only_call_max_mem_bytes)`. Equality: bytes read for the body (`body.len()`) must equal validated `content_len` bound by `maximum_call_argument_size`; if the HTTP framing layer hands `try_parse_request` a `body` slice longer than `content_len` asserted, `parse_read_only_call_body` may over-read/allocate beyond the intended cap. Impact: High (bounded compute DoS on a read endpoint) since Clarity argument parsing cost scales with actual body size, not the checked `content_len`. Proof idea: construct a preamble where `get_content_length()` returns a small value but pass a much larger `body: &[u8]` directly to `try_parse_request` in a unit test, and assert `parse_read_only_call_body` enforces `body.len()`, not just the header-declared length.
