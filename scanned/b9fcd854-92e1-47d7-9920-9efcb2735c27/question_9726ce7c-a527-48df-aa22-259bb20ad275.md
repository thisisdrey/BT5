Attacker signs a `MultiPayload` containing an `ft_withdraw`/`mt_withdraw`-style intent that debits `TokenId::Nep245(Nep245TokenId{contract_id:'mt.real.near', mt_token_id:'A'})` from their own account, immediately followed within the same batch by a deposit `msg` crafted so the resulting `mt_transfer` call on 'mt.real.near' actually targets sub-token 'A:extra' due to how the withdraw path serializes `mt_token_id` into the outbound `mt_transfer` call's token_id argument. Preconditions: attacker holds balance under TokenId::Nep245(contract_id='mt.real.near', mt_token_id='A'); 'mt.real.near' is a real contract the attacker does not control. Call sequence: execute_intents -> withdraw() subtracts amount keyed by mt_token_id='A' from the Verifier's internal ledger -> outbound near-contract-standards `mt_transfer` call is made with the token_id field taken from `Nep245TokenId.mt_token_id`. Binding: the (contract_id, mt_token_id) pair debited from token_balances == the (contract, token) pair whose real mt_transfer call moves value on-chain. Impact: Critical, a withdrawal could drain a different sub-token than the one debited from the ledger, either freezing the debited token's real backing or letting the attacker withdraw a token they never deposited. Whose funds: the Verifier's aggregate custody of 'mt.real.near' tokens, and any other account holding balance in the actually-drained sub-token. Proof: near-workspaces sandbox test depositing two distinct sub-tokens on a real MT contract, withdrawing one, and asserting the on-chain mt_transfer call's token_id argument exactly matches Nep245TokenId.mt_token_id and that the other sub-token's contract-side balance is untouched.
