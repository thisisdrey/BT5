Target: `Contract::mt_resolve_transfer`, field `token_ids.iter().map(|token_id| token_id.parse().unwrap_or_else(|e| panic!("{e}")))`. Attacker, as the token contract that called `mt_transfer_call`, crafts a `previous_owner_ids`/`token_ids` batch where a `TokenId::parse()` string is engineered to parse successfully into a *different* underlying `(contract, token)` identity than the one whose balance was actually debited during the original outgoing transfer (e.g. a Nep245TokenId string collision across two differently-cased or differently-delimited contract ids that the parser normalizes to the same TokenId). Preconditions: Verifier previously sent tokens for TokenId X to attacker's receiver via mt_transfer_call from a legitimate account; attacker's contract responds in the resolve callback with a refund JSON, and separately crafts token_ids strings such that `token_id.parse()` resolves to TokenId Y (a valuable token) instead of X. Call sequence: mt_resolve_transfer subtracts `refund.0` from `receiver.token_balances` keyed by parsed Y, and adds refund to `previous_owner_id`'s balance keyed by Y, even though the original debit during transfer was against X. Binding: the `(contract, token)` a `TokenId` names == the asset actually moved is broken if parse() maps the resolver's refund to token Y while the original withdraw/transfer moved token X. Scoped impact: Critical TokenId collision letting a worthless asset claim a valuable balance. Proof idea: unit test on the `TokenId`/`Nep245TokenId::parse` implementation directly, feeding two distinct token_id strings that produce equal parsed values, then run the sandbox mt_transfer_call/resolve flow and assert token_balances change lands on the intended TokenId, not the colliding one.
