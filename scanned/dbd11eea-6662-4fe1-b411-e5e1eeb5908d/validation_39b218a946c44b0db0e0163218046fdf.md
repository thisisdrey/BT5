Confirmed: `pallet-multi-asset-bounties` has no `update_due`/inactivity-timeout mechanism at all in its `unassign_curator` logic, unlike the legacy `pallet-bounties`/`pallet-child-bounties`, where any signed account can permissionlessly slash an unresponsive curator once `update_due < block_number`. This gives the curator (the "position owner" analog) unilateral control to keep the bounty stuck.

### Title
Multi-asset bounty curator can permanently block permissionless unassignment, indefinitely freezing bounty funds and curator deposit - (File: `substrate/frame/multi-asset-bounties/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
In the legacy `pallet-bounties`/`pallet-child-bounties`, `unassign_curator` allows *any signed account* to slash and remove an inactive curator once `update_due < current_block`, guaranteeing the bounty can always be recovered even if the curator disappears or refuses to act. `pallet-multi-asset-bounties::unassign_curator` [1](#0-0)  removes this inactivity-timeout escape hatch entirely: the third-party (`Some(sender)`) branch requires `sender == parent_curator`, with no time-based or “update_due” check enabling arbitrary community members to intervene, and this field/behavior is completely absent from the pallet (`grep` for `update_due` returns no matches in this pallet).

### Finding Description
Legacy `pallet-bounties::unassign_curator` [2](#0-1)  permits any signed origin to permissionlessly slash an unresponsive curator once `update_due` has elapsed — this is the safety valve that prevents a malicious/absent curator from permanently locking a bounty.

`pallet-multi-asset-bounties::unassign_curator`'s `Active` branch [1](#0-0)  only allows: (1) `RejectOrigin`, (2) the curator themselves, or (3) `parent_curator` (for child bounties). There is no path for an arbitrary community member to force-remove an inactive curator, and no `update_due`/timeout field exists anywhere in the pallet's status types to even support such a check. This means a curator (or a colluding attacker who becomes curator) who simply refuses to `award_bounty` or otherwise progress the bounty can hold the bounty account's funds and their own reserved `curator_deposit` in limbo indefinitely, with no permissionless recovery mechanism — mirroring the Frankencoin pattern where the "position owner" (here, the curator) unilaterally denies a state transition that should be triggerable by any other party, indefinitely freezing funds that were meant to be recoverable.

This is distinct from the already-patched authorization bypass in PR #11612 (`unassign_curator` incorrectly allowing anyone to act when parent bounty wasn't Active) — that fix tightened the "who can call" check, but doing so also removed (or never reintroduced) the original inactivity-based permissionless recovery path that `pallet-bounties` relies on for liveness.

### Impact Explanation
Bounty funds transferred to the bounty/child-bounty sub-account, and the curator's `curator_deposit` consideration/hold, can become permanently unrecoverable if `RejectOrigin` (typically requiring governance) does not act. On chains where `RejectOrigin` is a slow governance track, this is a de-facto permanent user-fund lock enforced by an uncooperative counterparty (the curator), satisfying the "permanent user-fund lock" impact category.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account that becomes curator of a funded bounty or child bounty can trigger this by simply not calling `award_bounty`/`claim_bounty` and never voluntarily unassigning. No privileged access, governance action, or malicious validator/relayer is required — only a signed extrinsic from an ordinary curator account, matching the “no malicious peer/admin/governance” constraint of an unprivileged attacker.

### Recommendation
Reintroduce an `update_due`-style inactivity timeout (as tracked by `pallet-bounties`/`pallet-child-bounties`) into the `Active`/child-bounty status in `pallet-multi-asset-bounties`, and allow any signed origin to slash and unassign the curator once that timeout has elapsed, matching the safety semantics of the legacy pallets.

### Proof of Concept
1. Fund a bounty via `fund_bounty`, propose and accept a curator via `propose_curator`/`accept_curator` so the bounty enters `BountyStatus::Active { curator, .. }`.
2. The curator never calls `award_bounty`/`claim_bounty`, and never calls `unassign_curator` themselves.
3. Any third-party account calls `Bounties::unassign_curator(RuntimeOrigin::signed(third_party), parent_bounty_id, None)`.
4. Per [3](#0-2) , the check `sender == parent_curator` fails for the third party (there is no parent_curator for a top-level bounty, or the third party is not it), returning `BadOrigin`.
5. The bounty remains `Active` forever unless `RejectOrigin`/governance intervenes — the bounty value and curator deposit stay locked, with no permissionless remedy, unlike `pallet-bounties`' `update_due` based recovery.
