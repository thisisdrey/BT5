Target: `WalletImpl::build_promise` and `RequestMessage::request.external` self-call restriction (README: 'For security reasons, self-calls are not supported', `ContractError::SelfCallsNotAllowed`). Attacker action: attacker who has ANY signed `Request` executed (even a benign empty one via relaying their own legitimately-owned wallet) tries to embed a `NearPromise` targeting `env::current_account_id()` itself (the wallet's own account) with a `FunctionCall` to `w_execute_extension` or `w_execute_signed`, attempting to re-enter the wallet contract from within its own promise execution to bypass the extension/signature checks via `Promise` composability (e.g. attaching full account NEAR balance as deposit to a self-targeted promise that then calls back with attacker-controlled `predecessor_account_id` spoofed as the wallet itself). Preconditions: attacker owns/controls the wallet whose `Request` is being crafted (attacker acting on their OWN wallet, testing the guard) or is an enabled extension attempting privilege replay. Call sequence: `execute_request`->`build_promise(promise)`->checks target != `env::current_account_id()`, else `Err(SelfCallsNotAllowed)`. Binding: no promise within an executed `Request` may target `env::current_account_id()`, preventing reentrant self-authorisation. Impact: High - if the self-call guard is bypassed, a promise could re-invoke `w_execute_extension`/`w_execute_signed` with `predecessor_account_id()==self`, effectively self-authorising further `NearAction`s with no independent signature, escalating to move Verifier balances without a fresh authorisation. Proof idea: sandbox test constructing a `Request.external` promise whose target equals the wallet's own `AccountId` and asserting `w_execute_signed`/`w_execute_extension` panics with `Error::SelfCallsNotAllowed`.
