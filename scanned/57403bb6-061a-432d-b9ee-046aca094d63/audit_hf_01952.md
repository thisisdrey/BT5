# [M] setFundingPeriod() can be DoSed due to calling settleFunding()

## Summary
Severity: Medium
Contest weight: 0.0448
Dataset id: 10792
Source: https://huggingface.co/datasets/Zaevlad/audit-findings-dataset
Type: audit-finding

## Details
The vulnerability is a denial‑of‑service condition that arises when the function responsible for updating the funding period invokes the settlement routine before the period change is applied. The root cause is the tight coupling of setFundingPeriod with a call to settleFunding, which can be front‑run by an attacker who submits a separate settleFunding transaction that executes just before the scheduled setFundingPeriod transaction. Because settleFunding may revert under certain state conditions, the subsequent call from setFundingPeriod also reverts, preventing the funding period from being updated. This can be exploited by an adversary who monitors the mempool, detects an upcoming setFundingPeriod transaction, and quickly submits a settleFunding call that forces the settlement logic to fail. When the attacker’s transaction is mined first, the later setFundingPeriod transaction encounters a reverted internal call and therefore does not change the funding interval. The impact is that the protocol’s funding mechanism stalls: funding rates are no longer refreshed, traders’ positions may accrue stale or incorrect funding payments, and the overall market may become unbalanced. Users experience symptoms such as missing funding payments, unchanged funding rates despite time passing, or positions that appear to earn or lose nothing when they should be receiving periodic funding. The issue occurs only when an attacker can successfully front‑run the setFundingPeriod call, which typically requires the ability to observe pending transactions and submit a higher‑gas transaction. All participants who rely on accurate funding calculations – traders, liquidity providers, and the protocol itself – are affected because the economic incentives built into the perpetual market are broken. The problem was discovered during a systematic audit of the clearing‑house contract where the call flow between setFundingPeriod and settleFunding was examined and a potential front‑run scenario was identified. The bug is subtle because the revert only happens under a specific ordering of transactions, making it easy to miss in normal testing where the functions are called in the intended sequence. To remediate the issue, the funding logic should be redesigned to calculate payments proportionally to elapsed time rather than in fixed intervals, thereby removing the need to call settleFunding inside setFundingPeriod. Alternatively, the contract could separate the settlement step from the period update, add a re‑entrancy guard, or enforce that settleFunding cannot be forced to revert by external calls. In any case, the fix must ensure that updating the funding period cannot be blocked by a malicious settlement call, preserving the continuity of funding payments and the integrity of the market’s accounting rules.

## Recommendation
Make the funding payments pro-rata to time instead of in fixed intervals. This would also solve the fact that settleFunding() calls can be frontrunned to avoid payments.
