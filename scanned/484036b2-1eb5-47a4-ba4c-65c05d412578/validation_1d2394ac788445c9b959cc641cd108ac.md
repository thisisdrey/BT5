### Title
Unverified claimed-score can permanently eject and slash an honest signed election submission before feasibility is checked - (File: `substrate/frame/election-provider-multi-block/src/signed/mod.rs`)

### Summary
The `pallet-election-provider-multi-block` signed-phase queue ejects the weakest submission whenever a **new registration with a higher `claimed_score`** arrives, and immediately burns a portion of the ejected submitter's deposit via `EjectGraceRatio` — all before the new, ousting solution's score is ever verified for feasibility. This mirrors the Taiko bug class: a bond is permanently destroyed based on an *unverified, interim* claim, without waiting to see whether that claim is ultimately correct. If the ousting solution later fails feasibility (wrong/forged score, missing pages, infeasible assignment), the previously-ejected — and possibly correct/best — submission's slashed deposit is not, and cannot be, recovered.

### Finding Description
Registration for the signed phase only requires an account and a `claimed_score`, not any actual solution pages:

`substrate/frame/election-provider-multi-block/src/signed/mod.rs` `try_register_inner` (lines 454-529) compares only `metadata.claimed_score` against the existing `SortedScores` queue: [1](#0-0) 

If the queue is full and the new claimed score is better than the weakest entry, the weakest submission is evicted immediately: its `SubmissionMetadataStorage` and `SubmissionStorage` (any already-submitted pages) are deleted, and `settle_deposit` is called with `T::EjectGraceRatio::get()`, which permanently burns the non-refunded portion via `T::Currency::burn_held`: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

Crucially, this decision is made purely on the **claimed** score at registration time — the module docs explicitly acknowledge the score is only checked later, during `Phase::SignedValidation`, when solutions are examined "from best-to-worst claim" and undergo the "expensive `feasibility_check`": [4](#0-3) 

Because eviction and slashing happen at `register` time (before verification), an attacker can submit a `register(origin, score)` call with a fabricated `claimed_score` that is strictly better than an honest, already-queued submission's score, without ever needing to submit valid (or any) solution pages. This immediately:
1. Deletes the honest submitter's stored pages/metadata.
2. Burns a fixed fraction (`1 - EjectGraceRatio`) of the honest submitter's deposit — a real, permanent value loss.

Later, when `SignedValidation` runs, the attacker's bogus solution fails `feasibility_check` (`FeasibilityError::InvalidScore`, `Incomplete`, etc., as shown in `verifier/impls.rs` `do_verify_synchronous_multi` and `finalize_async_verification`), at which point the attacker's own deposit is slashed via `handle_solution_rejection`. But this happens *after* the honest submitter has already been permanently ejected and partially slashed, exactly as in the Taiko report where Bob's validity bond was burned by a subsequent contest that itself never survives final verification. There is no mechanism analogous to compensating or restoring the wrongly-ejected party's lost deposit fraction once the ousting claim is shown to be false — the "history" of who was actually correct is not reconciled with the slashing that already occurred.

### Impact Explanation
Any unprivileged, signed account can register a solution with an inflated `claimed_score` to knock a legitimate, better-positioned submitter out of the signed queue and cause an immediate, permanent partial slash of that honest party's deposit — at zero cost beyond their own eventual (unrelated, later) slash when their bogus solution fails verification. This is a direct, protocol-level fund loss for honest participants of the NPoS election mechanism (used by `pallet-staking-async` / `AssetHub` chains per `cumulus/parachains/runtimes/assets/asset-hub-westend/src/staking.rs`), and can be used to grief specific submitters, discourage honest participation in the signed phase, or degrade the quality/availability of election solutions feeding into staking (chain-liveness-relevant, non-privileged, no malicious validator/relayer required).

### Likelihood Explanation
`register` is a public, permissionless, signed extrinsic; the eviction/slash logic runs synchronously and unconditionally whenever a better claimed score arrives and the queue is full — no feasibility pre-check gates this path. The only cost to the attacker is their own deposit reserved at `register` time, which they already expect to lose (or can attempt to game by submitting a technically-well-formed but marginally-better claimed score they intend to abandon/fail on purpose), making repeated griefing of specific well-scored honest submitters straightforward and cheap relative to the honest submitter's guaranteed partial loss.

### Recommendation
Do not permanently burn any portion of an ejected submitter's deposit until the ousting solution's `claimed_score` has passed at least a low-cost/plausibility or full feasibility check, or hold the ejected deposit in escrow/refundable state until the round's verification concludes; only finalize the slash if the ousting solution is confirmed valid and actually ends up queued/elected. Alternatively, allow re-registration/restoration of an ejected submission (with its original deposit) if the account that displaced it is subsequently found to be infeasible during `SignedValidation`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Round is in `Phase::Signed`; queue is full (`SortedScores` at `T::MaxSubmissions`), with Alice holding a genuinely valid, high-quality claimed score and matching submitted pages.
2. Attacker (Mallory) calls `register(origin, claimed_score)` with `claimed_score` set strictly above Alice's score, but never submits (or submits garbage for) the corresponding solution pages.
3. `try_register_inner` (lines 454-529) sees Mallory's claimed score beats Alice's (the weakest non-invulnerable entry), evicts Alice: clears her `SubmissionMetadataStorage`/`SubmissionStorage`, and calls `settle_deposit(&Alice, deposit, EjectGraceRatio)` — burning `deposit * (1 - EjectGraceRatio)` of Alice's funds irreversibly (`Event::Ejected` emitted).
4. Round proceeds to `Phase::SignedValidation`. Mallory's submission (empty/garbage pages) fails `feasibility_check`/`do_verify_synchronous_multi` with `FeasibilityError::Incomplete`/`InvalidScore`; Mallory is slashed via `handle_solution_rejection`.
5. Net outcome: Alice, who had the best legitimate solution, has permanently lost a fraction of her deposit and her already-computed/submitted work, purely because of Mallory's unverified claim — with no path to recover the burnt portion, matching the "bond burned despite correct/eventually-verified party" pattern from the Taiko report.

//! The signed phase of the multi-block election system.
//!
//! Signed submissions work on the basis of keeping a queue of submissions from unknown signed
//! accounts, and sorting them based on the best claimed score to the worst.
//!
//! Each submission must put a deposit down. This is parameterize-able by the runtime, and might be
//! a constant, linear or exponential value. See [`signed::Config::DepositPerPage`] and
//! [`signed::Config::DepositBase`].
//!
//! During the queuing time, if the queue is full, and a better solution comes in, the weakest
//! deposit is said to be **Ejected**. Ejected solutions get [`signed::Config::EjectGraceRatio`] of
//! their deposit back. This is because we have to delete any submitted pages from them on the spot.
//! They don't get any refund of whatever tx-fee they have paid.
//!
//! Once the time to evaluate the signed phase comes (`Phase::SignedValidation`), the solutions are
//! checked from best-to-worst claim, and they end up in either of the 3 buckets:
//!
//! 1. **Rewarded**: If they are the first correct solution (and consequently the best one, since we
//!    start evaluating from the best claim), they are rewarded. Rewarded solutions always get both
//!    their deposit and transaction fee back.
//! 2. **Slashed**: Any invalid solution that wasted valuable blockchain time gets slashed for their
//!    deposit.
//! 3. **Discarded**: Any solution after the first correct solution is eligible to be peacefully
//!    discarded. But, to delete their data, they have to call
//!    [`signed::Call::clear_old_round_data`]. Once done, they get their full deposit back. Their
//!    tx-fee is not refunded.

	fn settle_deposit(who: &T::AccountId, deposit: BalanceOf<T>, grace: Perbill) {
		let to_refund = grace * deposit;
		let to_slash = deposit.defensive_saturating_sub(to_refund);

		let _res = T::Currency::release(
			&HoldReason::SignedSubmission.into(),
			who,
			to_refund,
			Precision::BestEffort,
		)
		.defensive();
		debug_assert_eq!(_res, Ok(to_refund));

		let _res = T::Currency::burn_held(
			&HoldReason::SignedSubmission.into(),
			who,
			to_slash,
			Precision::BestEffort,
			Fortitude::Force,
		)
		.defensive();
		debug_assert_eq!(_res, Ok(to_slash));
	}
