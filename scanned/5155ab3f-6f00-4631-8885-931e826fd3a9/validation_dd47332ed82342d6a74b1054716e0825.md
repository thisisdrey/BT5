### Title
Unbounded AMM spot-price dependency in `SwapFirstAssetTrader::buy_weight` allows fee-manipulation via pool-reserve skew - (File: cumulus/primitives/utility/src/lib.rs)

### Summary
`SwapFirstAssetTrader::buy_weight` (used as a live `Trader` on Asset Hub Rococo/Westend and Penpal) pays XCM execution weight by calling `pallet_asset_conversion`'s `SwapCredit::swap_tokens_for_exact_tokens` [1](#0-0) . This function computes the required input amount purely from the pool's current on-chain reserves via `get_amount_in`, with no caller-supplied slippage/maximum-input bound [2](#0-1) . An unprivileged account holding enough of the pool's assets can transiently skew a pool's reserves in one extrinsic and consume the favorable spot price in the very next extrinsic within the same block, paying an artificially cheap amount of the "client" asset for a fixed amount of native weight/fee.

### Finding Description
`get_amount_in`/`get_amount_out` are pure spot-price AMM formulas over live reserves [3](#0-2) . The pallet's own documentation acknowledges the quote "is only guaranteed if no other swaps are made after the price is quoted and before the target swap" [4](#0-3) , i.e. it is explicitly a manipulable instantaneous price, not a TWAP — precisely the broken invariant in the CurveOracle report (spot price with no time-weighting, subject to reserve skew). Unlike the balance-based `Swap::swap_tokens_for_exact_tokens`, which lets callers pass `amount_in_max` for slippage protection [5](#0-4) , the credit-based `SwapCredit::swap_tokens_for_exact_tokens` used by the XCM fee trader has no such bound [2](#0-1) .

`SwapFirstAssetTrader::buy_weight` is wired as a production `Trader` on Asset Hub Rococo [6](#0-5)  and Asset Hub Westend [7](#0-6) , and on Penpal [8](#0-7) . An attacker who controls both legs of a trade (e.g., swaps native for asset X to inflate X's price in the pool, then immediately submits an XCM message paying fees with X, then reverses the swap) pays `swap_tokens_for_exact_tokens(vec![X, Target], credit_in, fee)` a price dictated by the skewed reserves rather than the pool's "fair" price, since no min/max bound guards the conversion.

### Impact Explanation
This lets an unprivileged attacker with capital (no flash loans needed on a chain without native flash-loan primitives, but a simple two-extrinsic same-block sandwich against any pool, especially thinly-liquidated pools) pay for XCM execution weight/blockspace at a fraction of its true cost, i.e., "public underpriced work that degrades block production" — the category explicitly called out in the impact gate. Because the trader is invoked on every XCM message that pays fees in a non-native pool asset, an attacker can repeatedly submit heavy XCM programs while draining/skewing a shallow pool to make weight artificially cheap, degrading fee-market integrity on Asset Hub.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only an unprivileged account with sufficient capital to move a pool's reserves for one block and submit an XCM extrinsic in the same block — no validator, collator, relayer, or governance access needed. Likelihood scales inversely with pool liquidity; newly created or thinly-liquid pools (which asset-conversion explicitly supports) are most exposed.

### Recommendation
Add a caller-supplied `amount_in_max` (slippage bound) to `SwapCredit::swap_tokens_for_exact_tokens`, mirroring the account-based `Swap` trait, and have `SwapFirstAssetTrader::buy_weight` compute a bound (e.g., using a pre-swap quote or an on-chain TWAP oracle over the pool) before performing the credit swap, rejecting execution if the realized price deviates unacceptably from the expected value.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker holds asset `X` and native `Target`; a shallow `(X, Target)` pool exists on Asset Hub.
2. Tx 1 (same block): attacker calls `pallet_asset_conversion::swap_exact_tokens_for_tokens` to dump `Target` into the pool, sharply raising `X`'s price relative to `Target`.
3. Tx 2 (same block): attacker submits an XCM message with a `BuyExecution` paying fees in `X`; `SwapFirstAssetTrader::buy_weight` calls `SwapCredit::swap_tokens_for_exact_tokens(vec![X, Target], credit_in, fee)` [1](#0-0) , which — because `get_amount_in` uses the now-skewed reserves — extracts far less `X` than the pool's normal fair price for the same fixed `Target` fee.
4. Tx 3 (same block): attacker reverses the initial swap, restoring the pool and recovering most of the `Target` spent in step 2, netting a large weight/fee discount at a fraction of true cost.

		pub fn get_amount_out(
			fee: Permill,
			amount_in: &T::Balance,
			reserve_in: &T::Balance,
			reserve_out: &T::Balance,
		) -> Result<T::Balance, Error<T>> {
			let amount_in = T::HigherPrecisionBalance::from(*amount_in);
			let reserve_in = T::HigherPrecisionBalance::from(*reserve_in);
			let reserve_out = T::HigherPrecisionBalance::from(*reserve_out);

			if reserve_in.is_zero() || reserve_out.is_zero() {
				return Err(Error::<T>::ZeroLiquidity);
			}

			let fee_complement = fee.left_from_one().deconstruct();
			let amount_in_with_fee = amount_in
				.checked_mul(&T::HigherPrecisionBalance::from(fee_complement))
				.ok_or(Error::<T>::Overflow)?;

			let numerator =
				amount_in_with_fee.checked_mul(&reserve_out).ok_or(Error::<T>::Overflow)?;

			let denominator = reserve_in
				.checked_mul(&T::HigherPrecisionBalance::from(Permill::ACCURACY))
				.ok_or(Error::<T>::Overflow)?
				.checked_add(&amount_in_with_fee)
				.ok_or(Error::<T>::Overflow)?;

			let result = numerator.checked_div(&denominator).ok_or(Error::<T>::Overflow)?;

			result.try_into().map_err(|_| Error::<T>::Overflow)
		}
