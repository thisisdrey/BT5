## Analysis

The external report's core broken invariant is: **raw integer division to compute a proportional share can floor to zero when the numerator's granularity is small relative to the denominator, causing the rightful claimant to receive nothing from a pool that is otherwise correctly funded/consumed.**

The closest local analog is `pallet-broker`'s `do_claim_revenue` in `substrate/frame/broker/src/dispatchable_impls.rs`, where a contributor's share of a timeslice's instantaneous-coretime-pool revenue is computed with plain integer division rather than a bounded fixed-point type (`Perbill`/`Perquintill`) as is used everywhere else in `substrate/frame/staking*` and `substrate/frame/nomination-pools`.

### Title
Integer-division rounding to zero in InstaPool revenue-share payout permanently forfeits a contributor's entitled share - (File: `substrate/frame/broker/src/dispatchable_impls.rs`)

### Summary
`do_claim_revenue` computes a private contributor's share of a timeslice's InstaPool revenue as a raw integer division `total_payout * contributed_parts / private_contributions` [1](#0-0) . Unlike the staking/nomination-pools payout paths, which use `Perbill`/`FixedU128`-based ratios with documented precision floors [2](#0-1) , this computation has no fixed-point scaling at all: when `contributed_parts` is small relative to `private_contributions` and `total_payout` is modest, the division floors to `0`, exactly the class of bug described in the external report (`optionShare = balance * 1e18 / totalSupply` rounding to 0).

### Finding Description
For each timeslice `r` in the claim range, the pool record's payout is split as:
```rust
let p = total_payout
    .saturating_mul(contributed_parts.into())
    .checked_div(&pool_record.private_contributions.into())
    .unwrap_or_default();
``` [3](#0-2) 

`contributed_parts` is derived from `region.mask.count_ones()`, a small integer bounded by the 80-bit `CoreMask` (i.e., at most 80) [4](#0-3) , and `private_contributions` accumulates from `InstaPoolIo` deltas that can be arbitrarily larger across many contributors and cores over a timeslice [5](#0-4) . There is no minimum-precision guard analogous to the `Perbill`/`FixedU128` scaling used in `pallet-staking`'s `calculate_staker_reward`/`payout_stakers_alive_staked` [6](#0-5)  or `pallet-nomination-pools`' `current_reward_counter`, which explicitly documents the precision floor at which claims collapse to zero [7](#0-6) .

Critically, after computing `p` (even when `p == 0`), the function unconditionally consumes this timeslice's opportunity for that contribution: `region.begin` is advanced past `r`, `contribution.length` is decremented, and `pool_record.private_contributions` is permanently reduced by `contributed_parts` regardless of whether `p` was zero [8](#0-7) . The remaining `total_payout - p` is redistributed to be shared among the *other* remaining contributors of that same timeslice, not returned to this contributor. Since `InstaPoolContribution::<T>::take(region)` already removed the caller's contribution record up front [9](#0-8) , there is no retry path: once the loop passes timeslice `r`, that specific contributor's entitlement for `r` is gone forever, exactly mirroring the "winner receives 0 rewards and loses initial wager deposit" pattern from the report — here, the contributor already paid (staked) coretime into the InstaPool for that timeslice and is entitled to a revenue share, but the division rounds their share to zero and the timeslice can never be reclaimed.

### Impact Explanation
This causes silent, permanent loss of legitimately earned revenue share for InstaPool contributors with small mask/parts counts relative to the total pool contribution for a timeslice, systematically under-rewarding smaller/single-core contributors while leaving the residual revenue to be absorbed by other claimants of the same period. This is a fund-conservation and rightful-beneficiary violation matching the "Balances ... must conserve value and settle exactly once to the rightful beneficiary and amount" pivot, without requiring any privileged actor, malicious peer, or governance action — any ordinary InstaPool contributor triggers this by simply calling `claim_revenue`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate-to-high in busy periods: `contributed_parts` is capped at 80 (one core's mask), while `private_contributions` for a timeslice sums contributions from every private-pool core assigned across the whole system for that timeslice, which realistically can be numerically much larger, making `contributed_parts * total_payout / private_contributions` round to `0` whenever `total_payout` is not proportionally large — a naturally occurring condition rather than an attacker-crafted edge case.

### Recommendation
Replace the raw `saturating_mul`/`checked_div` share calculation in `do_claim_revenue` with a fixed-point ratio (e.g., `Perquintill::from_rational(contributed_parts, private_contributions).mul_floor(total_payout)` or a `U256`-based scaled calculation as already used in `nomination-pools::point_to_balance` [10](#0-9) ) so that rounding error is minimized and documented, and consider carrying forward any dust/rounding remainder to the contributor (or to a global dust-collection account) rather than silently forfeiting it to other claimants.

### Proof of Concept
1. Configure a timeslice where 100 single-core (`mask.count_ones() == 1`) private contributions exist, so `private_contributions = 100`.
2. Set `total_payout` for that timeslice to `50` (any value where `50 * 1 / 100 == 0` under integer division).
3. Each of the 100 contributors calls `claim_revenue` for their region covering this timeslice.
4. Observe `p == 0` for every contributor via `saturating_mul(1).checked_div(100) == 0`, so no `RevenueClaimItem`/transfer occurs for that timeslice for any of them, yet `pool_record.private_contributions` is decremented by each contributor's `contributed_parts`, and the full `total_payout` remains in `InstaPoolHistory` marked `remaining_payout` until eventually removed with `InstaPoolHistory::<T>::remove(r)` once `private_contributions` reaches zero — confirming the revenue is drained from circulation without being paid to any of the entitled contributors [11](#0-10) .
