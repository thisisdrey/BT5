[Function: `action_delete_account`, beneficiary `Receipt::new_balance_refund`] Can an attacker set `beneficiary_id` to an account that no longer exists by the time the resulting balance-refund receipt executes (e.g. it self-deletes in the same chunk before the refund receipt is processed), causing the refund receipt to fail and `account_balance` to be burnt via the system-refund-failure path at `lib.rs:993-1000`, while the attacker also submits another action that assumes the beneficiary retains the funds? Preconditions: attacker controls both `account_id` (deleting) and `beneficiary_id` (a second account they also control). Sequence: chunk contains DeleteAccount(beneficiary=B) and DeleteAccount(B, beneficiary=C) processed such that B's delete-account receipt commits before its balance-refund receipt arrives. Invariant: value conservation (funds should not be silently burnt without predictable attacker awareness, but this is only exploitable if it desyncs total supply vs. per-account book-keeping). Impact: balance permanently burnt (already an accepted mechanic) — question is whether `stats.balance.other_burnt_amount` is credited in the SAME apply() as the debit from account_balance, avoiding a cross-chunk desync. Proof idea: apply() test deleting B then immediately deleting the beneficiary before the refund receipt executes; assert `other_burnt_amount == account_balance` exactly, not double-counted or dropped, matching the balance-checker's incoming/outgoing totals in `docs/RuntimeSpec/Components/RuntimeCrate.md`.
