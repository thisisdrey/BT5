### Title
`pallet-bounties::claim_bounty` sweeps the bounty sub-account's raw balance instead of the tracked bounty value, misappropriating any funds sent to it - (File: substrate/frame/bounties/src/lib.rs)

### Summary
`pallet-bounties` derives a deterministic, publicly-computable sub-account per bounty (`Self::bounty_account_id(bounty_id)`), and pays out based on that account's *entire live free balance* rather than the amount actually earmarked (`bounty.value`) for the bounty. This is the same broken invariant as the `CeFiSmartTransfer` report: value is pooled into a shared account without internal per-source accounting, so any balance parked there — regardless of who sent it or why — gets swept and redirected to whichever beneficiary/curator the payout call currently targets, with no path back to the original sender.

### Finding Description
`claim_bounty` computes the payout directly from the account's raw balance: [1](#0-0) 

```rust
let bounty_account = Self::bounty_account_id(bounty_id);
let balance = T::Currency::free_balance(&bounty_account);
let fee = bounty.fee.min(balance); // just to be safe
let payout = balance.saturating_sub(fee);
...
let res = T::Currency::transfer(&bounty_account, &curator, final_fee, AllowDeath);
let res = T::Currency::transfer(&bounty_account, &beneficiary, payout, AllowDeath);
```

`bounty_account_id` is a deterministic sub-account derived from the pallet ID and bounty index — it is not access-controlled, and any unprivileged account can send balance to it via an ordinary `transfer` before the bounty is claimed. `claim_bounty` itself is explicitly documented and coded to be callable by *anyone* once the payout delay has passed (`ensure_signed(origin)?; // anyone can trigger claim`), so no privileged operator action is required to trigger the sweep.

The codebase itself acknowledges that a bounty sub-account can hold more than its tracked `bounty.value`: `close_bounty` uses `T::TransferAllAssets::force_transfer_all_assets` to return *all* assets in the sub-account back to the treasury pot when a bounty is cancelled, precisely because extra/stray funds can accumulate there. However, this safety-net logic only exists on the cancellation path (`close_bounty`), not on the normal claim path (`claim_bounty`), and the identical unguarded pattern (`let balance = T::Currency::free_balance(&child_bounty_account); ... let payout = balance.saturating_sub(curator_fee);`) exists in `pallet-child-bounties::claim_child_bounty` as well. [2](#0-1) 

By contrast, the newer `pallet-multi-asset-bounties` computes payout from the tracked `value` field rather than the live account balance (`Self::calculate_payout(parent_bounty_id, child_bounty_id, value)`), confirming that the raw-balance approach used in `pallet-bounties`/`pallet-child-bounties` is the outlier and the actual defect.

### Impact Explanation
Any unprivileged account can top up a bounty's sub-account after it is `Active`/`PendingPayout` and before `claim_bounty`/`claim_child_bounty` executes. Because the payout amount is derived from the whole account balance minus the curator fee, that donated value is permanently and irrevocably transferred to the curator/beneficiary rather than being tracked and returned to the original sender — the exact "misallocation of funds... impossible to track individual deposits" failure described in the source report. This is a real value-conservation break: funds do not settle to the rightful party (the depositor who sent them), matching the "theft or unbacked... permanent user-fund lock" impact class, because there is no mechanism to reclaim or attribute the extra balance back to its sender once claimed.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attacker primitive is trivial and fully unprivileged: compute the deterministic bounty (or child-bounty) sub-account address, transfer any amount of native currency to it, and either wait for or themselves call `claim_bounty`/`claim_child_bounty` (both are callable by any signed account per the code comments/documentation). No governance, curator, admin, relayer, or validator collusion is needed to trigger the fund-sweeping behavior — only a normal, already-scheduled claim needs to occur, which happens routinely as part of pallet operation.

### Recommendation
Track bounty value internally instead of relying on the live free balance of the derived sub-account when computing payouts in `claim_bounty` (`substrate/frame/bounties/src/lib.rs`) and `claim_child_bounty` (`substrate/frame/child-bounties/src/lib.rs`). Payout amounts should be computed from `bounty.value`/`bounty.fee` (the values fixed at award time), and any balance in the sub-account beyond that tracked amount should be swept back to the treasury pot (mirroring the `TransferAllAssets`/`force_transfer_all_assets` logic already used in `close_bounty`) rather than forwarded to the curator or beneficiary.

### Proof of Concept
1. Council proposes and approves a bounty of value `V`; treasury funds `bounty_account_id(id)` with `V`.
2. Curator is assigned, accepts, and calls `award_bounty(id, beneficiary)`, moving the bounty to `PendingPayout`.
3. Before the payout delay elapses, an unrelated unprivileged account `Mallory` calls `Balances::transfer_allow_death(Mallory, bounty_account_id(id), X)`, donating `X` extra tokens to the sub-account (mistakenly or otherwise, e.g. thinking it's a deposit address).
4. Once `unlock_at` passes, anyone calls `claim_bounty(id)`.
5. `claim_bounty` reads `balance = free_balance(bounty_account) == V + X`, computes `payout = balance - fee`, and transfers the full `V + X - fee` to `beneficiary` (and `fee` to curator).
6. `Mallory`'s `X` tokens are now irreversibly in the beneficiary's account with no on-chain record connecting them back to `Mallory`, reproducing the exact "misappropriation due to missing internal accounting" scenario from the source report.
