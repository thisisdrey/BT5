Target: `Contract::simulate_intents` vs `Contract::execute_intents` consistency, contracts/defuse/src/contract/intents/mod.rs. Attacker action: call `simulate_intents(signed)` to obtain a `SimulationOutput{report, invariant_violated, state}` showing a favorable outcome (e.g., a resolver quoting a swap price to a counterparty off-chain based on this report), then race a separate `execute_intents([other_payload])` from a different account between simulate and the real settlement that changes `self.fee()` or `self.salts.current()`, making the real `execute_intents(signed)` produce a materially different outcome than what was reported. Preconditions: counterparty relies on `StateOutput{fee, current_salt}` returned by `simulate_intents`. Call sequence: `simulate_intents` runs on `self.cached()` (a snapshot) while `execute_intents` runs on live mutable state; the two calls are not atomic with each other. Binding: outcome of `execute_intents(signed)` == outcome reported by `simulate_intents(signed)` for the SAME state; assert that when the two calls are separated by an intervening `set_fee`/`update_current_salt`, the executed deltas differ from `report`. Scoped impact: High, a party settles on a `simulate_intents` report that `execute_intents` does not produce. Proof idea: cargo test: simulate, then mutate fee via a role-independent path (e.g., another user's independent intent affecting only their own token diff) is insufficient — instead assert via sandbox that non-atomicity exists by interleaving two transactions and comparing `report` to actual post-`execute_intents` balances.
