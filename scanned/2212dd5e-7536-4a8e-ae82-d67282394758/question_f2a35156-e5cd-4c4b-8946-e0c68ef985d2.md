Can an attacker who controls `State::is_valid_salt` acceptance criteria indirectly (by timing their submission around a legitimate `SaltManager` salt rotation, not by holding the role themselves) sign a `V1` nonce under an old `salt` that is still accepted as valid at submission time, settle it, and later, after the salt rotates again to re-include the old salt in whatever validity window `is_valid_salt` checks (e.g., a ring/window of N previous salts), resubmit the SAME already-committed nonce and have it re-pass `is_valid_salt` while `commit_nonce`'s `BitMap256::set_bit` still correctly rejects it as used? This question targets whether `is_valid_salt` re-acceptance alone (independent of the nonce-used bit) could ever let `commit_nonce` be skipped or bypassed on a retry path. Binding: settlement count == 1. Impact: Critical if the used-bit check can be bypassed by any code path that reads salt validity before the used-bit check without gating on it; otherwise not exploitable. Proof idea: cargo test cycling `is_valid_salt` acceptance across two salt rotations while confirming `commit_nonce` on the same nonce bytes always fails on the second attempt regardless of salt validity.
