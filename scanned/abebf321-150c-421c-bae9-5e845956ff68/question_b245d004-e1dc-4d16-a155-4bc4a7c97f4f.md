Target: Nonces::commit bit-packing (`self.old.get_bit(nonce) || self.current.set_bit(nonce)`). Attacker action: attacker submits two different signed RequestMessages whose 32-bit `nonce` values collide on the same underlying BitMap byte/prefix due to the '27-bit key + 5-bit position' packing scheme, hoping to trick the bitmap into marking an unrelated nonce as used (denying a legitimate future request) or, conversely, into treating a used nonce as unused after a `cleanup_by_prefix` on a shared prefix. Preconditions: two nonces N1, N2 sharing the same top-27-bit prefix but different bottom-5-bit positions. Call sequence: BitMap256/BitMap `get_mut_byte_with_mask` operates per-prefix on an 32-byte/32-bit bitmap; different bit positions within the same prefix must be independent. Binding: is_used(N1) must be independent of commit(N2) whenever N1 != N2, i.e. the sum of nonces marked used after committing N2 changes by exactly {N2}, not {N1, N2}. Impact: High — a nonce-bitmap aliasing bug could let an attacker replay a nonce whose bit was wrongly cleared, or could freeze a distinct nonce. Proof idea: proptest over pairs of nonces with a shared prefix but distinct bit positions asserting independent get_bit results after committing only one of them.
