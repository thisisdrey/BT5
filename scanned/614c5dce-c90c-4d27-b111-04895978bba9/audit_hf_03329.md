# [M] EDPU-2 | No Pool Amount Validation For Positive Impact

## Summary
Severity: Medium
Contest weight: 0.0474
Dataset id: 18180
Source: https://huggingface.co/datasets/Zaevlad/audit-findings-dataset
Type: audit-finding

## Details
The vulnerability is a missing validation of the pool amount when a transaction results in a positive impact on the tokenOut balance. In the contract, a function called validatePoolAmount is intended to ensure that the amount of a token held in a liquidity pool never exceeds a predefined maxPoolAmount. However, this check is only applied when the tokenOut amount is reduced (negative impact). When a user receives a positiveImpactAmount – an amount of tokenOut that is added to the pool as part of the transaction – the validation is bypassed, allowing the pool balance for that token to grow beyond the allowed maximum. This occurs because the code path that handles positive impact does not invoke the same invariant check, leaving the pool’s accounting unchecked. An attacker can craft a trade or synthetic operation that generates a positiveImpactAmount large enough to push the tokenOut balance over the limit. By doing so, the attacker can inflate the pool’s token reserves, potentially disrupting price calculations, breaking the protocol’s accounting assumptions, and enabling the extraction of excess tokens later. The impact is that the protocol’s liquidity pool may hold more tokens than intended, violating business logic that assumes a bounded pool size, which can lead to inaccurate pricing, loss of funds for other participants, or even a situation where the pool cannot correctly settle future trades. The issue manifests only when the transaction includes a positive impact on tokenOut; normal withdrawals or trades that only decrease the pool balance remain safe. Users may notice that after a trade the pool’s token balance appears higher than expected, or that subsequent withdrawals return more tokens than they should, creating a discrepancy between displayed balances and actual accounting. The flaw was discovered during a manual audit by the Guardian team, who identified that the validation call was omitted in the positive‑impact branch. It can be hard to detect because typical tests focus on preventing under‑flows or ensuring that withdrawals do not exceed balances, while an overflow of the pool’s upper bound is less obvious and may not surface until the pool is deliberately over‑filled. To remediate, the contract should invoke validatePoolAmount for tokenOut in both negative and positive impact scenarios, ensuring that after any operation the pool amount remains within the allowed range. In conceptual terms, the bug belongs to the class of “missing invariant enforcement” or “boundary check bypass” where a critical state variable is not validated under all code paths, leading to potential overflow of a resource limit.

## Recommendation
Validate that the _params.tokenOut pool amount is still within the valid range by using validatePoolAmount in the case where the user is positively impacted.
