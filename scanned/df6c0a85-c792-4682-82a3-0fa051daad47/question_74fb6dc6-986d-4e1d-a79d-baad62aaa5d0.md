[Function: remove_account] Attacker sets up a delayed/buffered receipt destined to account A (via congestion control causing the receipt to sit in TrieKey::DelayedReceipt or TrieKey::BufferedReceipt while A still exists), then deletes A and immediately recreates it. Preconditions: shard congestion high enough that an action receipt to A is buffered rather than applied immediately. Sequence: 1) attacker sends receipt R1 to A which gets buffered/delayed; 2) attacker (as A's owner) submits DeleteAccountAction; 3) attacker recreates A via CreateAccountAction from the same predecessor before R1 drains from the delayed/buffered queue. Invariant: authorization exactness — a receipt must execute against the account state that existed when its predecessor authorized it, not a later re-created namesake. Impact: R1, originally authorized to hit the old A, silently executes against the new A with predecessor_id == receiver_id in some crafted variant, potentially granting owner-level FunctionCall execution against the new account without ever having been re-authorized by the new account's key. Proof: runtime apply integration test forcing congestion (small gas limit) to delay R1, delete+recreate A within the delay window, and assert R1's effects land on the new account state rather than being dropped/rejected as belonging to a defunct account.
