## Confirmed Analog: `pallet-asset-conversion-tx-payment` — spot-price refund swap manipulable within a single atomic extrinsic

### Title
Fee-refund swap uses post-dispatch AMM spot price, letting a signer manipulate their own extrinsic's fee-asset conversion within a single atomic transaction - (File: `substrate/frame/transaction-payment/asset-conversion-tx-payment/src/payment.rs`)

### Summary
`ChargeAssetTxPayment` withdraws the fee **before** dispatch (using pre-call AMM reserves) and refunds unspent fee **after** dispatch by quoting `pallet-asset-conversion`'s spot reserves (`get_reserves`/`quote_price_exact_tokens_for_tokens`) at that post-dispatch moment [1](#0-0) . If the dispatched call itself moves the pool's reserves (e.g. the extrinsic's own call is, or contains via `pallet-utility`/nested dispatch, a large `swap_exact_tokens_for_tokens`), the refund-time quote is computed on reserves that the same signer just skewed inside the very same atomic extrinsic, exactly mirroring the reported Uniswap `slot0()` single-transaction manipulation pattern.

### Finding Description
`pallet-asset-conversion`'s pricing functions read live pool balances with no TWAP or staleness protection, and explicitly warn callers that "the price may have changed by the time the transaction is executed" [2](#0-1) . `get_reserves` simply reads the two current account balances of the pool [3](#0-2) , i.e. this is a spot price, structurally identical to the manipulable `slot0()` read in the external report.

`SwapAssetAdapter::correct_and_deposit_fee` (used by `ChargeAssetTxPayment::post_dispatch_details`) computes how much of the user's non-native fee asset to refund by quoting `S::quote_price_exact_tokens_for_tokens(A::get(), asset_id, refund_amount, true)` **after** the call has already dispatched [4](#0-3) , then actually executes `S::swap_exact_tokens_for_tokens` against the live pool at that quoted rate and credits the result to the same account `who` [5](#0-4) .

The extension pipeline confirms the exact ordering: `withdraw_fee` happens in `prepare` (pre-dispatch, using pre-call state) [1](#0-0) , the call is dispatched by the runtime executive in between, and `post_dispatch_details` runs afterward, calling `correct_and_deposit_fee` with the asset_id chosen by the signer up front [6](#0-5) . All three phases (withdraw, dispatch, refund) execute within one atomic extrinsic — there is no re-validation or slippage bound comparable to `amount_out_min`/`amount_in_max` protecting the refund swap; the refund path takes whatever price `quote_price_exact_tokens_for_tokens` returns at that instant.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker who signs a transaction with `asset_id = X` (a non-native fee asset) and whose call body (directly, or via `pallet-utility::batch_all`) performs a large swap that skews the `X`/native pool ratio, causes the post-dispatch refund conversion (native → `X`) to price `X` favorably to the attacker. The attacker extracts value from the pool's other liquidity providers because the AMM executes a real swap at the manipulated ratio and credits the attacker's account, at the expense of remaining LPs in that pool — the same beneficiary-mismatch/value-extraction pattern as the reported stNXM issue (attacker profits, other stakeholders in the shared pool lose). Because pools such as `pallet-asset-conversion` back real transferable balances used chain-wide (including for tx-fee payment on asset hubs), this degrades correctness of "public underpriced work" fee accounting and constitutes unbacked value transfer out of a shared liquidity pool triggered by an ordinary unprivileged signed extrinsic — no malicious validator, relayer, or admin is required.

### Likelihood Explanation
Moderate-to-high for chains that enable `pallet-asset-conversion-tx-payment` with shallow pools: the attacker needs only (a) to hold some of the pool's non-native asset and (b) fund a self-contained extrinsic (optionally wrapped in `pallet-utility::batch_all`) that swaps a large amount in the target pool before the extension's post-dispatch refund runs. No additional infrastructure, flash loans, or multi-block coordination is strictly required — the entire sequence is possible within a single atomic extrinsic because dispatch happens between the pre-charge and the post-dispatch refund.

### Recommendation
Bound the refund swap the same way ordinary swaps are slippage-protected (`amount_out_min`), or snapshot/cap the refund conversion rate at the pre-dispatch quoted rate (plus a small tolerance) instead of re-quoting spot reserves after the call has executed. Alternatively, disallow calls that mutate `pallet-asset-conversion` pool reserves for the `asset_id` being used to pay the fee in the same extrinsic, or move to a TWAP-based quote for fee refunds.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker holds `asset X` and signs an extrinsic: `call = Utility::batch_all([AssetConversion::swap_exact_tokens_for_tokens(path: [X, Native], amount_in: LARGE, amount_out_min: 0, ...)])`, with `ChargeAssetTxPayment { tip: 0, asset_id: Some(X) }`.
2. `prepare()` withdraws the fee in `X` at the pool's *pre*-swap ratio via `SwapAssetAdapter::withdraw_fee` (uses `quote_price_tokens_for_exact_tokens`) [7](#0-6) .
3. The batched call executes, draining `X` from the pool into `Native`, sharply changing the `X`/`Native` ratio in the pool the attacker controls.
4. `post_dispatch_details` -> `correct_and_deposit_fee` re-quotes `quote_price_exact_tokens_for_tokens(Native, X, refund_amount, true)` on the now-skewed reserves and swaps the unspent-fee `Native` credit back into `X`, crediting the attacker more `X` than the pre-manipulation rate would have yielded [8](#0-7) .
5. Repeat in later blocks with a reverse swap to restore price, realizing net `X` profit extracted from the pool's LPs — the on-chain analog of the reported spot-price flash manipulation.

Note: I was not able to execute this scenario in a live test environment (no sandbox/terminal access here); the analysis is based on static code-path tracing of `ChargeAssetTxPayment`/`SwapAssetAdapter` and `pallet-asset-conversion`'s pricing functions. A Devin session with repository/test access would be needed to build and run a concrete `#[test]` reproducing the exact profit numbers.
