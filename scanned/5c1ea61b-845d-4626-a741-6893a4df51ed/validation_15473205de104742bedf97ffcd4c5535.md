### Title
Permissionless `pallet-asset-rewards` pools let an attacker permanently lock legitimate stakers' rewards by weaponizing a self-created `reward_asset_id` - (File: `substrate/frame/asset-rewards/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
`pallet-asset-rewards` allows `create_pool` to be called by any signed account on Asset Hub (`CreatePoolOrigin = EnsureSigned<AccountId>`), with an attacker-chosen `reward_asset_id` [1](#0-0) . Because `pallet-assets` lets any signed account permissionlessly `create` an asset and become its Owner/Admin/Freezer [2](#0-1) , an attacker can create a reward pool denominated in an asset they fully control, let honest users stake and accrue rewards, then use their asset-admin privileges (`freeze`/`force_transfer`) to make the pool's reward account unable to pay out. This mirrors the Mantra `x/tokenfactory` bug: an attacker funds a reward stream with a token they control and then forcibly drains/freezes it so the payout transfer reverts, permanently trapping the rewards that were already accrued to legitimate stakers.

### Finding Description
`harvest_rewards` computes a staker's accrued reward and only commits the reward-reset to storage *after* a successful `T::Assets::transfer` from the pool account to the staker: [3](#0-2) 

The `reward_asset_id` is fully attacker-controlled at `create_pool` time and is never validated to be a "trusted"/system asset: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

Since asset creation in `pallet-assets` is permissionless and the creator becomes Owner/Admin/Freezer of the new asset class, the attacker can:
1. `pallet_assets::create` a new asset `X` and mint a supply to themselves.
2. Call `asset-rewards::create_pool` with `reward_asset_id = X` and some legitimate `staked_asset_id` (e.g. a real LP token), with themselves as pool `admin`.
3. `deposit_reward_tokens` some amount of `X` into the pool's reward account (`pool_info.account`, a deterministic sub-account derived from `PalletId`) [6](#0-5) .
4. Legitimate users `stake` real value (e.g. an LP token) into the pool and accrue rewards denominated in `X`.
5. Before a staker calls `harvest_rewards`, the attacker (still Admin/Freezer of `X`) calls `Assets::freeze` on the pool's reward account, or `Assets::force_transfer` to sweep `X` out of it: [7](#0-6) 
6. The staker's `harvest_rewards` call now fails inside `T::Assets::transfer(...)` with `Error::<T>::Frozen` or `BalanceLow`; because the extrinsic reverts atomically, the accrued `staker_info.rewards` value in storage is untouched and the staker can never successfully harvest — the reward is permanently stuck as long as the attacker keeps freezing/redraining `X`.

Unlike `pallet-treasury::spend`, which lets governance pick `AssetKind`/`Paymaster` and gates spending via `SpendOrigin`, `pallet-asset-rewards` on Asset Hub exposes pool creation to any signed account with an attacker-chosen asset, giving the attacker native control (as asset Owner/Admin/Freezer) over the exact token stream used to pay other people's rewards — an analog of Mantra's `x/tokenfactory` `MsgForceTransfer` primitive being reachable by the same actor who funded the reward.

There is no equivalent of `close_farms`'s automatic refund path or `SubMsg::reply_on_error` resilience in `pallet-asset-rewards`: nothing forces cleanup or refund once the pool is drained, and stakers have no way to recover value from a different, unaffected reward stream because rewards are tracked and paid per-pool/per-asset (so unlike Mantra there's no "aggregated BankMsg" blocking unrelated rewards — but the malicious pool's own rewards are permanently unclaimable).

### Impact Explanation
Stakers who legitimately staked value (e.g. LP tokens) into a pool created by an attacker permanently lose the rewards accrued in that pool, because the reward token supply/administration is entirely attacker-controlled and can be frozen or force-transferred away at will, at any time before `harvest_rewards` succeeds. This is a direct "public underpriced work / fund lock" impact: legitimate participants can be lured to stake in a seemingly normal pool and have their accrued incentive permanently denied, with no recovery path in the pallet.

### Likelihood Explanation
High: `create_pool` and asset creation are both permissionless on Asset Hub (`CreatePoolOrigin = EnsureSigned<AccountId>`, `pallet_assets::create` is open to any signed account). No governance or admin action is required by the attacker — they act purely within their own privileges (as creator/admin of their own asset and admin of their own pool), consistent with an "unprivileged attacker" primitive rather than a malicious validator/relayer/governance actor.

### Recommendation
- Do not allow `reward_asset_id` to be an asset whose Freezer/Admin is the pool creator/admin (or any account other than a trusted system account), or require reward assets to come from an allow-list of vetted asset classes.
- Alternatively, snapshot/escrow reward tokens into an account not subject to the reward asset's Freezer/Admin authority (e.g., hold via `pallet-assets` holds that the asset team cannot revoke), or use a reward-asset abstraction that cannot be frozen/force-transferred by an arbitrary team.
- Add a permissionless "top-up or fail forward" mechanism: if `harvest_rewards`'s transfer fails, still allow the staker's claim to be tracked/refunded via an alternate path (e.g., mark reward as unrecoverable and emit an event, or allow admin-independent pool destruction that returns staked collateral without requiring the reward-asset transfer to succeed).
- Consider decoupling `unstake` from reward payout entirely (it already only touches the staked-asset freeze), and ensure `harvest_rewards` failures don't lock users out of ever retrieving previously staked principal.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker calls `pallet_assets::create(origin=attacker, id=X, admin=attacker, min_balance=1)`, becoming Owner/Admin/Freezer of asset `X`, then mints `1_000_000 X` to themselves.
2. Attacker calls `asset_rewards::create_pool(origin=attacker, staked_asset_id=LP_TOKEN, reward_asset_id=X, reward_rate_per_block=100, expiry=After(1000), admin=Some(attacker))` — succeeds because `CreatePoolOrigin` is `EnsureSigned`.
3. Attacker calls `asset_rewards::deposit_reward_tokens(pool_id, amount=100_000)`, moving `X` into the pool's reward account (`pool_info.account`).
4. Victim stakes `LP_TOKEN` via `asset_rewards::stake(pool_id, amount)`; after some blocks, `PoolStakerInfo.rewards` accrues a nonzero amount of `X`.
5. Attacker calls `pallet_assets::freeze(origin=attacker, id=X, who=pool_info.account)` (attacker is asset `X`'s Freezer) — or `pallet_assets::force_transfer(origin=attacker, id=X, source=pool_info.account, dest=attacker, amount=all)`.
6. Victim calls `asset_rewards::harvest_rewards(pool_id, staker=None)`; `T::Assets::transfer(X, pool_info.account, victim, rewards, Preservation::Expendable)` fails with `Error::<T>::Frozen` (or `BalanceLow` after force-transfer), the extrinsic reverts, and `PoolStakers` storage still shows the same unclaimed `rewards` — permanently unclaimable as long as the attacker keeps re-freezing/re-draining `X`.
