## Details
The vulnerability is a manipulation of the accounting check that governs the closing of a distribution in the FantiumClaimingV1 contract. The root cause lies in the fact that the platform manager, who has permission to update share allocations in the associated NFT contract, can invoke the takeClaimingSnapshot function after altering those shares. The snapshot records the current share distribution, which is later used by the closeDistribution function to verify that the total funds allocated match the expected share totals. Because the share values can be changed after the snapshot but before the closing check, the contract’s verification logic can be bypassed or produce an incorrect result. An attacker (or a careless manager) can therefore create a state where the recorded snapshot expects a higher total payout than the actual funds deposited, forcing the athlete (the intended recipient) to inject additional ether to satisfy the closing condition, or causing the distribution to remain open indefinitely. This can be exploited by a malicious manager who deliberately updates shares to inflate the required payout, then calls takeClaimingSnapshot, and finally attempts to close the distribution. The impact is that legitimate participants may see their expected refunds disappear, receive zero payouts, or be prompted to add more funds to a distribution that should have been fully funded already. The condition occurs whenever the manager updates shares and calls the snapshot before the distribution is closed, which is allowed by the contract’s access control. Users affected include athletes who rely on the distribution to receive their earnings, as well as any other participants whose balances are tied to the distribution logic. The issue was discovered during a manual security audit that examined the flow of share updates and snapshot calls, noting that the check does not re‑validate share consistency after a snapshot. The bug is subtle because the contract appears to enforce a funding check, yet the underlying assumption that shares remain immutable after the snapshot is not enforced, making the problem easy to miss in testing. To remediate, the closeDistribution function should either prevent share updates after a snapshot, recompute expected payouts based on the current share state at closing time, or include an explicit invariant that guarantees share values cannot change between snapshot and closure. In abstract terms, this is a state‑inconsistency vulnerability where a contract’s accounting snapshot can be desynchronized from the actual share configuration, leading to incorrect fund distribution and potential loss of user confidence. From a user’s perspective, the UI may show a claim as ready to be closed, but the payout amount is zero or the system asks the athlete to deposit additional funds, contrary to the expectation that the platform handles all payouts automatically.") ,"qna

## Recommendation
We recommend changing the closeDistribution function.
