`Contract::do_mt_withdraw`/`mt_resolve_withdraw` (`contracts/defuse/src/contract/tokens/nep245/withdraw.rs`) and `do_nft_withdraw`/`nft_resolve_withdraw` follow the identical debit-before-promise, trust-the-return-value pattern as `ft_resolve_withdraw`. For `MtWithdraw`, can an attacker deploy a NEP-245 `token` contract that returns, from its `mt_batch_transfer_call`/equivalent, a `Vec<U128>` of `used` amounts that is shorter or reorders entries relative to `withdraw.token_ids`/`withdraw.amounts` zipped in `internal_mt_withdraw`, causing `mt_resolve_withdraw` to compute per-token refunds against the WRONG `token_id`, crediting `token_balances` for a `TokenId::Nep245` variant different from the one actually still held by the attacker's contract? Binding: for every index `i`, the `token_id`/`amount` refunded by `mt_resolve_withdraw` == the `token_id`/`amount` at the SAME index in the original `withdraw.token_ids`/`withdraw.amounts`. Impact: Critical, a refund credited to the wrong `TokenId` can let an attacker manufacture balance in a `TokenId` variant they never actually held custody of via mismatched index accounting. Proof idea: `cargo test` withdrawing two distinct `TokenId::Nep245` entries in one `MtWithdraw`, mocking the resolve callback's returned amounts array to be permuted, and asserting the credited `token_id` for the refund matches the original array position.
