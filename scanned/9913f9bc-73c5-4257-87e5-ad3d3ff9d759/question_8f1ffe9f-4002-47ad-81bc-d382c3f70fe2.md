[Function: liquidate / liquidate-redeem] `liquidate-redeeem` forces `(some current-contract)` as `collateral-receiver` inside its inner `liquidate` call, then calls `vault-redeem underlying-id collateral-seized min-underlying funds-receiver` where `funds-receiver` defaults to `contract-caller` unless `receiver` is supplied. Attacker action: call `(contract-call? .v0-4-market liquidate-redeem borrower .vault-ststx .vault-stx debt-amount u0 u0 (some ATTACKER) none)` directly as an ordinary unprivileged liquidator. Preconditions: a real borrower position exists that is liquidatable (LTV >= LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL) with zstSTX collateral and STX debt. Call sequence: attacker A (tx-sender=A, contract-caller=A) calls `liquidate-redeem` -> internal `liquidate` executes with `collateral-receiver` = market contract -> market holds zstSTX -> `vault-redeem` burns those zTokens and sends underlying stSTX to `funds-receiver` = ATTACKER (a receiver arg A fully controls, unrelated to the debt A actually repaid with A's own funds). Invariant tested: Liquidation fairness - collateral seized (scaled by penalty) must equal debt actually repaid, and the receiver of proceeds must be the party that funded the repayment (`tx-sender`=A here, consistent design) - test instead whether A can set `receiver` to a THIRD-PARTY principal that never funded the repay, and whether `vault-redeem`'s underlying transfer can be redirected to bypass A's own liquidation reward accounting, e.g. does `min-underlying` slippage check use `funds-receiver`'s balance or a hardcoded amount, letting A siphon MORE stSTX-equivalent than the collateral-seized amount would convert to. Impact: Critical, direct theft of user funds (of the liquidated borrower's remaining collateral or of protocol solvency if the conversion is decoupled from `collateral-seized`). Proof idea: Clarinet test comparing `collateral-seized` (zToken amount) times the vault's `convert-to-assets` rate at that block against the actual underlying amount transferred to `funds-receiver`; assert they are equal, and separately assert that no ordinary attacker can name a `receiver` in `liquidate-redeem` who receives underlying-value exceeding `debt-repaid * (BPS+liq-penalty)/BPS` worth of collateral.
