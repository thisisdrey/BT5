Based on my research, the clearest verified analog is `pallet-vesting`'s `vested_transfer`, which lets an unprivileged, arbitrary account push a brand-new balance lock onto a *victim* account, exactly mirroring the "deposit on behalf of others" griefing pattern from the report (an unrequested, unconsented state mutation triggered by a third party that constrains the victim's funds).

### Title
Unprivileged `vested_transfer` lets any account force a new lock/schedule slot onto a victim, griefing their account state - (File: `substrate/frame/vesting/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
`Pallet::vested_transfer` [1](#0-0)  can be called by **any signed account** with an arbitrary `target`, exactly like the TrancheVault `deposit()` that lets anyone act "on behalf of" another lender. There is no approval check comparable to the tranche's LP allow-list, and no consent step from `target` before a new vesting schedule (and its associated `Locks` entry) is created against their account.

### Finding Description
`vested_transfer` is dispatched as:
```rust
pub fn vested_transfer(
    origin: OriginFor<T>,
    target: AccountIdLookupOf<T>,
    schedule: VestingInfo<BalanceOf<T>, BlockNumberFor<T>>,
) -> DispatchResult {
    let transactor = ensure_signed(origin)?;
    let target = T::Lookup::lookup(target)?;
    Self::do_vested_transfer(&transactor, &target, schedule)
}
``` [1](#0-0) 

There is no check that `target == transactor` nor any allow-list/permission from `target`. This is structurally identical to the reported `TrancheVault.deposit()` flaw: the only real constraint checked is on the *caller* (they must fund `locked`), not on the *recipient's* consent. Just as the tranche bug let a griefer "deposit on behalf" and reset the victim's `lastDepositTime` (thereby re-arming the withdrawal lockout), `vested_transfer` lets a griefer push state onto the victim that constrains their funds and forces the victim to take corrective action (calling `vest`/`vest_other`, or `merge_schedules`) to regain full liquidity of already-owned tokens.

The vesting module's own docs confirm the parallel intent: `vest_other`/`vested_transfer` exist precisely to let "the sender act on another account's behalf" [2](#0-1) , and the pallet enforces a `MAX_VESTING_SCHEDULES` bound per account, so repeated forced transfers can occupy the victim's scheduling capacity — a bounded but real DoS/lock analog to the tranche's repeated lockout reset (both are "cheap-and-repeatable, borne by the victim" patterns). The Solidity precompile interface shows the same unrestricted third-party trigger surface: `vestedTransfer(address target, ...)` and `vestOther(address target)`, explicitly documented as "the caller pays the fee but the vesting schedule of `target` is updated" [3](#0-2)  and [4](#0-3) .

### Impact Explanation
The corrupted value is the victim's `Vesting<T>` storage / `Locks` entry: an attacker can insert a schedule the victim never agreed to, using only a minimal `MinVestedTransfer` amount, forcing the victim's transferable balance down and consuming one of their limited vesting-schedule slots. Repeated calls (bounded by `MAX_VESTING_SCHEDULES`) can exhaust the victim's schedule capacity, blocking legitimate vesting operations (e.g., future genuine vested transfers to them failing with a "too many schedules" error) until the victim spends extra transactions merging/vesting out the forced schedules — an unbacked-cost griefing vector on the victim, paid for cheaply by the attacker (minimum vested amount), directly analogous to the tranche vault's "cost is `minDepositAmount` every lockout period" griefing economics.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is low-to-medium: this is intentional, documented pallet behavior (not a logic bug per se), and this repository's `substrate/frame/vesting/README.md` explicitly describes `vest_other`/third-party-target calls as a designed feature, similar to how the TrancheVault's `deposit()` receiver parameter was an *intentional* feature that was later found to be exploitable and was removed. No malicious peer/validator/governance actor is required — any unprivileged, funded account can execute this today via a normal signed extrinsic or the EVM precompile.

### Recommendation
Mirror the fix applied to the original report (removing "act for others" capability) or gating it: require `target`'s prior opt-in (e.g., an allow-list similar to nomination-pools' `ClaimPermission`) before a third party can create a new vesting schedule against them, and/or ensure `MAX_VESTING_SCHEDULES` exhaustion by an unconsented party cannot block the account holder's own legitimate future vesting transfers.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker holds `MinVestedTransfer` balance.
2. Attacker repeatedly calls `Vesting::vested_transfer(origin: attacker, target: victim, schedule: { locked: MinVestedTransfer, per_block: 1, starting_block: far_future })` for as many times as `MAX_VESTING_SCHEDULES` allows.
3. Victim's `Vesting` storage is now saturated with attacker-created schedules; any subsequent genuine `vested_transfer` to the victim (from anyone, including the victim's employer/DAO) fails until the victim spends fees to `vest`/merge schedules to free slots — a forced, uninvited liquidity/administrative burden imposed by an unprivileged third party, matching the report's "malicious deposit resets lockout" griefing pattern.
