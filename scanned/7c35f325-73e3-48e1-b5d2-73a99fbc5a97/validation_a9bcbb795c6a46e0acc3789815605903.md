### Title
`privatize` burns full counterpart tokens but caps the pot transfer to available balance, silently destroying the shortfall - ([File: substrate/frame/nis/src/lib.rs])

### Summary
The NIS (Non-Interactive Staking) pallet's `privatize` extrinsic converts a communal receipt back into a private one: it burns the caller's `Counterpart` fungible tokens representing the receipt's full `proportion`, then transfers/holds the corresponding value from the pallet's pot account to the caller. When the pot's actual balance is lower than the value implied by the receipt's proportion, the transferred amount is silently capped to whatever the pot can afford, but the full counterpart-token amount is still burned and the receipt's recorded `on_hold` value is permanently set to the reduced amount — with no shortfall record kept anywhere, matching the `_safeRewardsTransfer` bug class (capping payout to available balance without tracking the deficit).

### Finding Description
In `Pallet::privatize`: [1](#0-0) 
the entitled amount `max_amount` is computed from `receipt.proportion * effective_issuance`, but the actually transferred `amount` is capped via `max_amount.min(T::Currency::balance(&our_account))` — i.e., limited to whatever liquid balance currently sits in the pallet's pot account.

Immediately after, the full counterpart amount (derived from the *uncapped* `receipt.proportion`, not from `amount`) is burned from the caller: [2](#0-1) 

Only the capped `amount` is then transferred into hold and recorded as the receipt's new value: [3](#0-2) 

The comment "Avoid trying to place more in the account's reserve than we have available in the pot" makes explicit that this is a deliberate safety cap, exactly like the `_safeRewardsTransfer` capping in the external report. However, unlike a reward-transfer shortfall that could in theory be retried, here the user's claim right (`Counterpart` tokens) is irreversibly destroyed in the same call for the *full* proportion, while the private receipt is only credited with the reduced `amount`. There is no mapping or storage field recording the difference (`max_amount - amount`) for later top-up, and no mechanism exists elsewhere in the pallet to compensate the caller — the deficit is simply lost.

Existing guards do not stop this: `ensure!(receipt.owner.is_none(), ...)` only checks that the receipt isn't already privatized, and there is no check that `T::Currency::balance(&our_account) >= max_amount` before proceeding. The `.min()` capping instead lets execution proceed with a reduced value, which is exactly the failure mode called out in the external report.

### Impact Explanation
This breaks the value-conservation invariant that "Balances, assets, NFTs, staking, pools, treasury spends, bridge rewards, and contract-held value must conserve value and settle exactly once to the rightful beneficiary and amount." A user's NIS receipt entitlement can be reduced without any recourse whenever the pot's liquid balance momentarily dips below the receipt's `effective_issuance`-derived value (which can fluctuate as the total issuance and other outstanding receipts change over time). Because the burn of `Counterpart` tokens is unconditional and uncapped while the transfer is capped, the caller permanently loses the difference — a form of unbacked value destruction/fund loss within a core Substrate pallet.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any signed account holding a communal receipt (obtained validly via `communify`) can call `privatize` themselves — no privileged, governance, or validator/collator role is needed. The pot balance vs. `effective_issuance`-derived `max_amount` relationship depends on global pallet state (aggregate outstanding receipts, thaw/communify/privatize activity from other users) rather than the caller's own actions, so the shortfall condition can arise organically under normal usage patterns, especially as the pot balance is shared across all receipts and can be drawn down by other users' concurrent `thaw`/`privatize` calls in the same or preceding blocks.

### Recommendation
Before burning any `Counterpart` tokens, verify that the pot can cover the full `max_amount` (e.g., `ensure!(T::Currency::balance(&our_account) >= max_amount, Error::<T>::Unfunded)`), or alternatively burn counterpart tokens proportionally to `amount` (the capped value) rather than to the full `receipt.proportion`, and track any residual proportion in the receipt so the user retains their remaining conversion right instead of losing it outright.

### Proof of Concept
1. Multiple users hold communal receipts backed by the shared pot (`Self::account_id()`), whose liquid balance corresponds to the sum of currently-communal receipts' value.
2. Attacker (or any receipt owner) calls `communify` to bring their receipt into communal mode, then observes/waits for other participants to draw the pot's balance down via their own `thaw`/`privatize` calls (normal pallet activity, not required to be malicious).
3. Attacker calls `privatize(index)` on their own receipt when `T::Currency::balance(&our_account) < max_amount` for their proportion.
4. `T::Counterpart::burn_from` executes for the full `T::CounterpartAmount::convert(receipt.proportion)`, but `T::Currency::transfer_and_hold` only moves the capped `amount`.
5. The receipt is now private with `on_hold == amount < max_amount`, and the attacker's counterpart tokens for the full proportion are gone — the difference is unrecoverable, with no event, storage record, or later top-up mechanism reflecting the shortfall.
