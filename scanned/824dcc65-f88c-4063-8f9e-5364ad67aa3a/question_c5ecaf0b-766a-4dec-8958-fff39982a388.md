Target: RequestMessage::request (Request::external NearPromise list) built via WalletImpl::build_promise in contracts/wallet/src/contract.rs. Attacker action: attacker signs a RequestMessage whose Request::external contains a NearAction::FunctionCall targeting an attacker-deployed receiver contract with a large deposit, then that receiver's own logic (fully attacker controlled) mutates its own accounted state; nothing in build_promise re-checks that the promise's total_deposit() was actually escrowed or reconciled against wallet's own NEAR balance after the promise resolves. Preconditions: wallet has NEAR balance >= Request::total_deposit(). Call sequence: execute_signed -> execute_request -> build_promise(promise).detach() fires-and-forgets all external promises concurrently with no resolve callback recorded in Nonces or State. Binding: sum of NearAction::Transfer/FunctionCall deposits actually sent == the amount the signer's request authorized, verified after promise settlement. Impact: Critical, since detach() means no `*_resolve_*` ever reconciles a failed/refunded promise back into wallet accounting, an attacker-controlled receiver returning failure still leaves the wallet believing the transfer succeeded, permanently misstating funds availability. Proof: cargo test in near-workspaces sandbox deploying a receiver that always fails FunctionCall, submitting a signed Request with that action, and asserting wallet state has no compensating credit though the deposit fund left the account.
