# [M] ConfigBuilder._prepareWithdrawals() assumes that reserve is always sold and never bought

## Summary
Severity: Medium
Contest weight: 0.0449
Dataset id: 13540
Source: https://huggingface.co/datasets/Zaevlad/audit-findings-dataset
Type: audit-finding

## Details
The vulnerability resides in the withdrawal preparation routine of the vault contract, specifically the function ConfigBuilder._prepareWithdrawals. The routine assumes that the first element of the withdrawals array always represents the reserve asset and that this reserve amount is present in withdrawals[0].amounts[RESERVE_INDEX]. This assumption is only valid when the rebalancing algorithm in RebalancingLib decides to sell the reserve, i.e., when the current reserve balance expressed in the base currency (currentInBase) is greater than the target balance (targetInBase). In that case the library pushes a sell delta and records the reserve amount in the first withdrawal slot. However, when the protocol needs to increase the reserve weight, the rebalancing logic may decide to buy the reserve (currentInBase is not greater than targetInBase). In that scenario the reserve is not placed in withdrawals[0] and the reserve‑specific logic in _prepareWithdrawals is executed on a non‑existent entry. The code subtracts Math.min(reserveBalance, homeWithdrawal.amounts[RESERVE_INDEX]) from the reserve amount and may also remove the RESERVE_INDEX from the currency set, even though the set never contained it. As a result the withdrawal data becomes inconsistent: the protocol believes the reserve has been sold when it has actually been bought, leading to an accounting mismatch. From a user’s perspective a withdrawal may appear to be missing part of the expected funds, or a UI may show a zero balance for the reserve while the protocol’s internal state still holds reserve tokens. The impact is a potential loss of funds or incorrect distribution of assets, which can break the economic guarantees of the vault and affect all liquidity providers and users who rely on accurate withdrawals. The issue is triggered only under the specific condition where a rebalance results in a reserve purchase, a path that is rarely exercised in normal operation and therefore easy to overlook during testing. The flaw was discovered during a manual audit of the contract’s withdrawal logic, where the auditor noticed that the code unconditionally accesses withdrawals[0].amounts[RESERVE_INDEX] without checking the presence of the reserve index in the currency set. To remediate the problem the function should first verify that homeWithdrawal.currencyIndexSet.contains(RESERVE_INDEX) before applying any reserve‑specific subtraction or removal logic, and simply skip that block when the reserve is not part of the withdrawal set. This change ensures that the withdrawal preparation correctly reflects the actual composition of assets, preserving accounting integrity and preventing unexpected loss of funds.

## Recommendation
If the homeWithdrawal.currencyIndexSet does not contain the RESERVE_INDEX, the correct behavior is to skip the reserve specific logic in ConfigBuilder._prepareWithdrawals().
