### Title
`lending_account_withdraw` burns shares for the full requested amount but transfers a clamped, lesser amount during tokenless-repayment sunset - ([File: programs/marginfi/src/instructions/marginfi_account/withdraw.rs])

### Summary
When a bank has `TOKENLESS_REPAYMENTS_COMPLETE` set (post-deleverage/bankruptcy sunset), `lending_account_withdraw` computes and burns asset shares based on the user's full requested/entitled `amount_pre_fee`, but then silently clamps the actual token transfer down to whatever remains in the liquidity vault. The difference between the entitled amount (shares burned for) and the actual amount received is never tracked or refunded — an on-chain analog of the yAxis `Vault.withdraw` M-03 finding ("burns too many shares" when the underlying source cannot cover the requested amount).

### Finding Description
In `lending_account_withdraw`, the share-burning decision and the token-transfer amount are computed in two separate, un-synchronized steps: [1](#0-0) 

Here `amount_pre_fee` is derived purely from the user's request (or `withdraw_all`'s full entitled balance), and `bank_account.withdraw(I80F48::from_num(amount_pre_fee))` immediately mutates both the user's and bank's share balances for that entire amount.

Only afterward is the actually-transferred amount clamped: [2](#0-1) 

The clamped `amount_pre_fee` (`u64::min(amount_pre_fee, actual)`) is what gets passed to `bank.withdraw_spl_transfer`: [3](#0-2) 

There is no recomputation of `share_amount`/the actual shares burned to match the clamped, lower amount — the shares were already irreversibly decremented for the full pre-clamp amount inside `bank_account.withdraw()` / `bank_account.withdraw_all()`. This is structurally identical to the yAxis bug: `_amount` is reduced to what's actually available (`_balance.add(_diff)` in the report vs. `u64::min(amount_pre_fee, actual)` here), but the corresponding share/burn amount is never reduced to match.

This code path is reachable by any unprivileged holder of a position in a bank that has completed its tokenless-repayment (deleverage) cycle — calling the ordinary, permissionless `lending_account_withdraw` instruction (`withdraw_all: Some(true)` or a partial `amount`) triggers it. It is not a privileged/admin action; the admin only sets `TOKENLESS_REPAYMENTS_ALLOWED`/triggers deleverage earlier, but the flawed accounting is executed by the withdrawing user's own unprivileged call.

### Impact Explanation
Affected depositors are charged the full share cost of their entitled withdrawal but receive strictly less in tokens whenever the liquidity vault balance is less than their entitled `amount_pre_fee`. Their position (or partial balance) is closed/reduced as if they had been paid in full, permanently forfeiting the shortfall with no accounting trail (unlike the legitimate floor-dust handling elsewhere, e.g. `collected_insurance_fees_outstanding` in `withdraw_all`, which only tracks rounding dust, not vault-insufficiency shortfalls). This is a direct, unrecoverable loss of user funds — the exact "burns too many shares" pattern from the M-03 report, materialized as: shares burned for `amount_pre_fee` (pre-clamp) while `withdraw_spl_transfer` only sends `min(amount_pre_fee, actual)`.

### Likelihood Explanation
The bank-level test suite (`tests/specs/bankruptcy/zb02_e2eSunset.spec.ts`) explicitly demonstrates and asserts this exact scenario — a depositor calling `withdrawAll` on a bank whose deleverage completed with insufficient liquidity gets exactly `liqVaultBefore` tokens while their full share balance is closed: [4](#0-3) 

This confirms the behavior is reachable and reproducible in the current codebase whenever a bank's liquidity vault balance is less than the sum of depositors' entitled claims after a tokenless-repayment/deleverage sunset — a realistic and documented lifecycle stage per `guides/ADMIN/BANK_STATE.md`. Whether this is an accepted/intended trade-off of the bankruptcy-sunset design (first-come-first-served liquidation of remaining vault funds) or an unintended bug is not fully resolvable from the available documentation and test comments alone; the test's framing ("gets just what's left") suggests the team is aware of and has tested this exact behavior, which may indicate it is treated as by-design rather than a defect requiring a fix.

### Recommendation
When `TOKENLESS_REPAYMENTS_COMPLETE` forces `amount_pre_fee` to be clamped to the vault's actual balance, recompute (or proportionally reduce) the shares to be burned based on the clamped amount rather than the original pre-clamp entitlement — mirroring the yAxis fix of recomputing `_shares` from the actually-paid `_amount`. Alternatively, explicitly track/queue the unpaid shortfall (similar to `collected_insurance_fees_outstanding`) so affected depositors are not silently shorted, and document this behavior clearly if it is intentional so it is not mistaken for an accounting bug.

### Proof of Concept
1. Set up a bank, have it enter the `TOKENLESS_REPAYMENTS_ALLOWED` deleverage flow, and let the risk admin discharge all debts such that `TOKENLESS_REPAYMENTS_COMPLETE` becomes set while the liquidity vault holds less than the sum of remaining depositors' entitled asset amounts (as constructed in `tests/specs/bankruptcy/zb02_e2eSunset.spec.ts`, lines 445-525).
2. A depositor (user 1 in the test) calls `lending_account_withdraw` with `withdraw_all: true` for their full position.
3. Internally, `bank_account.withdraw_all()` closes the user's full share balance based on their entitled asset amount (e.g., corresponding to `withdrawn` tokens).
4. Because `bank.get_flag(TOKENLESS_REPAYMENTS_COMPLETE)` is true, `amount_pre_fee` is clamped via `u64::min(amount_pre_fee, actual)` to `liqVaultBefore`, which is less than the entitled amount.
5. `bank.withdraw_spl_transfer` sends only `liqVaultBefore` tokens, confirmed at lines 599-608 of the spec (`assert.equal(lstAfter - lstBefore, liqVaultBefore)`), while the user's shares were already fully burned for the larger entitled amount — the shortfall is permanently lost with no compensating accounting.

    const [liqVault] = deriveLiquidityVault(bankrunProgram.programId, banks[1]);

        withdrawAll: true,
