## Title
Missing Slippage/Maximum-Amount Protection in `ExchangeInjectActuator` Allows Callers to Lose Funds to Front-Running - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java`)

### Summary
`ExchangeInjectContract` (bancor-style AMM liquidity injection) lets an owner add `tokenQuant` of one token to an exchange pair, and the actuator computes `anotherTokenQuant` — the amount of the paired token the caller is forced to pay — purely from the pool's current on-chain ratio at execution time. There is no caller-supplied maximum bound on `anotherTokenQuant` (unlike `ExchangeTransactionContract`, which has a `getExpected()` slippage-protection field checked in `ExchangeTransactionActuator`). This mirrors the reported Arrakis `mint()` issue: the caller can lose more funds than intended if the pool ratio shifts between transaction submission and execution.

### Finding Description
In `ExchangeInjectActuator.execute()`, `anotherTokenQuant` is derived from `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` at execution time: [1](#0-0) 

The corresponding `doValidate()` only checks that `tokenQuant > 0`, that the resulting balances stay under `balanceLimit`, and that the caller has enough balance for `tokenQuant` — it never validates `anotherTokenQuant` against any caller-specified maximum: [2](#0-1) 

By contrast, `ExchangeTransactionActuator` (the trading path) explicitly protects the caller with a `tokenExpected` field and a `require`-style check: [3](#0-2) 

The `ExchangeInjectContract` protobuf carries only `owner_address`, `exchange_id`, `token_id`, and `quant` — no equivalent bound for the second token, so a broadcaster cannot cap how much of the second asset they're willing to lock in.

### Impact Explanation
Because the pool ratio (`firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance`) can be moved by any other transaction acting on the same exchange (e.g., a preceding `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade or another `ExchangeInjectContract`) that lands in the same block or an earlier block before the victim's inject is packed, a block producer or any observer of the pending transaction can front-run/sandwich the inject to shift the ratio and force the caller to pay significantly more of the second token than they anticipated when signing the transaction — a direct loss of funds for the account. This is a concrete unauthorized/unexpected asset debit reachable by any account with an inject-eligible exchange (any account that created or is otherwise permitted to inject, per the actuator's checks).

### Likelihood Explanation
Any user broadcasting an `ExchangeInjectContract` transaction is exposed every time, since there is no built-in slippage limit; likelihood scales with pool ratio volatility and mempool dwell time, similar to standard AMM front-running/sandwich conditions. No special privileges beyond being able to submit `ExchangeInjectContract` (already gated by ownership/balance checks) are required.

### Recommendation
Add a caller-specified maximum (e.g., `expected`/`max_another_token_quant`) field to `ExchangeInjectContract`, and validate in `ExchangeInjectActuator.doValidate()` that the computed `anotherTokenQuant` does not exceed it, mirroring the existing slippage protection in `ExchangeTransactionActuator` (`tokenExpected`/`anotherTokenQuant < tokenExpected` check).

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker observes a pending `ExchangeInjectContract` transaction in the mempool that injects `tokenQuant` of `firstTokenID`, expecting to also lock roughly `X` of `secondTokenID` based on the current pool ratio.
2. Attacker submits (and gets included first, e.g. as block producer or via fee/ordering advantage) a trade or inject that shifts `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` significantly.
3. The victim's original transaction executes afterward; `ExchangeInjectActuator.execute()` recomputes `anotherTokenQuant` from the new (manipulated) ratio, forcing the victim to lock a much larger amount of `secondTokenID` than intended, with no on-chain mechanism to reject the trade since `doValidate()` never checks any caller-provided upper bound.
