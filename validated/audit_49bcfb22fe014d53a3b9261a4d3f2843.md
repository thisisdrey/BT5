## Analysis

The Frax bug class (reward-release timing that lets an attacker sandwich a yield injection to steal from other depositors) has a direct — and in some ways *stronger* — analog in marginfi's `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` instruction, which injects same-mint "emissions" directly into a bank's liquidity vault and instantaneously reprices `asset_share_value` for all current shareholders, with no vesting/cycle delay at all.

### Title
Permissionless `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` Enables Front-Run/Back-Run Theft of Injected Yield from Existing Depositors - (File: `programs/marginfi/src/instructions/marginfi_group/configure_bank.rs`)

### Summary
`lending_pool_emissions_deposit` immediately and fully applies an injected reward amount to `asset_share_value` for the entire current pool of `total_asset_shares`, with zero vesting period, unlike even the vulnerable Frax `xERC4626` (which at least linearly unlocks over a `rewardsCycleLength`). Because the repricing is instant and applied pro-rata to whoever currently holds shares, an attacker can deposit into the bank immediately before this instruction executes and withdraw immediately after, capturing a pro-rata slice of the injected reward without having held any economic exposure, diluting the yield legitimately owed to depositors who were in the bank before the injection.

### Finding Description
In `lending_pool_emissions_deposit`, the reward amount is added directly to `total_assets`, and `asset_share_value` is recomputed as `(total_assets + amount) / total_asset_shares` without minting new shares: [1](#0-0) 

This instruction is explicitly permissionless — any signer can call it (funding it themselves), and it is documented as such: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

Standard deposit/withdraw flows (`lending_account_deposit`/`lending_account_withdraw`) compute shares/assets using the bank's current `asset_share_value`, with no minimum holding period, cooldown, or vesting mechanism between deposit and withdrawal: [4](#0-3) 

This is the root cause: whenever *any* party (the group admin, an integrator, or an unrelated permissionless caller) is about to call `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` to fund a reward, an MEV-aware unprivileged user can:
1. Observe the pending `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` transaction in the mempool/leader schedule.
2. Front-run it with `lending_account_deposit` into the same bank, acquiring shares at the pre-injection `asset_share_value`.
3. Let the emissions-deposit transaction land, which raises `asset_share_value` pro-rata across *all* current `total_asset_shares` (including the attacker's freshly minted shares).
4. Back-run with `lending_account_withdraw` (or `withdraw_all`), realizing an immediate profit equal to their pro-rata share of the injected reward, with effectively zero holding duration and zero market risk.

This is a stronger variant of the Frax exploit "Case 2" (deposit right before rewards materialize, then redeem to capture multiple cycles of yield): in marginfi there is no cycle/vesting mechanism whatsoever, so the entire injected reward is exposed to instantaneous sandwiching in a single pair of adjacent transactions, rather than requiring the attacker to wait out a `rewardsCycleLength`.

### Impact Explanation
Each successful sandwich transfers value from bona fide, longer-term depositors (whose rightful share of the injected reward is diluted by the attacker's temporary shares) to the attacker. This is a direct, unauthorized transfer of yield/value away from other unprivileged users of the same bank — matching the "concrete theft" bar. The magnitude scales with the size of the emissions deposit and the attacker's ability to size their sandwich deposit within the bank's `deposit_limit`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is bounded by whether `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` transactions are visible ahead of confirmation (mempool/leader visibility) and whether the caller batches the funding transfer atomically with other guarded logic. Since the instruction is permissionless and unguarded by any commit-reveal, minimum notice period, or same-slot restriction, any searcher monitoring pending transactions targeting this instruction (or any bot triggering it periodically) can execute the sandwich. This requires no privileged access and is reachable by any unprivileged user, consistent with the required threat model.

### Recommendation
- Do not apply emission/reward injections instantaneously and fully to the current share pool. Introduce a linear vesting mechanism (analogous to `xERC4626`'s `rewardsCycleLength`) so that injected rewards unlock gradually rather than being immediately reflected in `asset_share_value`.
- Alternatively/additionally, enforce a minimum holding period (e.g., disallow withdrawal of shares deposited within N slots/seconds of an emissions deposit) or restrict who may call `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` (e.g., require it be paired atomically, in the same instruction, with the funding source, removing mempool visibility) to prevent sandwiching.
- Consider snapshotting eligible shares (e.g., based on `last_update` prior to the emissions-deposit slot) before applying the reward, so freshly-deposited shares are excluded from the current distribution.

### Proof of Concept
1. Group admin (or any other unprivileged caller) submits a transaction calling `lending_pool_emissions_deposit(bank, amount=X)` to reward depositors of `bank`.
2. Attacker observes this transaction pending and submits `lending_account_deposit(bank, amount=Y)` in the immediately preceding slot, minting `Y / asset_share_value_before` shares.
3. The emissions-deposit transaction executes: [1](#0-0)  — `asset_share_value` rises for the entire `total_asset_shares` pool, including the attacker's newly minted shares.
4. Attacker immediately submits `lending_account_withdraw(bank, withdraw_all=true)`, redeeming their shares at the new, higher `asset_share_value` and realizing `Y * (X / (total_assets_before + Y))` profit, funded at the expense of the pre-existing depositors' expected reward share.
