### Title
Fee-on-transfer / non-standard underlying tokens break vault accounting via unverified `receive-underlying` / `send-underlying` calls - ([File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar])

### Summary
Every vault (`v0-vault-usdc.clar`, `v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`, `v0-vault-stx.clar`) credits/debits its internal `assets` accounting variable using the *nominal* amount passed to `deposit`/`redeem`, rather than the amount actually moved by the underlying token's `transfer` call. If any of these underlying SIP-010 tokens (all externally-controlled contracts such as `usdcx`, `sbtc`, `wstx`, `ststx`, `ststxbtc`, `usdh`) ever transfers less than the requested amount (fee-on-transfer, deflationary/rebasing behavior, or any other non-1:1 transfer semantics), the vault's internal share-price accounting becomes permanently desynchronized from its real token balance.

### Finding Description
`receive-underlying` in the vault simply calls the underlying token's `transfer` and returns `(ok true)` without ever comparing the vault's token balance before and after the call: [1](#0-0) 

`deposit` then unconditionally increments `assets` by the full nominal `amount`, and mints shares computed from `convert-to-shares-preview(amount)`, which is derived from `total-assets-preview()` → `var-get assets` (not the real on-chain balance): [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

Symmetrically, `redeem` decrements `assets` by the nominal `inkind` amount and calls `send-underlying`, which also does not verify the amount actually received by the recipient: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

The same pattern (`receive-underlying`/`send-underlying` with no balance reconciliation, `assets` var driving `total-assets-preview`/`convert-to-shares-preview`/`convert-to-assets-preview`) is repeated identically across all six vaults, and is also relied upon by the market's combined supply/collateral flows (`supply-collateral-add`, `collateral-remove-redeem`) which transfer the underlying into the market and then call `vault-deposit`/`vault-redeem` on the user's behalf: [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

Because none of these underlying tokens are Zest-controlled contracts, if any of them applies a transfer fee/deduction (a behavior the token issuer can add or change at any time, exactly analogous to USDT's fee-on-transfer switch referenced in the report), an ordinary user calling `deposit` would have the vault credit itself (and mint shares) for more assets than it actually received. Over repeated deposits this inflates `assets` relative to the vault's real token balance, meaning `total-assets-preview`/`convert-to-assets-preview` overstate backing per share. Later redeemers computing `inkind` off this inflated preview will have their `send-underlying` calls attempt to move more real tokens out than the vault actually holds, eventually reverting for later redeemers (funds frozen) or, since `get-available-assets`/`available-assets` checks are also driven by the same unreconciled `assets` var, silently paying out from tokens that back other users' shares (a share-price/insolvency drift) — since redeem also uses the un-reconciled internal state as the sole gate.

### Impact Explanation
This lands in the "permanent freezing of funds" / "protocol insolvency" impact classes: once the underlying token diverges from a strict 1:1 transfer (fee-on-transfer or similar), the accounting drift is impossible to correct on-chain (there is no reconciliation call), and later legitimate redeemers cannot withdraw their full entitled `min-out` because the vault's real balance is insufficient relative to the inflated `assets`/share-price state — the same failure mode ("blocking all functions ... for that particular token") described in the referenced report, mapped onto Zest's vault deposit/redeem/collateral-add/collateral-remove-redeem paths.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is contingent on whether any of the six underlying tokens (`usdcx`, `sbtc`, `wstx`, `ststx`, `ststxbtc`, `usdh`) is fee-on-transfer today or becomes one via a future upgrade of its own (non-Zest) contract — this is outside Zest's control but is a normal supported-asset risk the vault code does nothing to defend against, unlike protocols that verify actual balance delta on every transfer-in/out. No privileged action or DAO compromise is required to trigger the drift; any ordinary `deposit`/`supply-collateral-add` call by any user is sufficient once such a token exists.

### Recommendation
In `receive-underlying` and `send-underlying` (all six vault contracts), measure the vault's actual underlying balance before and after the `transfer` call (using the existing `ubalance` helper) and use the *actual* delta, not the nominal `amount`, to update `assets` and to determine shares minted/burned and amounts paid out. Alternatively, revert the transaction if the actual amount transferred does not equal the requested nominal amount, refusing to onboard non-1:1-transfer tokens rather than silently mis-accounting them.

### Proof of Concept
1. Assume (or the token issuer for one of `usdcx`/`sbtc`/`wstx`/`ststx`/`ststxbtc`/`usdh`) enables a transfer fee, so `transfer amount from to` moves `amount * (1 - fee)` to `to`.
2. User calls `deposit(amount, min-out, recipient)` on e.g. `v0-vault-usdc.clar` (or `market.clar`'s `supply-collateral-add`, which is a reachable unprivileged entry point) [8](#0-7) .
3. `receive-underlying` succeeds (transfer doesn't revert, it just moves less) [9](#0-8) ; `deposit` still adds the full nominal `amount` to `assets` and mints shares for the full amount [10](#0-9) .
4. Vault's `assets` variable is now higher than its real token balance. Repeat across multiple deposits to widen the gap.
5. A later user calling `redeem` computes `inkind` from the inflated `total-assets-preview`, and `send-underlying` either reverts (funds temporarily/permanently frozen for that redeemer) once the real balance is exhausted, or pays out tokens that were actually backing other depositors' shares.
