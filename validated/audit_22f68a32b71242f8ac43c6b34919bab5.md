## Analog Found: Instant, Un-timelocked `consume_user_resource_percent` Changes via `UpdateSettingContract` Can Silently Shift Fee Burden Onto Trusting Callers

### Title
Un-timelocked `UpdateSettingContractActuator` lets a compromised contract-owner key retroactively shift 100% of energy cost onto unsuspecting callers with no warning - ([File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java])

### Summary
The Harpie report's core issue is that a single compromised privileged role (`feeController`) can instantly and unilaterally change a fee parameter that is applied to funds/assets already committed by users (via approvals), with no timelock to let victims react. The analogous role in java-tron is the smart-contract **origin/owner address** acting through `UpdateSettingContractActuator`, which can change `consume_user_resource_percent` on an already-deployed, already-integrated contract at any time, with immediate effect and no delay.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.validate()` only requires that the caller's `owner_address` match the contract's stored `origin_address` [1](#0-0) , and the only bound on the new value is `[0, 100]` [2](#0-1) . On `execute()`, the new percent is written directly into the `ContractCapsule` and takes effect for the very next transaction, with no delay, no proposal, and no notice to users who already interact with (and have TRC10/TRC20 approvals routed through) that contract [3](#0-2) .

This percentage directly controls how the TVM splits energy payment between the contract's `origin` account and the transaction `caller` in `getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio` and in the final billing step `TransactionTrace.pay()` [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) . If a contract owner's key is compromised, the attacker can instantly set `consume_user_resource_percent` to `100`, forcing every caller who has come to rely on the contract's energy subsidization (and who may have standing approvals expecting cheap interaction) to now pay full cost per call - exactly mirroring the Harpie pattern of a single compromised privileged address unilaterally and instantly altering the economic terms that users are locked into via prior approvals, with no timelock buffer for victims to revoke exposure.

### Impact Explanation
Users who integrate with or hold TRC10/TRC20 approvals routed through the affected contract can be forced to pay substantially higher (up to maximum) energy fees on every subsequent interaction the moment the owner key is compromised, with no on-chain delay to detect and react (e.g., revoke approvals or migrate) before being economically exploited. This is a direct value-extraction vector against unsuspecting counterparties of the compromised contract, analogous to the ransom-style fee-shift described in the source report.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires compromise of the specific contract's `origin_address` private key, but that key is a normal, unprivileged EOA reachable exactly like the `transferEOA`/`feeController` model in the report — no SR/witness/committee collusion is needed, and the actuator is reachable directly via a single signed `UpdateSettingContract` transaction from any client (including through the HTTP `/wallet/updatesetting` and gRPC APIs) [6](#0-5) .

### Recommendation
Introduce a timelock/delay (and/or an event/notice window) before a change to `consume_user_resource_percent` takes effect, similar to the report's recommendation for `feeController` updates, so that integrators and dependent callers have a window to detect the pending change and revoke approvals or otherwise adjust exposure before the new fee-split becomes active.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker compromises the private key of `origin_address` for a widely-used deployed contract that currently sets `consume_user_resource_percent` low (e.g., subsidizing user energy costs), attracting many callers with standing token approvals.
2. Attacker broadcasts an `UpdateSettingContract` transaction (`actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java`) setting `consume_user_resource_percent = 100`.
3. `execute()` writes the new percent immediately, with no timelock [7](#0-6) .
4. On the very next block, any caller triggering the contract via `TriggerSmartContract` is billed the full energy cost themselves per `TransactionTrace.pay()`'s `percent` computation [5](#0-4) , with no opportunity to have anticipated or avoided the change.
