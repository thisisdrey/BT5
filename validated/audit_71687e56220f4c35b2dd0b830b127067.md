Audit Report

## Title
Pool reserves are read from live account balance, enabling a first-LP donation/inflation attack that DOSes `pallet-asset-conversion` liquidity provision - (File: substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/lib.rs)

## Summary
`do_add_liquidity` and `do_remove_liquidity` derive `reserve1`/`reserve2` directly from the live token balance of the pool's sovereign account via `Self::get_balance`/`Self::get_reserves`, rather than from pallet-tracked accounting reconciled against LP-token issuance. An unprivileged first LP can mint a minimal LP position and then donate a large, out-of-band transfer of `asset1`/`asset2` directly to the pool's sovereign account (bypassing `add_liquidity` entirely), inflating the live balance used as "reserves" without minting corresponding LP tokens.

## Finding Description
`do_add_liquidity` reads reserves live from the pool account balance: [1](#0-0) 

For a non-zero `total_supply`, share minted is `min(amount1*total_supply/reserve1, amount2*total_supply/reserve2)`: [2](#0-1) 

`do_remove_liquidity` likewise computes payout from live reserves and total LP supply: [3](#0-2) 

Since the pool's sovereign account is an ordinary, deterministically-derivable `AccountId` (via `PoolLocator::address`), any unprivileged account can transfer tokens into it directly through `pallet-assets`/`pallet-balances`, outside of `add_liquidity`, inflating `reserve1`/`reserve2` without minting any LP tokens. Because the attacker who performs the initial deposit holds essentially all outstanding LP token supply at that point, a subsequent donation increases the *reserve* term in the payout formula while the attacker's proportional claim (`lp_redeem_amount/total_supply`) on that inflated reserve remains unchanged — letting them reclaim the donated funds via `remove_liquidity`. Meanwhile, any honest depositor calling `add_liquidity` after the donation but before the attacker withdraws will have `side1`/`side2` rounded down (due to the now-inflated `reserve1`/`reserve2` denominators), tripping `ensure!(lp_token_amount > T::MintMinLiquidity::get(), ...)` and reverting — a functional DoS on liquidity provision to that pool.

The two existing guards — `MintMinLiquidity` minted to the pool account on first liquidity, and the `lp_token_amount > MintMinLiquidity` check on subsequent mints — only bound rounding errors *within* the minting arithmetic at the moment of the call; neither reconciles the balance-derived reserve against the pallet's LP-token issuance, so an out-of-band balance transfer into the pool account is never detected or rejected.

## Impact Explanation
This matches the "public underpriced work that degrades ... processing" / value-conservation impact category: it is a public-entrypoint, unprivileged-attacker denial of service on a core AMM function (`add_liquidity`) that additionally allows the attacker to disproportionately reclaim reserve funds relative to their genuinely-minted LP share, violating the requirement that liquidity pools "conserve value and settle exactly once to the rightful beneficiary and amount." The DoS is pool-scoped (affects new/thin pools, most severely freshly created ones) rather than chain-wide, but it directly breaks the intended value-accounting invariant of the pallet used broadly for AssetHub XCM fee-swaps.

## Likelihood Explanation
`create_pool` and `add_liquidity` are ordinary signed extrinsics open to any account; the pool's sovereign account address is computable off-chain via `PoolLocator`, and transferring assets to an arbitrary `AccountId` via `pallet-assets`/`pallet-balances` requires no special privilege. No malicious validator, collator, relayer, or governance action is needed — this is executable end-to-end by a single unprivileged attacker with modest starting capital, and is repeatable against any newly created pool before a second honest LP successfully deposits.

## Recommendation
Track `reserve1`/`reserve2` as pallet-internal storage updated only through `add_liquidity`, `remove_liquidity`, and swap operations, instead of deriving them from the live balance of the pool's sovereign account. As a defense-in-depth alternative, implement "sync/skim" semantics (as in Uniswap V2/V3-style pools) so unaccounted balance deltas are separated from the reserve figure used for share-price computation, and/or reject liquidity operations when the balance-derived reserve diverges materially from a pallet-tracked reserve baseline.

## Proof of Concept
1. Attacker calls `create_pool(asset1, asset2)` then `add_liquidity` with `amount1 = amount2` large enough that `sqrt(amount1*amount2) - MintMinLiquidity > MintMinLiquidity` (per `calc_lp_amount_for_zero_supply`, `substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/lib.rs:1353-1368`), becoming the sole LP.
2. Attacker calls `pallet_assets::transfer`/`pallet_balances::transfer` directly to the pool's sovereign account (address derived via `PoolLocator::address`), sending a large amount of `asset1`/`asset2`, bypassing `add_liquidity` entirely.
3. A victim calls `add_liquidity` with a normal deposit; `reserve1`/`reserve2` read via `Self::get_balance` (`lib.rs:813-814`) reflect the inflated balances, `side1`/`side2` (`lib.rs:869-871`) round down toward zero, and `ensure!(lp_token_amount > T::MintMinLiquidity::get(), ...)` (`lib.rs:874-877`) reverts the victim's transaction — DoS confirmed.
4. Attacker calls `remove_liquidity`, burning their LP balance; `amount1 = lp_redeem_amount * reserve1 / total_supply` (`lib.rs:919-920`) returns a share of the inflated reserve, letting the attacker reclaim the donated funds disproportionate to genuinely minted LP tokens.
