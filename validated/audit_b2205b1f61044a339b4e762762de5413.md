### Title
Stale-oracle liquidation gate lets an underwater borrower self-liquidate and shift losses onto vault depositors - ([File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar])

### Summary
`liquidate` in `v0-4-market.clar` gates every price used for the health/LTV check through `price-resolve`, which reverts with `ERR-ORACLE-INVARIANT` whenever the feed is stale (`oracle-timestamp-fresh`). [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1)  Nothing in `liquidate` prevents `contract-caller` (the liquidator) from being the same principal as `borrower`, and the seized collateral (plus the liquidation bonus) is sent to `actual-receiver`, which defaults to `liquidator` if no separate `collateral-receiver` is given. [3](#0-2)  Combined with the in-band `price-feeds` parameter that lets any unprivileged caller push a fresh Pyth update atomically inside the same `liquidate` call, [4](#0-3)  this reproduces the HyperLend "stale price → delayed liquidation → profitable self-liquidation" bug class: while the on-chain price is stale, ordinary keepers cannot liquidate (call reverts), but the underwater borrower is free to wait, then submit the fresh price update and immediately self-liquidate their own position once it has fallen so far that liquidation is guaranteed to be profitable for the liquidator. Any resulting shortfall is socialized to the vault (lenders) via the bad-debt path. [5](#0-4) 

### Finding Description
`liquidate` computes `current-ltv` from `total-collateral-usd`/`total-debt-usd`, both of which are produced from `get-notional-evaluation`, which internally calls `price-resolve` for every relevant asset. [6](#0-5)  `price-resolve` enforces a per-asset `max-staleness` window and reverts the whole liquidation transaction with `ERR-ORACLE-INVARIANT` if any priced asset's timestamp is older than that window: [7](#0-6) 

```clarity
(define-private (oracle-timestamp-fresh (ts uint) (prev uint) (max-staleness uint))
  (let ((delta (if (> ts stacks-block-time) u0 (- stacks-block-time ts))))
    (and (<= delta max-staleness) (>= ts prev))))
...
(asserts! (and (oracle-price-legal final-price) (oracle-timestamp-fresh timestamp last-update-time max-staleness))
          ERR-ORACLE-INVARIANT)
```

This is functionally identical to the HyperLend `_isLoanLiquidatable` staleness guard cited in the report: any liquidation attempt made while the price is momentarily stale simply fails, regardless of how undercollateralized the position truly is off-chain. During that stale window a fast-moving collateral price can crash well past `LTV-LIQ-FULL`, so that by the time a fresh price lands the position is deeply insolvent rather than marginally liquidatable.

Unlike a naive lending market, Zest tries to solve staleness by letting the caller supply `price-feeds`, an optional list of raw Pyth update payloads that get verified and written atomically before the health check runs: [4](#0-3) [8](#0-7)  This removes the need to wait for a third party to post the update, but it does **not** close the exploit — it actually strengthens the borrower's hand, because the borrower (who is intimately aware of their own position's health and can watch the off-chain Pyth price feed) can hold the fresh price update in reserve and submit `liquidate` on themselves the instant the position has crashed far enough to guarantee a profitable self-liquidation, front-running any other keeper who would otherwise have picked up the position earlier (and closer to break-even for the vault).

`liquidate` has no check requiring `borrower != contract-caller`; the only identity-related assertion is `(asserts! (is-eq contract-caller tx-sender) ERR-AUTHORIZATION)`, which only blocks a proxy contract from acting on the caller's behalf, not self-liquidation. [9](#0-8)  The seized collateral (`coll-final`, including the liquidation bonus baked into `liq-penalty`) is transferred to `actual-receiver`, which defaults to `liquidator` (i.e., the borrower themselves) unless a different `collateral-receiver` was passed: [10](#0-9) 

If the position's remaining collateral is fully consumed by the seizure (`no-collateral-left`), the leftover unrepaid scaled debt is passed into `socialize-debt-asset`, which — per the print event `"bad-debt-socialized"` — spreads the residual loss across the vault's depositors (the lenders): [11](#0-10) 

So the exact same causal chain as the HyperLend report holds:
1. Stale-price revert blocks timely liquidation while collateral value is still crashing.
2. Position becomes severely undercollateralized before any fresh price lands on-chain.
3. The underwater borrower (with privileged knowledge of their own position and the ability to submit `price-feeds` themselves) self-liquidates at that point, collecting the liquidation bonus that a neutral third-party keeper would normally have earned.
4. Any resulting shortfall between debt and seized collateral value is socialized onto the vault, i.e., paid for by lenders/depositors rather than the borrower.

### Impact Explanation
This causes lenders/depositors to absorb bad debt that is created specifically because the liquidation-triggering price was allowed to go stale, while the borrower who caused/benefited from the delay captures the liquidation bonus for their own account. Depositor funds are directly reduced through `socialize-debt-asset` covering the shortfall the borrower's self-liquidation created — this is a direct loss of principal held in the vault, i.e., theft of/loss to funds at rest, and in aggregate (repeatable across many undercollateralized positions during volatile markets or oracle downtime) can push a vault into insolvency. This lands in the **Critical** impact bucket (direct theft of user funds at rest / protocol insolvency).

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate-to-high in volatile markets: Pyth staleness windows (`max-staleness`) are necessarily short but finite, and any borrower who is close to `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL`/`LTV-LIQ-FULL` has a direct financial incentive to monitor their own position and self-liquidate the instant a large price move happens, using the exact same `price-feeds` in-band update mechanism that the protocol offers to every unprivileged caller. No special privileges, front-running infrastructure, or DAO compromise are required — only the borrower's own principal calling `liquidate` on themselves with a `price-feeds` payload they source from Pyth's public network.

### Recommendation
- Disallow `contract-caller` (or `tx-sender`) from being equal to `borrower` in `liquidate`/`liquidate-redeem`, or at minimum cap/zero the liquidation bonus paid to the borrower when they liquidate their own position, so self-liquidation cannot be profitable at the vault's expense.
- Consider computing liquidation eligibility/penalty using the *worst* (most conservative) of the last-known-good price and any newly-submitted in-band price, rather than allowing the caller who supplies the price update to also be the immediate, sole beneficiary of the liquidation triggered by that same update.
- Add a minimum delay/challenge window between a price update landing on-chain and its use for liquidation of positions whose owner is the same principal executing the liquidation, to remove the incentive to "time" self-liquidation around staleness gaps.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual reproduction (Clarity/simnet, mirroring the referenced Solidity PoC):
1. Borrower opens a position: deposits collateral near the `LTV-BORROW` cap and borrows debt asset, e.g. via `collateral-add` and `borrow`. [12](#0-11) 
2. Let the Pyth feed for the collateral asset go stale beyond `max-staleness` (simply don't push updates) while the true off-chain price crashes.
3. Any keeper attempt to call `liquidate(borrower, ...)` without `price-feeds` reverts with `ERR-ORACLE-INVARIANT` because `oracle-timestamp-fresh` fails. [13](#0-12) 
4. Wait until off-chain the collateral has crashed well past `LTV-LIQ-FULL` (deep insolvency).
5. Borrower, as `contract-caller == tx-sender == borrower`, calls `liquidate(borrower, collateral-ft, debt-ft, debt-amount, 0, none, (some [fresh-pyth-vaa]))`. `write-feeds` verifies/writes the fresh price atomically, `price-resolve` now passes staleness check, `current-ltv` is computed against the crashed price, health-check passes (`>= current-ltv ltv-liq-partial`), and the borrower (as `liquidator`) receives `coll-final` (their own collateral plus bonus) as `actual-receiver` defaults to `liquidator`. [10](#0-9) 
6. If collateral is insufficient to fully cover debt at that crashed price, `no-collateral-left` triggers `socialize-debt-asset`, spreading the shortfall over vault depositors. [5](#0-4) 
7. Net effect: borrower retains the liquidation bonus value while the vault (lenders) absorbs the socialized shortfall — the same asymmetric loss pattern demonstrated in the HyperLend PoC.
