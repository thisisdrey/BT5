### Title
`unbond` extrinsic can be permanently blocked by unrelated auto-withdraw slash check - (File: substrate/frame/staking-async/src/pallet/mod.rs)

### Summary
`pallet_staking_async::Pallet::unbond` triggers an internal "auto-withdraw" of a nominator/validator's oldest unlocking chunk whenever their `unlocking` vector is full (`unlocking == MaxUnlockingChunks`). This internal step propagates the result with `?`, meaning any failure of the auxiliary withdraw call aborts the entire `unbond` call, blocking the unrelated primary intent (creating a new unbonding chunk) as a side effect.

when `unlocking == MaxUnlockingChunks`, `Self::do_withdraw_unbonded(&controller)?` is called and its error is propagated directly, failing the whole extrinsic.

`do_withdraw_unbonded` enforces a slash-processing invariant unrelated to the caller's unbonding intent: [2](#0-1) 

`ensure_era_slashes_applied` fails with `Error::UnappliedSlashesInPreviousEra` whenever the offence-processing pipeline (`OffenceQueueEras`) has not caught up for the previous active era — a condition entirely outside the unbonding user's control, driven only by chain-wide offence backlog, exactly as described in the test `withdrawals_are_blocked_for_unprocessed_and_unapplied_slashes`: [3](#0-2) 

This mirrors the Derby bug-class: a public wrapper function (`rebalance` / `unbond`) internally performs a secondary state-mutating action (`pullFunds` / auto-`do_withdraw_unbonded`) whose success depends on an external, unrelated invariant (TrueFi/Idle same-block restriction / offence-processing backlog). When that invariant is violated, the secondary action reverts and takes down the entire primary operation with it, even though the primary operation (bonding a new unlock chunk / rebalancing allocations) had nothing to do with the violated invariant.

### Impact Explanation
Once a staker's `unlocking` chunks reach `MaxUnlockingChunks` and the chain accumulates enough unprocessed offences to trip `ensure_era_slashes_applied` for the previous era, that staker's `unbond` calls will unconditionally fail with `UnappliedSlashesInPreviousEra` until the offence backlog is cleared by the runtime (via `apply_pending_slashes_from_previous_era` or natural block progression). This is a temporary but chain-state-driven denial of a core staking primitive (`unbond`) for any user whose unlocking chunks are saturated, with no way for the affected user to work around it, only wait for chain-level state to change — analogous to the Derby vault being "blocked indefinitely" until external state (balance top-up) is corrected.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reaching `MaxUnlockingChunks` is common for active nominators who unbond frequently (`MaxUnlockingChunks` is typically a small, fixed value, e.g. 32 or lower in some configurations), and offence backlogs building up for even one era (as demonstrated by the pallet's own test suite deliberately engineering this scenario) is a realistic operational condition during periods of high slashing activity or short eras. No privileged actor, admin, or malicious party is required — it is a natural consequence of chain load combined with normal user unbonding behavior.

### Recommendation
Decouple the user-intent unbonding operation from the best-effort auto-withdraw cleanup: if `do_withdraw_unbonded` fails due to `UnappliedSlashesInPreviousEra` (or similar transient conditions), treat this as best-effort and continue, or return a clearer error that distinguishes "cannot create new unlock chunk because slots are full and cleanup is blocked" from an opaque propagated internal error, and consider whether the primary intent (recording the new unbond request) can still succeed independent of the ability to consolidate/withdraw already-expired chunks.
