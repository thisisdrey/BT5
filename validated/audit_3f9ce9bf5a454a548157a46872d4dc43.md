### Title
Attacker can permanently block a victim from receiving or depositing new collateral/debt token types by filling the victim's `MAX_TOKENS_PER_USER` account list with dust transfers - ([File: contracts/Pool.sol](contracts/Pool.sol))

### Summary
`Pool` tracks per-account deposit tokens and debt tokens in `MappedEnumerableSet.AddressSet` lists and enforces a combined cap of `MAX_TOKENS_PER_USER = 30`. `DepositToken._transfer` adds the token to the recipient's list whenever their balance goes from 0 to >0, and `Pool.addToDepositTokensOfAccount` reverts with `UserReachedMaxTokens` once the combined list length reaches 30. Since `DepositToken.transfer`/`transferFrom` are public and only check the *sender's* unlocked balance, an attacker can deposit dust amounts of every listed collateral and transfer 1 wei of each `DepositToken` to a victim, filling all 30 slots. Every subsequent mint/transfer/issue of a token the victim does not already hold reverts, denying the victim the ability to deposit new collateral types, receive deposit tokens, or mint new debt tokens. If the victim's balances are locked by outstanding debt (`_revertIfLocked`/`unlockedBalanceOf`), the victim cannot even transfer the dust back out to free a slot.

### Finding Description
- `Pool.MAX_TOKENS_PER_USER` is 30 and `onlyIfAdditionWillNotReachMaxTokens` counts `debtTokensOfAccount + depositTokensOfAccount` (`Pool.sol:79`, `Pool.sol:143-148`).
- `addToDepositTokensOfAccount`/`addToDebtTokensOfAccount` are external and invoked by the token contracts (`Pool.sol:204-208`).
- In `DepositToken._transfer`, whenever the recipient's prior balance is 0 and `amount_ > 0`, the pool adds the token to the recipient's list (`DepositToken.sol:517-520`). `seize` uses the same path (`DepositToken.sol:343-345`), and `_mint` (i.e. `deposit`) does the same (`DepositToken.sol:485-488`).
- `DepositToken.transfer`/`transferFrom` only verify the sender's unlocked balance via `_revertIfLocked` (`DepositToken.sol:180-182`, `348-376`); there is no opt-in or allowance requirement on the recipient side.
- Attack steps: attacker deposits minimal underlying into each of the pool's `DepositToken`s, then calls `transfer(victim, 1)` on each until the victim's list holds 30 entries. From then on:
  - `deposit(amount, victim)` on any collateral the victim doesn't already hold reverts inside `_mint → addToDepositTokensOfAccount`.
  - `DebtToken.issue` reverts for any new synthetic (its `_mint` calls `addToDebtTokensOfAccount`).
  - Any `transfer`/`seize` crediting a new token to the victim reverts.
- Removal requires the victim to `transfer`/`withdraw` a dust position to zero, but `transfer`/`withdraw` are gated by `_revertIfLocked` (`DepositToken.sol:350`, `406-409`): while the victim carries debt, `unlockedBalanceOf` can be 0, so the dust slots cannot be freed and the block persists until the debt is fully repaid or the position is liquidated.

### Impact Explanation
Victim accounts are denied core protocol operations — depositing additional (new-type) collateral and issuing new synthetic debt — with no governance or privileged action required. For a victim whose collateral is locked by debt, this translates into a temporary (potentially long-lived) freezing of their ability to add new collateral types to rescue an unhealthy position; recovery is limited to repaying debt or depositing into already-held tokens. On accounts with no ability to repay (e.g. contracts, leveraged SmartFarmingManager positions), the freeze can be effectively permanent until forced liquidation.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack only requires an unprivileged EOA, dust amounts of each listed underlying (capped at 30 tokens by the pool itself), and ordinary ERC20 transfers. Cost scales linearly with the number of listed collaterals and is near zero when cheap collaterals exist. The attack is fully reproducible on a mainnet fork: deposit dust into each `DepositToken` and `transfer(victim, 1)` repeatedly until `UserReachedMaxTokens` is hit. It can also be applied preemptively against known counterparty contracts. The main limitation is that victims with unlocked balances can clear the dust themselves, and victims can still deposit into collateral types they already hold — so realized impact depends on the victim's debt state.

### Recommendation
- Stop tracking tokens added via unsolicited inbound transfers, or make list membership opt-in (only `deposit`/`issue` add tokens, not `transfer`/`seize` to a zero-balance recipient).
- Alternatively, remove the recipient-side cap check for `_transfer`-based additions, or allow anyone to call `removeFromDepositTokensOfAccount`-equivalent cleanup, or let users force-remove a token entry when balance is below a dust threshold.
- At minimum, document that unsolicited dust transfers consume `MAX_TOKENS_PER_USER` slots and provide a permissionless way to evict dust entries.
