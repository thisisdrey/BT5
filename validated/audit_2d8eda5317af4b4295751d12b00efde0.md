### Title
Staked-collateral NAV computed as unguarded sum of stake-account and on-ramp lamports allows arbitrary donation-based price inflation - ([File: programs/marginfi/src/state/price.rs])

### Summary
Marginfi's `StakedWithPythPush` price path computes an LST/SOL exchange rate ("price multiplier") as `pool_nav / lst_supply`, where `pool_nav` is the raw, unguarded sum of two independently-controllable lamport balances: the native stake account and the SVSP "on-ramp" account.

### Finding Description
`staked_pool_net_asset_value()` sums `main_stake_value` (stake account lamports minus rent) and `onramp_value` (on-ramp account lamports minus rent) with no validation of either component individually, and no bound on the resulting NAV or on its rate of change: [1](#0-0) 

This combined value feeds directly into the oracle price multiplier used for every deposit/borrow/health/liquidation calculation for the bank: [2](#0-1) 

Just like the HyperCore report — where `getTotalProtocolHype()` aggregated `delegatorSummary` (delegated/undelegated/pending) plus idle spot/liquidity balances into a single guarded value, so that idle liquidity present in a component not intended to move could silently absorb/mask a bad or manipulated read — marginfi's staked NAV aggregates two lamport balances into one number consumed by the price/exchange-rate logic without any per-component sanity check or bound on the combined delta. The on-ramp account in particular is explicitly designed to receive "staging free SOL," and both the stake account and on-ramp account are plain SPL-single-pool/native-stake-program-owned accounts that accept arbitrary System Program lamport transfers. The test suite itself demonstrates this: sending an arbitrary `SystemProgram.transfer` directly to `splOnRampPool` or `splSolPool` immediately and silently moves the on-chain price multiplier with no guard rejecting the change: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

There is no analog of an "exchange rate guard" (bounding the multiplier's rate of change, or validating `main_stake_value`/`onramp_value` individually against expected delegation/withdrawal deltas) anywhere in `price.rs`; the only checks performed are key/ownership validation (`check_primary_oracle_key`, `StakePoolValidationFailed`, `ZeroSupplyInStakePool`), not value-sanity checks on the aggregated NAV: [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation
Because the aggregated NAV has no per-component bound, any actor can inflate the reported collateral price of a staked bank at will by transferring lamports to the on-ramp or stake account (both discoverable, deterministic PDAs). While a naive attacker donating real SOL does not directly profit, this removes any guard that would otherwise catch an anomalous/bad NAV read (e.g., a stale or manipulated stake-account/on-ramp balance during validator transitions, deactivation, or partial withdrawal races), since the aggregate is accepted unconditionally as long as key/ownership checks pass. This can allow the reported LST price to jump instantly and be used to borrow against inflated collateral before the true, expected NAV is restored, or to mask an otherwise-detectable bad read in one of the two summed components (mirroring the HyperCore root cause: aggregation defeats a would-be sanity guard on the underlying source of truth).

### Likelihood Explanation
The on-ramp/stake-pool NAV computation runs on every price-based instruction (deposit, borrow, health pulse, liquidation) for staked banks and is permissionlessly triggerable via `lending_pool_pulse_bank_price_cache`. The precondition (sending lamports to a system-owned PDA) requires no special privilege and is demonstrated as trivially reachable in the existing test suite.

### Recommendation
Guard `main_stake_value` and `onramp_value` individually rather than only bounding/trusting their unguarded sum: validate each component's value against expected bounds (e.g., maximum allowed delta since last cached NAV, or cross-checking against the stake program's authoritative delegation state) before folding them into `sol_pool_adjusted_balance`, so that an anomalous jump in either component cannot silently pass through to the price multiplier used across the risk engine.

### Proof of Concept
Demonstrated directly by the existing test suite (no new PoC needed — this is reachable production behavior, not a mock-only scenario): [3](#0-2) 
1. An unprivileged user calls `SystemProgram.transfer` sending 9 SOL directly to `validators[0].splOnRampPool`.
2. On the next permissionless `pulseBankPrice` call, `staked_pool_net_asset_value()` folds this donated balance into NAV with no guard, immediately changing `bank.cache.priceMultiplier` from 1.025 to 1.25 as asserted by the test.
3. The same unguarded pattern applies to `splSolPool` directly (`s05_solAppreciates.spec.ts` lines 94-114), where an arbitrary transfer to the stake account instantly doubles the reported collateral price with no bound/sanity check rejecting the magnitude or rate of the change.

fn staked_pool_net_asset_value(
    pool_stake_info: &AccountInfo,
    pool_onramp_info: &AccountInfo,
    rent: &Rent,
) -> MarginfiResult<u64> {
    let pool_rent_exempt_reserve = rent.minimum_balance(pool_stake_info.data_len());
    let onramp_rent_exempt_reserve = rent.minimum_balance(pool_onramp_info.data_len());

    let main_stake_value = pool_stake_info
        .lamports()
        .saturating_sub(pool_rent_exempt_reserve);
    let onramp_value = pool_onramp_info
        .lamports()
        .saturating_sub(onramp_rent_exempt_reserve);

    Ok(main_stake_value.saturating_add(onramp_value))
}
