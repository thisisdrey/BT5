## Analysis

The Trail of Bits finding describes a protocol that lets users move *partial* debt/collateral between "providers," but computes interest/usage only from the *current* provider's state rather than accounting correctly for the fact that debt/collateral may be spread across multiple providers — leading to inaccurate accounting and potential fund loss.

The closest reachable analog in java-tron is in the `UnDelegateResourceActuator`/`UnDelegateResourceProcessor` flow, where a resource **receiver** can have BANDWIDTH/ENERGY delegated to it from **multiple, independent owners** (multiple "providers"), but the usage-transfer calculation performed when *any one* of those owners partially un-delegates is computed from the receiver's **aggregate, pool-wide** usage and frozen balance rather than from the state attributable to that specific delegator relationship.

### Title
Un-delegation transfers usage computed from receiver's aggregate resource pool instead of the specific delegator relationship, corrupting bandwidth/energy accounting across unrelated delegators - ([File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UnDelegateResourceActuator.java])

### Summary
`UnDelegateResourceActuator.execute` (and the mirrored `UnDelegateResourceProcessor.execute` used by TVM `unDelegateResource`) computes how much of the receiver's *consumed* bandwidth/energy usage should be transferred back to the un-delegating owner using the receiver account's **total** `getNetUsage()`/`getEnergyUsage()` and **total** `getAllFrozenBalanceForBandwidth()`/`getAllFrozenBalanceForEnergy()` — quantities that aggregate resources delegated from *every* delegator to that receiver, not just the one performing the un-delegation. [1](#0-0) 

### Finding Description
When account A delegates bandwidth to receiver R, and separately account B also delegates bandwidth to the same receiver R, R's `getAllFrozenBalanceForBandwidth()` and `getNetUsage()` reflect the **combined** contribution/consumption from both A and B. When A calls `UnDelegateResourceContract` to withdraw part of its delegation, the actuator computes:

```
transferUsage = receiverCapsule.getNetUsage() * (unDelegateBalance / receiverCapsule.getAllFrozenBalanceForBandwidth())
``` [2](#0-1) 

This ratio is applied against R's *entire* resource pool (from all delegators combined) rather than being scoped to the DelegatedResourceCapsule keyed specifically by `(A, R)` — the per-relationship accounting entity that actually tracks how much A delegated to R. [3](#0-2) 

The validate step confirms sufficient balance only from the specific `DelegatedResourceCapsule` for `(owner, receiver)`: [4](#0-3) 

but the *usage* returned to the un-delegating owner (and subtracted from the receiver) is drawn from the pooled totals, meaning A can claw back "usage" that was actually generated by B's resources, or vice versa — the exact same class of bug as the report: an operation that only correctly tracks the balance side of a multi-provider relationship while computing the "rate"/usage side from an aggregate that doesn't accurately reflect the specific provider relationship being unwound.

The identical pattern exists in the TVM-native path: [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation
Bandwidth/energy usage is a form of resource entitlement with real economic value (it substitutes for TRX fee payment). Incorrectly shifting consumed-usage accounting between unrelated delegators means:
- An owner un-delegating a portion of resources can receive back usage credit that was actually consumed by a *different* delegator's contribution, effectively taking value that isn't theirs.
- The other delegator(s) sharing the same receiver end up with corrupted `netUsage`/`energyUsage` and `latestConsumeTime`, which can cause them to be charged TRX for bandwidth/energy they should still have, or conversely get free resources — a concrete unauthorized resource/value transfer between accounts, reachable by any unprivileged account via a signed `UnDelegateResourceContract` transaction or TVM `unDelegateResource` call.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account can freely delegate resources to any other account (`DelegateResourceContract`), and it's common (e.g., for TVM contracts receiving resources from multiple sponsors, or exchanges/services delegating to shared hot accounts) for a single receiver to have delegated-resource relationships from multiple distinct owners simultaneously. Triggering the miscalculation only requires: (1) two or more owners delegate BANDWIDTH/ENERGY to the same receiver, (2) the receiver consumes some usage, (3) one owner partially un-delegates. No special privileges are needed — this is directly reachable by any transaction broadcaster.

### Recommendation
- **Short term:** Scope the usage-transfer calculation in `UnDelegateResourceActuator`/`UnDelegateResourceProcessor` to the specific `DelegatedResourceCapsule` for the `(owner, receiver)` pair being un-delegated, rather than the receiver's pool-wide `getNetUsage()`/`getAllFrozenBalanceForBandwidth()` (and the ENERGY equivalents), so that usage clawback is proportional only to that owner's contribution.
- **Long term:** Introduce per-delegator usage/consumption tracking (or an explicit accounting invariant test) verifying that the sum of usage transferred back across all delegators of a receiver during un-delegation never exceeds what that specific delegator contributed, and add regression tests covering receivers with delegations from multiple distinct owners.

### Proof of Concept
1. Owner A freezes and delegates `X` bandwidth to receiver R (`DelegateResourceContract`).
2. Owner B independently freezes and delegates `Y` bandwidth to the same receiver R.
3. Receiver R consumes bandwidth via transactions, increasing `netUsage` to some value `U`, drawn from the combined pool `X + Y`.
4. Owner A partially un-delegates a portion of its `X` via `UnDelegateResourceContract`.
5. Observe that `transferUsage` in `UnDelegateResourceActuator.execute` (lines 80-88) is computed as `U * (unDelegateBalance / (X + Y))` rather than being bounded by A's actual share of consumption, and R's `netUsage`/A's `netUsage` are adjusted using this pool-wide ratio — demonstrating usage misattributed away from B's contribution to A.
