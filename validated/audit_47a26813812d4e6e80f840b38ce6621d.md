Audit Report

## Title
Permissionless, unwhitelisted AssetConversion pools let a tx signer manipulate their own pool's spot price to distort fee refunds - (File: substrate/frame/transaction-payment/asset-conversion-tx-payment/src/payment.rs)

## Summary
`pallet_asset_conversion::do_create_pool` allows any signed account to create an AMM pool for an arbitrary asset pair with no whitelist or admin check, subject only to a setup fee [1](#0-0) . `pallet_asset_conversion_tx_payment::SwapAssetAdapter` uses that same pool's live spot reserves both to withdraw a fee-asset amount before dispatch and to compute a refund after dispatch, with no mechanism tying the two quotes to a consistent price state [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) .

## Finding Description
`withdraw_fee` quotes `S::quote_price_tokens_for_exact_tokens(asset_id, A::get(), fee, true)` against the pool's reserves *before* the wrapped call runs, and performs a real AMM swap that mutates the pool's reserves at that pre-dispatch state [4](#0-3) . After the wrapped `RuntimeCall` executes (which is fully attacker-controlled and dispatched within the same extrinsic), `correct_and_deposit_fee` computes the refund by quoting `S::quote_price_exact_tokens_for_tokens(A::get(), asset_id, refund_amount, true)` against the pool's *current* (potentially attacker-shifted) reserves, then executes a real swap at that post-dispatch price [5](#0-4) . Because pool creation is permissionless/unwhitelisted [6](#0-5)  and the AMM only exposes instantaneous spot quotes with no TWAP or manipulation resistance ("the price may have changed by the time the transaction is executed") [7](#0-6) , a signer can embed a large swap against the fee-paying pool inside the dispatched call to move the pool's reserve ratio between the two quotes. This breaks the implicit symmetry that would otherwise make a withdraw/refund round-trip net a small loss (LP fee only) into a net-favorable round-trip for the attacker, at the expense of the pool's other liquidity providers — nothing in `correct_and_deposit_fee` checks that the refund-direction price is consistent with the price used at withdrawal time.

## Impact Explanation
This falls under "public underpriced work that degrades block production" and value-conservation violations for pool LPs: an unprivileged signer can use a normal signed extrinsic (`ChargeAssetTxPayment` + an attacker-chosen `RuntimeCall` that manipulates the chosen fee-asset pool) to extract more value on the refund leg than was fairly owed, funded by the pool's liquidity providers, while the network still collects only the nominal `corrected_fee`. No validator, collator, governance, or relayer collusion is needed — it is reachable purely through `AssetConversion::create_pool` (or any existing pool) plus `ChargeAssetTxPayment::from(tip, Some(asset_id))`.

## Likelihood Explanation
High for a capital-equipped but fully unprivileged actor: pool creation/discovery is public, the attacker fully controls the dispatched call executed between `withdraw_fee` and `correct_and_deposit_fee`, and `pallet_asset_conversion` deliberately only exposes spot-reserve quotes. No admin or validator action is required.

## Recommendation
Do not use raw, permissionlessly-manipulable AMM spot reserves as the price source for fee correction without safeguards. Options: (1) whitelist eligible fee-payment pools/assets, (2) cache and reuse the pre-dispatch quote/reserve state for the refund direction instead of re-querying post-dispatch, or (3) bound the allowed divergence between the withdrawal and refund exchange rates.

## Proof of Concept
1. Identify or create an `asset_X`/`Native` `pallet_asset_conversion` pool with liquidity from other LPs (or self-seeded).
2. Submit a signed extrinsic using `ChargeAssetTxPayment::from(tip, Some(asset_X))` whose `RuntimeCall` performs a large `AssetConversion::swap_exact_tokens_for_tokens` (or similar) against the same `asset_X`/`Native` pool, shifting its reserve ratio.
3. `withdraw_fee` withdraws `fee_asset_amount` of `asset_X` using pre-swap reserves.
4. The dispatched call executes, moving the pool's reserves.
5. `correct_and_deposit_fee` computes `refund_asset_amount` via `quote_price_exact_tokens_for_tokens` on the now-manipulated reserves and swaps the refund back at that manipulated rate, producing a refund inconsistent with the fair value that should have been returned, extracting value from the pool's other liquidity providers.
