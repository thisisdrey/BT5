### Title
No Slippage Control on `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` Enables Sandwich Attack on TRC10 Bancor-style Exchange Liquidity Operations - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java`, `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java`)

### Summary
Java-tron implements a bancor-style token-swap AMM ("Exchange") with three actuators: `ExchangeTransactionActuator` (trade), `ExchangeInjectActuator` (add liquidity), and `ExchangeWithdrawActuator` (remove liquidity). The trade actuator was hardened against price manipulation by adding an `expected` field to `ExchangeTransactionContract`, checked in `doValidate()` against the pool-computed output before execution [2](#0-1) . However, `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` were never given an equivalent `expected`/`minOut` parameter [3](#0-2) , leaving inject/withdraw operations exposed to exactly the same sandwich-attack pattern flagged in the external report for `IbbtcVaultZap.sol`'s unguarded `deposit`.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeInjectActuator.doValidate()` computes `anotherTokenQuant` (the counterpart amount required/returned) purely from the exchange's current on-chain `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` ratio at validation time [4](#0-3) , with no caller-supplied bound to reject an unfavorable ratio. `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.doValidate()` does the same, only checking rounding precision ("Not precise enough"), not an economic minimum [5](#0-4) . Because Tron transactions are broadcast to the mempool and ordered by block producers, any account (not necessarily the exchange creator, who is the only one authorized to call inject/withdraw per the creator check at [6](#0-5) ) that has visibility into the pending transaction can front-run it with an `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade that skews `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance`, causing the inject/withdraw to execute at a manipulated ratio, then back-run with a reverse trade to restore the pool and capture the difference — a classic sandwich, mirroring the reported `IbbtcVaultZap.sol` L174 issue where `mint`/`deposit` price is computed at execution time with no `minOut` guard.

### Impact Explanation
The exchange creator (the only account authorized to inject/withdraw liquidity) can be forced to inject tokens at a manipulated ratio, over-contributing one side of the pool, or to withdraw at a manipulated ratio, receiving less value than expected — a direct value-extraction/theft-of-funds vector against the liquidity provider, without requiring any elevated privilege from the attacker (a plain signed `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade suffices).

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires only observing a pending inject/withdraw transaction and submitting ordinary trade transactions before/after it in the same or adjacent blocks — no special permissions, and the mechanism (`exchangeCapsule.transaction`/ratio-based `anotherTokenQuant` calculation) is deterministic and reproducible by any actor who can read mempool transactions. This is comparable in reachability to the original finding in Badger's zap, since both simply rely on the pool state being read live in an actuator/execute path without min-out protection.

### Recommendation
Add an `expected`/`min_another_token_quant` field to `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` (mirroring `ExchangeTransactionContract.expected`) and enforce it in `ExchangeInjectActuator.doValidate()` / `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.doValidate()`, rejecting the transaction if the computed `anotherTokenQuant` falls outside the caller's specified bound, consistent with the mitigation already applied to `ExchangeTransactionActuator` at [7](#0-6) .

### Proof of Concept
1. Exchange creator submits `ExchangeInjectContract` intending to inject `tokenQuant` of token A expecting a specific ratio-derived amount of token B.
2. Attacker observes the pending transaction in the mempool and submits an `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade that shifts `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` before the inject executes.
3. `ExchangeInjectActuator.doValidate()` recomputes `anotherTokenQuant` from the now-skewed balances [4](#0-3)  and `execute()` commits the injection at this unfavorable ratio [8](#0-7) , with no field in the protobuf contract for the creator to have bounded the acceptable ratio [9](#0-8) .
4. Attacker submits a reverse trade to restore the pool, profiting from the extracted value; the same pattern applies to `ExchangeWithdrawActuator`.
