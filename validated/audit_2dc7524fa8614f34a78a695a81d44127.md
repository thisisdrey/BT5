### Title
Unstaked attacker can exhaust `qos.max_concurrent_connections()` connection-slot headroom with stalled/half-handshake connections, causing legitimate connections to be refused - (File: streamer/src/nonblocking/quic.rs)

### Summary
`ClientConnectionTracker::new` reserves a slot against `stats.open_connections` counted against `qos.max_concurrent_connections()` (the staked+unstaked capacity * 5/4 headroom) at `Incoming` accept time, before the QUIC handshake completes and before any per-IP "control of address" verification occurs. An attacker can open connection attempts and stall the handshake to hold these slots for up to `QUIC_CONNECTION_HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT` (2 seconds) each, potentially exhausting the headroom and causing `incoming.refuse()` for legitimate connections.

### Finding Description
In `run_server` (`streamer/src/nonblocking/quic.rs`), for each accepted `Incoming`, after the overall and per-IP rate-limit checks, `ClientConnectionTracker::new(stats.clone(), qos.max_concurrent_connections())` is called [1](#0-0) . This increments `stats.open_connections` and fails only if the count already reached `max_concurrent_connections()`, which is `(max_staked_connections + max_unstaked_connections) * 5 / 4` in `SwQos` [2](#0-1)  (similarly in `SimpleQos`) [3](#0-2) .

On success, `incoming.accept()` is called and `setup_connection` is spawned, which awaits the QUIC handshake with a timeout of `QUIC_CONNECTION_HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT` (2 seconds) [4](#0-3) . The `client_connection_tracker` (holding the reserved slot) is only moved into the `ConnectionTable`/`ConnectionEntry` (which is what actually consumes stake-bounded capacity) after the handshake completes and `qos.try_add_connection` succeeds [5](#0-4) . If the handshake never completes, `setup_connection` returns without adding an entry to the table, dropping `client_connection_tracker`, and `Drop for ClientConnectionTracker` decrements `open_connections` [6](#0-5) .

This means an attacker never occupies a `ConnectionTable` slot (which is stake-weighted and per-peer-limited), but *does* occupy a "global open connection" slot for up to 2 seconds per stalled handshake, and this global counter is checked against a fixed cap (`max_concurrent_connections()`) that is shared by all incoming connections including legitimate/staked ones — before any pubkey/stake information is even known (peer identity is only established during/after the TLS handshake). The code's own comment acknowledges the underlying tension: "our connection/handshake abuse mitigation policy is one of shed fast and bound resource consumption... peers can attack one another via ip spoofing" [7](#0-6) .

The only defenses in this path prior to consuming a `ClientConnectionTracker` slot are:
1. A global token-bucket rate limiter (`overall_connection_rate_limiter`, burst `MAX_CONNECTION_BURST = 1000`, sustained `TOTAL_CONNECTIONS_PER_SECOND = 2500`) [8](#0-7) .
2. A per-IP rate limiter checked via `rate_limiter.is_allowed(ip)` [9](#0-8) , whereas `rate_limiter.register_connection(ip)` (which asserts real control of the IP) only happens *after* a successful handshake [10](#0-9) .

Neither of these limiters is scoped to `max_concurrent_connections()`; they limit new connection *attempts* per unit time/IP but do not bound how long a slot can be *held open* by a stalled handshake beyond the fixed 2-second timeout, nor do they distinguish attacker connections from legitimate ones once the global rate budget is consumed by attack traffic. Since the global rate limiter allows up to `MAX_CONNECTION_BURST` (1000) instantaneous connection attempts and ~2500/sec sustained, and `max_concurrent_connections()` is a small multiple of configured staked+unstaked capacity, an attacker with sufficient concurrent stalled attempts (potentially from multiple source IPs/spoofed IPs, since the per-IP rate limiter is bypassed for a real handshake commitment) can keep `open_connections` pinned at the cap continuously, causing legitimate `Incoming` connections to hit the `else` branch and be refused via `incoming.refuse()`, incrementing `refused_connections_too_many_open_connections` [1](#0-0) .

### Impact Explanation
This is a QoS-evasion / TPU-connection-starvation issue: an unstaked, unprivileged attacker can deny new TPU connections (including from staked/legitimate senders) without ever being admitted into the stake-weighted `ConnectionTable`, defeating the purpose of the staked-vs-unstaked QoS split. This falls into the general "ingress resource exhaustion disproportionate to actual verified identity" / DoS-via-QUIC-handshake-abuse bounty category referenced by the question. However, the 2-second `QUIC_CONNECTION_HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT` bounds each individual slot's held duration, and the global/per-IP rate limiters do provide some mitigation, so the practical severity depends heavily on how much concurrency/IP diversity the attacker can sustain relative to `MAX_CONNECTION_BURST`/`TOTAL_CONNECTIONS_PER_SECOND` and `max_connections_per_ipaddr_per_min`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Preconditions: unstaked attacker able to send QUIC Initial packets and simply stop responding before handshake completion (cheap - no crypto/compute cost proportional to a full handshake, and no stake required). This is directly enabled by the design tradeoff called out in the code comments themselves (avoiding "smarter" rejection before IP control is asserted, to avoid IP-spoofing amplification). Feasibility requires the attacker to sustain enough concurrent half-open attempts to fill `max_concurrent_connections()` continuously while contending with `overall_connection_rate_limiter` and per-IP limiter — this requires either many source IPs (feasible via UDP spoofing for the pre-handshake stage) or sufficiently high burst capacity relative to the configured limiter (`max_connections_per_ipaddr_per_min`). I could not fully verify within available context whether `ConnectionRateLimiter::is_allowed` state is keyed strictly per real IP with no spoof-independent enforcement, or whether other upstream firewall/anti-spoofing layers exist outside this crate — this affects the real-world exploitability estimate.

### Recommendation
- Decouple/reduce the pre-handshake global slot cap from the post-handshake stake-aware capacity, and/or scale `QUIC_CONNECTION_HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT` down for the pre-verification phase.
- Track handshake-pending connections and confirmed connections in separate counters, so `max_concurrent_connections()` headroom is not shared uniformly between "trying to prove IP ownership" traffic and connections that have already completed/verified handshake.
- Consider requiring QUIC Retry (address validation) more aggressively when `open_connections` approaches the cap, to force attackers to prove IP ownership before consuming a tracked slot.

### Proof of Concept
Integration test plan (Rust, in `streamer/src/nonblocking/quic.rs` test module, similar to existing `setup_quic_server`/`check_block_multiple_connections` helpers):
1. Spawn a QUIC server via `setup_quic_server` with a small `SwQosConfig` (e.g., `max_staked_connections = 2`, `max_unstaked_connections = 2` so `max_concurrent_connections() == 5`).
2. Open `K = max_concurrent_connections()` client QUIC endpoints that send an Initial packet (start `Endpoint::connect`) but never allow the handshake to complete (e.g., drop the client socket immediately after sending, or use a raw UDP socket to send a single Initial datagram without following the QUIC handshake protocol).
3. Concurrently, attempt one legitimate client connection (`make_client_endpoint` that completes handshake normally).
4. Assert: `stats.refused_connections_too_many_open_connections.load(Ordering::Relaxed) > 0` while the K stalled attempts are outstanding, and the legitimate connection either times out or is refused until enough of the K stalled attempts hit `QUIC_CONNECTION_HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT` and are dropped.
5. Assert a floor: after waiting `> QUIC_CONNECTION_HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT`, `stats.connection_setup_timeout` increases by K and the legitimate connection succeeds — demonstrating the window of denial is bounded by the timeout but nonzero and repeatable if the attacker continuously re-issues stalled attempts faster than `QUIC_CONNECTION_HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT`.
