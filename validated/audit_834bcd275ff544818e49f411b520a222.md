### Title
Missing `contract-caller == tx-sender` check in `borrow`/`collateral-remove` enables confused-deputy fund extraction from any intermediary contract holding a Zest position - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`)

### Summary
`collateral-add`, `supply-collateral-add`, `repay`, and `liquidate` in `v0-4-market.clar` all explicitly assert `(is-eq contract-caller tx-sender)` before acting on `account`/`account contract-caller` [1](#0-0) , [2](#0-1) , [3](#0-2) , [4](#0-3) . `borrow` and `collateral-remove`, however, derive `account`/`collateral-receiver` from `contract-caller` **without** this check [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) .

### Finding Description
The docs describe the intended pattern: "`Authorization: Uses contract-caller (M05 audit fix eliminates explicit account parameter)`" [7](#0-6) . To close the gap left by that M05 fix, `collateral-add`/`repay`/`liquidate` additionally require `contract-caller == tx-sender`, ensuring a position can only be acted on by the genuine originating account and not by an intermediary contract standing in the call chain. This guard is missing from `borrow` (`account contract-caller` at line 1242, no auth assert before the health checks) and `collateral-remove` (`account contract-caller` / `collateral-receiver` at lines 1111-1116, no auth assert at all). Both derive the acted-upon `account` (and, for `collateral-remove`, the fund `receiver`) purely from `contract-caller` — the immediate calling contract in the chain — rather than confirming that principal is also the transaction's true originator.

### Impact Explanation
Because of the missing check, any contract that itself already holds a Zest market position (collateral deposited when *it* was both `tx-sender` and `contract-caller`, i.e. it deployed/self-funded and called the market at the top of a transaction, or via any legitimate path that lets it become the account of record) exposes that position to any third party able to trigger its public entry points that call back into `market.borrow` or `market.collateral-remove`. Since `receiver`/`collateral-receiver` is an attacker-controllable optional principal on both functions, an unprivileged caller can redirect the withdrawn collateral or newly borrowed funds to itself — a direct theft of funds at rest/in motion from the intermediary contract's position, matching the Critical impact bucket (theft of user funds other than unclaimed yield).

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitability depends on the existence of a reachable, callable contract in the Zest ecosystem that (a) already holds collateral/debt under its own principal in `market-vault`, and (b) exposes some public function that forwards to `market.borrow`/`market.collateral-remove` without re-checking the ultimate caller. I was not able to fully confirm within the in-scope contracts a concrete such intermediary that both holds collateral and forwards these two calls with an attacker-supplied receiver (e.g., `supply-collateral-add` and `collateral-remove-redeem` call `collateral-add`/`collateral-remove` internally, but with `account`/`receiver` fixed to the enclosing call's own `contract-caller`, not attacker-supplied). The missing assert is nonetheless a genuine, verifiable deviation from the codebase's own established, otherwise-universal auth pattern for account-scoped market operations, and would become directly exploitable the moment any contract in the ecosystem (present or future) holds a position and calls back into these two functions.

### Recommendation
Add `(asserts! (is-eq contract-caller tx-sender) ERR-AUTHORIZATION)` to `borrow` and `collateral-remove` in `v0-4-market.clar` (and the corresponding `market.clar` in `local-testing`), consistent with `collateral-add`, `supply-collateral-add`, `repay`, and `liquidate`, so that no intermediary contract in the call chain can ever be treated as the `account` of record or dictate an arbitrary fund `receiver` on behalf of a position it does not directly, self-originated own.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy contract `X` that, at the top level of a transaction it itself originates (`tx-sender == X`'s deploying flow is not directly possible for a contract... — instead, consider any existing/future Zest-integrated contract `X` that already holds a market position because some legitimate flow allowed `X` to be `contract-caller == tx-sender` momentarily, e.g. via an `as-contract?` self-call pattern) has non-zero collateral under its principal in `market-vault`.
2. `X` exposes a public function `withdraw-for(receiver)` that calls `(contract-call? .v0-4-market collateral-remove ft amount (some receiver) none)`.
3. Any unprivileged principal calls `X.withdraw-for(attacker)`. Inside `collateral-remove`, `contract-caller = X` and `tx-sender = attacker` (mismatched), but since no `is-eq contract-caller tx-sender` assertion exists, the call proceeds, `account = X`, `collateral-receiver = attacker`, and `X`'s collateral is sent to `attacker` — without `X`'s genuine consent path being invoked.
4. The same pattern applies to `borrow`, letting an attacker cause debt to be opened against `X`'s collateral with borrowed funds sent to an attacker-chosen `receiver`.
