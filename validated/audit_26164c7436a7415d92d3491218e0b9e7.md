### Title
Owner of an undercollateralized `MarginfiAccount` can front-run liquidation by self-migrating the account via `TransferToNewAccount`, resetting/relocating the liquidatable position - ([File: programs/marginfi/src/instructions/marginfi_account/transfer_account.rs])

### Summary
`transfer_to_new_account` / `transfer_to_new_account_pda` let the account `authority` move all lending balances from an existing `MarginfiAccount` to a brand-new one under a self-chosen (`new_authority` is fully unchecked) pubkey [1](#0-0) . The only guards applied are "not in flashloan", "not in receivership", "not mid order-execution", "no active orders", and "not already migrated" [2](#0-1) . There is no check on account health/liquidatability before the transfer proceeds. The old account's `lending_account` (all balances) is then zeroed and the account is flagged `ACCOUNT_DISABLED` [3](#0-2) .

### Finding Description
This is directly analogous to the reported ShortRecord (SR) NFT-transfer front-run in the Ditto report: the owner of an unhealthy position can transfer/relocate the position itself just before a permissionless liquidation instruction lands, invalidating the pending liquidation call against the position's old identifier.

In marginfi, a liquidator calls `lending_account_liquidate` against a specific `liquidatee_marginfi_account` pubkey, and `check_pre_liquidation_condition_and_get_account_health` requires the account to hold the specific active liability balance being repaid, or it reverts with `LendingAccountBalanceNotFound`, and requires the account to be unhealthy or it reverts with `HealthyAccount` [4](#0-3) .

If the account owner sees a liquidation transaction targeting their account (via mempool/searcher visibility, private RPC, or simply pre-emptively/JIT before their health crosses the maintenance threshold), they can submit `TransferToNewAccount` first. This:
1. Copies `lending_account` (i.e., the exact liability/asset balances that made the account liquidatable) to a brand new `MarginfiAccount` under an authority the caller controls [5](#0-4) .
2. Zeroes the old account's `lending_account` and sets `ACCOUNT_DISABLED` [3](#0-2) .

Any in-flight or subsequently-submitted liquidation transaction that references the old account pubkey then fails, because the old account no longer holds the targeted liability balance (`LendingAccountBalanceNotFound`) — functionally identical to how the SR-transfer front-run turned the SR `Cancelled`, causing `flagShort`/`liquidateSecondary` to revert due to `onlyValidShortRecord`/status checks. The liquidator must re-discover the new account pubkey and resubmit, and the owner can repeat this maneuver (each new account can itself be migrated again, since the one-time "already migrated" check only applies per specific account, not to the position/authority chain) to perpetually dodge liquidation while the position continues to deteriorate.

Unlike the SR case there is no explicit CR/health gate in `transfer_to_new_account`, confirming the root cause: no analog of the recommended `CR > primaryLiquidationCR` check exists here either.

### Impact Explanation
Repeatedly delaying/evading liquidation of an underwater position allows the debt/collateral ratio to worsen further before liquidation eventually succeeds (if at all, since the liquidator must re-target a moving pubkey), directly risking under-collateralized liquidation and bad debt/insolvency for the lending pool, matching the "insolvency/bad debt" and "unauthorized state change" (forced invalidation of a pending liquidation instruction) acceptance criteria.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires only a signer holding the account authority (unprivileged) and knowledge/anticipation of an incoming liquidation call — feasible via Solana's transaction-visibility window (private RPC/Jito bundles/mempool-like visibility) or simply as a just-in-time self-migration performed whenever health approaches the maintenance threshold, exactly mirroring the front-running technique described in the source report. No special permissions or admin cooperation are needed; `authority`, `fee_payer`, and `new_authority` are all attacker-controlled [6](#0-5) .

### Recommendation
Add a health check to `transfer_to_new_account` (and the PDA variant) mirroring the recommended SR fix: require the account to pass a maintenance/liquidation health check (i.e., `check_pre_liquidation_condition_and_get_account_health` returns healthy) before allowing migration, so that unhealthy/liquidatable accounts cannot be transferred away from a pending liquidation.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual reproduction path (mirrors the report's front-run pattern; a full on-chain PoC requires a test harness/mempool simulation not available in the static index):
1. Attacker opens a leveraged position via `MarginfiAccountInitialize` + deposit/borrow until near the maintenance threshold.
2. Attacker (or a bot) monitors for an incoming `lending_account_liquidate` transaction targeting their account, or proactively self-migrates whenever health nears the liquidation boundary.
3. Attacker submits `TransferToNewAccount`/`TransferToNewAccountPda` with `new_authority` set to another address they control [7](#0-6) , landing before the liquidator's transaction.
4. Old account's `lending_account` is zeroed and flagged `ACCOUNT_DISABLED` [3](#0-2) ; the pending liquidator transaction against the old pubkey reverts (`LendingAccountBalanceNotFound`/`HealthyAccount`) per `check_pre_liquidation_condition_and_get_account_health` [4](#0-3) .
5. The identical risky position now lives at a new pubkey; attacker repeats the process to keep evading liquidation while market conditions worsen the position's collateralization.

fn finalize_migrated_old_account(
    old_account: &mut MarginfiAccount,
    new_account_key: Pubkey,
    current_timestamp: u64,
) {
    old_account.migrated_to = new_account_key;
    old_account.last_update = current_timestamp;
    old_account.lending_account = LendingAccount::zeroed();
    old_account.set_flag(ACCOUNT_DISABLED, true);
    old_account.sync_indexer_flags();
}

            let a = old_marginfi_account.load()?;

        } @ MarginfiError::AccountFrozen,
        constraint = {
            let a = old_marginfi_account.load()?;
