Audit Report

## Title
Unchecked refund `resolve()` result silently burns the fee-payer's transaction fee refund instead of returning or re-routing it - (File: `substrate/frame/transaction-payment/asset-tx-payment/src/payment.rs`)

## Summary
`FungiblesAdapter::correct_and_deposit_fee` withdraws a predicted fee up front, and after dispatch splits off the overpaid portion as `refund`, then calls `<T::Fungibles as Balanced<T::AccountId>>::resolve(who, refund)` and discards the result with `let _ = ...`. If `resolve` fails (e.g., crediting `who` would breach the existential deposit), the `refund` `Credit` imbalance is dropped, which triggers `OnDropCredit::handle` and permanently decreases `total_issuance` rather than returning the funds to the payer or to any accounted destination.

## Finding Description
In `correct_and_deposit_fee`, the refund is computed via `paid.split(converted_fee)` producing a `Credit<T::AccountId, T::Fungibles>` imbalance, then passed to `resolve`: [1](#0-0) . The code comment explicitly acknowledges that `resolve` can fail if the account dropped below the existential balance, yet the error is discarded with `let _ = ...`, and the function still returns `Ok((converted_fee, converted_tip))`. Because `Imbalance` is `#[must_use]` and has a `Drop` impl that invokes `OnDrop::handle` for non-zero amounts [2](#0-1) , and `Credit`'s drop handler is documented to generally decrease `total_issuance` [3](#0-2) , a failed refund is silently annihilated from total issuance instead of being credited to the payer or routed to a beneficiary.

This contrasts with the two sibling implementations in the same repository, both of which check the `resolve`/`merge` outcome and route the un-refunded amount back into the fee credit that is ultimately handled by `OU::on_unbalanceds`/`HC::handle_credit`:
- `pallet_transaction_payment::FungibleAdapter::correct_and_deposit_fee` merges the failed refund back into `fee_credit`.
- `asset-conversion-tx-payment::correct_and_deposit_fee` merges the failed refund back into `adjusted_paid` with a `defensive!` log on merge failure.

`FungiblesAdapter` has no equivalent recovery path, so the amount is unconditionally lost whenever `resolve` fails.

## Impact Explanation
This is a value-conservation violation: an unprivileged, honest fee payer using `pallet-asset-tx-payment` with `FungiblesAdapter` can lose their legitimate fee overpayment refund whenever their post-dispatch balance is too low to receive the refund without breaching the existential deposit for the fee asset. The refund amount is destroyed via decreasing `total_issuance` rather than being credited to the payer or forwarded to any beneficiary, and the extrinsic still reports success. This matches the required impact of value not settling exactly once to the rightful beneficiary/amount.

## Likelihood Explanation
This is reachable by any ordinary account using the fee-asset payment flow — no privileged access or malicious actor is required. Any transaction where the dispatched call itself reduces the payer's fee-asset balance close to or below the existential deposit (e.g., a `transfer_all` or similar balance-draining action in the same extrinsic) deterministically triggers the `resolve` failure path on every occurrence, making it easily and repeatably reproducible under realistic usage patterns.

## Recommendation
Do not discard the `resolve` result. On failure, merge the un-refunded `Credit` back into `final_fee` (mirroring `pallet_transaction_payment::FungibleAdapter` and `asset-conversion-tx-payment`) before passing it to `HC::handle_credit`, so the amount is deterministically accounted for and routed to a defined beneficiary instead of being silently burned out of total issuance.

## Proof of Concept
1. Configure a runtime with `pallet-asset-tx-payment` using `FungiblesAdapter<CON, HC>` for fee payment in a non-native fungible asset.
2. Account `A` holds exactly `ED + predicted_fee` of the fee asset before dispatch.
3. `A` submits a transaction whose `withdraw_fee` withdraws `predicted_fee` (leaving `A` at exactly `ED`), and whose dispatched call itself transfers or burns from `A` such that after execution `A`'s remaining asset balance is `0` or insufficient to receive the refund while staying above ED.
4. Post-dispatch, `correct_and_deposit_fee` computes `refund = paid - actual_fee > 0` and calls `resolve(A, refund)`, which fails.
5. The `refund` `Credit` is dropped, invoking `OnDropCredit::handle`, decreasing `total_issuance` by `refund` — `A` never receives the refund and it is not routed elsewhere, while the extrinsic reports success with `Ok((converted_fee, converted_tip))`.
