### Title
Exchange Withdrawal Lacks Slippage/Minimum-Output Protection Enabling Sandwich Attacks Against Liquidity Providers - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java)

### Summary
`ExchangeWithdrawContract`, unlike `ExchangeTransactionContract`, carries no user-supplied minimum/expected output field, so `ExchangeWithdrawActuator` computes the counter-asset amount returned to a liquidity-providing exchange creator purely from the bonding-curve state at execution time, with no slippage guard. An attacker can sandwich the withdrawal with `ExchangeTransactionContract` trades to shift the pool ratio and force the withdrawer to receive fewer tokens than the pool state implied when they signed their transaction — the same root cause as the referenced Uniswap `NonfungiblePositionManager.decreaseLiquidity()` sandwich issue (missing `amount0Min`/`amount1Min`).

### Finding Description
`ExchangeWithdrawContract` only carries `owner_address`, `exchange_id`, `token_id`, `quant` — there is no `expected`/minimum-output field, in contrast to `ExchangeTransactionContract` which explicitly includes an `expected` field used for slippage protection [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) .

In `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.doValidate()`, `anotherTokenQuant` is derived solely from the exchange's current pool balances and the requested `tokenQuant` (a bonding-curve ratio computation), with checks only for precision loss and non-zero — never against a user-specified minimum: [3](#0-2) 

The same unguarded computation is repeated in `execute()` immediately before crediting the account, meaning whatever the pool ratio is at the moment of on-chain execution (which can be manipulated by intervening trades within the same block) determines the payout: [4](#0-3) 

Because `ExchangeTransactionContract` lets any account trade against the same bonding-curve pool and change `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` [5](#0-4) , an attacker (or a block-producing SR acting as a normal trader, or simply a searcher submitting transactions in sequence) can:
1. Front-run the creator's `ExchangeWithdrawContract` with a large `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade that skews the pool ratio unfavorably for the withdrawer's `tokenID`.
2. Let the victim's withdrawal execute, receiving a reduced `anotherTokenQuant`.
3. Back-run with a reverse trade to restore the pool and capture the extracted value.

This mirrors exactly the reported vulnerability class: an on-chain AMM/bonding-curve operation that removes liquidity/value without enforcing a caller-specified minimum acceptable output, making it susceptible to sandwich manipulation.

### Impact Explanation
An exchange creator withdrawing liquidity can be forced to receive materially less of the counter-token than the pool state implied when they signed the transaction, resulting in a quantifiable, unauthorized transfer of value from the liquidity provider to the attacker executing the sandwich — a concrete "theft of funds" style impact reachable purely via ordinary signed transactions (`ExchangeTransactionContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract`), satisfying the Medium-severity bar analogous to the original report.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account can submit `ExchangeTransactionContract` transactions against any exchange, and exchange creators are ordinary permissionless actors (the only restriction is that the *withdrawer* must be the exchange's creator) [6](#0-5) . Because pending transactions are visible before block inclusion and multiple transactions can land in the same block, an attacker can reliably construct front-run/back-run pairs around a visible withdrawal, making exploitation practical whenever a withdrawal transaction is broadcast to the mempool.

### Recommendation
Add a `min_expected` (or `another_token_min`) field to `ExchangeWithdrawContract`, and enforce in `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.doValidate()`/`execute()` that the computed `anotherTokenQuant` is not less than the caller-specified minimum, mirroring the existing `expected` check already implemented for `ExchangeTransactionContract` at [7](#0-6) .

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker observes a pending `ExchangeWithdrawContract` transaction from the exchange creator for `exchange_id=X`, `token_id=A`, `quant=Q`.
2. Attacker submits an `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade against the same `exchange_id` that shifts `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` unfavorably for token A's ratio, executed before the victim's withdrawal in the same block [8](#0-7) .
3. The victim's `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()` computes `anotherTokenQuant` from the now-skewed balances with no floor, crediting the victim less than expected [9](#0-8) .
4. Attacker submits a reverse `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade to restore the pool ratio and lock in the extracted value.
