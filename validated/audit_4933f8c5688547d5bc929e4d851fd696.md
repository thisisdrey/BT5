### Title
Rounding-down division in `ParticipateAssetIssueActuator` causes TRC10 participants to permanently lose value without refund - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ParticipateAssetIssueActuator.java`)

### Summary
`ParticipateAssetIssueActuator.execute()` deducts the full TRX `cost` from the caller but computes the number of TRC10 tokens returned to them using an integer, floor-rounding division (`exchangeAmount = floorDiv(cost * num, trxNum)`). Any remainder from that division is silently discarded instead of being refunded to the caller, so a `ParticipateAssetIssueContract` sender always loses the fractional value whenever `cost * num` is not exactly divisible by `trxNum`.

### Finding Description
In `execute()`, the caller's full `cost` (in TRX/sun) is subtracted from their balance and unconditionally credited to the asset issuer (`toAccount`): [1](#0-0) 

The amount of TRC10 asset the caller receives in exchange is then computed with integer division that always rounds down, with no accounting for the remainder: [2](#0-1) 

The same floor-rounding formula is repeated in `validate()`, and the only check performed is that the resulting `exchangeAmount` is strictly greater than zero — there is no check for exact divisibility and no refund path for the truncated remainder: [3](#0-2) 

This is structurally identical to the reported bug class: a `volume / price`-style division that always truncates toward zero, so the token-buying side receives less than the economically correct amount while the counterparty (here, the asset issuer's account, analogous to the auction's `reserves`) keeps the full payment. The remainder of `cost * num / trxNum` is neither refunded to the caller nor kept as an explicit "protocol fee" — it is simply lost, i.e. paid for but never issued.

`trxNum` and `num` are `int` fields set by the asset issuer at `AssetIssueContract` creation time and can be arbitrary values (up to `Integer.MAX_VALUE`), so the exchange ratio granularity is fully out of the participant's control, and losses recur on essentially every `ParticipateAssetIssueContract` call whose `amount` is not a multiple of `trxNum / gcd(trxNum, num)`.

### Impact Explanation
Every unprivileged account that broadcasts a `ParticipateAssetIssueContract` transaction (an "order placer" purchasing a TRC10 token during its crowdsale window) can lose a portion of the TRX it pays with no corresponding token issued and no refund — the paid TRX is fully transferred to the asset issuer's account while the truncated remainder of tokens is never minted/transferred to the buyer. This is a genuine, unbacked value transfer from the buyer to the issuer's balance sheet (the issuer's TRX balance increases by the full `cost`, while the buyer receives strictly less asset value than paid for), matching the "theft or permanent freezing of funds" bar via unauthorized value loss on the buyer's side.

### Likelihood Explanation
High. This occurs deterministically whenever `cost * num` is not an exact multiple of `trxNum`, which is the common case for almost any TRX amount a buyer might legitimately choose to spend, since `trxNum`/`num` are set independently by the asset issuer. No special conditions, race, or malicious cooperation is required — a single, ordinary `ParticipateAssetIssueContract` transaction triggers the loss.

### Recommendation
Compute the remainder of `cost * num / trxNum` in `execute()` and refund it to the caller (e.g., reduce the amount debited from the owner's balance by the remainder, or credit it back before the balance write), mirroring the report's recommendation of adding the division remainder back into the refund path. Alternatively, require in `validate()` that `cost * num` be an exact multiple of `trxNum`, rejecting otherwise-lossy participation amounts.

### Proof of Concept
1. Asset issuer creates an `AssetIssueContract` with `trxNum = 3`, `num = 1` (i.e., 3 sun buys 1 unit of the asset).
2. A buyer sends a `ParticipateAssetIssueContract` with `amount = 5` (5 sun).
3. In `execute()`: `exchangeAmount = floorDiv(5 * 1, 3) = 1`.
4. The buyer's account is debited the full `5` sun (`cost`), and the `toAccount` (issuer) balance is credited `5` sun in full.
5. The buyer only receives `1` unit of the asset — worth `3` sun at the stated exchange rate — while having paid `5` sun. The `2` sun difference is never refunded or otherwise accounted for; it is simply retained by the issuer's account with no offsetting increase in circulating asset supply. [4](#0-3)
