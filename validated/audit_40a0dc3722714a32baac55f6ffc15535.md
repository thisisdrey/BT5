### Title
Depositors can dodge socialized bad debt by redeeming vault shares before a foreseeable liquidation and re-depositing after — shifting the loss to remaining depositors - ([File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-stx.clar], [File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar])

### Summary
Zest v2 does not use Liquity-style per-Trove redistribution; instead, bad debt from an under-collateralized liquidation is "socialized" by writing down the shared `lindex` of the relevant lending vault (`v0-vault-stx.clar`, `v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, etc.), which lowers the redemption value of every outstanding zToken/vault-share holder. Because `redeem`/`deposit` have no timelock, cooldown, or exit fee, any depositor (an unprivileged principal) can watch a borrower's position approach the liquidation threshold, `redeem` their shares before the liquidation/`socialize-debt` call executes, and `deposit` again immediately afterward. This lets that depositor dodge their proportional share of the write-down while increasing the *relative* loss borne by depositors who stayed in the vault — the same economic pattern as the reported "close/reopen Trove" bug, applied to the supplier side of the market instead of the borrower side.

### Finding Description
Bad debt socialization is implemented in `socialize-debt-asset` inside the market contract, which calls `vault-socialize-debt` and then refreshes the cached index because the vault's `lindex` decreased: [1](#0-0) 

The actual write-down happens in each vault's `socialize-debt`, which reduces `lindex` in proportion to the loss versus `old-total-assets`, permanently lowering the redemption rate for every zToken holder of that vault at the time of the call: [2](#0-1) 

`redeem` and `deposit` have no lock-up, cooldown, or penalty — a depositor can exit and re-enter at will, subject only to pause flags and available liquidity: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

The liquidation path in the market contract determines when a position has no collateral left and triggers `socialize-debt-asset` for the remaining scaled debt of that borrower: [5](#0-4) 

Because a position nearing `ltv-liq-partial`/`ltv-liq-full` is fully observable on-chain (health factor, oracle price, collateral/debt amounts are all public state read via `get-notional-evaluation` and `calc-liquidation-params`), any depositor can predict that a liquidation with bad debt is imminent well before it executes, and is not required to race a single block — unlike a same-block MEV attack. A depositor can therefore:
1. `redeem` their zTokens for underlying before the liquidation (and resulting `socialize-debt` write-down) executes, locking in their share value at the pre-loss `lindex`.
2. Wait for (or themselves call) `liquidate`, which triggers `socialize-debt-asset` → `vault-socialize-debt` → write-down of `lindex` for the *remaining* zToken supply only.
3. `deposit` again after the write-down, continuing to earn yield without ever having absorbed their share of the loss.

Because the same absolute bad-debt token amount is spread across a **smaller pool of remaining zToken supply**, the depositors who did not withdraw absorb a strictly larger proportional loss than they would have if the exiting depositor had remained. This is the exact economic mechanic described in the report (Bob avoiding bad debt by closing/reopening his Trove around a liquidation event), transposed from borrower Troves onto lender/vault shares.

### Impact Explanation
This causes a permanent, avoidable transfer of loss from a sophisticated depositor onto the remaining depositors of the vault — i.e., permanent freezing/loss of principal value for the depositors who remain, and a path toward protocol insolvency for that vault if repeated by large suppliers around every liquidation event with bad debt. Since no fee, penalty, or lock applies to `redeem`/`deposit`, this can be done for free and repeated indiscriminately.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likely to be exploited by any sophisticated LP/market participant, since liquidation health data (LTV, collateral, debt, oracle prices) is fully public and does not require transaction-ordering games or same-block execution — the depositor can act any time before the liquidation transaction is confirmed, given typically minutes-to-hours of lead time as a position's LTV crosses partial/full liquidation thresholds. No special privileges are required; only capital and monitoring of on-chain state.

### Recommendation
Introduce a withdrawal cooldown/timelock (or a decaying exit fee that funds the vault's reserve) for `redeem`, similar in spirit to the report's recommendation for Trove closures — ensuring that a depositor cannot fully exit and re-enter around a known, imminent socialize-debt event. Alternatively, socialize expected bad debt continuously (e.g., via a reserve/insurance buffer funded from ongoing fees) rather than as a single discrete `lindex` write-down tied to specific liquidation transactions, so timing an exit provides no advantage.

### Proof of Concept
1. Depositor D holds zTokens in `v0-vault-stx` (or any lending vault) worth `X` underlying at current `lindex`.
2. D observes (via public reads of `get-notional-evaluation`/liquidation params in `v0-4-market.clar`) that borrower B's position has crossed `ltv-liq-partial` and has no remaining collateral value to fully cover debt — bad debt socialization is imminent on liquidation.
3. D calls `redeem` on the vault, converting all zTokens to underlying at the pre-write-down rate: [3](#0-2) .
4. Anyone (or D) calls `liquidate` on B's position in `v0-4-market.clar`, triggering `socialize-debt-asset` and the vault's `socialize-debt`, which writes down `lindex` proportionally against the now-smaller total zToken supply: [5](#0-4) , [2](#0-1) .
5. D calls `deposit` again for `X` underlying, receiving zTokens at the new, lower/reset conversion rate, having never absorbed a share of the socialized loss, while the other remaining LPs now bear a larger proportional loss than if D had stayed in the pool.

I could not verify whether any off-chain/DAO-configured parameter (e.g., a withdrawal delay flag) exists elsewhere in the DAO registry that might mitigate this, since DAO-controlled configuration is explicitly out of scope per the rules; the on-chain `redeem`/`deposit` functions themselves contain no such lock.
