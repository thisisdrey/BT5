### Title
Treasury interest fee (`fee-reserve`) can be permanently rounded to zero by keeping `accrue()` calls frequent, denying the DAO treasury its yield share - ([File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar])

### Summary
The reported Panoptic bug shows how an integer-division based fee calculation (`s_ITMSpreadFee = (ITM_SPREAD_MULTIPLIER * _poolFee) / DECIMALS`) can silently round to zero for certain (governance-influenced) inputs, permanently disabling an intended commission. The same rounding-to-zero class exists in Zest's vault treasury-fee accrual logic: `reserve-inc` is computed as `mul-div-down(debt-delta, fee-reserve, BPS)`, and any unprivileged caller can keep `debt-delta` arbitrarily small by triggering `accrue()` every block, causing the treasury's cut of interest to round to zero on every call, indefinitely.

### Finding Description
`accrue()` recomputes the borrow index and the treasury fee share: [1](#0-0) 

The treasury share is `reserve-inc = mul-div-down(debt-delta, fee-reserve, BPS)`, where `debt-delta = new-debt - old-debt` since the last accrual: [2](#0-1) 

`mul-div-down` truncates (floor division), identical in structure to the Panoptic `_poolFee / DECIMALS` truncation: [3](#0-2) 

`accrue()` is invoked from every unprivileged, permissionless entry point, including `transfer` (which accepts `amount 0`), `deposit`, and `redeem`: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

Because `debt-delta` only reflects interest accrued since `last-update`, and `last-update` is refreshed every time `index`/`lindex` change, an attacker who calls `accrue()` (e.g., via `transfer(u0, self, self, none)`) as frequently as the chain allows (every block) keeps `time-delta` and therefore `debt-delta` extremely small relative to the fixed `BPS = u10000` denominator. Whenever `debt-delta * fee-reserve < BPS`, `reserve-inc` truncates to `0`, so `treasury-lp` (line 848) is `u0` and no shares are minted to `.dao-treasury`. Because the truncation happens on every call rather than accumulating, the treasury's fee is never captured — it's not merely delayed, it's permanently lost each time the accrual window is kept below the rounding threshold, exactly as `s_ITMSpreadFee` being zero permanently prevents commission collection in the Panoptic analog.

This is an instance of the "vault share math and interest accrual" / "treasury LP minting" attack surface explicitly in scope.

### Impact Explanation
The underlying debtors still pay interest (the `index` itself still advances and depositors still receive it via `lindex`), but the protocol's/DAO treasury's cut of that interest — an unclaimed yield stream — is permanently denied whenever the attacker artificially forces the per-call `debt-delta` below the rounding threshold. This matches the in-scope High-severity impact class: permanent loss/freezing of unclaimed yield accruing to the treasury, achievable purely through repeated, low-cost calls from an ordinary principal, with no DAO/oracle compromise required.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account can call `transfer` with `amount u0` (or make trivial deposits/redeems) every block at negligible cost, since `accrue()` is invoked unconditionally at the top of these public functions. The threshold below which `reserve-inc` rounds to zero depends on `fee-reserve` (bps) and current debt size, but for realistic reserve factors and normal debt levels, single-block interest deltas are commonly small enough that the floor division yields zero, especially for lower-decimal, lower-price assets or during low-utilization periods. The attack requires no privileged access and no protocol misconfiguration — only repeated ordinary transactions.

### Recommendation
Accumulate un-minted fractional treasury interest across accrual calls (e.g., track a running remainder of `debt-delta * fee-reserve` that carries over between calls) instead of discarding the truncated remainder on every call, or rate-limit/aggregate accrual to a minimum time window so that `debt-delta` cannot be kept perpetually below the rounding threshold.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker (or anyone) observes a vault (e.g., `v0-vault-usdc.clar`) with nonzero `fee-reserve` and outstanding debt.
2. Each block, attacker calls `transfer(u0, attacker, attacker, none)` (line 750), which triggers `accrue()` (line 833) before any other check.
3. Because `last-update` was just refreshed in the previous block, `time-delta` is minimal (~1 block), making `debt-delta` (line 846) tiny relative to `BPS`.
4. `reserve-inc = mul-div-down(debt-delta, fee-reserve, BPS)` (line 847) truncates to `0` on each call.
5. `treasury-lp` stays `0` every block (line 848), so `.dao-treasury` never receives its fee shares, even though interest is continuously accruing to the index that benefits lenders — the treasury's cut of that same interest is permanently lost call after call.
