### Title
Stale `PostponedReceipt`/`PendingDataCount`/`PostponedReceiptId` bookkeeping survives `DeleteAccount` and lets an attacker's pre-deletion self-authorized receipt hijack a subsequently recreated account - (File: `runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs`, `core/store/src/utils/mod.rs`, `runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
`action_delete_account` removes the `Account`, `AccessKey`/gas-key, `ContractCode`, and `ContractData` trie rows via `remove_account`, but never touches `PostponedReceiptId`, `PendingDataCount`, or `PostponedReceipt` rows keyed by that account as receiver. An attacker can leave a postponed self-receipt (with arbitrary privileged actions such as `DeleteAccount{beneficiary_id: attacker}` or `AddKey`) parked on their own account, self-delete the account, wait for someone else (e.g. a factory contract) to recreate the same account name, and only then deliver the missing `DataReceipt` — causing the stale receipt to execute against the freshly recreated account under its own baked-in permission context.

### Finding Description
`remove_account` (`core/store/src/utils/mod.rs:504-575`) only removes `TrieKey::Account`, `TrieKey::ContractCode`, access/gas keys, and `TrieKey::ContractData`: [1](#0-0) 
It never removes `TrieKey::PostponedReceiptId`, `TrieKey::PendingDataCount`, or `TrieKey::PostponedReceipt` for the account, and `action_delete_account` (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:314-390`) performs no such cleanup either — it only checks storage usage, gas-key balances, refunds the balance, calls `remove_account`, and sets `*account = None`. [2](#0-1) 

These bookkeeping trie keys are indexed purely by `receiver_id`/`receipt_id`/`data_id`, with no notion of an "account incarnation": [3](#0-2) 

When a later `DataReceipt` for the stale `data_id` finally arrives, `process_receipt` looks up `PostponedReceiptId` keyed only by `receiver_id`, decrements `PendingDataCount`, fetches the `PostponedReceipt`, and executes it via `apply_action_receipt` — with no check that the account is still in the same state (or even the same "generation") it was in when the receipt was postponed: [4](#0-3) 

`apply_action_receipt` re-derives `actor_id` fresh from the stale receipt's own `predecessor_id` field (baked in when the receipt was originally created, before deletion): [5](#0-4) 

Because the attacker crafted the original postponed receipt as a self-receipt (`predecessor_id == receiver_id == X`), `check_actor_permissions` trivially allows privileged actions on it — including `DeleteAccount` — regardless of who currently controls `X`: [6](#0-5) 

Exploit flow:
1. Attacker's account `X` sends itself receipt `R0` with `input_data_ids=[d]` and `actions=[DeleteAccount{beneficiary_id: attacker}]` (or `AddKey{attacker_pubkey}`). Since `d` is unmet, `process_action_receipt` postpones `R0`, storing `PostponedReceiptId{X,d}`, `PendingDataCount{X,R0}`, `PostponedReceipt{X,R0}` (`runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs:1608-1655`).
2. Attacker self-deletes `X` via a normal `DeleteAccount` receipt (`predecessor_id==receiver_id==X`), passing `check_actor_permissions`. `action_delete_account`/`remove_account` wipe the account/keys/code but leave the three postponed-receipt rows untouched.
3. A third party (e.g. a factory contract `F`, unaware of the stale bookkeeping) later creates `[CreateAccount, Transfer(deposit)]` (or `[CreateAccount, FunctionCall]`) targeting the same name `X`, per the actor-propagation rule in `docs/RuntimeSpec/Actions.md:34-35`. `X` now exists again with fresh balance/state controlled by `F`'s intended owner.
4. The attacker (who fully controls the timing of the promise/callback chain producing data `d`, e.g. by delaying a cross-contract call they control) finally delivers the `DataReceipt` for `d`. `process_receipt` resolves `PendingDataCount{X,R0}` to 0, fetches `PostponedReceipt{X,R0}`, and executes it via `apply_action_receipt` against the *new* `X` account.
5. `R0`'s `DeleteAccount{beneficiary_id: attacker}` action passes `check_actor_permissions` (actor/receiver both `X`, as stored) and deletes the just-recreated account, refunding its balance to the attacker — funds the factory/recreator never intended to give up.

None of the existing checks catch this: `check_account_existence`/`check_actor_permissions` only validate the *current* account against the *stored* receipt fields, and no code path invalidates postponed bookkeeping when the underlying account is deleted.

### Impact Explanation
This is a real theft/authorization-escalation primitive: an attacker who once owned an account name can plant a self-authorized time-bomb receipt, delete the account, and later trigger it to drain or hijack whatever balance/keys a third party (a factory contract, a naming/registration service, or any other party that reuses the account name) deposits into the recreated account — without that third party ever signing an authorizing action. This matches the "theft of user funds" / "authorization escalation across accounts or promises" bounty categories.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attacker only needs ordinary, unprivileged capabilities: deploy their own contract, create/delete their own sub-account (or a sufficiently long implicit top-level account, which anyone may create), and control the timing of a cross-contract promise callback they own so the resolving `DataReceipt` arrives after the account has been deleted and recreated by someone else. No validator, node-operator, or protocol-level access is required, and the attack is repeatable for any account name the attacker can regain control of or predict reuse of (e.g. deterministic/implicit account names, or `factory`-style subaccount naming schemes that reuse user-chosen names).

### Recommendation
When `action_delete_account` deletes an account, also purge any outstanding `PostponedReceiptId`, `PendingDataCount`, and `PostponedReceipt` entries keyed by that `receiver_id` (iterating the relevant trie prefixes, similar to how `remove_account` already iterates access keys and contract data), so a self-authorized receipt cannot resurrect against a later, unrelated incarnation of the same account name. Alternatively, tie postponed-receipt storage keys to an account "generation"/nonce that is invalidated on `DeleteAccount`, and reject resurrected postponed receipts whose generation doesn't match the current account.

### Proof of Concept
Add an integration test in `runtime/runtime/src/tests/apply.rs` (mirroring the harness used by `test_function_call_after_same_chunk_delete_recreate_resolves_fresh_code`):
1. Create account `X` (attacker-controlled) and account `F` (third-party "factory").
2. Apply a receipt to `X` with `input_data_ids=[d]` and `actions=[DeleteAccount{beneficiary_id: attacker}]`; assert `get_postponed_receipt`, `PendingDataCount`, `PostponedReceiptId` are all `Some` for `X`.
3. Apply a self `DeleteAccount` receipt for `X` (beneficiary = attacker, or any refund target); assert the account is gone but re-assert the postponed-receipt rows from step 2 are still present (demonstrating the missing cleanup).
4. Apply a receipt from `F` with `actions=[CreateAccount, Transfer(deposit=N)]` targeting `X`, simulating a factory funding a "fresh" account.
5. Apply a `DataReceipt{receiver_id: X, data_id: d}`; assert this triggers execution of the stale postponed receipt, that `X`'s `DeleteAccount` action succeeds, and that the `N` deposit ends up refunded to `attacker` rather than remaining with `X`/`F`'s intended owner, and that `X` is deleted again — all without `F` ever authorizing the deletion.
