## Title
On-chain exchange inject/withdraw operations compute counter-token amounts without slippage protection, enabling sandwich attacks - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java`, `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java`)

### Summary
TRON's built-in bancor-style exchange (`Exchange*Contract` actuators) lets an exchange creator inject liquidity or withdraw liquidity from a token pair pool. Both operations compute the "other side" token amount purely from the current on-chain pool ratio at execution time, with no caller-supplied minimum/maximum bound. Any unprivileged account can broadcast `ExchangeTransactionContract` (buy/sell) transactions to shift the pool ratio immediately before the inject/withdraw transaction executes and reverse it afterward, sandwiching the creator's operation exactly like the reported LMPVault swap-without-slippage-protection issue.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeTransactionActuator` (the actual swap/trade operation) does carry caller-specified slippage protection: the contract has an `expected` field and `doValidate()` enforces `anotherTokenQuant >= tokenExpected` before executing the trade. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

In contrast, `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` have no equivalent `expected`/min-output field at all. [3](#0-2) 

`ExchangeInjectActuator.execute` derives `anotherTokenQuant` (the paired amount that must additionally be pulled from the creator's balance) directly from the live pool balances (`firstTokenBalance`, `secondTokenBalance`) at execution time, with no bound check against what the creator expected when they signed the transaction: [4](#0-3) 

Similarly, `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute` computes the returned `anotherTokenQuant` from the live pool ratio, with no minimum-received guard: [5](#0-4) 

Because any account can submit `ExchangeTransactionContract` trades against the same pool (the actual swap execution uses `ExchangeCapsule.transaction`, a bancor-formula update to `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance`), an attacker who observes a pending inject/withdraw transaction in the mempool can:
1. Front-run with a large buy/sell trade that skews the pool ratio.
2. Let the victim's inject (forced to pay a manipulated, unfavorable counter-token amount) or withdraw (forced to receive a manipulated, reduced counter-token amount) execute against the skewed ratio.
3. Back-run with a reverse trade to restore the ratio and capture the extracted value, net of trading fees embedded in the bancor curve.

### Impact Explanation
The exchange creator, when injecting liquidity, can be forced to pay far more of the counter token than intended for a fixed injected amount, or when withdrawing, can receive far less of the counter token than the pool ratio implied at signing time. This is a direct, unauthorized value-extraction/theft-of-funds vector against the transaction issuer, reachable purely by an unprivileged party broadcasting ordinary signed transactions (`ExchangeTransactionContract` sandwich trades) against the public on-chain exchange — no special privilege or malicious-SR/validator behavior required.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account can call `ExchangeTransactionActuator` to trade against any exchange pair at will, and mempool transaction ordering (or same-block ordering by the same/witness block producer) allows front-run/back-run sandwiching around a pending `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` transaction. Since these two contract types carry no expected/min-output parameter, there is no way for the actuator's `validate()`/`execute()` to reject an execution that occurred against a manipulated ratio — the exploit requires only ordinary transaction broadcast capability.

### Recommendation
Add an `expected`/`min_receive` (for withdraw) and `max_cost`/`expected` (for inject) field to `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract`, analogous to the `expected` field already present in `ExchangeTransactionContract`, and enforce the bound in `ExchangeInjectActuator.doValidate()`/`ExchangeWithdrawActuator.doValidate()` before execution, mirroring the check at `ExchangeTransactionActuator.java:219`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Exchange creator signs and broadcasts `ExchangeWithdrawContract` for token pair `(A, B)` expecting to receive `X` of token `B` for withdrawing `Y` of token `A`, based on the pool ratio visible when they signed.
2. Attacker observes the pending transaction and broadcasts `ExchangeTransactionContract` selling a large amount of `B` into the pool, shifting `secondTokenBalance` up and `firstTokenBalance` down (or vice versa) in the same or an earlier block.
3. The victim's `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute` runs against the now-skewed `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance`, computing `anotherTokenQuant` (see `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java:77-89`) far lower than the creator expected, with no validation to reject it.
4. Attacker broadcasts a reverse `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade to restore the ratio, pocketing the difference extracted from the victim's withdrawal.
