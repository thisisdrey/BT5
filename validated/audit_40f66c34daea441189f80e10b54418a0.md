### Title
Front-runnable `asset_share_value` inflation via permissionless `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` allows theft of yield from existing depositors - (File: `programs/marginfi/src/instructions/marginfi_group/configure_bank.rs`)

### Summary
`lending_pool_emissions_deposit` is a **permissionless** instruction that transfers reward tokens directly into a bank's liquidity vault and immediately raises `asset_share_value` for *all* current shareholders, proportional to their existing share count at the moment the instruction executes. Because there is no deposit cooldown, minimum holding period, or entry/exit fee, a user who deposits immediately before this instruction lands and withdraws immediately after captures a share of the injected reward disproportionate to their actual time-weighted contribution — diluting the return legitimate long-term depositors should have received. This mirrors the root cause of the reported Peapods `AutoCompoundingPodLp::setYieldConvEnabled` bug: an externally-triggerable event that sharply and atomically increases the internal price-per-share, which is exploitable by depositing right before it fires and withdrawing right after.

### Finding Description
`lending_pool_emissions_deposit` snapshots `total_asset_shares` before the transfer, adds the incoming reward `amount` to `total_assets`, and recomputes `asset_share_value = (total_assets + amount) / total_asset_shares`: [1](#0-0) 

This instruction is explicitly permissionless — anyone (not just the reward-token owner or bank admin) can trigger it once reward tokens are available: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

The share-value bump is applied to **all** `total_asset_shares` outstanding at call time, without any check on how recently those shares were minted. Standard `lending_account_deposit`/`lending_account_withdraw` paths accrue interest but impose no minimum holding time, entry fee, or exit fee that would neutralize a same-block or adjacent-block deposit-then-withdraw around this instruction. This is structurally identical to the Peapods root cause: an event that causes `_totalAssets`/`asset_share_value` to jump sharply, which a user can position themselves in front of (deposit) and immediately after (withdraw) to capture value that should accrue only to shareholders who held their position while the reward was earned.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker who deposits a large amount right before calling (or observing/triggering) `lending_pool_emissions_deposit`, then withdraws immediately afterward, receives a pro-rata cut of the injected reward based on their newly-minted shares — shares that existed for zero economic time before the reward hit. This directly transfers value away from genuine depositors who held their position and were the intended beneficiaries of the reward. Because the attacker also controls the timing of the trigger (the instruction is permissionless), they can self-schedule the attack rather than needing to "front-run" someone else's mempool transaction — an even stronger position than the original report's threat model. This is a concrete value-transfer/theft-of-yield issue affecting core accounting (`asset_share_value`) shared by every depositor in the bank.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is bounded by the reward size relative to total deposits: the attacker's gain is proportional to `(attacker_shares / total_shares) * reward_amount`, so the attack is most profitable when the attacker can temporarily dominate the share pool (e.g., low-liquidity or newly-launched banks, or via flash-loan-funded deposits if the token/instruction allows atomic deposit+withdraw in one transaction). Since the instruction is permissionless and requires no privileged role, and Solana composability allows same-transaction deposit → emissions_deposit → withdraw sequencing, this is practically executable by any unprivileged actor without needing real mempool front-running.

### Recommendation
- Require the `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` caller/owner role to be trusted, or restrict the instruction to a designated emissions-funding authority instead of leaving it fully permissionless.
- Alternatively, spread reward injections over time (e.g., a streaming/vesting rate similar to `emissions_rate` bit-flag emissions) rather than an instantaneous lump-sum bump to `asset_share_value`.
- Add a minimum holding period or withdrawal cooldown for freshly deposited shares so they are not eligible for instantaneous reward capture.
- Consider disallowing deposit and withdraw of the same position within the same transaction/slot to close the atomic sandwich window.

### Proof of Concept
1. Bank `B` has existing depositors with total_asset_shares `S` and asset_share_value `V0`.
2. Attacker observes (or independently decides to trigger) that `lending_pool_emissions_deposit(amount)` is about to be called for bank `B`.
3. Attacker calls `lending_account_deposit` with a large amount `D`, minting `D/V0` shares just before the emissions deposit lands.
4. `lending_pool_emissions_deposit(amount)` executes: [4](#0-3)  raises `asset_share_value` for the whole share pool, including the attacker's freshly minted shares.
5. Attacker immediately calls `lending_account_withdraw` for their full position, realizing `(D/V0) * V1` where `V1 > V0`, capturing a slice of `amount` proportional to `D/(S+D)` despite holding the position for effectively zero time — value taken directly from the reward that should have accrued to pre-existing depositors.

    let total_assets = bank.get_asset_amount(total_asset_shares)?;
