### Title
Bancor-style TRC10 Exchange inject/withdraw lack slippage protection, enabling sandwich attacks that drain liquidity providers - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java`, `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java`)

### Summary
java-tron's on-chain TRC10 bancor-style Exchange (accessible via `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` broadcastable transactions) computes the counter-token amount transferred to/from a liquidity provider purely from the exchange's current pool ratio at execution time, with no user-supplied minimum/maximum bound. This mirrors the reported ERC4626 vault issue: any unprivileged account broadcasting an inject/withdraw transaction is exposed to sandwich attacks by MEV actors who can manipulate the pool ratio via `ExchangeTransactionContract` trades immediately before and after the victim's transaction lands.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeInjectActuator.execute` computes `anotherTokenQuant` strictly from the current `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` ratio in the `ExchangeCapsule` at execution time: `anotherTokenQuant = floorDiv(multiplyExact(secondTokenBalance, tokenQuant), firstTokenBalance)` (or the symmetric branch) [2](#0-1) . This value is deducted directly from the sender's balance/asset with no cap the caller can enforce [3](#0-2) . Its validation logic re-derives the same ratio and only checks balance sufficiency and pool limits, never a user-supplied bound [4](#0-3) .

Likewise, `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute` computes `anotherTokenQuant` from the live pool ratio via BigInteger division and pays it out to the withdrawing creator with no minimum-received guarantee [5](#0-4) . Its validation only enforces a "precision" check (that the ratio divides evenly within a small remainder tolerance), not a user-chosen slippage bound [6](#0-5) .

By contrast, `ExchangeTransactionContract` (the trade/swap path) does carry an `expected` field and `ExchangeTransactionActuator.doValidate()` explicitly rejects the transaction if `anotherTokenQuant < tokenExpected` [7](#0-6) , proving the protocol authors recognized the need for slippage protection for one Exchange operation but omitted it for inject/withdraw. The proto definitions confirm this asymmetry: `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` have no `expected` (or min/max) field, while `ExchangeTransactionContract` does [1](#0-0) .

### Impact Explanation
Any account holding the relevant TRX/TRC10 assets can broadcast `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` transactions through the public mempool. An attacker observing a pending inject/withdraw transaction can front-run it with an `ExchangeTransactionContract` swap to skew `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance`, causing the victim's inject to deposit an unfavorable (larger) amount of the counter-token or causing the victim's withdraw to receive an unfavorable (smaller) amount, and then back-run to restore the ratio and capture the difference. This results in direct, quantifiable theft of funds from liquidity providers/exchange creators interacting with the on-chain Exchange feature, with no way for the victim to protect themselves at the protocol level.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation only requires the standard sandwich-attack capability available to any transaction broadcaster/miner-adjacent actor watching the mempool and does not require any privileged role, malicious SR/witness behavior, or special network position beyond typical MEV tooling. The Exchange feature and its actuators are part of the standard actuator dispatch reachable directly from a signed transaction.

### Recommendation
Add a user-supplied slippage bound to `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` (e.g., `expected`/min-received or max-paid fields, analogous to `ExchangeTransactionContract.expected`), and enforce it in `ExchangeInjectActuator.doValidate()`/`execute()` and `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.doValidate()`/`execute()` by rejecting the transaction when the computed `anotherTokenQuant` falls outside the caller's specified bound.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker monitors mempool for a victim's `ExchangeInjectContract` (or `ExchangeWithdrawContract`) transaction on exchange `exchangeId`.
2. Attacker submits an `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade with higher gas/priority to shift `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` in `ExchangeCapsule`, as processed in `ExchangeTransactionActuator.execute` [8](#0-7) .
3. Victim's inject/withdraw executes against the now-skewed ratio, computing `anotherTokenQuant` per `ExchangeInjectActuator.execute`/`ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute` lines cited above, transferring an unfavorable amount with no bound check.
4. Attacker submits a reverse `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade to restore the ratio, realizing profit extracted from the victim, since neither inject nor withdraw contracts carry a caller-enforceable slippage/expected field to prevent this.
