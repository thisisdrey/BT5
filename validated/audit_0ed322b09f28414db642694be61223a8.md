### Title
Self-triggered partial liquidation can strategically restore "healthy" LTV status, delaying necessary liquidation and increasing bad-debt risk - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`)

### Summary
`liquidate` in `v0-4-market.clar` gates liquidation on a single point-in-time check (`current-ltv >= ltv-liq-partial`) evaluated once at the start of the call, and lets the caller pick an arbitrary `debt-amount` (only upper-bounded, never lower-bounded) to repay. Because a partial liquidation always improves the position's LTV, anyone — including the borrower themselves acting as their own liquidator — can submit a minimally-sized `liquidate` call that nudges the position's LTV just under `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL`, causing every subsequent liquidation attempt to revert with `ERR-HEALTHY` until price drops further. This is the same root cause as the referenced LoopFi finding: partial liquidation can be weaponized to game the binary "safe/unsafe" liquidation gate.

### Finding Description
The health gate is:
```
(health-check  (asserts! (>= current-ltv ltv-liq-partial) ERR-HEALTHY))
``` [1](#0-0) 

`current-ltv` is computed fresh at the top of `liquidate` from the position's current collateral/debt notional values [2](#0-1) . If this check passes, the amount actually liquidated is derived from `debt-amount`, a caller-supplied parameter that is only capped from above by `max-debt-usd` (itself derived from `current-ltv` via the graduated `calc-liquidation-params`/`calc-liq-factor` formula) — there is no floor other than `debt-to-repay > 0`:
```
(debt-actual-usd (if (> debt-usd max-debt-usd) max-debt-usd debt-usd))
``` [3](#0-2) 
```
(asserts! (> debt-to-repay u0) ERR-ZERO-LIQUIDATION-AMOUNTS)
(asserts! (> coll-final u0) ERR-ZERO-LIQUIDATION-AMOUNTS)
``` [4](#0-3) 

Because collateral seized is proportional to debt repaid (plus penalty), even a tiny `debt-amount` strictly decreases the position's LTV. There is no restriction anywhere in the function preventing `borrower == contract-caller` (self-liquidation) — the only authorization check is `(asserts! (is-eq contract-caller tx-sender) ERR-AUTHORIZATION)` [5](#0-4) , which merely disallows contract-vs-tx-sender mismatch, not self-targeting. The borrower can therefore call `liquidate(self, ..., small debt_amount, ...)` themselves to precisely land `current-ltv` just below `ltv-liq-partial`, re-arming the `ERR-HEALTHY` gate for any third party (or for a further price drop) that would otherwise warrant continued/larger liquidation.

This mirrors the LoopFi PoC pattern exactly: Alice's unsafe position is nudged safe by a partial liquidation sized just enough to cross the threshold, after which a legitimate liquidator's attempt reverts with the "not unsafe"/`ERR-HEALTHY` equivalent, even though price continued to decline into unsafe territory relative to where a full/larger liquidation should have occurred.

### Impact Explanation
Delaying liquidation by strategically choosing the repay amount lets a borrower's position remain under-collateralized for longer while blocking honest liquidators from stepping in when they otherwise could/should. During a falling-price scenario this increases the probability that the position's collateral value ends up insufficient to cover debt + liquidation penalty by the time it can be liquidated again, directly increasing protocol bad-debt/socialization exposure (`socialize-debt-asset` path) beyond what would occur under prompt liquidation — i.e., increased protocol insolvency risk.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any borrower with an unsafe position can self-trigger this at will with a single transaction and no special privileges, capital cost is limited to a small liquidation penalty on a minimal slice of their own debt, and no minimum liquidation size or self-liquidation restriction exists to prevent it. This makes the analog trivially reachable from an ordinary principal's own account.

### Recommendation
- Disallow `borrower == contract-caller` (or `tx-sender`) in `liquidate`/`liquidate-multi`/`liquidate-redeem` to prevent self-liquidation gaming.
- Enforce a minimum meaningful liquidation size (e.g., require `debt-actual-usd` to represent at least a floor fraction of `max-debt-usd`, or require repayment down to at or below `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL` minus a safety margin) so a partial liquidation cannot be sized purely to just barely clear the health threshold.
- Consider tracking "was-unsafe" state so a position that crossed into unsafe territory remains eligible for continued liquidation attention until fully restored via genuine repayment/collateral top-up rather than via liquidation itself.

### Proof of Concept
1. Borrower deposits collateral and borrows debt such that `current-ltv` sits just above `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL` (e.g., price drop pushes LTV from safe to slightly above partial threshold).
2. Borrower (or a colluding party) calls `liquidate(borrower, collateral-ft, debt-ft, small_debt_amount, ...)` with `small_debt_amount` chosen so `debt-actual-usd` is far below `max-debt-usd` but large enough that, after collateral seizure at `calc-liq-collateral-repay`, the resulting `current-ltv` for the position drops just under `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL`.
3. A legitimate liquidator subsequently calls `liquidate(borrower, ...)`; `(asserts! (>= current-ltv ltv-liq-partial) ERR-HEALTHY)` [1](#0-0)  now reverts even though the position remains economically risky and price continues to move against it.
4. Only after a further price decline sufficient to push `current-ltv` back above `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL` can liquidation resume, during which time the protocol carries elevated under-collateralization risk that prompt/full liquidation would have avoided.
