`create` (mainnet/contracts/market/v0-market-vault.clar:150) is a purely bookkeeping private function that assigns a fresh sequential id via `increment` and returns a struct with `mask: u0` — it performs no numeric conversion, no share-price math, and no rounding of any kind. [1](#0-0) 

`create` is only reachable indirectly through `resolve-or-create`, which is called by `collateral-add` and `debt-add-scaled` when a principal has no existing `reverse` map entry yet — it has no coupling whatsoever to `vault-redeem`'s share price or to `liquidate-redeem`'s flow. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

`liquidate-redeem` calls `liquidate` (which uses `collateral-remove`, not `resolve-or-create`/`create`) and then `vault-redeem` to convert seized zToken collateral into underlying, sending it to `funds-receiver` — none of this touches or depends on the `create`/id-assignment logic at all. [4](#0-3) 

The premise of the question — that manipulating share price via `liquidate-redeem` can turn `create`'s "rounding direction" into a repeatable extraction — has no basis in the actual code: `create` contains no rounding, no conversion, and no arithmetic tied to share price or vault redemption at all. There is no reachable path connecting the two as described, and no concrete loss, freezing, or state corruption can be demonstrated.

  (let ((id? (map-get? reverse account)))
    (match id?
      id (lookup id)
         (create account)
    )))
