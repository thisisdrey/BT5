Audit Report

## Title
Donation attack on `pallet-asset-conversion` pool reserves can transiently distort LP-mint ratio, but is not a permanent lock - (File: `substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/lib.rs`)

## Summary
`do_add_liquidity` does read live `get_balance(&pool_account, asset)` as "reserves" for LP-mint ratio math, and an unprivileged actor can transfer tokens directly to the deterministically-derivable pool account to inflate one side of the reserve pair, as the claim describes. However, this same reserve is also used by the pool's swap pricing (constant-product formula), so any donated excess on one side is economically arbitrageable — a modest, ordinary-sized swap on the other asset can drain the bulk of the donated excess back out, since the AMM output for a swap scales with the *disproportion* between reserves. This means the "permanent" denial-of-service framing in the claim is not substantiated: the pool is not durably bricked, it is a self-correcting market condition that any profit-seeking actor (including the victim who wants to add liquidity) can resolve with a normal-sized transaction before retrying `add_liquidity`.

## Finding Description
The claim accurately describes the mechanics of `do_add_liquidity`: reserves are read live via `Self::get_balance(&pool_account, asset1/2)` [1](#0-0) , LP-mint amounts are computed via `mul_div(amount, total_supply, reserve)` which rounds toward zero [2](#0-1) , and a resulting `0`/near-`0` `lp_token_amount` reverts with `InsufficientLiquidityMinted` [3](#0-2) . The pool account address is indeed derivable and donatable by anyone, as demonstrated by the existing `cannot_block_pool_creation` test which shows the codebase is aware of pre-funding of pool accounts.

The critical flaw in the claim's severity assessment is that it treats the reserve corruption as *permanent*. In a constant-product AMM, the same live-balance reserve used for LP-mint ratio math is also used for swap pricing. When one side of the reserve pair is artificially inflated relative to the other, the pool's swap price for that asset becomes far below fair market value, creating an arbitrage opportunity: a normal-sized swap trading the *non-inflated* asset into the pool can extract a disproportionately large amount of the *inflated* asset back out, quickly re-balancing the ratio toward parity with the actual amounts being traded. This is a standard, expected AMM self-correction mechanism, not a pallet bug being "fixed" by an attacker's action — and it means any user blocked by `InsufficientLiquidityMinted` (or an unrelated arbitrageur incentivized by free value sitting in the pool) can restore normal reserve proportions with an ordinary transaction before retrying `add_liquidity`. The claim's PoC and "Impact"/"Likelihood" sections do not account for this arbitrage dynamic, and do not demonstrate that the donation-induced revert survives a single counter-swap, which is required to substantiate a "permanent" lock.

Additionally, extreme reserve inflation (sufficient to make `mul_div` round any realistic deposit to zero even after arbitrage narrows the ratio) requires the attacker to permanently give up real value roughly proportional to the magnitude of distortion sought — this is materially different from XDeFi's cited bug, where a single wei-cost action corrupted a ratio variable with no economic counter-force restoring it. Here, the corrupted state is subject to continuous market-based correction inherent to the AMM design itself.

## Impact Explanation
Because the described "lock" is not permanent and is remediable through normal, permissionless, economically-incentivized swap activity (using the same public extrinsics available to any user, at ordinary transaction cost), this does not qualify as a "permanent user-fund or bridge-state lock" under the impact gate. It is, at most, a transient griefing/self-resolving condition inherent to constant-product AMM design, which does not meet the required severity bar.

## Likelihood Explanation
While the donation action itself is cheap and permissionless (a plain asset transfer to a derivable address), sustaining the described denial-of-service outcome requires that no one — including the victim — ever performs a counter-swap that arbitrages the inflated reserve, which is economically implausible given the natural incentive to capture the resulting arbitrage profit. The claim's PoC does not test or rule out this remediation path.

## Recommendation
N/A — claim rejected due to insufficient evidence of "permanent" impact; the underlying live-balance reserve read behavior is a known, deliberate design choice (per `pr_12408`) and is self-correcting via swap arbitrage, not a standalone exploitable invariant break.

## Proof of Concept
N/A — the claim's PoC does not demonstrate the lock survives a subsequent arbitrage swap, which is required to substantiate permanence; without that, the finding does not meet the required impact bar.
