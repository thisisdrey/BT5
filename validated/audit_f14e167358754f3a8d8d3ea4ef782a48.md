### Title
Spot-price AMM reserves used for XCM fee swaps without TWAP/slippage protection allow reserve manipulation to underpay or drain fees - (File: cumulus/primitives/utility/src/lib.rs)

### Summary
`SwapFirstAssetTrader` (used as an XCM `WeightTrader` on AssetHub and other parachains, wired via `asset-hub-rococo/src/xcm_config.rs` and `asset-hub-westend/src/xcm_config.rs`) converts a user-supplied fee asset into the `Target` fee asset by calling `pallet-asset-conversion`'s `SwapCredit::swap_tokens_for_exact_tokens` / `swap_exact_tokens_for_tokens`. These calls compute the exchange rate purely from the AMM pool's instantaneous reserve balances (`get_reserves`/`get_amount_in`/`get_amount_out` in `substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/lib.rs`), with no TWAP, no observation window, and — critically — no slippage bound (`amount_in_max`/`amount_out_min` is not supplied by the trader). This is the same broken invariant as the external report: a price derived from a single, instantaneously-manipulable observation is used to settle real value, with no averaging or bound to resist manipulation.

### Finding Description
`Pallet::get_reserves` in `substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/lib.rs:1499-1514` reads the pool account's live balances with no history or averaging: [1](#0-0) 

`SwapFirstAssetTrader::buy_weight` swaps the user's fee asset for the `Target` asset using `swap_tokens_for_exact_tokens`, taking the full `credit_in` and letting `get_amount_in` (spot formula, no bound) dictate how much is consumed at the current reserve ratio: [2](#0-1) 

`refund_weight` similarly swaps `Target` back into the original fee asset via `swap_exact_tokens_for_tokens` with `amount_out_min = None`, i.e., no slippage protection whatsoever, and the refunded amount is entirely determined by whatever the pool reserves are at that exact moment: [3](#0-2) 

Both `get_amount_in` and `get_amount_out` (`substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/lib.rs:1421-1463` and the paired function) are pure constant-product formulas over the pool's *current* balances — there is no cardinality, no accumulator, no minimum number of observations, exactly analogous to a Uniswap V3 pool left at `observationCardinality = 1`.

An unprivileged actor who can add/remove liquidity or perform swaps against the same pool used by the trader (all `create_pool`/`add_liquidity`/`remove_liquidity`/`swap_*` calls are permissionless, see `substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/lib.rs:442-573`) can skew the reserve ratio immediately before an XCM message that triggers `buy_weight`/`refund_weight` executes in the same block, extract a favorable rate, and reverse the skew afterward — a single-block sandwich, with no oracle averaging or slippage bound to block it.

### Impact Explanation
Because the fee-swap price is taken from unaveraged, freely-manipulable pool reserves and neither `buy_weight` nor `refund_weight` enforce a slippage bound, an attacker who transiently distorts the `Target`/fee-asset pool can cause XCM fee accounting to settle at an economically incorrect rate. This can result in underpriced/overcharged fee settlement for the `Target`/native asset (impacting collator/treasury revenue — “public underpriced work”) or in value being extracted from the AMM pool's liquidity providers via the refund path, since `refund_weight`'s swap has no `amount_out_min` floor at all. This maps to the "public underpriced work" and "conserve value / settle exactly once to the rightful beneficiary and amount" pivots for balances/pools/bridge-rewards value flows on AssetHub-class chains.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium: exploitation requires enough capital to move the specific `Target`/fee-asset pool's reserves within a single block window and knowledge of when weight-buying/refund XCM messages will execute, but no privileged role, governance action, or off-chain infrastructure compromise is needed — all `pallet-asset-conversion` calls used to move the reserves are permissionless extrinsics available to any signed account.

### Recommendation
- Add explicit slippage bounds to both legs of `SwapFirstAssetTrader` (`amount_in_max` in `buy_weight`, `amount_out_min` in `refund_weight`) rather than relying purely on `SwapCredit`'s exact-amount semantics.
- Avoid pricing fee swaps off the single instantaneous pool reserve snapshot; either use a time-weighted price (requiring `pallet-asset-conversion` or a wrapping oracle to accumulate multiple observations over a window) or cap the fraction of pool reserves that a single fee-swap may move, to bound the manipulability of a single-block sandwich.
- Consider rejecting the swap (falling back to `TakeFirstAssetTrader`/direct-asset payment) if the realized rate deviates materially from a recent moving-average rate.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker observes/knows that some upcoming XCM message will pay/refund weight fees via `SwapFirstAssetTrader` against the `(Target, feeAsset)` pool on AssetHub.
2. In the same block (or immediately prior), the attacker calls the permissionless `pallet_asset_conversion::Pallet::swap_exact_tokens_for_tokens` or `add_liquidity`/`remove_liquidity` (see `substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/lib.rs:521-545`) to sharply skew the `(Target, feeAsset)` reserve ratio.
3. The XCM message executes; `SwapFirstAssetTrader::buy_weight` (`cumulus/primitives/utility/src/lib.rs:471-489`) calls `SwapCredit::swap_tokens_for_exact_tokens`, which internally reads the now-skewed `get_reserves` and computes `get_amount_in` off that single manipulated observation — settling the fee swap at the distorted rate, with no `amount_in_max` to reject it.
4. Attacker reverses their swap/liquidity action, restoring the pool and pocketing the difference between the fair rate and the manipulated settlement rate (or, on the refund path via `refund_weight`, extracting excess `feeAsset` from the pool since `amount_out_min = None`).
