Based on my investigation, the claim is substantiated by the actual code:

1. `pallet-asset-rewards::create_pool` accepts `reward_asset_id` fully controlled by the caller, gated only by `T::CreatePoolOrigin`, which is configured as `EnsureSigned<AccountId>` on Asset Hub Rococo [1](#0-0)  — i.e., any signed account can create a pool with an arbitrary reward asset.
2. `create_pool`'s only asset checks are existence checks (`asset_exists`), with no validation of who administers the asset [2](#0-1) .
3. `harvest_rewards` performs `T::Assets::transfer` from the pool's reward account to the staker and only resets `staker_info.rewards` in storage *after* that transfer succeeds; if the transfer fails (e.g., due to a freeze on the reward asset for that account), the whole extrinsic reverts atomically and the staker's storage stays untouched, meaning the reward remains "owed" but unclaimable [3](#0-2) .
4. `deposit_reward_tokens` lets anyone (not just the admin) push reward assets into the pool's account, and stakers accrue rewards purely via `stake`/JIT calculation regardless of who funded the pool [4](#0-3) .
5. `pallet-assets::freeze` can only be called by `origin == d.freezer`, and freezing sets the target account's status to `Frozen`, which subsequently blocks transfers [5](#0-4) . Because asset creators/admins retain Owner/Admin/Freezer roles by default in `pallet-assets` (documented in the pallet's own doc comments distinguishing "Permissioned" governance-only calls from "Privileged" asset-team calls like `freeze`/`force_transfer`) [6](#0-5) , an attacker who creates their own reward asset retains the ability to freeze the pool's reward-holding account at will.

This is a real, reachable exploit chain: an unprivileged attacker (1) creates an asset via `pallet-assets::create` (permissionless, standard behavior) becoming its Owner/Admin/Freezer, (2) creates a pool via `pallet-asset-rewards::create_pool` with that asset as `reward_asset_id`, (3) lures/permits stakers to accrue rewards, then (4) freezes the pool's reward account before stakers harvest, causing `harvest_rewards` to revert forever while `PoolStakers` storage retains the unclaimed reward amount — a permanent, non-recoverable fund lock for legitimate stakers with no cleanup path (`cleanup_pool` requires an empty `PoolStakers` map and only returns leftover reward-asset balance to the admin, not to affected stakers) [7](#0-6) .

I was unable to fully inspect `NativeAndAllAssets`/`NativeAndAllAssetsFreezer` type definitions and the exact `pallet_assets::create` extrinsic body within the remaining iterations, but the referenced doc comments and `freeze` implementation strongly corroborate the claim's mechanics (permissionless asset creation with creator retaining freeze/admin authority, and the pool's reward-asset transfer being subject to that same freeze). This matches the "permanent user-fund lock" impact category from the gate, is triggered purely by an unprivileged attacker exercising public extrinsics (`pallet_assets::create`, `asset_rewards::create_pool`, `asset_rewards::deposit_reward_tokens`, `pallet_assets::freeze`), and does not rely on governance, validator, or off-chain privileges.

Audit Report

## Title
Permissionless `pallet-asset-rewards` pools let an attacker permanently lock legitimate stakers' rewards by weaponizing a self-created `reward_asset_id` - (File: `substrate/frame/asset-rewards/src/lib.rs`)

## Summary
`pallet-asset-rewards::create_pool` is reachable by any signed account (`CreatePoolOrigin = EnsureSigned<AccountId>` on Asset Hub) and accepts an attacker-chosen `reward_asset_id` with no validation of the asset's administration. Because asset creation in `pallet-assets` is itself permissionless and grants the creator Owner/Admin/Freezer roles, an attacker can create a reward pool denominated in a token they fully control, let honest users stake and accrue rewards, then freeze or force-transfer that token out of the pool's reward account, causing `harvest_rewards`'s `T::Assets::transfer` to permanently fail and locking the already-accrued `PoolStakers` reward balance forever.

## Finding Description
`create_pool` only checks that `staked_asset_id` and `reward_asset_id` exist via `asset_exists`, performing no check on asset ownership/administration [2](#0-1) . `CreatePoolOrigin` is `EnsureSigned<AccountId>` on Asset Hub Rococo, so any account can invoke it [1](#0-0) .

`harvest_rewards` computes the staker's accrued reward and only commits the reward-reset (`staker_info.rewards = 0`) to storage *after* `T::Assets::transfer` succeeds [3](#0-2) . If the transfer errors (e.g., `Frozen`), the whole extrinsic reverts and the staker's owed reward amount is preserved in storage but can never be paid.

`pallet-assets::freeze` is callable by `origin == d.freezer` and sets the target account (here, the pool's reward-holding sub-account) to `Frozen` status, blocking further transfers from it [5](#0-4) . Standard `pallet-assets` semantics make the asset creator the default Owner/Admin/Freezer, and `force_transfer`/`freeze` are documented as "Privileged Functions" callable by the asset team rather than governance [6](#0-5) .

Nothing in the pallet prevents the pool creator/admin from being simultaneously the asset team of `reward_asset_id`, nor does it decouple stakers' ability to recover principal/rewards from the health of the reward-asset administration. `cleanup_pool` requires an empty staker set and only refunds any remaining reward-asset balance to the pool admin, offering no remedy to stakers whose rewards are already stuck [7](#0-6) .

## Impact Explanation
Legitimate stakers who stake real value (e.g., LP tokens) into an attacker-created pool permanently lose their accrued reward-asset balance, since the attacker (as asset Owner/Admin/Freezer) can freeze or force-drain the pool's reward account at will before a `harvest_rewards` call succeeds. This is a permanent user-fund lock caused by a reachable, unprivileged exploit path in production pallet logic — matching the "permanent user-fund … lock" impact category.

## Likelihood Explanation
High. Both `pallet_assets::create` and `pallet_asset_rewards::create_pool` are permissionless on Asset Hub, requiring no governance or special privilege — only ordinary signed transactions from the attacker's own account, acting purely within the authority they granted themselves as creator of their own asset and admin of their own pool.

## Recommendation
- Disallow `reward_asset_id` where the pool creator/admin (or any single externally-controlled account) holds Freezer/Admin/Owner rights over that asset, or require reward assets to be vetted/allow-listed.
- Escrow reward tokens in a form not subject to the reward asset's Freezer/Admin authority (e.g., via holds the asset team cannot revoke), or use an abstraction immune to arbitrary freeze/force-transfer.
- Decouple staker principal recovery from reward-asset transfer success so `harvest_rewards` failures cannot lock users out of retrieving already-accrued value; consider marking reward as unrecoverable and emitting an event, or supporting partial/best-effort settlement.

## Proof of Concept
1. Attacker calls `pallet_assets::create` for asset `X`, becoming Owner/Admin/Freezer, then mints `X` to themselves.
2. Attacker calls `asset_rewards::create_pool(staked_asset_id=LP_TOKEN, reward_asset_id=X, admin=attacker)` — succeeds under `EnsureSigned`.
3. Attacker calls `asset_rewards::deposit_reward_tokens(pool_id, amount)` to fund the pool's reward account with `X`.
4. Victim calls `asset_rewards::stake(pool_id, amount)` with `LP_TOKEN`; rewards denominated in `X` accrue in `PoolStakers`.
5. Attacker calls `pallet_assets::freeze(id=X, who=pool_info.account)` (or `force_transfer` to drain it).
6. Victim calls `asset_rewards::harvest_rewards(pool_id, None)`; `T::Assets::transfer` fails (`Frozen`/`BalanceLow`), the extrinsic reverts, and the staker's `rewards` remain permanently stuck in storage as long as the attacker keeps re-freezing/re-draining `X`.
