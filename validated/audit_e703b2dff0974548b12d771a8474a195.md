### Title
Bancor-style Exchange withdrawal lacks slippage protection, allowing frontrunning to force imbalanced/manipulated payout - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java`)

### Summary
`ExchangeWithdrawContract`, used to remove liquidity from a Tron `Exchange` (the Bancor-formula AMM pool for TRX↔TRC10 pairs), has no minimum-output / expected-amount field, unlike its sibling `ExchangeTransactionContract` which does [1](#0-0) . Any account can submit an `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade against the same pool to shift `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` immediately before a pending withdrawal is packed into a block, causing the withdrawer to receive a far different (and attacker-favorable) split of the two pool assets than what the ratio was at signing time.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()` computes `anotherTokenQuant` purely from the exchange's *current* `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` at execution time, using the pool ratio: `anotherTokenQuant = secondBalance * tokenQuant / firstBalance` (or vice versa) [2](#0-1) . There is no field in `ExchangeWithdrawContract` to bound the acceptable `anotherTokenQuant`, and `doValidate()` only checks internal consistency of the ratio ("Not precise enough"), never comparing against a user-supplied minimum [3](#0-2) .

By contrast, `ExchangeTransactionActuator` (the trade/swap path) explicitly requires and validates `tokenExpected` before executing a trade: `if (anotherTokenQuant < tokenExpected) throw ContractValidateException("token required must greater than expected")` [4](#0-3) . This shows the codebase is aware slippage protection is needed for pool-ratio-dependent operations, but it was omitted for withdrawals.

Because Tron's Bancor-formula exchange pool balance can be arbitrarily moved by any account issuing an `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade (bounded only by `getExchangeBalanceLimit()` [5](#0-4) ), an attacker observing a pending `ExchangeWithdrawContract` in the mempool can frontrun it with a large trade that skews the `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` ratio, then optionally back-run to restore/rebalance the pool and capture the difference. This directly mirrors the report's "vault withdrawal frontrun" pattern: the withdrawing party burns their LP-equivalent position and receives an amount computed against a manipulated, attacker-controlled state, with no on-chain guarantee tying the payout to the price the user expected when signing.

### Impact Explanation
An exchange creator withdrawing liquidity can be forced to receive an economically unfavorable split of `firstTokenID`/`secondTokenID` (e.g., overwhelmingly the depreciated/less desired asset), while the attacker profits from the price impact created by their own frontrunning trade and any follow-up trade to re-balance the pool. This is a direct value-extraction/theft-of-funds vector reachable purely through two ordinary signed transactions (`ExchangeTransactionContract` + naturally pending `ExchangeWithdrawContract`), requiring no special privilege — satisfying the "unauthorized account operation / theft of funds" bar.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation only requires: (1) visibility of a pending `ExchangeWithdrawContract` in the transaction pool/mempool (standard MEV/frontrunning capability on any public chain), and (2) submitting an `ExchangeTransactionContract` with sufficient fee/bandwidth priority to be included first. No special account permissions, SR/witness status, or contract deployment is needed — any account holding TRX/TRC10 balance in the relevant tokens can execute the trade. Given java-tron's Exchange (Bancor) pools are a core, long-standing production feature, this is a realistically reachable path for any market participant.

### Recommendation
Add an `expected`/minimum-output field to `ExchangeWithdrawContract` (mirroring `ExchangeTransactionContract.expected`), and have `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.doValidate()`/`execute()` reject the withdrawal if the computed `anotherTokenQuant` falls below the caller-specified minimum, consistent with the slippage check already present in `ExchangeTransactionActuator`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Exchange pool holds 1,000,000 unit of TokenA and 2,000,000 unit of TokenB (ratio 1:2).
2. Creator broadcasts `ExchangeWithdrawContract` to withdraw 100,000 TokenA, expecting ~200,000 TokenB in return per current ratio.
3. Attacker observes this pending transaction and frontruns it with an `ExchangeTransactionContract` trading a large amount of TokenB into the pool for TokenA, sharply changing the ratio (e.g., to 1:5) — this trade is fully valid since it only requires meeting its own `expected` slippage check [4](#0-3) .
4. The creator's withdrawal then executes against the now-skewed ratio in `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()` [6](#0-5) , yielding a far smaller/larger amount of TokenB than the creator anticipated, with no on-chain mechanism to reject the trade based on a minimum acceptable output.
5. Attacker back-runs with a reverse `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade to restore the pool ratio, capturing the spread extracted from the victim's withdrawal.
