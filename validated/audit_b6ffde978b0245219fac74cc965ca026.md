### Title
Unbounded low-liquidity Exchange pool creation enables Bancor-formula price manipulation and fund extraction - ([File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeCreateActuator.java])

### Summary
Java-tron's built-in decentralized `Exchange` (a Bancor-formula AMM for TRX/TRC10 pairs) can be created by any account with an arbitrarily small starting liquidity — the only checks in `ExchangeCreateActuator.doValidate()` are that each side's balance is `> 0` and below an *upper* `ExchangeBalanceLimit`. There is no minimum-liquidity floor. This mirrors the WBGL report's root cause: an on-chain price source backed by de-minimis, easily-manipulated liquidity, which can be leveraged to extract funds from other users who trade against it.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeCreateActuator.doValidate()` only enforces: [1](#0-0) 
i.e. `firstTokenBalance > 0`, `secondTokenBalance > 0`, and both below `dynamicStore.getExchangeBalanceLimit()`. Nothing prevents creating a pool with, e.g., `firstTokenBalance = 1`, `secondTokenBalance = 1` (or any similarly tiny values), which is trivially affordable for any account with a small TRX/TRC10 balance and the fixed create fee.

Once created, the pool's exchange rate is fully determined by the Bancor-style relay-token math in `ExchangeCapsule.transaction()` / `ExchangeProcessor.exchange()`: [2](#0-1) 
With such a thin pool, a single `ExchangeTransactionContract` or `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` submitted by the creator (or any account, since anyone can also inject/withdraw/trade against any exchange ID) shifts the implied price disproportionately, exactly as WBGL's thin liquidity allowed cheap price manipulation of the oracle. `ExchangeInjectActuator` and `ExchangeWithdrawActuator` recompute the counter-token amount proportionally from current pool ratios with no minimum-liquidity re-check after the operation: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

`ExchangeTransactionActuator` then executes trades against this manipulable price and directly credits/debits real TRX and TRC10 asset balances between the trading account and the pool: [5](#0-4) 
The only protection a counterparty has is the `expected` slippage-floor check in validate: [6](#0-5) 
which is client-supplied and does not prevent an attacker from first manipulating the pool ratio (via self-serve inject/withdraw/trade) and then luring/front-running a victim trade at the skewed rate, or performing a classic "sandwich" around a victim's `ExchangeTransactionContract`.

### Impact Explanation
Because pool creation has no minimum-liquidity floor, an attacker can create or target a thin Exchange pool and cheaply skew its price with negligible capital (as in the WBGL analog, where thin liquidity let an attacker cheaply manipulate the collateral price). Victims who trade against that pool via `ExchangeTransactionContract` receive a manipulated conversion rate, resulting in real TRX/TRC10 value being extracted from them into the attacker's account — an unauthorized transfer of value (theft) enabled purely by an unprivileged sequence of signed transactions (`ExchangeCreateContract`, `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract`, `ExchangeTransactionContract`).

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account can reach this path by submitting standard, broadcastable Exchange-family contracts (`ExchangeCreateContract`, `ExchangeInjectContract`, `ExchangeWithdrawContract`, `ExchangeTransactionContract`) processed by `ExchangeCreateActuator`/`ExchangeInjectActuator`/`ExchangeWithdrawActuator`/`ExchangeTransactionActuator`. No special privileges, SR/witness status, or off-chain assumptions are required — only that a victim chooses to trade against a pool the attacker controls or has skewed, which is a normal and expected usage pattern of the public Exchange feature.

### Recommendation
Introduce a minimum-liquidity requirement in `ExchangeCreateActuator.doValidate()` (and correspondingly re-validate post-inject/withdraw balances in `ExchangeInjectActuator`/`ExchangeWithdrawActuator`) so pools cannot be created or drained down to economically trivial balances that make price manipulation cheap. Consider also enforcing minimum post-transaction pool balances and/or a maximum single-transaction price-impact cap in `ExchangeCapsule.transaction()`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker submits `ExchangeCreateContract` with `firstTokenBalance = 1`, `secondTokenBalance = 1` (units of TRX/TRC10) — passes `doValidate()` since both values are `>0` and below `ExchangeBalanceLimit` [7](#0-6) .
2. Attacker submits a small `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeTransactionContract` to shift the pool ratio arbitrarily, since the counter-token amount is a simple proportional (or Bancor) function of current tiny balances [8](#0-7) .
3. A victim (unaware of the skew, or lured/front-run) submits `ExchangeTransactionContract` against this exchange ID; `ExchangeTransactionActuator.execute()` settles the trade at the attacker-controlled rate and moves real balance from victim to pool/attacker [9](#0-8) .
4. Attacker withdraws/trades back out at the now-restored fair-ish rate, realizing profit extracted from the victim.
