Audit Report

## Title
Direct token donation to an asset-conversion pool account can manipulate `reserve1`/`reserve2` used for liquidity accounting, allowing an attacker to dilute/steal value from subsequent liquidity providers - (File: `substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/lib.rs`)

## Summary
`do_add_liquidity` derives `reserve1`/`reserve2` directly from the pool account's live on-chain balances via `Self::get_balance(&pool_account, asset)` rather than from any pallet-tracked "contributed liquidity" state. [1](#0-0)  Because `pool_account` is an ordinary account whose deterministic address is derivable via `T::PoolLocator::address`, anyone can inflate these reserves with a plain balance/asset transfer outside of `add_liquidity`/`swap`, corrupting the LP-token minting ratio used for later deposits and allowing an existing LP holder to capture a disproportionate share of a subsequent depositor's contribution upon `remove_liquidity`.

## Finding Description
For a non-zero `total_supply`, the LP tokens minted to a new depositor are computed as `side1 = amount1 * total_supply / reserve1` and `side2 = amount2 * total_supply / reserve2`, taking the minimum. [2](#0-1)  Both `reserve1` and `reserve2` are read as the pool account's balance *before* the current caller's own transfer is applied. [1](#0-0) [3](#0-2)  Any tokens sitting in `pool_account` due to a prior, out-of-band transfer are indistinguishable from tokens contributed through `add_liquidity`, and they are counted toward the reserve denominator without any corresponding LP-token mint. An attacker who is the pool's initial LP (holding the bulk of `total_supply`) can donate tokens to `pool_account` in the pool's current price ratio (avoiding a swap-arbitrage signal), then wait for a legitimate depositor to call `add_liquidity`. That depositor's minted shares are computed against the inflated reserves and are therefore diluted relative to the fair value of their contribution; the attacker, still holding a large fraction of `total_supply`, can then call `remove_liquidity`, which also reads reserves from live balances via `get_reserves`, and redeem a share of the pool that includes part of the victim's newly added funds. [4](#0-3) 

The `MintMinLiquidity` check (`ensure!(lp_token_amount > T::MintMinLiquidity::get(), Error::<T>::InsufficientLiquidityMinted)`) only prevents a depositor's minted amount from being at-or-below the minimum threshold — it forces a clean revert (and since the transfer earlier in the same dispatchable is rolled back atomically on `Err`, the victim risks no direct fund loss in that failing case) but does not prevent the depositor from minting a small-but-nonzero, unfairly-diluted amount above that threshold, i.e., partial value siphoning rather than all-or-nothing griefing. [5](#0-4)  There is no reserve-tracking storage item alongside `Pools<T>` and no reconciliation of un-attributed balance changes.

The codebase's own tests confirm awareness of donations landing in `pool_account` (e.g. `add_tiny_liquidity_directly_to_pool_address`, `cannot_block_pool_creation`, `quote_price_ignores_unrelated_non_sufficient_assets_in_pool_account`), but these only verify that small/dust donations don't break functionality or block pool creation — they do not test the large-scale, price-ratio-preserving donation used to dilute a subsequent depositor's minted shares. [6](#0-5) 

## Impact Explanation
This corrupts the LP-token minting ratio (the exact wrong values are `reserve1`/`reserve2` used in `side1`/`side2` at `substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/lib.rs:869-871`), breaking the invariant that liquidity providers' pool balances and payout shares conserve value and settle to the rightful beneficiary. An unprivileged attacker who is a pool's dominant LP holder can capture value contributed by a subsequent, unrelated depositor through `remove_liquidity`, without governance, validator, or off-chain privileges — a public-entrypoint value-misattribution vector against `pallet-asset-conversion`.

## Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires only ordinary permissionless actions: creating a pool and becoming its first LP via `create_pool`/`add_liquidity`, then a plain balance/asset transfer to the deterministic `pool_account` address. Realizing profit is opportunistic rather than a guaranteed single-transaction exploit — it depends on some future depositor calling `add_liquidity` while the donation-inflated reserve persists, and the attacker back-running that call with `remove_liquidity` before the imbalance is corrected. This gives it moderate likelihood: no sandwiching within a single block is required, but the attacker cannot force a victim's deposit and must calibrate the donation to avoid triggering an outright `InsufficientLiquidityMinted` revert for the victim.

## Recommendation
Do not compute LP-minting ratios purely from `pool_account`'s live balance. Track reserves in pallet storage (e.g., alongside `Pools<T>`), updating them only on `add_liquidity`/`remove_liquidity`/`swap`, and either ignore/reconcile any balance surplus not attributable to tracked contributions, or require that unaccounted-for balance increases be swept/donated proportionally to existing LP holders rather than folded into the pricing denominator for new depositors.

## Proof of Concept
1. Attacker calls `create_pool(asset1, asset2)` then `add_liquidity` with amounts just above `MintMinLiquidity`, becoming the dominant LP holder of `pool.lp_token`.
2. Attacker transfers additional `asset1`/`asset2` directly to the deterministic `pool_account` (via `Balances::transfer`/`Assets::transfer`), in the pool's current price ratio, inflating `reserve1`/`reserve2` as read at `substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/lib.rs:813-814` without minting any LP tokens.
3. A victim calls `add_liquidity` with a fair-value `amount1_desired`/`amount2_desired`; `side1`/`side2` at lines 869-871 divide by the inflated reserves, minting the victim fewer LP tokens than their contribution is worth (assuming the result still exceeds `MintMinLiquidity` so the call succeeds).
4. Attacker calls `remove_liquidity`, redeeming their LP-token share against the now-larger pool balances (computed via `get_reserves` at lines 913-920), capturing a disproportionate amount including part of the victim's contribution.
