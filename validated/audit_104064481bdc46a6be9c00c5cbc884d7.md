## Finding [1](#0-0) 

### Title
Liquidation reverts when the liab bank's liquidity vault lacks sufficient real tokens to pay the insurance fee - (File: `programs/marginfi/src/instructions/marginfi_account/liquidate.rs`)

### Summary
`lending_account_liquidate` unconditionally attempts an SPL transfer of the insurance fee (`insurance_fee_to_transfer`) out of the liability bank's real liquidity vault after updating internal share accounting, without first checking whether the vault actually holds enough tokens to cover that fee. This mirrors the `PlatformLogic.sol` fee-transfer bug: an accounting-derived fee amount is pushed through a real token transfer that can fail for reasons unrelated to the accounting correctness of the underlying operation, causing the whole beneficial state transition (a liquidation that would improve/resolve an unhealthy account) to revert.

### Finding Description
The liquidation flow computes `insurance_fund_fee` from the liability spread (`q_ll - q_lf`), splits it into `insurance_fee_to_transfer` (integer part) and `insurance_fee_dust` (fractional remainder), and then calls `liab_bank.withdraw_spl_transfer(insurance_fee_to_transfer, bank_liquidity_vault, bank_insurance_vault, ...)` [2](#0-1) . This happens *after* the liquidatee's liability shares have already been repaid via `liquidatee_liab_bank_account.repay(liab_amount_final)` in the same in-memory state, and *before* any check that `bank_liquidity_vault` actually holds `insurance_fee_to_transfer` real tokens.

`withdraw_spl_transfer` itself performs a raw `transfer`/`transfer_checked` CPI with no balance pre-check: [3](#0-2)  — if the vault's real balance is less than the requested amount (which is very plausible in a share-based lending pool where high utilization means most deposited liquidity is out on loan), the CPI call returns `TokenError::InsufficientFunds` and the whole instruction — and thus the whole liquidation transaction — reverts.

This is exactly the same bug class as the report: a fee-transfer step, sized purely from internal accounting rather than the actual liquidity available in the vault it draws from, can fail and roll back an operation (liquidation) whose core purpose (repaying a liability, seizing collateral, restoring account health) had nothing to do with the fee itself.

Notably, this exact failure mode is acknowledged as reachable in the project's own fuzz harness comments: `programs/marginfi/fuzz/src/lib.rs` explicitly whitelists `ProgramError::Custom(TokenError::InsufficientFunds as u32)` as an allowed liquidation error, with the comment: "because updates in 1.5 allow liquidation of underwater banks, it is now possible for a bank's liquidity value to become empty in the fuzz suite, which leads to the `liquidatee_liab_bank_account.withdraw_spl_transfer` failing" [4](#0-3) .

By contrast, the sibling fee-collection path in `collect_bank_fees.rs` was written defensively: it computes `available_liquidity` and clamps every fee transfer to `min(outstanding, available_liquidity)` before calling `withdraw_spl_transfer` [5](#0-4) . The liquidation path's insurance-fee transfer has no equivalent clamp/fallback.

### Impact Explanation
When a liab bank is highly utilized (most of its deposited liquidity out on loan) and the vault's spare real-token balance is smaller than the freshly-computed `insurance_fee_to_transfer`, any attempt to liquidate an unhealthy position against that bank reverts entirely — including the repayment/seizure that would have reduced the liquidatee's debt and improved account health. This is precisely the scenario ("underwater/high-utilization banks") the codebase's own fuzz comments call out as newly reachable. Because liquidation is a core solvency-protection mechanism, a liquidator being unable to complete a liquidation on a bank that most needs it (low spare liquidity, i.e., near-insolvent) increases the risk of bad debt accumulating and can leave the account unliquidated for longer than intended, worsening protocol insolvency risk in exactly the high-utilization conditions where liquidations matter most.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is bank/market-condition dependent: it requires the liab bank's liquidity vault to have less spare real liquidity than the insurance fee owed on a given liquidation, which is realistic during high-utilization or bank-run-like conditions — precisely when liquidations are most critical. This is an unprivileged-user-reachable path (anyone can call `lending_account_liquidate`), not requiring any admin/validator action.

### Recommendation
Clamp the insurance fee transfer to the actual available vault balance (mirroring the pattern already used in `lending_pool_withdraw_fees_permissionless`, which does `u64::min(amount, fee_vault.amount)` [6](#0-5) ), or defer/accrue any shortfall as outstanding insurance fee dust (as is already done for the fractional remainder) rather than letting the transfer instruction hard-fail the whole liquidation.

### Proof of Concept
1. Set up a liab bank (e.g., USDC) with high utilization: most deposited liquidity is borrowed out, leaving the `liquidity_vault` real token balance very low.
2. Create a liquidatee position that becomes unhealthy and is liquidatable against this bank as the liability bank.
3. Have a liquidator call `lending_account_liquidate` with an `asset_amount` sized such that `insurance_fund_fee` (and hence `insurance_fee_to_transfer`) exceeds the liab bank's current real `liquidity_vault` balance.
4. The internal share-based `repay()` call succeeds in updating accounting, but the subsequent `liab_bank.withdraw_spl_transfer(insurance_fee_to_transfer, ...)` CPI fails with `TokenError::InsufficientFunds`, reverting the entire transaction — the liquidatee remains unhealthy and unliquidated despite a valid, otherwise-executable liquidation being available.
