### Title
Lack of minimum debt threshold enables unliquidatable dust positions - (File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar)

### Summary
`borrow` and `repay` in `v0-4-market.clar` only check `(> amount u0)`, with no minimum position-size / minimum-debt enforcement, and `liquidate`'s liquidation-amount calculation is graduated and proportional to LTV/debt size, meaning tiny debt balances yield economically negligible (or zero) liquidator rewards.

### Finding Description
`borrow` only requires `(> amount u0)` and health checks, with no floor on the resulting debt size: [1](#0-0) . Similarly, `repay` allows any partial repayment down to `(> repaid-scaled-debt u0)`, i.e., leaving behind an arbitrarily small remaining scaled-debt balance (e.g., 1 unit) with no minimum-debt-remaining check: [2](#0-1) .

This is confirmed by the project's own test suite, which documents this exact edge case and asserts that borrowing an amount as small as `1n` succeeds and produces "minimal debt" rather than being rejected: [3](#0-2) .

When such a dust position later needs liquidation, `liquidate` computes the liquidatable amount via a graduated curve (`calc-liquidation-params` → `calc-liq-factor` / `calc-liq-factor-exp` / `calc-liq-factor-bound`) that scales `max-debt-usd` proportionally to `total-debt-usd` and the degree of unhealthiness: [4](#0-3) . For a dust debt balance, `total-debt-usd` is minuscule, so `max-debt-usd`, and consequently `debt-actual`, `debt-final`, and `debt-to-repay`, are all correspondingly minuscule: [5](#0-4) . The liquidator's collateral reward is directly proportional to this tiny `debt-to-repay` via `calc-liq-collateral-repay`, so the bonus received is far below the gas cost of calling `liquidate` on Stacks mainnet: [6](#0-5) .

The root cause mirrors the Notional Exponent analog: there is no protocol-enforced minimum debt/borrow size, and partial `repay` calls are unrestricted in how small a residual debt they can leave, so a borrower (or normal usage drift via partial repayments/interest accrual) can create positions whose debt is too small to be profitably liquidated, while the position still consumes egroup/mask bits and appears in the borrower's obligation list.

### Impact Explanation
Dust debt positions that fall below liquidator profitability accumulate indefinitely as effectively permanent bad debt, since no rational liquidator will pay gas to seize a reward smaller than the transaction cost. Over time, on the same collateral asset, this could combine with price drops to leave uncollateralized/under-collateralized dust obligations sitting on the books, degrading the protocol's aggregate debt accounting and contributing to under-collateralization/insolvency at the margin — matching the "protocol insolvency" impact class. The severity is bounded by the fact that each individual position's absolute dollar exposure is tiny by construction (that's exactly what makes it unliquidatable), so this is a slow, cumulative solvency-erosion issue rather than an immediate large-scale drain.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: any ordinary borrower can trigger this unintentionally or deliberately simply by calling `repay` for all but a tiny amount of their debt, or by borrowing a very small amount initially, both of which are explicitly permitted (and exercised in the test suite) by current logic. No privileged access or governance action is required, and the `borrow`/`repay` health and amount checks do not prevent it: [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) .

### Recommendation
Enforce a protocol- or asset-level minimum debt-notional threshold: reject `borrow` calls that would leave a position's per-asset (or aggregate) debt below the minimum, and in `repay`, require that a partial repayment either fully closes the debt for that asset or leaves the remaining balance at or above the minimum threshold (forcing "repay all or leave enough to remain economically liquidatable").

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice deposits collateral and calls `borrow` for `1n` unit of debt (or repays down to `1n` scaled debt via `repay`), which succeeds with no minimum-debt check as shown in `edge-cases.test.ts`: [3](#0-2) .
2. Price moves adversely (or interest accrues) such that this dust position becomes liquidatable (LTV ≥ `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL`).
3. A liquidator calling `liquidate` would receive a `coll-final`/`debt-to-repay` reward proportional to the tiny `total-debt-usd`, per `calc-liquidation-params`/`process-debt-asset`: [9](#0-8) , well under the Stacks transaction fee, so no liquidator executes it — the position remains open indefinitely as unliquidated bad debt.

  (current-ltv uint)
  (ltv-liq-partial uint)
  (ltv-liq-full uint)
  (liq-penalty-min uint)
  (liq-penalty-max uint)
  (curve-exponent uint)

      liq-penalty: liq-penalty,

    }))
