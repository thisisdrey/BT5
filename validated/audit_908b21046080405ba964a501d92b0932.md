### Title
Shared unbounded-priority `packet_sender` channel allows aggregate unstaked flood to starve staked packet delivery - ([File: streamer/src/nonblocking/quic.rs])

### Summary
The QUIC ingress path funnels both staked and unstaked packets into a single `crossbeam_channel::bounded` `packet_sender` (created e.g. as `TPU_CHANNEL_SIZE` in `core/src/tpu.rs:176`, or `bounded(1024)` in tests) via `handle_chunks` → `packet_sender.try_send(packet_batch)` at `streamer/src/nonblocking/quic.rs:816`. Because per-connection throttling caps unstaked throughput on a *per-connection* basis rather than a global aggregate budget, an attacker opening many unstaked connections can produce channel-saturating aggregate throughput, causing `TrySendError::Full` to indiscriminately drop staked packets that are queued concurrently.

### Finding Description
`SwQos`'s stream-rate limiter (`streamer/src/nonblocking/stream_throttle.rs`) enforces `MAX_UNSTAKED_TPS = 200` via `available_load_capacity_in_throttling_duration` at [1](#0-0) , but this cap is applied through a `ConnectionStreamCounter` that is instantiated *per connection* (`|| Arc::new(ConnectionStreamCounter::new())` in `try_add_connection`, `streamer/src/nonblocking/swqos.rs:209-219`), not shared across all unstaked connections. The EMA load tracker `increment_load` only accumulates load from staked traffic (`if peer_type.is_staked() { load_in_recent_interval.fetch_add(...) }`, `streamer/src/nonblocking/stream_throttle.rs:160-165`), meaning unstaked aggregate volume is never fed back into throttling decisions.

With `max_unstaked_connections` defaulting to 2000 (`DEFAULT_MAX_UNSTAKED_CONNECTIONS`, `streamer/src/quic.rs:48`) and each connection independently permitted up to ~200 streams/sec, the theoretical aggregate unstaked throughput (~400k pps) vastly exceeds the bounded channel capacity used for `packet_sender` (e.g., `bounded(1024)` in tests, `TPU_CHANNEL_SIZE` in production, `core/src/tpu.rs:176`). Once the shared channel fills, `handle_chunks` at `streamer/src/nonblocking/quic.rs:816-832` calls `packet_sender.try_send(packet_batch)` and on `TrySendError::Full(_)` simply drops the packet and bumps `total_handle_chunk_to_packet_send_full_err` — with **no distinction by `ConnectionPeerType`**. Staked packets queued concurrently by a legitimate fee-paying sender face the same drop probability as the unstaked flood packets that caused the congestion, because there is no separate lane, priority queue, or staked-preserving eviction policy at this boundary (contrast with `EvictingSender` in `streamer/src/evicting_sender.rs`, which is used for vote packets but not for this TPU packet path).

### Impact Explanation
This is a QoS/fee-prioritization evasion: cheap, unstaked, unprivileged traffic can cause disproportionate drop of staked/fee-paying transactions before they ever reach sigverify/dedup, undermining the intended stake-weighted quality-of-service guarantee that the whole `SwQos`/`stream_throttle` subsystem exists to provide. This matches an Agave bounty category of "QoS evasion" / "grossly underpriced pre-fee work crowding out fee-paying work."

### Likelihood Explanation
Feasibility requires the attacker to sustain a large number of concurrent unstaked QUIC connections (bounded by `max_connections_per_unstaked_peer` per IP = 8 by default, and `max_unstaked_connections` = 2000 globally) each sending near their individual 200 tps cap. This is achievable by an unstaked, unprivileged attacker using multiple source IPs/sockets against the leader's public TPU port, requiring no special privileges, staked keys, or validator control — only volume. The attack is repeatable for the duration a leader is producing blocks and does not require exploiting any bug beyond the architectural gap in throttling aggregation.

### Recommendation
Track and throttle unstaked load in aggregate (not solely per-connection), and/or split `packet_sender` into stake-aware lanes (e.g., separate bounded channels or an `EvictingSender`-style policy that preferentially evicts unstaked-origin packets on `Full` rather than dropping whichever packet is currently being enqueued), so a purely unstaked flood cannot force drops of concurrently-queued staked packets.

### Proof of Concept
Integration test plan (extending existing patterns in `streamer/src/nonblocking/quic.rs` tests, e.g. `test_throttling_check_no_packet_drop`):
1. Spawn `spawn_stake_weighted_qos_server` with a small `bounded(N)` `packet_sender` (e.g., N=64) and `SwQosConfig { max_unstaked_connections: 200, max_streams_per_ms: default, ..Default::default() }`, with a `StakedNodes` map containing one staked pubkey with meaningful stake.
2. Spawn many (e.g., 50) unstaked QUIC client connections concurrently, each opening uni-streams as fast as allowed, continuously for several seconds without draining `receiver` fast enough (simulate slow/backed-up consumer, as would occur transiently under real load).
3. Concurrently, from a staked client (keypair matching the staked pubkey), open a connection and attempt to send M known transactions (e.g., 100), recording how many arrive on `receiver` vs. `stats.total_handle_chunk_to_packet_send_full_err` and `stats.total_staked_packets_sent_for_batching`.
4. Assert that staked-packet delivery rate collapses well below 100% (e.g., <90%) purely due to unstaked flood filling the shared channel, demonstrating that `TrySendError::Full` drops staked packets indiscriminately alongside unstaked ones, with no preferential delivery despite `ConnectionPeerType::Staked` context being available at the drop site (`streamer/src/nonblocking/quic.rs:816`).
