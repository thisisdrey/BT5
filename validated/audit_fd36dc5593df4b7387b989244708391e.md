### Title
Bankrupt accounts can permanently evade `HandleBankruptcy` by depositing a small (>$0.10) amount, blocking bad-debt socialization - (File: programs/marginfi/src/state/marginfi_account.rs)

### Summary
`check_account_bankrupt` requires `equity_assets < BANKRUPT_THRESHOLD` ($0.10) as an absolute, liability-size-independent condition, ANDed with `liabilities_exceed_assets`. An account authority whose account has already been fully liquidated (assets ≈ 0, large residual liability) can call `lending_account_deposit` (or any other deposit path) to add any amount ≥ $0.10 of collateral, which flips `below_bankruptcy_threshold` to false and makes `is_bankrupt` false regardless of how enormous the outstanding liability is.

### Finding Description
`check_account_bankrupt` computes unweighted equity assets/liabilities via `get_health_components(..., RequirementType::Equity, ...)` and applies: [1](#0-0) 

`BANKRUPT_THRESHOLD` is a fixed $0.10 absolute value, not proportional to the account's liabilities: [2](#0-1) 

The account owner (an "unprivileged" authority per the bounty rules) is not blocked from depositing once liquidation/receivership has concluded — `lending_account_deposit` only rejects deposits when `ACCOUNT_DISABLED` or `ACCOUNT_IN_RECEIVERSHIP` is set, and `ACCOUNT_DISABLED` is only set *after* `lending_pool_handle_bankruptcy` successfully runs: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Deposits also have no risk-engine/health check (deposits can only improve health), so this deposit will always succeed as long as the account isn't yet disabled: [5](#0-4) 

Exploit flow: (1) account becomes fully liquidated with `assets ≈ 0` and `liabilities = B` (large); (2) before any keeper calls `lending_pool_handle_bankruptcy`, the account authority deposits any asset worth just over $0.10; (3) `check_account_bankrupt` now sees `equity_assets ≥ 0.1`, so `below_bankruptcy_threshold` is false, `is_bankrupt` is false, and `HandleBankruptcy` reverts with `AccountNotBankrupt`; (4) the account keeps accruing interest on `B`, growing the bad debt, while the fixed ~$0.10 deposit permanently satisfies the threshold check. This can be repeated indefinitely (each deposit only needs to keep equity_assets ≥ $0.10, a fixed, non-scaling bound) to indefinitely block bankruptcy processing on that account.

This does not require admin/governance control, oracle manipulation, or any special privilege — only the ordinary, always-permitted `lending_account_deposit` instruction available to the account authority.

### Impact Explanation
Per `guides/RISK_AND_LIQUIDATORS/BANKRUPTCY.md`, if bankruptcy is never executed on a bankrupt user, "remaining depositors can never withdraw the whole balance in the bank": [6](#0-5) 

By permanently blocking `check_account_bankrupt`'s success condition with a fixed, liability-size-independent threshold, an unprivileged account authority can prevent the bad-debt-socialization mechanism from ever running on their own bankrupt account, indefinitely freezing the proportional share of depositor funds in the affected bank(s) and letting interest accrue on an ever-growing unrecoverable liability. This matches the accepted "permanent lock/freeze of funds" and "insolvency/bad debt" categories.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires no special conditions beyond: (1) an account reaching a liquidated state where `liabilities > assets` (a rare but expected event per the bounty's own docs), and (2) the account authority being willing to spend a small, fixed amount (~$0.10+ of any liquid asset) repeatedly to keep gaming the check. The mechanism is fully deterministic — the threshold comparison in `check_account_bankrupt` is absolute, so no oracle manipulation or timing race is needed; the attacker fully controls when and how much to deposit. This is realistically exploitable by any borrower who wants to avoid `ACCOUNT_DISABLED`/`has_been_bankrupted` marking, or by a griefer who is also the account authority (e.g. via a compromised/self-created delegate).

### Recommendation
Make the bankruptcy threshold proportional to (or at least bounded relative to) the account's liabilities rather than a fixed absolute dollar amount — e.g., require `equity_assets < max(BANKRUPT_THRESHOLD, materiality_fraction * equity_liabs)`, or simply drop the `below_bankruptcy_threshold` absolute check entirely and rely on `liabilities_exceed_assets` (with a small rounding-noise floor) as the primary bankruptcy trigger. Additionally, consider disallowing/limiting deposits into accounts whose liabilities already exceed assets beyond a materiality bound, or require post-deposit re-evaluation that keeper/permissionless bankruptcy calls cannot be starved by trivial top-ups.

### Proof of Concept
Rust integration test plan (extending `programs/marginfi/tests/admin_actions/bankruptcy.rs`):
1. Set up a group/bank pair, have user borrow debt against collateral, then use `nullify_assets_for_bank` (as in existing bankruptcy tests) to zero the collateral, leaving `liability_value_equity = B` (large) and `asset_value_equity ≈ 0`.
2. Before calling `try_handle_bankruptcy`, have the (still-active, non-disabled) user account deposit a small amount of any bank's token worth just over `BANKRUPT_THRESHOLD` ($0.10), e.g. 0.1 unit of a $2 asset.
3. Call `lending_pool_handle_bankruptcy` and assert it now fails with `MarginfiError::AccountNotBankrupt`, even though `equity_liabs` (B) is orders of magnitude larger than `equity_assets` (~$0.10-$1).
4. Advance the clock to accrue interest on `B` (increasing bad debt further), and re-assert that `check_account_bankrupt` still returns `AccountNotBankrupt` as long as `equity_assets` stays ≥ threshold, demonstrating the block persists and liabilities keep growing unresolved — i.e., invariant "once `liabilities_exceed_assets && has_liabilities` by a material margin, bankruptcy cannot be indefinitely stalled by a fixed-size deposit" is violated.
