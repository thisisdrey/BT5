## Analog Found

### Title
Unrestricted `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` frequency allows the exchange creator to manipulate the Bancor-style pool price against other traders - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java`, `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java`)

### Summary
Like the `MultiStrategy.setStrategy()` finding — a privileged, allocation-changing call with no cooldown or frequency restriction that lets its caller reshape a shared pool at will — java-tron's `ExchangeInjectActuator` and `ExchangeWithdrawActuator` let the exchange **creator** call `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` an unlimited number of times, in any order, with no time-lock or per-block restriction, directly rewriting the pool's `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` (the constant-product/Bancor curve state) that determines swap prices for every other trader.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeInjectActuator.execute()` and `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()` recompute the pool's `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` and call `exchangeCapsule.setBalance(...)` every time the contract is executed, with the only authorization check being that `accountCapsule.getAddress()` equals `exchangeCapsule.getCreatorAddress()`: [1](#0-0) 

There is no cooldown, minimum interval, or per-block limitation on how often the creator may call inject/withdraw — `doValidate()` only checks balance sufficiency, token identity, and arithmetic bounds: [2](#0-1) 

The actual balance mutation happens unconditionally on every successful call: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Other users trade against this same pool via `ExchangeTransactionActuator`, whose only protection is a per-call `tokenExpected` minimum-output check supplied by the trader themselves: [5](#0-4) 

Because the creator can inject/withdraw arbitrarily many times back-to-back (even within the same block, before or after a victim's swap transaction), they can shift the pool ratio to a favorable price, execute or induce a trade, and then reverse the injection/withdrawal — a classic pool-manipulation pattern enabled by the exact same root cause as the reported bug: **no restriction on the frequency of an allocation-mutating privileged call**.

### Impact Explanation
An exchange creator can move the pool's `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` ratio arbitrarily and instantly between successive transactions with no cooldown, extracting value from other traders' concurrent `ExchangeTransactionContract` swaps (front/back-running a victim's trade by injecting and withdrawing around it), causing them to receive far less than expected despite passing their own `tokenExpected` slippage check computed against a stale quote. This is a fund-loss vector for pool participants, directly reachable by any account that created an exchange (an unprivileged, permissionless action anyone can perform via `ExchangeCreateContract`) and callable by ordinary signed transactions — matching the "asset issuer"/pool-operator role permitted for this analog.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exchange creation is permissionless — any account can create an exchange and become its "creator," then repeatedly call `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` with no restriction, at arbitrary times relative to other users' swap transactions within the same block or across close blocks. No special privilege beyond being the pool creator is required, and the actuators impose no rate limit, cooldown, or minimum liquidity lock period, making this readily exploitable by anyone willing to create their own exchange pool and lure counterparties into trading against it.

### Recommendation
Introduce a minimum time/block interval between successive `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` calls per exchange (a cooldown similar to what the reported `setStrategy()` finding recommends), and/or require a minimum liquidity lock-up period after injection before it can be withdrawn again, to prevent creators from freely oscillating pool ratios around other users' trades within the same or adjacent blocks.

### Proof of Concept
1. Account A creates an exchange pool for TRX/TokenX via `ExchangeCreateContract`, becoming `creatorAddress`.
2. Account A calls `ExchangeWithdrawContract` to pull most liquidity out, skewing the price.
3. Account B submits an `ExchangeTransactionContract` swap based on the skewed price, receiving a poor rate but still passing their own `tokenExpected` check (computed against the current, manipulated pool state).
4. Immediately after, Account A calls `ExchangeInjectContract` to restore the original liquidity/ratio, having captured the spread from Account B's trade.
5. Repeat steps 2–4 arbitrarily since `ExchangeInjectActuator`/`ExchangeWithdrawActuator` impose no frequency restriction beyond the creator-address check shown at [1](#0-0) .
