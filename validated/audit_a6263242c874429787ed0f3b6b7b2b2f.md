### Title
Stranded cashflows and NAV undercount via partial batch processing - ([File: tare-io__tare-contracts/contracts/PortfolioVault.sol])

### Summary
The `PortfolioVault.collectCashflows` function processes a caller-supplied array of loan IDs to withdraw principal and interest from `Loans.sol`. However, it does not verify that the provided list covers all loans currently in the vault's curated NAV list. Furthermore, the vault enforces a strict invariant that only loans currently in the NAV list can have their cashflows collected. If a loan is removed from the NAV list (e.g., due to impairment) before its accumulated cash is pulled, that cash becomes stranded in the `Loans` contract with no way for the vault to recover it, leading to a permanent loss of value for shareholders.

### Finding Description
The vault's NAV is derived from its stablecoin balance plus the discounted value of loans in its curated list `_navLoanIds` [1](#0-0) . Cashflows (interest and principal) are not automatically pushed to the vault; they must be pulled via `collectCashflows(uint64[] calldata loanIds, ...)` [2](#0-1) .

The implementation has two critical flaws:
1. **Partial Batching**: The function iterates only over the provided `loanIds` [3](#0-2) . If the manager omits a loan that has collected borrower payments, that cash remains in the `Loans` contract. Because it is no longer in the loan's "receivable" ledger (it moved to `ACC_CASH` on `Loans.sol`), the `NavCalculator` may no longer value it, and since it hasn't reached the vault's bank balance, it is missing from NAV.
2. **Exclusion Deadlock**: The function reverts if a `loanId` is not in the curated NAV list: `require(_navLoanIndex[loanIds[i]] != 0, LoanNotInNav())` [4](#0-3) . If a manager removes a loan from NAV using `removeLoansFromNav` (intended for impaired loans) [5](#0-4) , any cash already sitting in that loan's `ACC_CASH` bucket becomes un-collectable by the vault.

### Impact Explanation
This leads to material corruption of the vault's NAV and potential permanent lock of claimable assets. 
- **NAV Undercount**: Omitted cashflows lead to an undervalued share price, allowing new depositors to mint shares at an unfair discount or causing exiting redeemers to receive less than their fair share.
- **Asset Lock**: Once a loan is removed from the curated list, its cashflows cannot be pulled. Since the vault owns the NFT, only the vault (the investor) can call `investorWithdraw` on `Loans.sol`. If the vault's own logic blocks this call, the USDC is stuck in the `Loans` contract indefinitely [6](#0-5) .

### Likelihood Explanation
High. The protocol expects managers to manually manage the `loanIds` arrays for batch operations. Operational errors or a malicious `PORTFOLIO_MANAGER` can easily trigger these states. The exclusion deadlock is a structural flaw in the `LoanNotInNav` check.

### Recommendation
1. Remove the `LoanNotInNav` restriction in `collectCashflows`. As long as the vault owns the NFT, it should be able to collect cashflows. The concern about "excluded loans silently re-entering NAV" is better handled by ensuring `updateNav` reconciles all vault-owned cash, regardless of the loan's status in the curated list.
2. Implement a "collect all" variant or a paginated collection that iterates through the vault's actual NFT holdings to ensure no cash is left behind.

### Proof of Concept
1. Vault owns Loan A and Loan B. Both are in `_navLoanIds`.
2. Borrower pays 10,000 USDC for Loan A. This is recorded in `Loans.sol`.
3. Manager calls `collectCashflows([Loan B])`, omitting Loan A.
4. `totalTransfer` in `Loans.investorWithdraw` only includes Loan B's amounts [7](#0-6) .
5. The 10,000 USDC for Loan A remains in `Loans.sol`.
6. Manager calls `removeLoansFromNav([Loan A])`.
7. Future attempts to call `collectCashflows([Loan A])` revert with `LoanNotInNav` [4](#0-3) .
8. The 10,000 USDC is now permanently trapped in the protocol.
