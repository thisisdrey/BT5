### Title
Chunked liquidations let an attacker walk the LTV-dependent penalty curve upward, extracting more collateral than a single liquidation would allow - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`)

### Summary
`liquidate()` recomputes the borrower's liquidation percentage and bonus (`liq-pct-scaled`, `liq-penalty`) fresh, from the position's *current* LTV, on every call via `calc-liquidation-params`. Because the liquidation bonus removes proportionally more USD value of collateral than of debt, a single partial liquidation on a sufficiently unhealthy position can *raise* the post-liquidation LTV instead of lowering it. Since the percentage/penalty curve is monotonically increasing in LTV, repeating `liquidate()` in small chunks lets a liquidator ratchet the position's LTV upward between calls, unlocking a higher penalty and a higher liquidatable percentage on each subsequent chunk than the formula would have granted for a single liquidation sized to the position's original LTV. This is the same bug class as the WooFi report: a state-dependent pricing/percentage formula that is not invariant to how an operation is partitioned, so splitting into many small operations extracts more value than doing it once.

### Finding Description
`calc-liquidation-params` derives the liquidatable percentage and bonus purely from the LTV read at the start of the call: [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

`liq-pct-scaled` and `liq-penalty` are both strictly increasing functions of `current-ltv` (via `calc-liq-factor`, `calc-liq-factor-exp`, `calc-liq-factor-bound`). In `liquidate()`, `current-ltv` is recomputed from the borrower's live `total-debt-usd`/`total-collateral-usd` at the top of every call: [3](#0-2) 

The collateral seized always includes the liquidator bonus on top of debt repaid: [4](#0-3) 

Deriving the effect of one liquidation on LTV: with debt repaid `D` (USD) and collateral seized `C = D*(1+penalty)` (USD), the post-liquidation LTV is lower than the pre-liquidation LTV `L` only when `L < 1/(1+penalty)`. For a max penalty of 10% (`LIQ-PENALTY-MAX` = 1000 bps, a realistic egroup config per `docs/egroups.md`), that threshold is `L < 1/1.10 ≈ 90.9%`. Any position whose LTV is between the partial-liquidation threshold (e.g. 85%) and that break-even point will have its LTV *increase* after a liquidation call executes — worsening its health rather than improving it.

Because the next `liquidate()` call re-reads this now-higher LTV, `calc-liquidation-params` returns a strictly higher `liq-pct-scaled` (more of the total debt is liquidatable this round) and a strictly higher `liq-penalty` than the first call received. There is no cooldown between successive `liquidate()` calls (the only same-block guard, `ERR-LIQUIDATION-BORROW-SAME-BLOCK`, only blocks liquidating right after a borrow, not sequential liquidations): [5](#0-4) 

An attacker liquidator can therefore call `liquidate()` repeatedly with small `debt-amount` chunks (in a loop from their own contract, or across several blocks/transactions) on a borrower that just crossed into partial-liquidation territory, and each chunk pushes the LTV further up the curve, unlocking progressively larger percentage caps and progressively larger bonuses. This lets the attacker end up liquidating a much larger fraction of the position, at a materially higher blended penalty, than a single liquidation sized against the position's original LTV would ever have permitted — the exact analog of the WooFi "partial swaps are more profitable than one big swap" bug: a formula whose per-operation output depends on a state variable that the operation itself moves in the attacker's favor, and which is not invariant to how the total amount is split across calls.

### Impact Explanation
The excess collateral extracted above what the single-call, LTV-based percentage/penalty curve would have granted is collateral taken from the borrower beyond the protocol's intended liquidation design — a direct transfer of the borrower's funds at rest to the liquidator. This is theft of user funds and also undermines the `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL`/`LTV-LIQ-FULL`/`LIQ-CURVE-EXP` risk parameters that egroups are supposed to enforce, since the effective per-borrower liquidation percentage and bonus become a function of how many calls the liquidator chooses to make rather than of the position's actual health.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any unprivileged principal can call `liquidate()` as many times as they like on any liquidatable position with no cooldown between calls, and only needs the position's LTV to sit in the range between `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL` and the `1/(1+penalty)` break-even point (a realistic band for typical egroup configurations, e.g. 85%–90.9%). The attack requires no special privileges, only enough capital to repay the chunked debt amounts, which can even be sourced from a flashloan used purely as capital (per the scope rules, flashloan-as-capital for a different attack is in scope).

### Recommendation
Compute the liquidation percentage/penalty once from the position's LTV as it exists before any liquidation activity in the current "liquidation session" (e.g., snapshot LTV per block or require the whole eligible amount be settled atomically), or redesign the curve so that penalty/percentage is derived from a quantity that cannot be pushed upward by the liquidation itself (e.g., base it on the pre-liquidation shortfall rather than a live-recomputed LTV that the liquidation's own bonus mechanically worsens).

### Proof of Concept
1. Configure/observe an egroup with `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL = 8500`, `LTV-LIQ-FULL = 9500`, `LIQ-PENALTY-MIN = 100`, `LIQ-PENALTY-MAX = 1000` (values consistent with `docs/egroups.md` examples).
2. Borrower's position sits at LTV = 87% (within partial-liquidation zone, below the ~90.9% break-even for a 10% max penalty).
3. Liquidator calls `liquidate()` with a small `debt-amount` (well under `max-debt-usd` for LTV=87%), receiving `coll-final` collateral at the corresponding `liq-penalty`.
4. Because `L=87% > 1/(1+penalty_at_87%)`, the resulting LTV after step 3 is higher than 87%.
5. Liquidator calls `liquidate()` again; `calc-liquidation-params` now returns a higher `liq-pct-scaled` and `liq-penalty` than in step 3, letting the liquidator repay a larger slice of remaining debt at a richer bonus.
6. Repeating steps 3–5 in a loop (e.g., from an attacker-controlled contract in one transaction) accumulates total collateral extracted and effective average penalty that exceed what a single `liquidate()` call sized to the original 87% LTV would have paid out, following the exact pattern demonstrated by the WooFi `sell1000Parts`-style test in the source report.

```text
;; Calculate liquidation factor: ((ltv-curr - ltv-liq-partial) * BPS) / (ltv-liq-full - ltv-liq-partial)
;; Capped at BPS (100%) to prevent over-liquidation
(define-private (calc-liq-factor (ltv-curr uint) (ltv-liq-partial uint) (ltv-liq-full uint))
  (min BPS (div-bps-down (- ltv-curr ltv-liq-partial) (- ltv-liq-full ltv-liq-partial))))

;; Apply curve exponent for graduated liquidation
;; liq-factor = liq-factor^alpha
(define-private (calc-liq-factor-exp (factor uint) (exp uint))
  (if (is-eq exp BPS) 
    factor
    (if (> exp BPS) 
        (/ (pow factor (/ exp BPS)) (pow BPS (- (/ exp BPS) u1)))
        (sqrti (* factor BPS))))) ;; assume factor^0.5

;; Scale penalty between min and max using liquidation factor
;; liq-penalty = liq-penalty-min + (liq-factor * (liq-penalty-max - liq-penalty-min) / BPS)
;; Capped at bound-max to handle cases where liq-factor > BPS
(define-private (calc-liq-factor-bound (liq-factor uint) (bound-min uint) (bound-max uint))
  (min bound-max (+ bound-min (mul-bps-down liq-factor (- bound-max bound-min)))))
```

```text
;; Graduated liquidation parameter calculation
;; Combines the 4-step liquidation factor calculation into a single helper
;; Returns: { liq-pct-scaled: uint, liq-penalty: uint, max-debt-usd: uint }
(define-private (calc-liquidation-params
  (current-ltv uint)
  (ltv-liq-partial uint)
  (ltv-liq-full uint)
  (liq-penalty-min uint)
  (liq-penalty-max uint)
  (curve-exponent uint)
  (total-debt-usd uint))
  
  (let ((liq-pct-linear (calc-liq-factor current-ltv ltv-liq-partial ltv-liq-full))
        (liq-pct-scaled (calc-liq-factor-exp liq-pct-linear curve-exponent))
        (liq-penalty (calc-liq-factor-bound liq-pct-scaled liq-penalty-min liq-penalty-max))
        (max-debt-usd (calc-liq-debt-repay total-debt-usd liq-pct-scaled)))
    {
      liq-pct-scaled: liq-pct-scaled,
      liq-penalty: liq-penalty,
      max-debt-usd: max-debt-usd
    }))
```
