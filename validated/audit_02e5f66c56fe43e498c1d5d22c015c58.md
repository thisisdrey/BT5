### Title
Missing slippage protection in `ExchangeInject`/`ExchangeWithdraw` (TRC10 Bancor-style exchange) allows front-running loss for liquidity providers - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java, actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java)

### Summary
The java-tron TRC10 `Exchange` (bancor-like AMM) contracts `ExchangeInjectContract` (add liquidity) and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` (remove liquidity) compute the counterpart token amount from the pool's reserve ratio at the moment of execution, with no user-supplied minimum/maximum bound and no deadline. This is directly analogous to the reported `ProvideLiquidity`/`WithdrawLiquidity` slippage issue.

### Finding Description
In `ExchangeInjectActuator.execute`, the `anotherTokenQuant` a liquidity injector must supply/receive is derived purely from `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` read from the `ExchangeCapsule` at execution time: `anotherTokenQuant = floorDiv(multiplyExact(secondTokenBalance, tokenQuant), firstTokenBalance)` (or the symmetric case) [4](#0-3) . The `ExchangeInjectContract` protobuf message carries only `owner_address`, `exchange_id`, `token_id`, and `quant` — no minimum-accept or maximum-pay parameter [5](#0-4) .

Similarly, in `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute`, the refund amount of the other asset is computed from the live pool ratio at execution: `anotherTokenQuant = bigSecondTokenBalance.multiply(bigTokenQuant).divide(bigFirstTokenBalance).longValueExact()` [6](#0-5) , and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` has the identical minimal field set with no slippage bound [7](#0-6) .

By contrast, the trading operation `ExchangeTransactionContract` explicitly includes an `expected` field intended as slippage protection for swaps [1](#0-0) , showing the protocol designers recognized the need for this protection for trades but omitted it for liquidity provision/removal.

Any account can permissionlessly create an exchange pool and become its "creator" via `ExchangeCreateActuator` (no privileged role required) [3](#0-2) , and only the creator address is authorized to call inject/withdraw (`account[...] is not creator` check) [8](#0-7) . This creator is an ordinary, unprivileged transaction broadcaster/asset issuer reachable by the threat model in scope — they sign an inject/withdraw transaction expecting a certain ratio, but between signing and block inclusion, any account can submit an `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade against the same pool (trading is fully permissionless) that shifts `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance`, changing the effective ratio the inject/withdraw executes against.

The `ExchangeWithdrawActuator` does include a "Not precise enough" check (`allowHarden`/legacy path), but this only bounds internal rounding/consistency of the ratio calculation between validate() and execute() time drift tolerance (~0.01%) [9](#0-8)  — it is not a user-specified minimum-received guard against market movement, so a large intervening trade that shifts the ratio well beyond 0.01% simply changes both the validate-time and execute-time computed ratio consistently and passes the check while still returning a materially different asset mix than the signer expected.

### Impact Explanation
A liquidity provider/withdrawer on the TRC10 exchange can be front-run: an attacker observes a pending `ExchangeInjectContract` or `ExchangeWithdrawContract` in the mempool, submits a large `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade to shift the pool ratio, and the victim's inject/withdraw then executes against the manipulated ratio. For injection, the victim deposits assets and receives a far worse-than-expected pool position (their reduceAssetAmountV2 debits are fixed by the tx but the resulting pool share is skewed). For withdrawal, the victim receives a worse composition/lower value of the two assets than expected at signing time, permanently locking in a loss once the transaction is mined — this is a concrete unauthorized-value-transfer/loss-of-funds scenario for an ordinary unprivileged account.

### Likelihood Explanation
Both `ExchangeTransactionContract` and `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` are broadcastable by any account with no special permission, and Tron's blockspace/mempool ordering does not prevent an attacker from ordering their trade transaction immediately before the victim's inject/withdraw within the same block or block sequence. The precondition is simply that the target exchange has non-trivial liquidity depth/imbalance potential, which is common for actively used TRC10 exchange pairs.

### Recommendation
Add an explicit minimum-received (for withdraw) / maximum-required-other-token (for inject) bound field to `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract`, validated in `ExchangeInjectActuator`/`ExchangeWithdrawActuator` against the actual computed `anotherTokenQuant` at execution time, failing the transaction if the bound is violated. Optionally add an expiration/deadline field, consistent with the pattern already used for `ExchangeTransactionContract`'s `expected` field.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker/observer monitors the mempool for a pending `ExchangeWithdrawContract` from victim `V` on exchange `E` with reserves `(A, B)`, expecting a payout ratio close to `A:B`.
2. Attacker submits an `ExchangeTransactionContract` trading a large amount of token A into E, shifting reserves to `(A', B')` such that `A'/B'` differs substantially from `A/B`; this executes before V's withdrawal.
3. V's `ExchangeWithdrawContract` executes against `(A', B')`: `anotherTokenQuant` in `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute` (lines 74-89) is computed from the manipulated reserves, so V receives an unfavorable, unexpected mix of the two assets with no way to reject the trade since no minimum-amount field exists to enforce.
4. The attacker may optionally reverse their trade in a subsequent transaction, profiting from V's slippage while V has no recourse since the transaction already succeeded per validation logic that only checks internal ratio precision, not adherence to any user-specified bound.
