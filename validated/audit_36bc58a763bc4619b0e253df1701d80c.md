### Title
Public `vested_transfer` front-running of deterministic `pallet-revive` contract addresses permanently locks funds sent to the contract - (File: `substrate/frame/revive/src/exec.rs`)

### Summary
The Nibiru report's broken invariant is: an unprivileged, public message (`vesting.create-permanent-locked-account`) can be sent to a *deterministically predictable* future contract address before it is deployed, permanently attaching account-level state that the contract-deployment code path never checks for or clears, resulting in permanently stuck funds. `pallet-revive` computes contract addresses deterministically (`create1`/`create2`) exactly like the Cosmos/EVM case, and it inherits a structurally identical gap: contract lifecycle code (`instantiate`/`terminate`) operates purely on its own `AccountInfoOf`/`ContractInfo` bookkeeping and never inspects or purges pre-existing `pallet-balances` locks placed on the same `AccountId` by `pallet-vesting`, a fully public, unprivileged pallet.

### Finding Description
`pallet-revive` derives contract addresses deterministically from the deployer and nonce/salt (`create1`/`create2` in `substrate/frame/revive/src/address.rs:263-285`), and `ContractInfo::new` only guards against re-using an address that is *already* a contract (`<AccountInfo<T>>::is_contract(address)` in `substrate/frame/revive/src/storage.rs:196-203`). It does not, and cannot, know about locks that `pallet-vesting` may have already placed on the underlying `AccountId` via `T::Currency::set_lock(VESTING_ID, who, locked, reasons)` (`substrate/frame/vesting/src/lib.rs:281`, and the public dispatchable `vested_transfer` at `substrate/frame/vesting/src/lib.rs:372-380`).

Because `pallet-vesting` locks operate on the raw `AccountId`/free balance rather than on any "account type" pallet-revive tracks, an attacker can:
1. Predict the address of a contract that a victim factory/deployer will create (`create1`/`create2` are fully deterministic given deployer+nonce or deployer+salt+initcode).
2. Call the public, unprivileged `vested_transfer` extrinsic (`substrate/frame/vesting/src/lib.rs:372`) with the predicted address as `target` and an arbitrarily long vesting duration, locking a (even tiny) balance under `VESTING_ID`.
3. When the victim later instantiates the contract at that same address, `ContractInfo::new` only checks `is_contract` — it does not check for or reject the pre-existing lock — so instantiation proceeds normally, and `insert_contract` (`substrate/frame/revive/src/storage.rs:180-188`) just flips `AccountType` to `Contract`, preserving the account's `dust` field and, more importantly, doing nothing to the untouched pallet-balances lock still bound to that `AccountId`.
4. Any value later sent to that address, or held by the contract, is now subject to the vesting lock's `ensure_can_withdraw` at the `Currency` level. When the contract is later `terminate`d (or self-destructs), `do_terminate` in `substrate/frame/revive/src/exec.rs:1809-1849` performs the final balance drain via `Self::transfer(..., Preservation::Expendable, ...)`. The code's own comment documents the consequence: "To fail termination if any locks or holds prevent to completely empty the account" (`substrate/frame/revive/src/exec.rs:1820-1822`) — i.e., termination (and thus recovery of the contract's balance) simply cannot succeed while the lock is active, and the whole `with_transaction` is rolled back (`substrate/frame/revive/src/exec.rs:1854-1865`).

Since `pallet-vesting`'s lock is reduced only over time according to a schedule the *attacker* chooses (`VestingInfo::new(locked, per_block, begin)`), the attacker can pick a schedule whose `per_block` unlock rate is negligible and whose duration effectively spans the lifetime of the chain, making the lock functionally permanent. Unlike a normal user, the deployed contract has no ability to call `vest`/`vest_other` early or to bypass the lock — its funds (and, for `Preservation::Expendable` transfers, potentially its entire remaining balance) are pinned behind a schedule the attacker fully controls, with no cancellation mechanism from the contract side.

### Impact Explanation
This is a public-entrypoint, unprivileged-attacker path (`pallet_vesting::vested_transfer`, no special origin required) that causes a **permanent lock of contract-held value**, matching the "permanent user-fund ... lock" impact category. It can be used to grief any deterministically-addressed contract deployment (factories, CREATE2-based deployments, predictable nonce-based deployments), preventing the contract's balance from ever being fully drained/terminated as designed, and — because `do_terminate` explicitly rolls back the whole termination when it cannot fully empty the account — it can also block the pallet's own scheduled-deletion/refund flow for that contract, wasting the contract's storage deposit machinery indefinitely.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is significant for any application that relies on deterministic contract addressing (CREATE1/CREATE2, factory patterns) which the codebase itself documents and tests extensively (`create2_works`, `existential_deposit_shall_not_be_charged_twice` in `substrate/frame/revive/src/tests/`). The attack requires no privileged role, no validator/collator compromise, and no governance action — only a single public `vested_transfer` extrinsic sent before the victim's deployment transaction is included, which is realistically achievable given typical mempool visibility of pending deployment transactions with known deployer/nonce.

### Recommendation
- In `ContractInfo::new` (`substrate/frame/revive/src/storage.rs`), reject instantiation (or require prior lock cleanup) when the destination `AccountId` has any active `pallet-balances` locks/holds that are not owned by `pallet-revive` itself, surfacing a clear error instead of silently allowing a contract to be created over a locked account.
- Alternatively/additionally, when `do_terminate`'s final drain fails due to an external lock, avoid indefinitely rolling back and instead expose a mechanism (e.g., a privileged/queued cleanup path) rather than leaving the deposit/consumer bookkeeping (and any legitimately-sent contract funds) stuck behind a lock the contract can never clear.
- Consider disallowing `pallet-vesting` targets that correspond to not-yet-existing accounts predicted to become contract addresses, or require `vested_transfer`/`force_vested_transfer` to check `!AccountInfo::<T>::is_contract` and add a symmetric check on the `pallet-revive` side against outstanding vesting locks.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deployer intends to deploy a contract via CREATE1 at nonce `n` from address `D` (or via CREATE2 with known `salt`/initcode); compute `addr = address::create1(&D, n)` (or `create2(...)`), which is fully public/deterministic (`substrate/frame/revive/src/address.rs:264-285`).
2. Attacker calls `pallet_vesting::vested_transfer(target = to_account_id(addr), schedule = VestingInfo::new(locked_amount, tiny_per_block, far_future_or_current_begin))` (`substrate/frame/vesting/src/lib.rs:372-380`), locking a small `locked_amount` on that `AccountId` for a very long effective duration.
3. Deployer sends the instantiate transaction; `ContractInfo::new` only checks `is_contract(address)` (false) and `NativeDepositOf` (empty), so instantiation succeeds and `insert_contract` marks the address as `AccountType::Contract` (`substrate/frame/revive/src/storage.rs:196-203`, `180-188`), leaving the pre-existing `pallet-balances` lock untouched.
4. Later, the contract (or its owner) attempts `terminate`/`SELFDESTRUCT`; `do_terminate`'s drain transfer with `Preservation::Expendable` fails because the lock prevents emptying the account, and the whole termination transaction rolls back (`substrate/frame/revive/src/exec.rs:1809-1865`), permanently trapping the contract's balance and blocking cleanup as long as the attacker-chosen vesting schedule remains unvested.
