## Title
Market/Exchange order & AMM pricing in java-tron ignores TRC10 asset `precision`, causing decimal-mismatched trades - (`actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/MarketSellAssetActuator.java`, `chainbase/src/main/java/org/tron/core/capsule/utils/MarketUtils.java`)

### Summary
This is the direct analog of the Mantra `compute_d` bug: Curve-style pools assume all deposited amounts are decimal-normalized before being combined in an invariant/price formula, and Mantra failed to normalize for tokens with different decimals. java-tron's own on-chain trading primitives — the TRC10 `Exchange` (constant-product AMM) actuators and the `Market*` order-book actuators — have the exact same flaw: TRC10 assets can be issued with a configurable `precision` (0–6 decimals) via `AssetIssueContract.precision`, but the trading code treats `sellTokenQuantity`/`buyTokenQuantity`/`firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` as raw integer amounts with no decimal normalization anywhere in the pricing, matching, or invariant math.

### Finding Description
`AssetIssueActuator.validate()` allows a TRC10 issuer to set `precision` between 0 and 6 (`ActuatorConstant.PRECISION_DECIMAL`), stored per-asset in `AssetIssueCapsule`: [1](#0-0)  and [2](#0-1) .

`MarketSellAssetActuator.validate()` reads `sellTokenQuantity`/`buyTokenQuantity` straight from the contract and only checks they are positive and below a global `quantityLimit` — it never fetches or applies each token's `precision`: [3](#0-2) , [4](#0-3) .

These raw quantities are used to build a `MarketPrice` and to key/compare orders directly, with no decimal adjustment: [5](#0-4) . The GCD-reduced price key construction and price comparison operate purely on the raw integers: [6](#0-5) , [7](#0-6) . Order matching (`matchSingleOrder`) similarly multiplies/divides raw sell/buy quantities of the maker/taker orders with no precision scaling: [8](#0-7) .

The same class of bug exists in the legacy `Exchange` (bancor-style AMM) actuators: `ExchangeInjectActuator` computes the paired token amount to inject as `secondTokenBalance * tokenQuant / firstTokenBalance` with no decimal normalization between `firstTokenID` and `secondTokenID`, which can have different `precision` values: [9](#0-8) . `ExchangeCapsule.transaction`/`ExchangeProcessor.exchange` likewise perform the bancor-relay math directly on raw `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance`/`sellTokenQuant` values without any precision scaling: [10](#0-9) , [11](#0-10) .

In effect, exactly as in the Mantra report where a stableswap `D` invariant summed raw `1e18`-scaled and raw `1e6`-scaled amounts as if they were equal-weight units, java-tron's `Exchange` invariant and `Market` order price/match logic sum/ratio raw integer amounts of TRC10 tokens that can be issued at different precisions (0–6), silently treating "1 unit" of a 0-decimal token as equal in weight to "1 unit" of a 6-decimal token.

### Impact Explanation
Any account can issue two TRC10 assets with different `precision` values (e.g., 0 and 6) via `AssetIssueContract`, then create an `Exchange` pool or place `MarketSellAsset` orders pairing them. Because pricing/matching never accounts for the 10^precision scale difference, a counterparty (maker or LP) unaware of, or unable to detect, the precision mismatch will receive/give up value at an effectively 10^precision-times wrong exchange rate. This allows an attacker to drain the other side's token balance in the `Exchange` pool, or to have `MarketSellAsset` orders match at grossly mispriced ratios, resulting in theft of funds — an unbacked-value/misallocation impact directly analogous to the confirmed Mantra High-severity finding.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any unprivileged account: issuing a TRC10 asset with arbitrary `precision` (0–6) is permissionless via `AssetIssueContract`, and creating/injecting an `Exchange` or placing `MarketSellAssetContract` orders across two differently-precisioned assets requires no special privilege beyond normal transaction fees. No malicious SR/witness/committee/peer role is needed — this is purely a broadcastable-transaction-level flaw in actuator validation/execution.

### Recommendation
Normalize all token quantities/balances to a common decimal base (e.g., scale every amount by `10^(maxPrecision - assetPrecision)`) before: (1) computing the AMM invariant/relay math in `ExchangeProcessor`/`SafeExchangeProcessor`/`ExchangeCapsule.transaction`, (2) computing `anotherTokenQuant` in `ExchangeInjectActuator`/`ExchangeWithdrawActuator`, and (3) building `MarketPrice`, price keys, and doing quantity comparisons/matching in `MarketSellAssetActuator`/`MarketUtils`. Alternatively, reject cross-precision trading pairs, or require `MarketSellAssetContract`/`Exchange` creation to explicitly bake in each asset's `precision` into the price ratio validation.

### Proof of Concept
1. Issue TRC10 asset `A` with `precision = 0` and TRC10 asset `B` with `precision = 6` via `AssetIssueContract` (both permitted per `AssetIssueActuator.validate()` precision check at lines 176-181).
2. Create an `Exchange` with initial balances intended to represent "equal value," e.g. `firstTokenBalance(A) = 1_000_000`, `secondTokenBalance(B) = 1_000_000_000000` (accounting for B's 6 decimals) — the `ExchangeCapsule.transaction`/`ExchangeProcessor.exchange` math (lines 124-158, 41-45) treats these purely as raw longs, matching the Mantra `compute_d` flaw of summing non-normalized amounts.
3. Call `ExchangeTransactionActuator`/`ExchangeInjectActuator` selling `A`; because the bancor relay formula and the `secondTokenBalance * tokenQuant / firstTokenBalance` ratio (lines 71-83) don't rescale for `B`'s extra 6 decimals, the attacker receives `B` at a rate off by `10^6`, draining the pool's `B` balance relative to fair value.
4. Equivalently, place a `MarketSellAssetContract` selling `A` for `B` and a matching maker order selling `B` for `A`; `MarketUtils.priceMatch`/`multiplyAndDivide` (lines 236-249 and matching logic in `MarketSellAssetActuator` lines 393-404) match orders at the raw quantity ratio, which is off by `10^6` relative to the tokens' true relative value, letting the taker extract the maker's `B` tokens far below fair price.
