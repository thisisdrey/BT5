### No vulnerability found for this question.

**Analysis:**

The claimed exploit path does not hold up under tracing. Key facts from `contracts/defuse/src/contract/tokens/nep245/resolver.rs`:

1. **Refunds are capped by live receiver balance, not by trust in the callback race.** At each iteration of the loop, `receiver_balance` is freshly re-read via `receiver.token_balances.amount_for(&token_id)` and `refund.0 = refund.0.min(receiver_balance)` is applied before any `sub`/`add` [1](#0-0) . Even if `token_ids` contained duplicates (single call) or if a reentrant transfer had already moved balance out of the receiver, the amount that can be refunded back can never exceed what the receiver *currently* holds at the time each iteration executes. This means the refund logic self-corrects against any prior draining of the receiver's balance — it cannot double-credit beyond the receiver's real balance.

2. **`previous_owner_id` is checked against the true sender for every entry** via `require!(sender_id == previous_owner_id, ...)` [2](#0-1) , so a refund can only ever route back to the account that was originally debited for that specific transfer call — it cannot be redirected to benefit an unrelated reentrant flow.

3. **There is no shared "promise chain/callback index" between two independent `mt_batch_transfer_call` invocations.** Each call to `internal_mt_batch_transfer_call` performs its own synchronous `internal_mt_batch_transfer` (debit sender / credit receiver) *before* scheduling `notify_and_resolve_transfer`, and the resulting `.then()` callback captures its own `previous_owner_ids`, `receiver_id`, `token_ids`, and `amounts` as call arguments [3](#0-2) . A reentrant call made from inside `mt_on_transfer` (e.g., the receiver contract calling `mt_batch_transfer_call` again) produces an entirely separate receipt with its own independent debit/credit and its own independent resolve callback with its own promise index — NEAR does not let two unrelated cross-contract calls "share" a callback's `promise_result(0)`. Each `mt_resolve_transfer` invocation only ever reads the promise result of the specific `mt_on_transfer` call it was chained to [4](#0-3) .

4. Since the reentrant transfer performed by the malicious receiver is itself a fully balanced `internal_mt_batch_transfer` (debit A / credit A2, netting to zero) [5](#0-4) , and the original resolver's refund is bounded by A's actual remaining balance, the two flows cannot combine to credit more than was ever debited from the original sender.

The binding "number of times a (sender, token, amount) tuple is refunded == exactly 1 per originating debit" holds because refunds are bounded by live balance state rather than by trusting duplicate or racing promise results, and because separate `mt_batch_transfer_call` invocations do not share callback/promise indices in NEAR's execution model. No reproducible double-credit path exists.

        sender_id: AccountId,
        receiver_id: AccountId,
        token_ids: Vec<defuse_nep245::TokenId>,
        amounts: Vec<U128>,
        notify: NotifyOnTransfer,
    ) -> PromiseOrValue<Vec<U128>> {
