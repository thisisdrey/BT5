### No vulnerability found for this question.

The claimed reentrancy path does not produce a broken binding. In `mt_on_transfer` [1](#0-0)  the deposit is credited to `receiver_id`'s internal balance **synchronously, before** the `notify_on_transfer` cross-contract promise is even scheduled [2](#0-1) . So by the time an attacker-controlled receiver's callback runs and re-enters `execute_intents` to withdraw, the balance being withdrawn is balance that has already been legitimately credited — this is not a reentrancy bypass, it's the intended sequencing (deposit-then-notify).

The refund logic in `resolve_deposit_internal` explicitly guards against any double-credit: `refund_amount = requested_refund.min(*deposited).min(balance_left)`, where `balance_left` is read fresh from the receiver's current token balance at resolution time [3](#0-2) . If the attacker already withdrew the deposited amount via a reentrant `execute_intents` call inside the notify callback, `balance_left` reflects that withdrawal, so the resolver cannot refund more than what remains — preventing the "refund plus goods" double-spend the question hypothesizes. The binding `refund_amount <= balance_left` holds before and after the reentrant call by construction, since `balance_left` is read at resolve time, not cached from deposit time.

Additionally, since `token` in this scenario is the attacker's own malicious NEP-245 contract, any funds deposited and later withdrawn or refunded are the attacker's own tokens — there is no path to move other users' custodied funds through this flow. [4](#0-3)
