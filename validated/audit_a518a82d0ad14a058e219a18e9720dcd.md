### Title
Borrower can act as their own liquidator with no economic check on self-liquidation, converting the liquidation penalty into a mechanism to force bad-debt socialization onto depositors - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`)

### Summary
`liquidate()` never checks whether the caller (`liquidator`/`contract-caller`) is the same principal as `borrower`. This mirrors the reported bounty bug class where `approveSubmission()`/`makeSubmission()` never checked whether the caller was the bounty's own funder — an unprivileged principal is allowed to act on both sides of a transaction that was designed to involve two independent, adversarial parties.

### Finding Description
`liquidate` takes `borrower` as an arbitrary parameter and sets `(liquidator contract-caller)` [1](#0-0) , with the only sender-identity assertion being `(asserts! (is-eq contract-caller tx-sender) ERR-AUTHORIZATION)` [2](#0-1) . Nowhere is `(not (is-eq liquidator borrower))` enforced. A borrower can therefore call `liquidate` on their own account once their position crosses `ltv-liq-partial` [3](#0-2) , repay their own debt via `vault-system-repay`, and receive their own seized collateral back (since `actual-receiver` defaults to `liquidator` when no receiver is specified) [4](#0-3) .

Critically, the liquidation math grants the liquidator collateral valued *above* the debt repaid, scaled by `liq-penalty` — this bonus is the intended incentive for third-party liquidators [5](#0-4) . When borrower and liquidator are the same principal, this "bonus" collateral simply returns to the position owner instead of compensating an external liquidator, while `remaining-debt-to-repay`/`other-debt-repayable` logic can leave residual scaled debt on the position that is then written down via `socialize-debt-asset` → `vault-socialize-debt` when `no-collateral-left` is true [6](#0-5)  and [7](#0-6) . `socialize-debt` in each vault directly reduces `total-assets`/`lindex`, socializing the loss across all vault depositors [8](#0-7) .

This gives a borrower a self-serve lever: intentionally push (or wait for) their own position to the liquidation threshold, self-liquidate to extract their own collateral at the liquidation bonus while leaving/forcing the untouchable remainder of debt to be socialized onto the vault's depositors, instead of a competitive/adversarial liquidator taking the discount and the borrower absorbing 100% of the deficiency.

### Impact Explanation
This lands on **Critical – theft of user funds / protocol insolvency**: value that should flow to third-party liquidators (as compensation for taking on the risk of closing an undercollateralized position) instead flows back to the delinquent borrower, while the debt shortfall the position leaves behind is paid for by all depositors in the affected vault through `socialize-debt`. This is a direct transfer of value from vault depositors to the misbehaving borrower with no capital cost to the attacker other than the same collateral they already control, exactly analogous to the bounty-report pattern where the funder recoups their own funding plus stakers' failed-submission stakes because no self-dealing check exists.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires the borrower's own position to actually cross `ltv-liq-partial`, which they fully control by borrowing to the edge of their LTV limit and letting price movement or accrued interest push them over (or by manipulating price feeds within oracle staleness/confidence tolerances, which is a related but separate issue). No third-party cooperation, flashloan, or privileged access is needed — only an ordinary market participant calling the public `liquidate` entry point on their own address.

### Recommendation
Add an explicit check in `liquidate` (and `liquidate-redeem`/`liquidate-multi`, which call into it) rejecting `(is-eq liquidator borrower)`, or alternatively require the `collateral-receiver` never resolve to `borrower` when `contract-caller` equals `borrower`. If self-liquidation is a deliberate design choice (as the original report shows the client can decide either way), document the decision explicitly and verify that the bad-debt-socialization path cannot be steered by the same self-liquidating principal to externalize losses onto depositors.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker deposits collateral and borrows against it up to just under `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL` for the chosen egroup.
2. Attacker waits for interest accrual (or a minor price move) to push `current-ltv` at/above `ltv-liq-partial`.
3. Attacker calls `liquidate(borrower: <self>, collateral-ft, debt-ft, debt-amount, min-collateral-expected, collateral-receiver: none, price-feeds)` from their own principal — `contract-caller == tx-sender == borrower`, which passes every check in the function [9](#0-8) .
4. `vault-system-repay` pulls the discounted debt amount from the attacker, and `collateral-remove` sends the bonus-adjusted collateral back to the attacker (default receiver = liquidator = attacker) [10](#0-9) .
5. If the resulting position has no collateral left and residual scaled debt, `socialize-debt-asset`/`vault-socialize-debt` writes down the vault's `lindex`/`total-assets`, socializing the shortfall to all depositors [11](#0-10) .

Note: I could not find any test (`local-testing/tests/security/liquidation.test.ts` or similar) that explicitly asserts self-liquidation is blocked or explicitly condones it as intended behavior; the index only surfaced generic liquidation flow tests, so I cannot confirm whether the team has already acknowledged/accepted this exact behavior the way they did for the bounty analog. This should be verified directly against the full liquidation test suite and any related audit findings before treating it as unconfirmed.

(define-public (socialize-debt (scaled-amount uint))
  (let ((scaled-principal (var-get principal-scaled))
        (borrowed (var-get total-borrowed))
        (idx (var-get index))
        (current-assets (var-get assets))
        (current-lindex (var-get lindex))
        (old-total-assets (total-assets))
        (debt-reduction (mul-div-down scaled-amount idx INDEX-PRECISION))
        (principal-reduction (if (> scaled-principal u0)
                                (mul-div-down scaled-amount borrowed scaled-principal)
                                u0))
        ;; Write down lindex proportionally to loss in total-assets
        (new-lindex (if (and (> old-total-assets u0) (> old-total-assets debt-reduction))
                       (mul-div-down current-lindex (- old-total-assets debt-reduction) old-total-assets)
                       u0)))

    (try! (check-caller-auth))
    (asserts! (> scaled-amount u0) ERR-AMOUNT-ZERO)

    (var-set lindex new-lindex)
    (var-set principal-scaled (if (> scaled-principal scaled-amount) (- scaled-principal scaled-amount) u0))
    (var-set total-borrowed (if (> borrowed principal-reduction) (- borrowed principal-reduction) u0))
    (var-set assets (if (> current-assets principal-reduction) (- current-assets principal-reduction) u0))
