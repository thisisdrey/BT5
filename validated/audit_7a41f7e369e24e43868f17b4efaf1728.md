### Title
Contract owner can front-run `TriggerSmartContract` calls via `UpdateSettingContract` to force victims to pay unbounded extra energy fees - ([File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java])

### Summary
`UpdateSettingContractActuator` lets a contract's deployer (`origin_address`) instantly set `consume_user_resource_percent` to any value in `[0,100]` with a single signed transaction and no delay, no supermajority approval, and no per-caller opt-in check, unlike genuine chain-parameter changes which go through the committee `ProposalCreate`/`ProposalApprove`/maintenance-cycle process in `ProposalService`.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` directly overwrites the deployed contract's `consumeUserResourcePercent` field in the same block/transaction it is submitted in: [1](#0-0) 

The only validation is a range check `[0,100]` and that the caller is the contract's `origin_address`: [2](#0-1) 

This percentage directly determines how much of the energy cost of a `TriggerSmartContract` call is charged to the calling user versus the contract owner, computed in `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio`/`getTotalEnergyLimitWithFloatRatio`: [3](#0-2) 

and in the final billing logic of `ReceiptCapsule.payEnergyBill`, where `percent` (derived from `consumeUserResourcePercent`) directly controls the split of energy usage charged to `caller` vs `origin`: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

Because there is no time-lock, no maximum-change cap per period, and no notification/commit-delay (contrast with real chain parameters, which are only changeable via a committee-approved `ProposalApproveActuator`/`ProposalService` that only takes effect at the next maintenance cycle: `framework/src/main/java/org/tron/core/consensus/ProposalService.java`), the contract owner can watch the mempool for a victim's pending `TriggerSmartContract` transaction with a high `fee_limit`, then broadcast an `UpdateSettingContract` transaction (or have it mined in the same/preceding block due to fee/priority ordering) that raises `consume_user_resource_percent` to 100 right before the victim's call executes. If the victim previously relied on the contract's advertised low `consume_user_resource_percent` (e.g., 0-10%) and set a correspondingly large `fee_limit`/energy budget expecting the owner to cover most of the cost, the victim instead gets billed for up to 100% of the energy usage out of their own frozen energy / TRX balance in `ReceiptCapsule.payEnergyBill`, unexpectedly draining TRX from their account.

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged but authorized actor (any contract's `origin_address`, i.e., anyone who deploys a contract) can unilaterally and instantly change the fee-sharing parameter that other unrelated accounts' transactions rely on, causing those accounts to lose TRX or energy resources they did not intend to spend. This is a direct case of theft of funds from an unsuspecting caller via front-running a fee-parameter change, matching the reported centralization-risk bug class.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account that has deployed a smart contract can trigger this at will, with no cooldown, no proposal/voting delay, and no notification mechanism, making the attack cheap and repeatable against any caller who trusts the contract's currently-configured `consume_user_resource_percent`.

### Recommendation
- Add a time-delay (e.g., effective only after N blocks or the next maintenance cycle) before a `UpdateSettingContract`/`UpdateEnergyLimitContract` change takes effect, similar to how committee proposals only apply after the maintenance cycle in `ProposalService`.
- Alternatively, cap the maximum allowed change per time window, or require the caller to specify (and validate against) an expected `consume_user_resource_percent`/`origin_energy_limit` at call time so the transaction reverts if the value has changed since it was signed (similar to slippage protection).

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker deploys `ContractA` with `consume_user_resource_percent = 0` (owner pays for all energy).
2. Victim observes the low percent, submits `TriggerSmartContract` against `ContractA` with `fee_limit` sized only for their small share of energy, expecting the deployer to cover most costs (per `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio`).
3. Attacker (contract owner), seeing the victim's pending transaction in the mempool, submits `UpdateSettingContract` setting `consume_user_resource_percent = 100`, validated only by the `[0,100]` range check in `UpdateSettingContractActuator.validate()`.
4. If the attacker's transaction lands in the same or an earlier block, `TransactionTrace.pay()` computes `percent = 100 - 100 = 0` for the origin share, forcing the caller to pay 100% of energy usage in `ReceiptCapsule.payEnergyBill`, exceeding the victim's expected/budgeted cost and draining TRX/energy the victim did not intend to spend.
