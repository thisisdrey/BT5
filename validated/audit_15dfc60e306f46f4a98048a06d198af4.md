### Title
Unequal loss distribution in vault share redemption due to delayed bad-debt socialization - (File: `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar`, `v0-vault-stx.clar`, `v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`)

### Summary
Zest's tokenized vaults price `redeem()` shares using `total-assets`/`convert-to-assets-preview`, which include the full face value of outstanding scaled debt (`principal-scaled * index`) even after a borrower position has become deeply underwater and the debt is effectively uncollectible. The write-down of that bad debt only happens inside `liquidate()`'s `socialize-debt-asset` call, which is a discrete, permissionless-but-not-automatic transaction. Between the moment a position becomes insolvent (e.g., a collateral price crash) and the moment someone actually calls `liquidate()`/`liquidate-multi()` to trigger `vault-socialize-debt`, share price is stale/inflated. Any depositor who redeems in that window exits at the pre-markdown price, while depositors who redeem after `socialize-debt` runs absorb the entire loss. This mirrors the LMPVault M-7 pattern: losses are not distributed pro-rata across all shareholders — timing of withdrawal relative to loss realization determines who bears it.

### Finding Description
- `redeem()` in each vault (e.g. `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar` lines 795-833) computes the payout via `convert-to-assets-preview(amount)`, which is derived from `total-assets` — a function of `assets` var plus outstanding `principal-scaled` valued at the current (not-yet-written-down) `index`/`lindex`. [1](#0-0) 
- The only mechanism that marks down this debt for insolvent positions is `socialize-debt` on the vault, which reduces `lindex` (and thus share value) proportionally to the loss: [2](#0-1) 
- `socialize-debt` is only invoked from `market.clar`'s `liquidate()` flow, specifically when a liquidation empties a borrower's collateral and leaves residual unpayable debt (`no-collateral-left` → `fold socialize-debt-asset ...`): [3](#0-2) 
- This means the vault's `lindex`/share price is only corrected for a bad debt at the moment `liquidate()` executes — not at the moment the position actually became insolvent (e.g., right after an oracle price update showing collateral < debt). Any depositor who observes the crashed price/impending insolvency and calls `redeem()` before anyone bothers to (or is able to) call `liquidate()` withdraws at the old, uncorrected exchange rate, extracting more than their fair share of the vault's real (post-loss) assets. Depositors who redeem after socialization bear a proportionally larger loss than they should, exactly as in the referenced Sherlock report where faster LMPVault withdrawers escaped depreciation at the expense of slower ones.
- Note: `market.clar` includes a `liquidate-multi` explicitly to "prevent front-running attacks that prevent bad debt socialization" [4](#0-3) , showing the team is aware liquidation/socialization timing is attacker-relevant, but this only protects against liquidators being front-run out of collateral seizure — it does nothing to prevent ordinary depositors from redeeming vault shares before the write-down is realized.

### Impact Explanation
This is a temporary/permanent freezing (and effective transfer) of unclaimed value among vault depositors: informed or fast depositors can withdraw at an inflated share price ahead of an unavoidable loss event, and the deficit is then borne disproportionately — potentially entirely — by depositors who redeem afterward, some of whom may be unable to recover their full principal. This falls under the in-scope "vault share math and interest accrual" category and lands on the **High** impact class (temporary freezing of funds / unclaimed yield for the remaining depositors, whose recoverable share of assets is reduced below their fair pro-rata entitlement).

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate-to-high in practice: oracle price updates are public (Pyth/DIA feeds resolved in `market.clar`), so any depositor or bot can detect that a borrower's position has become undercollateralized before anyone executes `liquidate()`. There is no requirement for the depositor to be privileged — any holder of vault shares (zUSDC, zSTX, etc.) can call `redeem()` directly on the vault contract. The window of exposure exists for every liquidatable-but-not-yet-liquidated position with residual bad debt, which is a normal, expected occurrence in a lending protocol during sharp price moves.

### Recommendation
Do not rely solely on discrete `socialize-debt` calls triggered by `liquidate()` to correct vault share pricing. Consider either (a) incorporating a real-time, health-check-aware valuation of at-risk/underwater debt into `total-assets`/`convert-to-assets-preview` so redemptions can't be executed at a price that ignores known insolvent positions, or (b) restricting/delaying redemptions when the protocol is aware of outstanding unliquidated bad debt for that asset, or (c) socializing bad debt as soon as a position is detected to be underwater (independent of/before an actual liquidation executes), so the loss is baked into share price before any withdrawal can front-run it.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice borrows USDC against sBTC collateral near the max LTV.
2. BTC price crashes so that Alice's collateral value < her debt (as constructed in the existing test `local-testing/tests/security/liquidation.test.ts:153-189`, "should socialize bad debt when collateral is exhausted"), but no one has called `liquidate()` yet.
3. At this moment, the USDC vault's `total-assets`/`lindex` still reflect Alice's full scaled debt as fully collectible — the vault has not yet marked it down.
4. Any USDC vault-share holder (zUSDC holder) calls `redeem()` and receives underlying USDC valued at the pre-markdown exchange rate, extracting assets that do not reflect the real (impaired) backing.
5. Later, `liquidate()` is called on Alice's position; `no-collateral-left` is true, `socialize-debt-asset`/`vault-socialize-debt` is invoked, writing down `lindex` for all *remaining* zUSDC holders.
6. Depositors who redeemed in step 4 paid none of the loss; the entire loss is absorbed by the depositors still holding zUSDC after step 5 — an unequal, timing-dependent loss distribution identical in character to the LMPVault M-7 report.
