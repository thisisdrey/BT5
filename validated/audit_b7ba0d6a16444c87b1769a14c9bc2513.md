### Title
Divergent debt-reduction math between market-level (`repay`) and vault-level (`system-repay`) scaled-debt accounting can desynchronize aggregate vault debt from the sum of per-account debts - ([File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar], [File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-stx.clar])

### Summary
Zest v2 tracks debt in two places that must stay mathematically consistent: an aggregate `principal-scaled` at the vault level (used for `total-debt`, utilization, interest accrual, and `total-assets`) and a per-account `scaled` debt map at `market-vault` (used for individual position accounting and health checks). Reducing debt on `repay`/`liquidate` computes the scaled amount to remove from each ledger with two **different formulas**, which is exactly the bug class described in the report: `SaiTub.ice` (aggregate) and `SaiTub.tab` (per-CDP) being computed with different rounding logic, so the two entangled values can drift apart.

### Finding Description
At the market layer, `repay` in [1](#0-0)  computes the scaled debt to remove from the *individual* account using an index-ratio formula:
```
max-repay-tokens   = mul-div-up(account-scaled-debt, borrow-index, INDEX-PRECISION)
safe-amount        = min(amount, max-repay-tokens)
scaled-debt-repayment = mul-div-down(safe-amount, INDEX-PRECISION, borrow-index)
repaid-scaled-debt = min(account-scaled-debt, scaled-debt-repayment)
```
This value is subtracted from the user's balance via `debt-remove-scaled` at `market-vault` [2](#0-1) .

At the vault layer, `system-repay` reduces the *aggregate* `principal-scaled` using an entirely different computation - a ratio of the capped token amount to total outstanding debt, not the index-based `mul-div-down`/`mul-div-up` conversion used at the market layer:
```
debt              = total-debt                                   ;; calc-cumulative-debt(principal-scaled, index)
capped-amount     = min(amount, debt)
principal-reduction = calc-principal-ratio-reduction(capped-amount, scaled-principal, debt)
capped-reduction  = min(principal-reduction, scaled-principal)
updated-scaled-principal = scaled-principal - capped-reduction
``` [3](#0-2) 

`total-debt` itself is derived from `principal-scaled` via `calc-cumulative-debt` [4](#0-3) , and both `total-assets` and `total-assets-preview` (which drive LP share pricing via `convert-to-shares`/`convert-to-assets`) are computed from that same aggregate debt figure [5](#0-4) .

Because `market.repay`'s per-account scaled-debt reduction and `vault.system-repay`'s aggregate scaled-principal reduction are computed with two structurally different formulas (index-ratio at the market vs. a proportional "principal-ratio" reduction at the vault), rounding on each side does not need to agree. Over repeated `repay`/`borrow`/`liquidate` cycles across many users, the vault's `principal-scaled` (and hence `total-debt`) can drift from the true sum of all per-account `scaled` balances tracked in `market-vault` - the same root cause identified in the Sai report for `SaiTub.ice` vs. `SaiTub.tab`.

### Impact Explanation
If the vault's aggregate `principal-scaled` ends up strictly *greater* than the sum of remaining per-account debts (e.g., because the market-side formula rounds a user's removed scaled-debt down more aggressively than the vault-side ratio formula), then after every borrower for an asset has fully repaid via `market.repay` (each capped and reduced to `u0` scaled debt at `market-vault`), the vault can still be left holding residual, un-attributable `principal-scaled` > 0. This phantom debt continues to accrue "interest" through `total-debt`/`total-assets` with no real borrower or backing token balance, inflating the LP share price (`total-assets-preview` / `convert-to-assets`) beyond what the vault's real underlying balance (`ubalance`) can honor. The last LP(s) attempting to withdraw would then be unable to redeem their full expected share of assets because the contract does not actually hold enough underlying tokens to back the phantom-debt-inflated NAV - a permanent freezing of a portion of LP funds. Conversely, if the drift goes the other way, the aggregate under-counts real debt, distorting utilization/interest-rate calculations used for `interest-rate`/`calc-treasury-lp-preview` and misallocating protocol fee minting.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires no privileged access - any ordinary borrower calling `repay` (or being liquidated) repeatedly exercises the divergent code paths, and drift accumulates naturally over normal protocol usage (many small partial repayments, borrows at different indexes, and full pay-offs) without any attacker action. However, I was not able to fully inspect the exact implementations of `calc-principal-ratio-reduction` and `convert-to-scaled-debt` in this session (tool-call budget was exhausted before their bodies could be retrieved), so the exact magnitude/direction of the rounding divergence, and whether it is bounded by existing `min`/capping guards, is unverified. This introduces meaningful uncertainty into the severity assessment; the finding rests on the confirmed fact that two different mathematical formulas are used for what is meant to be the same conceptual value (scaled debt removed) in the per-account vs. aggregate ledgers.

### Recommendation
Ensure the same mathematical formula is used to compute scaled-debt deltas at both the per-account (`market-vault`) and aggregate (`vault`) layers - e.g., always derive the aggregate reduction as the sum of the exact per-account `mul-div-down`/`mul-div-up` scaled amounts actually removed, rather than independently re-deriving it via `calc-principal-ratio-reduction`. Where two entangled values must be maintained (aggregate `principal-scaled` and the sum of individual `scaled` balances), store and update only one canonically and derive the other, or add invariant tests/assertions (e.g., periodic reconciliation) proving `vault.principal-scaled == Σ market-vault.debt[*].scaled` for each asset.

### Proof of Concept
Full exploitation requires exact rounding behavior of `calc-principal-ratio-reduction` and `convert-to-scaled-debt`, which could not be retrieved in this session. Structurally: (1) multiple users borrow asset X across different `system-borrow` calls at different indexes; (2) each fully repays via `market.repay`, which zeroes their `market-vault` scaled debt using the index-ratio formula; (3) each corresponding `vault.system-repay` call reduces the aggregate `principal-scaled` using the separate `calc-principal-ratio-reduction` formula; (4) if the two formulas round differently, after all users have repaid to zero at `market-vault`, `vault.principal-scaled` is left nonzero, causing `total-debt`/`total-assets` to keep growing from phantom debt with no real backing, until the last LP's withdrawal reverts or is short-paid due to insufficient real underlying balance.
