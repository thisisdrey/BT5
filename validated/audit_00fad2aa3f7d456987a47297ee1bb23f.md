This confirms the exploit path is real and matches the claim precisely. `do_remove_liquidity` transfers `asset1`/`asset2` out of `pool_account` via `T::Assets::transfer` [1](#0-0) , and `T::Assets::transfer` for a `pallet-assets`-backed asset ultimately calls `reducible_balance`, which explicitly rejects any withdrawal from an account whose `status.is_frozen()` is true with `Error::Frozen` [2](#0-1) . `do_create_pool` performs zero validation on the freezer/admin roles of the asset kinds being paired — it only checks the pair is distinct, unused, and pays the setup fee [3](#0-2) , and `do_add_liquidity` similarly performs a plain `T::Assets::transfer` into the pool account with no freezer check [4](#0-3) .

Audit Report

## Title
Permissionless `pallet-asset-conversion` pool creation lets an unprivileged asset creator become the pool's `freezer` and permanently DoS a liquidity pool - ([File: substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/lib.rs])

## Summary
`do_create_pool` accepts any two `T::AssetKind` values from a signed caller with no check on who controls administrative roles (issuer/admin/freezer) of those assets. When an asset kind is backed by `pallet-assets`, `create` is a fully permissionless dispatchable that lets the caller set themselves as `issuer`, `admin`, and `freezer` of a new asset in one atomic step, and later call `freeze` against any account holding that asset — including a pool's account — with no restriction preventing this.

## Finding Description
`pallet_assets::create` lets any signed account become the asset's `owner` and set an arbitrary `admin` who is simultaneously made `issuer`/`admin`/`freezer` [5](#0-4) . `pallet_asset_conversion::do_create_pool` pairs any two distinct, unused `T::AssetKind`s with no check on their freezer/admin roles, only requiring the pool-setup fee [6](#0-5) . `do_add_liquidity` then transfers real value from any LP into the pool account via a plain `T::Assets::transfer` [4](#0-3) . The asset's `freezer` can call `Assets::freeze` against the pool account, since `freeze` only requires the caller to equal `d.freezer` and the target to already have an `Account` entry [7](#0-6) . Once frozen, `reducible_balance` rejects any transfer out of that account for that asset with `Error::Frozen` [2](#0-1) , which is exactly what `do_remove_liquidity` (and swaps) rely on when moving assets out of `pool_account` [1](#0-0) . No guard in any of these functions validates that the paired asset kind has no freezer or that the freezer is neutral/trusted.

## Impact Explanation
This is a public, chain-native pathway to permanent denial-of-service and fund lock for a permissionless liquidity pool. Legitimate LPs who deposit real value (native token or a trusted asset) into a pool paired against an attacker-created `pallet-assets` asset can have their pooled position permanently frozen, since `remove_liquidity` requires atomically transferring both assets out of the pool account and the frozen side will always fail. This matches the "permanent user-fund lock" category of the impact gate.

## Likelihood Explanation
Both `Assets::create` and `AssetConversion::create_pool` are public, permissionless, low-cost operations gated only by standard deposits/fees. No governance, validator, collator, or leaked-key assumption is required — an ordinary user can execute the entire attack using intended, unprivileged extrinsics.

## Recommendation
- In `do_create_pool` (and/or `do_add_liquidity`), reject asset kinds whose `fungibles::roles::Inspect` reports a freezer/admin that is not a neutral/no-op value, or restrict AMM pairing to an allowlist of trusted/sufficient assets.
- Alternatively, make pool accounts immune to `Assets::freeze`/`block`, e.g. by checking `T::PoolLocator` in `pallet-assets`' freeze logic (crosses pallet boundaries, so likely better handled via an allowlist/asset-kind filter in `pallet-asset-conversion` itself).
- At minimum, require that an asset's freezer role be renounced (set to a burn/no-op address) before it becomes eligible for AMM pairing.

## Proof of Concept
1. Attacker calls `Assets::create(signed(attacker), id=42, admin=attacker, min_balance=1)`, becoming `issuer`/`admin`/`freezer` of asset 42 [8](#0-7) .
2. Attacker mints supply of asset 42 to themselves via `Assets::mint`.
3. Attacker calls `AssetConversion::create_pool(signed(attacker), Native, WithId(42))`, creating pool account `P` — no check rejects this pairing [9](#0-8) .
4. Victim LP calls `AssetConversion::add_liquidity(signed(victim), Native, WithId(42), ...)`, depositing native tokens and asset-42 into `P` [10](#0-9) .
5. Attacker calls `Assets::freeze(signed(attacker), 42, P)` — succeeds since attacker is `freezer` of asset 42 and `P` already holds an `Account` entry for it [7](#0-6) .
6. Any subsequent `remove_liquidity`/swap involving this pool fails with `Error::Frozen`, since `T::Assets::transfer(asset2, &pool_account, ...)` hits `reducible_balance`'s frozen-account check [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) . The victim's native-token liquidity in `P` becomes unrecoverable, as `remove_liquidity` must move both assets out of `P` atomically and only the asset-42 `admin` (the attacker) can call `thaw`.
