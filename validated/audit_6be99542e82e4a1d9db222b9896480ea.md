### Title
Contract owner can instantly raise `consume_user_resource_percent` via `UpdateSettingContractActuator`, forcing unwitting callers to pay full energy fees with no timelock - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java`)

### Summary
`UpdateSettingContractActuator` lets a deployed smart contract's owner change `consume_user_resource_percent` for their contract at any time, with the new value taking effect immediately on the very next transaction, exactly the same "privileged creator instantly changes a fee-like parameter with no timelock, unprivileged users bear the cost" pattern flagged in the cred report.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` unpacks the `UpdateSettingContract` message and directly overwrites the contract's `consume_user_resource_percent` field in `ContractStore`, with no delay and `calcFee()` returning 0: [1](#0-0) 

`validate()` only checks that the caller is the `originAddress` (deployer) of the contract and that the new percent is within `[0, 100]` — there is no cooldown, no minimum-notice period, and nothing preventing the owner from applying the change in the very same block a victim's call transaction is broadcast: [2](#0-1) 

This percentage directly determines how much of the energy bill for a `TriggerSmartContract` call is charged to the caller vs. the contract's creator, computed in `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio()` and applied at payment time in `TransactionTrace.pay()`: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Any unprivileged caller who interacts with the contract expecting the creator to subsidize most of the energy cost (e.g. `consume_user_resource_percent` = 10%) can be front-run: the owner submits `UpdateSettingContract` setting the percent to 100 right before (or in the same block as) the victim's `TriggerSmartContract` transaction, forcing the caller to pay 100% of the energy cost for that call — analogous to the cred creator raising `sellShareRoyalty_` to 50% right before a user's sell transaction.

### Impact Explanation
A malicious or compromised contract owner can unilaterally and instantly shift the entire energy-fee burden onto callers who had no reason to expect it, causing unexpected loss of TRX/frozen-energy value for users interacting with the contract, mirroring the "up to 50% unexpected fee" loss scenario in the report. There is no on-chain signal, delay, or opt-out mechanism for affected users, and the change can be timed adversarially (front-running a known pending call) to maximize extracted value from a specific victim.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires only that the attacker be the `originAddress` of a deployed contract that other users are calling with `feeLimit` funded by themselves — a normal, unprivileged, permissionless configuration reachable via a single signed `UpdateSettingContract` transaction (also exposed over HTTP at `/wallet/updatesetting`). No special node privileges (SR/witness/committee) are needed, matching the allowed "contract deployer" actor category.

### Recommendation
Introduce a timelock/delay for `UpdateSettingContract` (and the related `UpdateEnergyLimitContract`) similar to the report's suggested two-step "request + approve after N blocks" pattern, and emit an on-chain event/log when a change is scheduled so callers can avoid interacting with the contract during the pending window, or bound the maximum allowed jump in `consume_user_resource_percent` per update.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deployer creates a contract and initially sets `consume_user_resource_percent = 10` (users mostly pay little, contract subsidizes energy).
2. A user submits a `TriggerSmartContract` call expecting to pay ~10% of the energy cost, as computed in `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio()`.
3. Immediately before/alongside that call, the deployer broadcasts `UpdateSettingContract` with `consume_user_resource_percent = 100`, processed instantly by `UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` with no delay.
4. If the deployer's transaction lands first (trivially achievable by fee/ordering or same-block placement), the victim's subsequent call is billed at 100% caller-paid energy per `TransactionTrace.pay()`, resulting in unexpected fee loss — directly analogous to the cred `sellShareRoyalty_` front-run scenario in the source report.
