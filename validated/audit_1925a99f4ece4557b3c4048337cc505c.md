### Title
Incorrect balance accounting for origination fees during loan disbursement - ([File: tare-io__tare-contracts/contracts/Loans.sol])

### Summary
In the `Loans.disburse` function, the protocol calculates the `commitment` amount based on the ledger's `ACC_UNFUNDED_COMMITMENT` balance. When a loan is disbursed with an `originationFee`, the contract correctly withholds the fee from the borrower's transfer but incorrectly stores the ledger entries in a way that allows the total amount recorded in the ledger to diverge from the actual `ACC_CASH` balance if the `originationFee` is not exactly the difference between the total commitment and the net disbursed amount. While there is a check `netDisbursedAmount + originationFee == commitment`, the double-entry ledger relies on the caller-provided `netDisbursedAmount` and `originationFee` to settle the `ACC_UNFUNDED_COMMITMENT`. If the fee accounting is inconsistent with the actual cash flow (e.g., due to rounding or fee calculation errors in the external report's analog), the ledger's internal balances for `ACC_CASH` and `ACC_ORIGINATOR_FEE_PAYABLE` will not reflect the true state of the protocol's assets.

### Finding Description
The vulnerability stems from how `_disburse` (called by `disburse`) handles the `originationFee` and `netDisbursedAmount`. 
1. The function `disburse` requires that `netDisbursedAmount + originationFee == commitment` [1](#0-0) .
2. In `_disburse`, two ledger entries are created:
    - Entry 1: Moves `originationFee` from `ACC_ORIGINATOR_FEE_PAYABLE` to `ACC_UNFUNDED_COMMITMENT` [2](#0-1) .
    - Entry 2: Moves `netDisbursedAmount` from `ACC_CASH` to `ACC_UNFUNDED_COMMITMENT` [3](#0-2) .
3. Finally, it performs an external transfer of `netDisbursedAmount` to the borrower [4](#0-3) .

The root cause is that the `ACC_CASH` ledger balance is only reduced by `netDisbursedAmount`, while the `originationFee` is assumed to stay in the contract's physical balance (as it was part of the original `fund` deposit). However, if there is any discrepancy between the intended fee and the amount recorded in the ledger, the `ACC_ORIGINATOR_FEE_PAYABLE` (a liability) will be incorrectly inflated or deflated relative to the actual USDC held in the contract for that purpose. This mirrors the seed bug where the "staked amount" stored was the gross amount instead of the net amount after fees, leading to corruption of the protocol's internal accounting of user entitlements.

### Impact Explanation
Material corruption of ledger balances and originator entitlement. If the `originationFee` is recorded incorrectly in the ledger (e.g., exceeding the actual withheld amount), the protocol will record a liability to the originator (`ACC_ORIGINATOR_FEE_PAYABLE`) that is not backed by physical `ACC_CASH`. This allows an originator to withdraw more funds than were actually withheld from the borrower, leading to a loss of protocol funds or underbacking of other loans' cashflows within the shared `Loans` contract.

### Likelihood Explanation
Medium. The protocol relies on the `originator` (who is a semi-privileged but scoped role) to provide the `netDisbursedAmount` and `originationFee` values. While there is a check against the total `commitment`, the split between the two is provided by the caller. If the originator provides values that satisfy the sum but misrepresent the actual fee agreement, they can manipulate the internal liability accounting.

### Recommendation
Ensure that the `ACC_CASH` balance and the `originationFee` liability are derived from a single authoritative source of truth regarding the fee calculation, rather than trusting the split provided in the `disburse` parameters. Additionally, verify that the physical `currency.balanceOf(address(this))` matches the sum of all `ACC_CASH` balances across all loans after disbursement.

### Proof of Concept
1. A loan is created with a `principalAmount` (commitment) of 1000 USDC.
2. The investor funds the loan with 1000 USDC. `ACC_CASH` for the loan is now 1000.
3. The originator calls `disburse(loanId, 900, 100, ...)` where 100 is the intended fee.
4. The ledger records:
   - `ACC_ORIGINATOR_FEE_PAYABLE`: -100 (Liability to originator)
   - `ACC_CASH`: 1000 - 900 = 100 (Remaining cash in ledger)
5. If the originator (or a malicious actor with disburse rights) provides `disburse(loanId, 800, 200, ...)` for a loan that was only supposed to have a 100 USDC fee:
   - `ACC_ORIGINATOR_FEE_PAYABLE` becomes -200.
   - The borrower only receives 800 USDC (unauthorized diversion of 100 USDC).
   - The originator can now call `originatorWithdraw` to claim 200 USDC, even though the protocol only intended for a 100 USDC fee, effectively stealing 100 USDC from the borrower's principal via the ledger's misaccounting.
