### Title
Account-wide `last-borrow-block` same-block guard lets an attacker delay liquidation of an unrelated, already-underwater debt asset - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-market-vault.clar`)

### Summary
The obligation registry keyed by `resolve-or-create` stores a single `last-borrow-block` field per account (not per asset). `debt-add-scaled` overwrites this field on every borrow regardless of which asset is borrowed, so borrowing a trivial amount of a second, unrelated debt asset refreshes the same-block guard for the entire position and blocks liquidation of an already near-liquidatable, different asset for that block.

### Finding Description
The `registry` map stores one entry per account with a single `last-borrow-block: uint` field, keyed by the account's obligation `id`, not by `{id, asset}`: [1](#0-0) 

`resolve-or-create` looks up this single entry for the account via the `reverse` map: [2](#0-1) 

`debt-add-scaled` unconditionally merges `last-borrow-block: stacks-block-height` into the account-wide entry on every call, irrespective of `asset-id`: [3](#0-2) 

Because this field is not partitioned by asset, an attacker who already holds a large, near-liquidatable debt in `asset-id-1` can call `borrow` for `asset-id-2` (any other enabled debt asset) with a minimal amount (e.g. `u1`). This calls `debt-add-scaled` for `asset-id-2`, which overwrites `last-borrow-block` for the whole account entry to the current `stacks-block-height`. The market's liquidation same-block guard (`ERR-LIQUIDATION-BORROW-SAME-BLOCK`) reads this account-wide `last-borrow-block` and reverts any `liquidate` call in the same block, including one that targets `asset-id-1`'s debt — a debt asset that was not touched by the triggering borrow at all. No existing check in `debt-add-scaled` or the liquidation guard scopes the freshness check to the specific asset being liquidated, so the guard is trivially and repeatably bypassable to stall liquidation of an unrelated already-unsafe position.

### Impact Explanation
This allows an attacker with an underwater (or near-liquidatable) position to indefinitely delay liquidation of that position for as long as they are willing to repeat a `u1` borrow of an unrelated asset each block, at negligible capital cost. Delayed liquidation lets debt continue accruing interest/oracle-price drift against suppliers while the position remains unliquidated, increasing bad debt beyond what timely liquidation would have captured, directly worsening protocol insolvency risk — matching the in-scope Critical impact category "protocol insolvency."

### Likelihood Explanation
Preconditions are minimal and fully attacker-controlled: the attacker only needs an existing debt position in `asset-id-1` near liquidation threshold and the ability to also borrow `u1` of any other enabled debt asset (`asset-id-2`), a capital cost effectively equal to zero, repeatable every block indefinitely and by any unprivileged principal.

### Recommendation
Scope the same-block anti-frontrunning guard per asset instead of per account: track `last-borrow-block` (or an equivalent freshness marker) keyed by `{id, asset-id}` so that borrowing one asset cannot refresh the freeze window for a different, unrelated asset in the same account's position. Update `debt-add-scaled` to only update the per-asset marker, and update the liquidation check in the market contract to compare against the specific asset being liquidated.

### Proof of Concept
Clarinet/vitest simnet plan:
1. Set up two enabled debt assets, `asset-1` and `asset-2`, and a collateral asset with price feeds.
2. Have `attacker` deposit collateral and `borrow` a large amount of `asset-1` such that the position sits near partial-liquidation LTV.
3. Advance one or more blocks so `last-borrow-block` for `asset-1`'s borrow is in the past (guard would normally allow liquidation).
4. Move oracle price / accrue interest so `asset-1`'s debt becomes liquidatable.
5. In the same block: call `borrow(asset-2-ft, u1, none, none)` for `attacker`, then call `liquidate(attacker, coll-ft, asset-1-ft, ...)`.
6. Assert the `liquidate` call reverts with `ERR-LIQUIDATION-BORROW-SAME-BLOCK`, even though the borrow that triggered the guard was for `asset-2`, not `asset-1`.
7. Quantify the extra block of interest accrual on `asset-1`'s unpaid debt (via `debt-scaled`/index growth) as the supplier-side loss caused by the forced delay, and show the attacker can repeat this every block at `u1` cost.
