No vulnerability found for this question.

**Analysis:** The scenario described is inherent, documented, probabilistic behavior of a bloom-filter-style deduper, and is additionally shielded by existing QoS/rate-limiting infrastructure that an unstaked attacker cannot bypass.

Key points:
- `Deduper::maybe_reset` explicitly documents that reset "is not synchronized with concurrent `dedup()` calls" and that saturation causing a fresh window is an accepted, intended tradeoff [1](#0-0) .
- `Deduper::false_positive_rate` and the saturation-triggered reset in `maybe_reset` are tested (`test_dedup_saturated`, `test_dedup_capacity`) to confirm the structure self-resets to `popcount == 0` once saturation is observed, and stays fixed-size (`Vec<AtomicU64>` sized once at construction) — it never grows unboundedly regardless of packet volume [2](#0-1) .
- The `SigVerifyStage::servicer` loop calls `maybe_reset` on a 10ms cadence with `DEDUPER_FALSE_POSITIVE_RATE = 0.001` and `MAX_DEDUPER_AGE = 2s` [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) , and any transient overshoot between checks only affects the probabilistic false-positive rate of an anti-spam optimization, not signature verification correctness, block validity, or memory bounds.
- Critically, an unstaked attacker cannot realistically drive the packet volume needed (millions of unique packets within a 10ms window) because QUIC ingress already enforces per-IP connection rate limits (`ConnectionRateLimiter`) [5](#0-4) , global connection burst limits [6](#0-5) , and a hard-coded 200 TPS cap per unstaked connection plus a 20% share of total stream capacity for the entire unstaked pool [7](#0-6) , verified by `test_throttling_check_no_packet_drop` demonstrating unstaked traffic is throttled well below line rate [8](#0-7) .

Since (1) the deduper's bounded, self-resetting design and transient false-positive behavior is an accepted, tested tradeoff rather than an invariant violation, and (2) existing QoS/stream-throttling already prevents an unstaked attacker from reaching the packet volumes needed to meaningfully saturate the 63,999,979-bit deduper within a single 10ms reset interval, this does not constitute a reachable, unmitigated vulnerability under the stated rules.

    /// see an inconsistent snapshot across the old/new hash state and the
    /// cleared/refilled bitset, but that is acceptable because reset already
    /// starts a fresh deduplication window.
    fn reset<R: Rng>(&self, rng: &mut R) {
        for bits in &self.bits {
            bits.store(0, Ordering::Relaxed);
        }
        self.popcount.store(0, Ordering::Relaxed);
        self.state.store(Arc::new(DeduperGeneration::new(rng)));
    }
