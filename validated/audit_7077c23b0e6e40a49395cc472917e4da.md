### Title
Meta-transaction (Delegate Action) deposit refunds are misrouted to the sender instead of the paying relayer, enabling theft of relayer funds - (File: `runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs`)

### Summary
When a relayer submits a meta transaction (`DelegateAction`/`DelegateV2`), the relayer pays for all attached deposits of the inner actions up front. If the wrapped inner action receipt subsequently fails on the receiver's shard, the deposit refund is sent to `predecessor_id` of the newly-spawned receipt, which `apply_delegate_action` sets to the DelegateAction's `sender_id` (Alice) rather than to the relayer who actually paid.

### Finding Description
`total_deposit` explicitly documents this design: "here Relayer pays the deposit but if actions fail, the deposit is refunded to Sender of DelegateAction" [1](#0-0) . In `apply_delegate_action`, the inner receipt is constructed with `predecessor_id: sender_id.clone()` (Alice, the DelegateAction sender) while `signer_id`/`signer_public_key` (used only for gas refunds) are copied from the outer `action_receipt`, i.e. the relayer [2](#0-1) . The comment in the same function makes the mismatch explicit: "Gas is refunded to the signer, this is Relayer... If something goes wrong, deposit is refunded to the predecessor, this is sender_id/Sender in DelegateAction" [5](#0-4) .

When this receipt fails, `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` sends the deposit refund to `receipt.balance_refund_receiver()` [4](#0-3) , which defaults to `predecessor_id` because the receipt created by `apply_delegate_action` is a plain V1 `ActionReceipt`/`ReceiptV0` with no `refund_to` set [6](#0-5) . The result: the relayer's NEAR that funded the deposit is transferred to Alice (the sender), not returned to the relayer, exactly analogous to the JOJO `GeneralRepay#repayJUSD` bug where excess funds are refunded to the wrong party (the receiver/beneficiary `to` instead of the payer `msg.sender`).

This is not merely a hypothetical edge case — the exact scenario is spelled out in the project's own architecture docs: "what if the transaction fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is Alice and therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached but will fail on Bob's shard." [7](#0-6) 

### Impact Explanation
Alice can craft a meta transaction (with the relayer's consent to pay gas/deposit) targeting an action she knows will fail on the receiver's shard (e.g., a `FunctionCall` with a large deposit to a nonexistent method, or one that will exceed some receiver-side check). The relayer pays the deposit balance up front (deducted from the relayer's account when the transaction is converted / charged), but on failure the whole deposit refund is credited to Alice's account instead of the relayer's. This is concrete theft of funds from the relayer, repeatable at will since Alice fully controls whether the inner action succeeds or fails.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account can act as "Alice" and only needs a cooperating (or automated/permissionless) relayer service willing to sponsor meta-transactions — a normal, expected usage pattern for NEP-366 meta transactions. No privileged access or validator/node compromise is required; this is reachable purely through ordinary transaction submission (a `Delegate`/`DelegateV2` action signed by Alice, relayed by any relayer).

### Recommendation
Route deposit refunds for delegate-action-spawned receipts to the relayer (the outer transaction's signer) by default, or require `apply_delegate_action` to set `refund_to` on the generated `ActionReceiptV2` to the relayer's account (mirroring how gas refunds already go to `signer_id`). Alternatively, require relayers to use a `refund_to`-aware SDK path and document/enforce that deposit-bearing delegate actions must not be relayed without an off-chain reimbursement/insurance mechanism.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice signs a `DelegateAction` containing a `FunctionCall` action with a nontrivial deposit, targeting a contract/method that Alice knows will fail (e.g., wrong `receiver_id` vs. access key permission, or a method that will panic).
2. Relayer signs and submits the outer transaction; relayer's account balance is charged the deposit + gas at transaction-to-receipt conversion.
3. `apply_delegate_action` creates the inner `ActionReceipt` with `predecessor_id = sender_id` (Alice) and forwards it to the receiver's shard [8](#0-7) .
4. The inner action fails as intended.
5. `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` issues `Receipt::new_balance_refund(receipt.balance_refund_receiver(), deposit_refund)` where `balance_refund_receiver()` resolves to Alice (`predecessor_id`) rather than the relayer [4](#0-3) [3](#0-2) .
6. Alice receives the deposit that the relayer paid for; the relayer has no recourse on-chain.
