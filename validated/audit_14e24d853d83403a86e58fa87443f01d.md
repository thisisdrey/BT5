### Title
Operational currency rescue causes ledger insolvency and cross-loan fund theft - ([File: tare-io__tare-contracts/contracts/misc/Rescuable.sol])

### Summary
The `Rescuable` contract, inherited by `Loans` and `PortfolioVault`, provides a mechanism for the Guardian to recover tokens accidentally sent to the protocol. However, the `rescueERC20Tokens` function lacks a check to prevent the rescue of the protocol's primary operational currency (`currency` in `Loans` or `assetToken` in `PortfolioVault`). Rescuing these tokens creates a discrepancy between the physical token balance and the internal ledger accounting, leading to protocol insolvency and the potential for one loan's funds to be used to satisfy another's withdrawal.

### Finding Description
The `Rescuable.rescueERC20Tokens` function allows the `GUARDIAN_ROLE` to transfer any amount of any ERC20 token held by the contract to a pre-defined `recoveryAddress` [1](#0-0) . 

In `Loans.sol`, the contract's primary `currency` (typically USDC) is used to back the `ACC_CASH` balances of all individual loans [2](#0-1) . The protocol relies on the invariant that the contract's total `currency` balance must be greater than or equal to the sum of all `ACC_CASH` balances across all loans [3](#0-2) .

Because `rescueERC20Tokens` does not exclude the operational `currency`, a Guardian can "rescue" funds that are actually backed by ledger entries [4](#0-3) . This operation does not update the `LoansLedger`, meaning the `ACC_CASH` balances remain unchanged while the underlying tokens are removed.

### Impact Explanation
1.  **Insolvency & Denial of Service**: If the operational currency is rescued, the contract will not have enough funds to satisfy all legitimate withdrawal requests (`investorWithdraw`, `servicerWithdraw`, etc.). Users attempting to withdraw will face reverts due to `InsufficientBalance` at the ERC20 level [5](#0-4) .
2.  **Cross-Loan Fund Diversion**: Because all loans share the same `currency` pool, if a portion of the pool is rescued, the remaining funds are no longer sufficient to cover all loans. Early withdrawers from "Loan A" will successfully receive their funds by effectively siphoning the cash belonging to "Loan B" [5](#0-4) .
3.  **Vault NAV Corruption**: In `PortfolioVault`, rescuing the `assetToken` causes `updateNav` to underflow-revert because the balance becomes lower than the pending deposit/redeem counters, effectively bricking the vault's share pricing [6](#0-5) .

### Likelihood Explanation
The likelihood is low as it requires a `GUARDIAN_ROLE` action, which is gated by a `TimelockController` [7](#0-6) . However, the protocol's design explicitly attempts to protect against Guardian errors or compromises, and this function provides a direct path for a Guardian to violate core accounting invariants without triggering any on-chain reconciliation.

### Recommendation
Modify the `rescueERC20Tokens` function to prevent rescuing the operational currency of the inheriting contract. In `Loans.sol` and `PortfolioVault.sol`, override the function or provide a hook to check against the `currency` or `assetToken`.

```solidity
// In Loans.sol
function rescueERC20Tokens(address token, uint256 amount) public override returns (uint256) {
    require(token != address(currency), "Cannot rescue operational currency");
    return super.rescueERC20Tokens(token, amount);
}
```

### Proof of Concept
1.  A `Loans` contract is initialized with USDC as the `currency`.
2.  Loan ID 1 is funded with 100,000 USDC. `ACC_CASH[1]` is now 100,000. Total contract balance is 100,000 USDC.
3.  Loan ID 2 is funded with 100,000 USDC. `ACC_CASH[2]` is now 100,000. Total contract balance is 200,000 USDC.
4.  The Guardian calls `rescueERC20Tokens(USDC, 150,000)`.
5.  150,000 USDC is transferred to the `recoveryAddress`. The contract now holds only 50,000 USDC.
6.  The Investor of Loan ID 1 calls `investorWithdraw(1, 100,000)`.
7.  The transaction succeeds because the contract has 50,000 USDC (Wait, it would revert if the amount is 100k).
8.  If the Investor of Loan ID 1 withdraws 50,000 USDC, they successfully take the remaining balance.
9.  The Investor of Loan ID 2 now has an `ACC_CASH` balance of 100,000 but the contract balance is 0. Their funds have been stolen/diverted.

- **On `Loans`**: every loan's cash bucket (`ACC_CASH`) is custodied in the same contract. The invariant `currency.balanceOf(loans) >= Σ ACC_CASH[loanId]` underpins per-loan cash segregation. Rescuing `currency` silently violates this invariant: subsequent withdrawals either revert (insufficient balance) or — worse — succeed by drawing against another loan's cash, cross-contaminating loan segregation.
- **On `PortfolioVault`**: `updateNav` finalization computes `lastNav = assetToken.balanceOf(this) − totalPendingDepositAssets − totalClaimableRedeemAssets + adjustedNav`. Rescuing `assetToken` lowers the balance without touching the counters; the next finalize underflow-reverts and bricks NAV until the rescued amount is returned.

**Impact:** Guardian compromise (or honest mistake) of `rescueERC20Tokens` on `Loans` permits arbitrary loss of loan custody funds. On the vault, NAV becomes unupdatable until reconciled off-chain.
