### Title
Deployed-contract owner can freely re-tune `consume_user_resource_percent` / `origin_energy_limit` at any time to arbitrarily shift energy costs onto callers - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java`, `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator.java`)

### Summary
The external report flags a Dutch-auction contract where price-defining variables (`startPrice`, `lowestPrice`, `daPriceCurveLength`, `daDropInterval`) can be re-set an unlimited number of times after the sale starts, letting the owner manipulate the price mid-auction. The equivalent bug class in java-tron is the pair of actuators that let a deployed-contract's origin address change the economic parameters that split TVM energy costs between the contract owner and the transaction caller: `UpdateSettingContract` (`consume_user_resource_percent`) and `UpdateEnergyLimitContract` (`origin_energy_limit`). Both actuators allow the owner to change these "critical" values as many times as desired, with no lock, no monotonic constraint, and no cooldown — directly analogous to the unrestricted price-curve setters in the audited contract.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` unconditionally overwrites `consumeUserResourcePercent` on the `ContractCapsule` every time the owner submits the contract: [1](#0-0) 

`UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator.execute()` does the same for `origin_energy_limit`: [2](#0-1) 

`validate()` for both only checks that the caller is the contract's `originAddress` and that the new value is within a static range (`[0,100]` for percent, `> 0` for energy limit) — there is no check preventing the value from being changed again after it was already set, and no restriction on how often or when (e.g., mid-flight of pending calls) it can be updated: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

The test suite explicitly documents that repeated updates are allowed and succeed silently: [5](#0-4) 

These two values directly determine, inside `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio`, how much energy is charged to the caller versus drawn from the contract owner's frozen energy for every subsequent `TriggerSmartContract` execution: [6](#0-5) 

Because a single signed `UpdateSettingContract`/`UpdateEnergyLimitContract` transaction from the contract owner can be broadcast at any point — including between two of a caller's own transactions in the same block, or right before/after a caller's `TriggerSmartContract` call — the owner controls, transaction-by-transaction, how much energy (and thus how much TRX, via the bandwidth/energy fee model) each caller must burn to interact with the contract. This mirrors the Dutch Auction bug: a "critical" economic parameter that should be fixed once (or change only under strict, auditable rules) is instead freely mutable by a single, unprivileged, self-interested party (the contract deployer), and callers have no way to know or lock in the parameter that will apply to their transaction.

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged contract deployer can:
- Set `consume_user_resource_percent` to `0` while advertising/soliciting interactions (making calls appear "free" of energy cost to callers, all paid from the owner's frozen energy), then flip it to `100` immediately afterward so future callers unexpectedly pay full energy cost — or the reverse, to unpredictably drain the caller's own frozen energy/TRX.
- Front-run their own contract's popularity: after building trust with a low percent, raise it to shift cost burden onto users without any timelock or notice, similar to an owner front-running a Dutch auction by dropping `lowestPrice` for insiders and restoring conditions afterward.
- Lower `origin_energy_limit` to effectively force `ContractExeException`/insufficient-energy failures for callers who set fee limits based on stale assumptions, or raise it later to selectively subsidize favored callers.

This does not directly move TRX out of arbitrary user balances, but it does let a single unprivileged actor manipulate a chain-tracked cost-splitting parameter that decides how much of a user's TRX/energy is consumed on each call, at arbitrary frequency, breaking users' economic expectations for interacting with the contract — a loss-of-funds-via-unexpected-fee-consumption scenario for callers who cannot observe the parameter change before their own transaction is included.

### Likelihood Explanation
High reachability: any account that deployed a smart contract can call `UpdateSettingContract`/`UpdateEnergyLimitContract` (via `wallet/updatesetting` and `wallet/updateenergylimit` HTTP/gRPC endpoints) as often as desired, with no cooldown, no lock, and no historical constraint enforced in `validate()`. The behavior is demonstrably intentional/unrestricted per the existing `twiceUpdateEnergyLimitContract` test, meaning exploitation requires no special conditions beyond owning a popular contract and timing ordinary transaction submission.

### Recommendation
Add safeguards mirroring the audit's mitigation for the Dutch Auction: (1) allow `consume_user_resource_percent`/`origin_energy_limit` to be changed at most once, or (2) enforce a mandatory timelock/notice period (e.g., N blocks) between an update and its effect, so callers can observe the pending change before their transaction executes, and/or (3) cap the frequency of updates per contract (e.g., once per N blocks) to prevent per-transaction manipulation.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy a contract `C` with `consume_user_resource_percent = 0` (owner pays for callers) and publicize it to attract interaction.
2. Once callers begin issuing `TriggerSmartContract` transactions expecting near-zero energy cost, the owner submits `UpdateSettingContract{consume_user_resource_percent=100}` (as shown to succeed unconditionally and repeatedly in `UpdateSettingContractActuatorTest`/`UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuatorTest`).
3. Subsequent caller transactions, processed by `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio`, now compute `creatorEnergyLimit = 0` and force the full energy cost onto the unaware caller: [7](#0-6) 
4. The owner can revert the setting afterward, repeating the cycle indefinitely — extracting more caller-side energy/TRX than callers reasonably anticipated, with no on-chain mechanism to prevent or delay the change.

  @Test
  public void twiceUpdateEnergyLimitContract() throws InvalidProtocolBufferException {
    UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator actuator = new UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator();
    actuator.setChainBaseManager(dbManager.getChainBaseManager())
        .setAny(getContract(OWNER_ADDRESS, CONTRACT_ADDRESS, TARGET_ENERGY_LIMIT));

    UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator secondActuator = new UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator();
    secondActuator.setChainBaseManager(dbManager.getChainBaseManager())
        .setAny(getContract(OWNER_ADDRESS, CONTRACT_ADDRESS, 90L));

    TransactionResultCapsule ret = new TransactionResultCapsule();
    try {
      // first
      actuator.validate();
      actuator.execute(ret);

      Assert.assertEquals(OWNER_ADDRESS,
          ByteArray.toHexString(actuator.getOwnerAddress().toByteArray()));
      Assert.assertEquals(ret.getInstance().getRet(), Protocol.Transaction.Result.code.SUCESS);
      Assert.assertEquals(
          dbManager.getContractStore().get(ByteArray.fromHexString(CONTRACT_ADDRESS))
              .getOriginEnergyLimit(), TARGET_ENERGY_LIMIT);

      // second
      secondActuator.validate();
      secondActuator.execute(ret);

      Assert.assertEquals(ret.getInstance().getRet(), Protocol.Transaction.Result.code.SUCESS);
      Assert.assertEquals(
          dbManager.getContractStore().get(ByteArray.fromHexString(CONTRACT_ADDRESS))
              .getOriginEnergyLimit(), 90L);
    } catch (ContractValidateException | ContractExeException e) {
      Assert.fail(e.getMessage());
    }
  }
