The refund path in `resolve_deposit_internal` bounds the refund by three independent minimums: the requested refund, `*deposited` (the exact amount credited to `receiver_id` in *this* deposit flow), and the receiver's current balance — `let refund_amount = requested_refund.min(*deposited).min(balance_left);` [1](#0-0) . Because `*deposited` is bound to the amount just credited in this same `mt_on_transfer`/`mt_resolve_deposit` call (passed as `&mut amount.0` from the original `amounts` vector), the resolver can never refund more than what flowed into the account during this exact deposit, regardless of any additional balance the account may hold from before being locked [2](#0-1) .

The use of `as_inner_unchecked_mut()` in the resolver is intentional and documented: deposits and refunds are deliberately allowed to bypass lock checks so that senders don't lose in-flight funds when a receiving account becomes locked mid-flow, as stated explicitly in the analogous `mt_resolve_transfer` comment block [3](#0-2) . Locking accounts requires a privileged role (`UnrestrictedAccountUnlocker`/other role holders), which is out of scope for an unprivileged attacker, and even granting the premise that a receiver becomes locked between transfer and resolve, the refund cap by `*deposited` ensures only the in-flight amount can ever leave — exactly the invariant the question asks to validate. Both sides of the equality (`assets leaving == refund of the just-received transfer`) match; no path lets the attacker extract pre-existing locked balance.
