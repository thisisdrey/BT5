The reported bug class centers on a **hardcoded, protocol-level minimum slippage tolerance** (`MAX_SWAP_SLIPPAGE_BPS`) that is set tighter than an unavoidable pool fee, causing every swap to permanently revert. I searched java-tron's analogous token-exchange paths — `ExchangeTransactionActuator` and `MarketSellAssetActuator` — and neither implements a similar fixed internal slippage floor.

In `ExchangeTransactionActuator`, the only "slippage protection" is the caller-supplied `expected` field, which is validated against the actual output of the bancor-style `ExchangeCapsule.transaction` calculation with no additional protocol-imposed minimum: [1](#0-0) 

The exchange formula itself has no separate fee percentage baked in that would conflict with a hardcoded tolerance: [2](#0-1) 

Similarly, `MarketSellAssetActuator` matches taker/maker orders purely by user-specified `sellTokenQuantity`/`buyTokenQuantity` ratios, with no internal slippage-bound constant that could cause systemic order-matching failure: [3](#0-2) 

A grep for `slippage`/`SLIPPAGE` across the whole repository returned no matches, confirming there is no equivalent constant or mechanism in scope that could reproduce the "internal minimum stricter than achievable price" DoS pattern described in the report.
