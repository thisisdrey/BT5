### Title
Contract owner can instantly change `consume_user_resource_percent` via `UpdateSettingContractActuator` with no delay, causing unexpected energy-fee cost shifts onto callers - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java)

### Summary
`UpdateSettingContractActuator` lets any smart-contract owner (an unprivileged account, i.e. "asset issuer/contract deployer" in the reachable-actor list) change `consume_user_resource_percent` for their deployed contract instantly, in the same block their transaction is processed, with no analog of a time-delay/two-step proposal mechanism. This mirrors the reported `Vault_Base_ERC20.setOpenLoanFee` issue: a value that materially changes the economic terms borne by a counterparty (here, callers of the contract who pay a share of energy fees) can be altered with zero warning period.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute` unpacks the new percent and immediately persists it to the `ContractCapsule`, then evicts the repository LRU cache so the new value is visible to the very next transaction in the same block: [1](#0-0) 

`validate()` only checks that the caller is the contract's origin address and that the percent is within `[0, 100]` — there is no cooldown, no pending-value/"propose-then-apply-after-delay" pattern, and no restriction on how often or how quickly it can be changed: [2](#0-1) 

At execution time, `consume_user_resource_percent` directly determines how the energy cost of a `TriggerSmartContract` call is split between the contract's frozen energy (creator-paid) and the caller's own energy/fee limit: [3](#0-2) 

Because the update takes effect immediately (no `VAULT_TIME_DELAY`-style buffer), a contract owner can advertise/maintain a low `consume_user_resource_percent` (e.g. 0%, so calls appear "free" for the caller) to attract usage, then submit an `UpdateSettingContract` transaction to raise it to 100% and have it packed into the same or an immediately following block right before/around a victim's call, shifting the entire energy cost onto the caller without any notice.

### Impact Explanation
A caller who submitted a `TriggerSmartContract` transaction expecting little/no personal energy cost (based on the previously observed percent) can be made to pay for energy out of their own account/`fee_limit` once the percent is flipped, up to their fee limit — an unbacked, unexpected balance debit the caller did not consent to at submission time. This is a direct fund-loss/griefing vector reachable by any unprivileged contract owner against any unprivileged caller, with no mitigating delay, matching the root cause of the reported bug class (fee/parameter changes must be delayed to give counterparties time to react).

### Likelihood Explanation
Any address that deployed a contract can call this actuator via `/wallet/updatesetting` or the `UpdateSetting` RPC at any time, for free (fee is 0), and the change is visible starting from the next transaction/block. No special privilege is required beyond being the contract's origin address, so likelihood of exploitation by a malicious/opportunistic contract owner is high whenever their contract has real usage. [4](#0-3) 

### Recommendation
Introduce a delay/queue mechanism analogous to the recommended fix in the source report: require `UpdateSettingContract` (and similarly `UpdateEnergyLimitContract`) changes to be proposed first and only take effect after a fixed delay (e.g. one or more blocks/maintenance cycles), similar to how `UpdateBrokerageContract` changes are deferred to the next voting cycle rather than applied instantly: [5](#0-4) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker deploys a contract and calls `UpdateSetting` to set `consume_user_resource_percent = 0`, making calls appear cost-free to callers.
2. A victim submits a `TriggerSmartContract` transaction with a modest `fee_limit`, relying on the observed 0% split.
3. Attacker submits `UpdateSetting` with `consume_user_resource_percent = 100` targeting the same block/shortly before the victim's transaction is packed (both transactions go through `UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute` → `ContractCapsule` update → `RepositoryImpl.removeLruCache`, taking effect immediately).
4. The victim's transaction is now charged the full energy cost against their own account/`fee_limit`, an amount they did not anticipate when submitting the transaction.
