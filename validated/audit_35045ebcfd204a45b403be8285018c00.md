### Title
Double-rounded-down `mul-div-down` in `accrue()` permanently truncates protocol treasury fee shares - ([File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar])

### Summary
Every Zest v0 vault (`v0-vault-usdc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`, `v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-stx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`) computes the protocol's reserve fee and the corresponding treasury LP (zft share) mint amount using floor-rounding integer division twice in a row inside `accrue()`. Because `mul-div-down` truncates towards zero and there is no mechanism to carry forward the truncated remainder, a fractional amount of the DAO treasury's protocol fee is silently and permanently lost on every single accrual, exactly the bug class described in the MultiRewards report (`reward.div(duration)` truncation with no residual carried to the next call).

### Finding Description
`accrue()` — called at the start of essentially every user-facing operation (`deposit`, `redeem`, `system-borrow`, `system-repay`, etc., since interest must be up to date before any share math) — computes the protocol's cut of freshly accrued interest and mints it as vault shares (zft) to `dao-treasury`: [1](#0-0) 

The intermediate quantities are both computed with floor division: [2](#0-1) 

`reserve-inc = mul-div-down(debt-delta, fee-reserve, BPS)` rounds the fee down; the result is then divided again to compute `treasury-lp = mul-div-down(reserve-inc, total-supply, ta-preview - reserve-inc)`, which floors a second time. The same two-step floor computation appears identically as `calc-treasury-lp-preview` (used for preview/UI purposes) in every vault: [3](#0-2) 
(see equivalent `calc-treasury-lp-preview` at e.g. lines 352-363 of `v0-vault-usdh.clar`)

Unlike a debt/index computation (where floor/ceil choice is a deliberate protection against user-favorable rounding), this specific computation determines how many zft shares the DAO treasury receives for its already-realized share of interest (`debt-delta * fee-reserve / BPS`, itself already floored). Any fractional remainder of `reserve-inc` (from the first division) and any fractional remainder of the LP-share conversion (from the second division) is never minted to anyone — it is neither carried over to the next `accrue()` call nor separately tracked. It becomes de-facto backing for existing zft holders' `total-assets`, i.e. it is transferred away from the treasury's fee entitlement permanently, indistinguishable from lost yield, on every accrual across every one of the six mainnet vaults.

This mirrors the reported MultiRewards defect precisely: a rate/amount computed via one (here: two) floor divisions, invoked on virtually every state-changing call, with no residual-carry-forward fix, causing continuous small unrecoverable losses to the intended reward/fee recipient.

### Impact Explanation
The DAO treasury's protocol fee is a form of protocol yield/royalty on interest generated by borrowers. Because the reserve-fee-to-shares conversion floors twice per accrual, and `accrue()` runs on nearly every deposit/borrow/repay/redeem across six live vaults, a portion of the treasury's entitled yield is permanently and irrecoverably lost with every call — it is captured implicitly by existing depositors instead of minted to `dao-treasury`. This is a permanent loss of unclaimed yield to the protocol treasury, matching the in-scope "theft/permanent freezing of unclaimed yield" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
High. `accrue()` is invoked automatically as part of ordinary, unprivileged user actions (deposits, redemptions, borrows, repayments) on every vault, so the truncation triggers continuously without any attacker action being required — identical to the "high likelihood" characterization in the original report, since normal usage alone causes the loss to accumulate over the vault's lifetime.

### Recommendation
Track the truncated remainder from the `reserve-inc` and `treasury-lp` computations (e.g., a `dust`/`fee-remainder` data-var per vault) and fold it into the next accrual's calculation, mirroring the fix recommended for MultiRewards (carry forward `reward % rewardsDuration`). Alternatively, round the reserve-fee computation up (`mul-div-up`) so the treasury is never under-compensated, with any excess reconciled against depositor accounting.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual reproduction (applies to any of the six vaults, e.g. `v0-vault-usdc.clar`):
1. Deposit funds, then let a small amount of interest accrue such that `debt-delta * fee-reserve` is not evenly divisible by `BPS` (trivially true for almost any realistic `debt-delta`).
2. Call any state-changing function that triggers `accrue()` (e.g. `deposit` with amount `u1`, or a direct call to `accrue`).
3. Observe `reserve-inc` in the emitted event is `floor(debt-delta * fee-reserve / BPS)` — the fractional part is dropped.
4. Observe `treasury-lp` minted to `.dao-treasury` is `floor(reserve-inc * total-supply / (ta-preview - reserve-inc))`, again dropping a fractional remainder.
5. Repeat step 2 across many small accruals (as happens naturally with routine protocol usage): the cumulative sum of both floored remainders is fee revenue that was computed as owed to the treasury but never minted to it, and is unrecoverable, matching the original PoC's "reward stuck / underpaid" pattern but for protocol fee shares instead of MultiRewards tokens.
