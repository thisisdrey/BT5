### Title
Interest-reserve fee rounds to zero on frequent `accrue()` calls, permanently denying treasury its protocol fee share - ([File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar])

### Summary
The `accrue` function in every Zest vault contract (`v0-vault-usdc.clar`, `v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`, `v0-vault-stx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`) computes the protocol's reserve/treasury cut of newly accrued interest as `reserve-inc = mul-div-down debt-delta fee-reserve BPS`, an integer division that truncates towards zero exactly like the `wadMul` rounding issue described in the referenced report. Because `accrue()` is a public, permissionless, unauthenticated function, any caller can invoke it as often as desired, fragmenting the natural interest-per-period into arbitrarily small `debt-delta` increments. When `debt-delta * fee-reserve < BPS`, `reserve-inc` truncates to `0`, and the treasury LP mint (`treasury-lp`) is skipped entirely for that increment — with no accumulation or catch-up mechanism, since `old-debt`/`index` are advanced unconditionally on every call regardless of whether the fee was charged.

### Finding Description
`accrue()` is defined identically across all vault contracts: [1](#0-0) 

The key computation is:
```
(old-debt (mul-div-down scaled-principal idx INDEX-PRECISION))
(new-debt (mul-div-down scaled-principal next INDEX-PRECISION))
(debt-delta (if (> new-debt old-debt) (- new-debt old-debt) u0))
(reserve-inc (mul-div-down debt-delta (var-get fee-reserve) BPS))
(treasury-lp (if (> reserve-inc u0) (mul-div-down reserve-inc (total-supply) (- (total-assets-preview) reserve-inc)) u0))
``` [2](#0-1) 

`mul-div-down` truncates: `(/ (* x y) z)`, so `reserve-inc` is `0` whenever `debt-delta * fee-reserve < BPS`. Since `fee-reserve` is expressed in basis points out of `BPS = 10000`, this means any `debt-delta` below `BPS / fee-reserve` (e.g., roughly 10 raw units for a 10% reserve factor) yields zero fee, exactly mirroring the referenced `calculateLoanOriginationFee` bug where the multiplication-before-division ordering causes amounts below a threshold to compute a zero fee.

Crucially, `accrue()` unconditionally advances `index`/`lindex` (and thus the debt basis used for the *next* call's `old-debt`) regardless of whether `reserve-inc` was non-zero: [3](#0-2) 

This means there is no "carry-over" of the un-billed interest increment to a future call — each call resets the base for the next `debt-delta` calculation. Because `accrue()` has no access control (`try! (check-caller-auth)` is notably absent from `accrue`, unlike `system-borrow`/`system-repay`), any unprivileged principal (or their own deployed contract) can call it as frequently as desired — even every block — to keep every individual `debt-delta` below the rounding threshold, permanently preventing the treasury (`.dao-treasury`) from ever minting its `treasury-lp` share of interest for those slivers of accrued interest.

### Impact Explanation
Every time `reserve-inc` rounds to `0`, the DAO treasury permanently loses the reserve-factor's cut of that interest increment — the same yield instead accrues undivided to all `zft` shareholders instead of being partially routed to the treasury via `ft-mint? zft treasury-lp .dao-treasury`. By repeatedly and deliberately invoking the permissionless `accrue()` function, an attacker (or any actor, without needing DAO compromise) can systematically suppress essentially all treasury/protocol fee revenue from interest accrual across the lifetime of a vault. This is theft of unclaimed yield/royalties belonging to the protocol (the DAO treasury), which falls into the in-scope **High** impact category ("theft of unclaimed yield or royalties, permanent freezing of unclaimed yield or royalties").

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high: `accrue()` is public, requires no special permission or capital, and can be called at negligible cost (a public read/write call) as frequently as once per block. The rounding threshold is trivially small (a handful of raw token units, since `fee-reserve` is bounded by `BPS`), so keeping `debt-delta` below it only requires calling `accrue()` at a normal cadence relative to the vault's outstanding debt and interest rate — no unusual market conditions or large capital are needed. The exact same flaw is replicated identically across all six vault contracts.

### Recommendation
Do not reset the "billing" basis for the reserve fee on every `accrue()` call independent of whether a fee was actually minted. Instead, accumulate the un-billed remainder (e.g., `debt-delta - reserve-inc * BPS / fee-reserve`, or track a running "unbilled interest" accumulator) across calls so that fractional/rounded-down amounts are not permanently lost, and/or compute the reserve share using higher precision (e.g., compute in `INDEX-PRECISION` units before dividing by `BPS`) so that truncation to zero only occurs for genuinely negligible dust rather than for adversarially-fragmented calls. Additionally, consider rate-limiting or restricting the caller cadence of `accrue()`, or asserting a minimum time delta between accruals, to make griefing harder.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy/observe a Zest vault (e.g. `v0-vault-usdc.clar`) with an active borrow position so `principal-scaled > 0` and `fee-reserve > 0` (e.g. `fee-reserve = 1000` → 10%).
2. An attacker (unprivileged principal, or a bot contract) calls the public `accrue()` function every block (or as often as the chain allows) instead of allowing natural, infrequent invocation (e.g., once per user interaction over hours).
3. On each call, `debt-delta = new-debt - old-debt` reflects only one block's worth of interest, which for typical position sizes/rates is a very small raw-unit amount (well under `BPS / fee-reserve`, e.g. under 10 units).
4. `reserve-inc = mul-div-down(debt-delta, fee-reserve, BPS)` truncates to `0` on every call because `debt-delta * fee-reserve < BPS`.
5. `treasury-lp` mint is skipped (`if (> reserve-inc u0) ... u0`), and `index`/`last-update` still advance, discarding the opportunity to ever bill that interest increment to the treasury.
6. Repeating this every block over the life of the position causes the treasury to receive zero (or drastically reduced) `treasury-lp` shares compared to the fee-reserve percentage it is entitled to, while lenders (`zft` holders) receive the full undivided yield — a systematic, engineered denial of the protocol's designed fee revenue.
