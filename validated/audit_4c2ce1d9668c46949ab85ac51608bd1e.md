### Title
Missing slippage protection in ExchangeInjectActuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator lets sandwiched exchange creators lose funds - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java, actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java)

### Summary
The Aloe BoostManager finding flags that `increaseLiquidity`/`decreaseLiquidity` hard-code `amount0Min`/`amount1Min` to zero, leaving liquidity add/remove ops unprotected from price movement between submission and execution (classic sandwich attack). Java-tron's TRC10 AMM-style `Exchange*` actuators show the identical class of bug: `ExchangeTransactionContract` (a swap) carries an `expected` field that is checked in `ExchangeTransactionActuator.doValidate()` — but `ExchangeInjectContract` (liquidity add) and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` (liquidity remove) carry no such bound at all.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeInjectActuator` lets the exchange creator specify `token_id`/`quant` for one side of the pool; the actuator computes the required amount of the other token purely from the *current* on-chain pool ratio at execution time and unconditionally debits it from the creator's balance: [2](#0-1) 
There is no user-supplied maximum for `anotherTokenQuant`, so the creator has no way to cap how much of the second asset they are willing to spend.

`ExchangeWithdrawActuator` has the mirror problem: the creator specifies `quant` of one token to withdraw, and the amount of the other token they *receive* is derived from the live pool ratio, again with no user-specified minimum: [3](#0-2) 

By contrast, `ExchangeTransactionActuator` (a normal swap) explicitly takes an `expected` minimum and reverts if the computed output is below it: [4](#0-3) 

Because any account can move the pool ratio in the same block window by issuing an `ExchangeTransactionContract` swap immediately before the creator's inject/withdraw transaction is packed into a block, an attacker can shift `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` right before the victim's inject/withdraw executes:
- On inject: the victim ends up paying far more of `anotherTokenID` than intended for the same `tokenQuant` injected.
- On withdraw: the victim ends up receiving far less of `anotherTokenID` than intended for the same `tokenQuant` withdrawn.

This is a sandwichable, single-block-reachable MEV primitive: the attacker's front-run swap and back-run reversal swap bracket the victim's inject/withdraw transaction, extracting value from the exchange creator's liquidity operation exactly as described in the Aloe `BoostManager` report.

### Impact Explanation
An exchange creator (a normal, unprivileged account — the account that created the TRC10 exchange pool) performing `ExchangeInjectContract` or `ExchangeWithdrawContract` operations can be sandwiched by any other account broadcasting ordinary `ExchangeTransactionContract` swaps around the victim's transaction, causing the victim to lose value (pay too much on inject, or receive too little on withdraw) with no way to bound the loss. This is a direct loss-of-funds condition for the affected account, matching the "Medium" severity of the analog report.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account can submit `ExchangeTransactionContract` swaps against the same `exchange_id` at will and control transaction ordering economics via standard MEV/fee-bumping techniques common on TRON; the exchange creator regularly needs to call inject/withdraw to manage liquidity, so the attack surface is continuously exposed whenever such a transaction is pending.

### Recommendation
Add explicit minimum/maximum bound fields to `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` (analogous to the `expected` field already present on `ExchangeTransactionContract`), and enforce them in `ExchangeInjectActuator.execute()`/`doValidate()` and `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()`/`doValidate()` — e.g. reject the inject if the computed `anotherTokenQuant` exceeds a caller-supplied max, and reject the withdraw if the computed `anotherTokenQuant` received is below a caller-supplied min.

### Proof of Concept
1. Exchange creator broadcasts `ExchangeInjectContract` for `exchange_id=X`, `token_id=A`, `quant=Q`, expecting to also spend `anotherTokenQuant` of token B computed from the current pool ratio `secondTokenBalance/firstTokenBalance` [5](#0-4) .
2. Attacker observes the pending transaction and broadcasts an `ExchangeTransactionContract` swap on the same exchange in the same block window that shifts `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` unfavorably for the victim (e.g. buys a large amount of token A, reducing `firstTokenBalance` and inflating the ratio).
3. The victim's `ExchangeInjectActuator.execute()` then computes a much larger `anotherTokenQuant` than the creator anticipated and reduces that full amount from the creator's balance via `accountCapsule.reduceAssetAmountV2` [6](#0-5) , with no revert since there is no min/max check.
4. Attacker optionally reverses their swap afterward to restore the pool ratio and keep the excess extracted from the victim's inject (or, for withdraw, keep the reduced amount owed to the victim). Because neither `ExchangeInjectContract` nor `ExchangeWithdrawContract` expose a bound field, the victim has no on-chain mechanism to prevent this loss.
