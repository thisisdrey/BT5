Audit Report

## Title
XCM fee refund re-quotes the AMM swap rate instead of reusing the rate actually paid, enabling value extraction from the liquidity pool - (File: cumulus/primitives/utility/src/lib.rs)

## Summary
`SwapFirstAssetTrader::buy_weight` converts a user's fee asset into `Target` at whatever rate the `pallet-asset-conversion` pool quotes at that moment, recording only the resulting `total_fee` (in `Target`) and `last_fee_asset`, with no record of the effective exchange rate paid. `refund_weight` later re-converts the unused portion of `total_fee` back into the original asset via a brand-new `SwapCredit::swap_exact_tokens_for_tokens` call with no minimum-output bound (`None`), pricing the refund against the pool's state at that later point instead of the rate captured during `buy_weight`. This is confirmed live code, used in production XCM configs such as `cumulus/parachains/runtimes/testing/penpal/src/xcm_config.rs` and `substrate/frame/staking-async/runtimes/parachain/src/xcm_config.rs`, and historically Asset Hub Rococo/Westend (`prdoc/stable2506/pr_8376.prdoc` documents it replacing `TakeFirstAssetTrader` there).

## Finding Description
`buy_weight` performs `SwapCredit::swap_tokens_for_exact_tokens(vec![swap_asset, Target::get()], credit_in, fee)`, storing only the resulting `Target` amount in `self.total_fee` and the asset id in `self.last_fee_asset` — no rate or per-unit-cost is snapshotted. [1](#0-0) 

`refund_weight` computes `refund_amount` in `Target` terms via the weight-to-fee formula (correctly proportional to unused weight), extracts it from `total_fee`, and then calls `SwapCredit::swap_exact_tokens_for_tokens(vec![Target::get(), refund_swap_asset], refund, None)` — a fresh AMM quote with **no slippage/minimum-output protection** and no reference to the rate paid in `buy_weight`. [2](#0-1) 

Because `Target::get()` is a fixed weight-to-fee-denominated amount that is decoupled from the AMM's floating price of `Target` versus the fee asset, and because the same XCM program executing `BuyExecution` and `RefundSurplus` can also contain an `ExchangeAsset` instruction trading against the identical pool (confirmed in Penpal's config, where `AssetExchanger = PoolAssetsExchanger` uses the same `crate::AssetConversion` pallet as `SwapFirstAssetTrader`'s `SwapCredit`), an unprivileged sender fully controls the pool state between the buy-time quote and the refund-time quote within a single atomic message. By shifting the pool price of `Target` upward (in fee-asset terms) between the `BuyExecution` and the implicit/explicit refund, the refund leg converts the unused `Target` back into the fee asset at a more favorable rate than was paid, extracting the difference from the pool's reserves. [3](#0-2) 

## Impact Explanation
The underlying `pallet-asset-conversion` pool holds liquidity-provider funds and other users' in-flight assets. The refund's use of a freshly re-quoted price with `None` minimum output, decoupled from the rate actually charged in `buy_weight`, allows value to be settled to the message sender at an amount inconsistent with what was actually paid — a cost-correctness violation against the shared pool, matching the "public underpriced work" / value-extraction category of the impact gate.

## Likelihood Explanation
No privileged role is required: a normal, unprivileged account can construct a self-contained XCM program (`ExchangeAsset` + `BuyExecution` + `RefundSurplus`) targeting any chain that uses `SwapFirstAssetTrader`, such as the Penpal test-parachain configuration or the staking-async parachain runtime. Profitability is bounded by pool depth and LP fees (the attacker must self-fund the price-moving trade and pay its fee), so it is more exploitable against shallow pools or when large weight/fee amounts are used, but the exploit path itself requires no collusion, leaked keys, or governance access.

## Recommendation
Do not re-quote the swap rate at refund time. Either record the effective per-`Target`-unit rate obtained in `buy_weight` and compute the refund pro-rata from that stored rate instead of issuing a fresh `SwapCredit::swap_exact_tokens_for_tokens` call, or enforce a minimum-output/maximum-slippage bound in `refund_weight` tied to the buy-time rate so that refunds deviating materially from the pro-rata share of the original conversion are rejected rather than settled via `None`.

## Proof of Concept
1. Deploy/target a chain using `SwapFirstAssetTrader` as (part of) its `Trader`, with an `AssetExchanger` backed by the same `pallet-asset-conversion` pool (e.g., Penpal's `XcmConfig`).
2. Submit an XCM program from an unprivileged account:
   - `BuyExecution` with a large `weight_limit`, causing `buy_weight` to swap fee-asset `X` into `Target` at pool rate P1, recording `total_fee` (in `Target`) and `last_fee_asset = X`.
   - `ExchangeAsset` trading `X` for `Target` in the same pool, shifting the pool price so that `Target` becomes more valuable in `X` terms.
   - A low-weight instruction sequence, followed by `RefundSurplus`, triggering `refund_weight`, which converts most of `total_fee` back into `X` via `swap_exact_tokens_for_tokens(..., None)` at the now-shifted rate P2.
3. Verify (via a unit/integration test extending `cumulus/primitives/utility/src/tests/swap_first.rs` with a mock AMM pool) that the `X` amount refunded to the sender, net of the `ExchangeAsset` trade's LP fees, exceeds the pro-rata share of `X` originally spent for the corresponding unused weight — demonstrating value drained from pool reserves beyond what the sender contributed.
