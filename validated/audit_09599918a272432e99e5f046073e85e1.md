### Title
Attacker can freeze a victim's debt position at `debtFloorInUsd` via `DebtToken.repay(onBehalfOf_)`, making it permanently non-liquidatable - (File: contracts/DebtToken.sol)

### Summary
`DebtToken.repay` accepts an arbitrary `onBehalfOf_` beneficiary, and both `repay` (DebtToken.sol:448) and `Pool.liquidate` (Pool.sol:571-578) forbid any repayment that would leave remaining debt strictly between `0` and `debtFloorInUsd`. Combined with `maxLiquidable` (50%) in `Pool.liquidate`, an unprivileged attacker can repay a victim's debt down to exactly the floor value, after which every non-dust liquidation attempt reverts with `RemainingDebtIsLowerThanTheFloor`. The position is stuck in a non-liquidatable state until interest accrual slowly lifts the debt above the floor — a "stuck, requires manual recovery" analog of the Zigbee router DoS.

### Finding Description
`repay(address onBehalfOf_, uint256 amount_)` burns synth from the caller and reduces the victim's debt; its floor check only rejects remaining debt `> 0 && < debtFloorInUsd`, so repaying down to *exactly* `debtFloorInUsd` is allowed (DebtToken.sol:442-451). In `Pool.liquidate`, `amountToRepay_` must satisfy `amountToRepay_ ≤ maxLiquidable * debt` and `debt - amountToRepay_ ≥ debtFloorInUsd or == 0` (Pool.sol:565-579). Once `debt == debtFloorInUsd`, any `amountToRepay_ > 0` leaves `< debtFloorInUsd` and reverts. `repayAll` would clear the debt but is voluntary for the victim's counterparties; a liquidator cannot use it, and the victim has no incentive to repay an already-underwater position.

### Impact Explanation
Positions pushed to exactly the floor cannot be liquidated, so if collateral value continues falling the protocol accrues bad debt with no recovery path other than waiting for interest accrual to push debt above the floor or governance intervention. Effect: temporary freezing of the liquidation pathway and protocol insolvency risk capped per-position near `debtFloorInUsd` plus subsequent accrual.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires `debtFloorInUsd > 0` (deployed config-dependent) and costs the attacker `victimDebt - debtFloorInUsd` in synth tokens, since repayment benefits the victim. The cost bounds the attack to positions where driving them to the floor is cheap or where the attacker's gain (e.g., blocking liquidations during a price move) justifies it. No privileged role, malicious oracle, or endpoint is needed — only public `DebtToken.repay`.

### Recommendation
Track per-account last-repayer or disallow repayments to third parties that bring debt to the exact floor boundary; alternatively, allow `liquidate` to perform a full repay (bypassing `maxLiquidable`) when the remaining debt would otherwise fall below the floor, so dust-sized positions can be fully closed.

### Proof of Concept
Hardhat sketch: deploy Pool with `debtFloorInUsd = 1000 USD`. Victim deposits collateral and issues 5000 msUSD. Price drops so position is unhealthy. Attacker calls `debtToken.repay(victim, 4000e18)` — remaining debt = 1000 USD = floor, tx succeeds. Any `pool.liquidate(msUSD, victim, amountToRepay_, msdToken)` with `amountToRepay_ > 0` now reverts `RemainingDebtIsLowerThanTheFloor`, and `amountToRepay_ == 0` reverts `AmountIsZero`. Position is frozen until accrued interest raises debt above the floor.

Note: validity depends on `debtFloorInUsd > 0` in the deployed configuration and whether the attacker-cost vs. freeze-impact trade-off is considered in scope; if governance sets the floor to 0, the attack does not apply.
