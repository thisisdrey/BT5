### Title
Rewards emitted while a rewarded token has zero supply are permanently lost - (File: contracts/RewardsDistributor.sol)

### Summary
`RewardsDistributor` advances a token’s reward timestamp even when its `totalSupply()` is zero and therefore accrues a zero reward index delta for that interval. [1](#0-0)  The elapsed emissions are not tracked as pending rewards, so they remain contract balance without becoming claimable by any account. [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`updateBeforeMintOrBurn()` is permissionless and calls `_updateTokenIndex()` whenever a reward state exists. [3](#0-2)  `_calculateTokenIndex()` computes `delta * speed` but sets the ratio to zero when `token_.totalSupply() == 0`, while still returning the current timestamp. [1](#0-0)  `_updateTokenIndex()` persists that timestamp, permanently discarding the interval’s accrued amount rather than deferring or rolling it over. [4](#0-3) 

This condition is reachable during the initial bootstrapping of a rewarded `DepositToken`, after all holders exit, or through the public `updateBeforeMintOrBurn()` entry point itself. [3](#0-2)  A normal public deposit calls `DepositToken.deposit()` and mints the deposit position, with reward indexes updated before the balance change. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation
Rewards emitted during zero-supply intervals become undistributable protocol value. [7](#0-6)  Later suppliers only receive rewards beginning after the timestamp is advanced, while the skipped amount remains in the distributor because claims only transfer amounts recorded in `tokensAccruedOf`. [2](#0-1)  This permanently freezes unclaimed yield unless the implementation is upgraded or funds are recovered outside the shown payout path. [8](#0-7) 

### Likelihood Explanation
The trigger requires only a nonzero `tokenSpeed`, an initialized token state, and zero token supply for a nonzero interval. [1](#0-0)  The state-update function has no caller restriction and can be invoked before the first deposit, while the next real deposit also updates the index before minting. [3](#0-2)  `DepositToken.deposit()` is public and its `whenNotPaused`, `nonReentrant`, and token-existence checks do not prevent the zero-supply accounting case. [9](#0-8) 

### Recommendation
Do not permanently consume an emission interval when `totalSupply == 0`. [7](#0-6)  Either keep the previous timestamp until supply exists, or add an explicit `undistributedRewards`/`pendingRewards` accounting bucket and roll that amount into a later reward period according to the intended emissions policy. [4](#0-3)  Add a regression test that activates rewards at zero supply, advances time, performs the first deposit, advances another interval, and verifies that no emitted amount silently disappears. [5](#0-4) 

### Proof of Concept
1. Configure a production-registered `DepositToken` with `tokenSpeed = 10 reward tokens/second`; its initialized `TokenState` starts at `INITIAL_INDEX` and the current timestamp. [10](#0-9) 
2. Fund `RewardsDistributor` with `200` reward tokens while `depositToken.totalSupply() == 0`. [2](#0-1) 
3. Advance ten seconds and call `updateBeforeMintOrBurn(depositToken, attacker)`; `tokensAccrued = 100`, but `ratio = 0`, so only the timestamp advances. [1](#0-0) [4](#0-3) 
4. Deposit through `depositToken.deposit(amount, attacker)`; the same timestamp produces no additional index delta before minting. [5](#0-4) 
5. Advance another ten seconds and call `claimRewards(attacker)`; the attacker receives only `100` rewards, while the first interval’s `100` rewards remain unaccrued and undistributed. [11](#0-10)
