### Title
Stale accrue() applies a single point-in-time interest rate/utilization to an entire multi-block gap, letting a utilization spike be amplified over the whole elapsed period - ([File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar])

### Summary
Each vault's `accrue`/`next-index`/`next-liquidity-index` functions compute `time-delta = stacks-block-time - last-update` and apply a *single* interest-rate sample (taken from the utilization at the moment `accrue` finally executes) linearly across that entire elapsed period. If `accrue` has not been triggered for a while (low-traffic vault, quiet period, or simply waiting a block/period), a caller who manipulates utilization immediately before triggering `accrue` causes that one manipulated rate to be multiplied by the whole stale `time-delta`, exactly mirroring the reported Lido-hurdle-rate bug class: "timing of updates is not considered, so a delayed/compounded update sample gets applied disproportionately."

### Finding Description
`next-index` and `next-liquidity-index` read `(interest-rate)`, which is derived from `(utilization)` computed from the vault's *current* `total-debt`/`available-assets` at call time, and then apply that single rate to the full `time-delta` since `last-update`: [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

The same pattern of minting `treasury-lp` based on `debt-delta` computed from `old-debt`/`new-debt` (i.e., the manipulated-rate-driven index jump) is used to mint protocol fee shares to `dao-treasury`, and this reserve/fee amount is likewise derived from the same stale-period, single-sample rate: [3](#0-2) 

Because `utilization` is read live at the moment `accrue` executes (not integrated across the elapsed window), any principal who can spike utilization (e.g., via a large borrow, possibly funded by a flashloan used purely as capital) immediately before an action that triggers `accrue` after a long idle `time-delta` causes the elevated rate to be charged/credited over the *entire* stale window rather than just the instant of manipulation. Since liquidity index (supplier yield) and borrow index (borrower debt) are both computed from this same single-sample rate, a supplier holding vault shares before triggering this accrual captures an outsized share of interest extracted from all outstanding debt across that window, while borrowers are charged disproportionately for a rate that existed only momentarily.

This exactly matches the report's core defect: "the system does not take into account the timing of updates... no time aspects being considered during the [rate] calculation," except here it is exploitable on-demand by any principal rather than merely a risk from an offchain bot's downtime.

### Impact Explanation
This allows theft of unclaimed yield: an attacker who holds/acquires vault shares, then manipulates utilization and forces `accrue` to fire after an artificially stale `last-update`, extracts interest computed over the whole elapsed window at the manipulated rate rather than the "fair" integrated rate, at the expense of borrowers (excess debt charged) and/or other suppliers/treasury (dilution of the correct reserve split). This falls under the in-scope High-impact category: theft of unclaimed yield.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood depends on finding vault windows where `accrue` has not been triggered for a meaningful period (e.g., low-traffic vault types such as `usdh`/`ststxbtc`, or simply timing an action after a dormant interval) combined with the ability to spike utilization just before the triggering call, capital for which can legitimately come from a flashloan used purely to fund the position. This requires no privileged access and no DAO compromise - only ordinary market entry points (borrow/supply/withdraw) that call into `accrue` via the vault's system-* functions reached through the market contract.

### Recommendation
Replace the single point-in-time rate sample with either (a) accruing more frequently / forcing `accrue` at fixed intervals regardless of caller activity, or (b) integrating the rate over sub-periods (e.g., splitting `time-delta` at utilization-affecting events) so that a rate manipulated only momentarily cannot be scaled across an entire stale window. At minimum, cap the multiplier's effective `time-delta` contribution per discrete rate sample, or checkpoint utilization changes so `accrue` is forced whenever utilization changes materially.

### Proof of Concept
1. Let a given vault go without any `accrue`-triggering call for a nontrivial `time-delta` (e.g., due to low activity).
2. Attacker (holding/acquiring vault shares beforehand, or acting as first party to touch the vault) executes a large borrow — potentially flashloan-funded — that spikes `utilization` sharply.
3. In the same transaction, the attacker triggers any vault operation that calls `accrue`; `next-index`/`next-liquidity-index` read the now-spiked `utilization`/`interest-rate` and apply it via `calc-multiplier-delta` across the entire stale `time-delta`.
4. `debt-delta` jumps disproportionately; `reserve-inc`/`treasury-lp` and the liquidity index both reflect this one-sample rate over the whole window, crediting excess yield to current shareholders (including the attacker) at borrowers' expense.
5. Attacker unwinds the utilization-spiking position (e.g., repays the flashloan) after capturing the disproportionate share of interest, leaving borrowers overcharged for interest attributable to a rate that existed only for the manipulation instant. [4](#0-3) [2](#0-1)
