### Title
Rebasing collateral rewards become permanently unclaimable in Treasury - (File: contracts/DepositToken.sol)

### Summary

`DepositToken` mints fixed `msdTOKEN` balances based on the collateral amount received, while all underlying collateral remains pooled in `Treasury` [1](#0-0) . A positive rebase increases `Treasury`’s underlying balance without increasing `DepositToken.totalSupply()`, but withdrawals only permit burning existing `msdTOKEN` and pulling the corresponding fixed underlying amount [2](#0-1) . Consequently, `underlying.balanceOf(treasury) - depositToken.totalSupply()` accrues as a surplus that no depositor can claim [3](#0-2) .

### Finding Description

`DepositToken.deposit()` correctly uses a balance delta when receiving collateral, so fee-on-transfer and rebasing deposits do not over-mint at deposit time [4](#0-3) . However, the credited amount is only the delta observed during that transaction; later positive rebases do not increase `balanceOf`, `totalSupply`, or any per-user claim [5](#0-4) .

Withdrawals are strictly receipt-based: `_withdraw()` calculates the amount from the burned `msdTOKEN`, burns `_withdrawn`, and calls `Treasury.pull(to_, _withdrawn)` [6](#0-5) . `Treasury.pull()` accepts calls only from registered deposit tokens and transfers exactly the requested underlying amount [3](#0-2) .

There is no accounting variable or pro-rata mechanism representing rebasing rewards attributable to depositors [2](#0-1) . `Treasury` also does not inherit `TokenHolder`, so it has no generic `sweep()` recovery function for surplus collateral [7](#0-6) . Its `claimFromVesper()` path is restricted to governor-initiated Vesper reward claims and only subtracts collateral supply for tokens returned by that external pool’s reward-token list [8](#0-7) .

### Impact Explanation

All positive rebase yield accruing to collateral held by `Treasury` becomes excess backing rather than depositor-owned balance [5](#0-4) . Even after every depositor withdraws the full `msdTOKEN` supply, the rebased surplus remains in `Treasury`, because `totalSupply == 0` leaves no registered deposit token capable of pulling it [3](#0-2) .

This is permanent freezing or theft of unclaimed yield unless governance performs a contract migration or upgrade [9](#0-8) . A negative rebase creates the reciprocal problem: fixed `msdTOKEN` claims remain unchanged while Treasury backing decreases, potentially leaving the last withdrawers unable to redeem in full [2](#0-1) .

### Likelihood Explanation

The issue requires a rebasing ERC20 to be configured as a `DepositToken` underlying; the implementation accepts an arbitrary ERC20 and does not reject or specially account for balance-changing tokens [10](#0-9) . Once configured, no malicious privileged action is needed because ordinary token rebases alter `Treasury.balanceOf()` independently of the protocol [11](#0-10) .

The invariant `underlying.balanceOf(treasury) >= depositToken.totalSupply()` may hold after positive rebases, but there is no corresponding claimable-share invariant for the excess [12](#0-11) .

### Recommendation

Do not support rebasing collateral directly, or wrap it in a non-rebasing share token before accepting it as underlying. If support is required, `DepositToken` should mint shares rather than fixed underlying units, or track each depositor’s proportional claim on `underlying.balanceOf(treasury)` so rebase gains and losses are distributed pro-rata. Treasury should additionally expose an explicit, bounded mechanism for distributing or recovering only `underlying.balanceOf(treasury) - totalSupply`, rather than relying on unrelated Vesper reward claiming or migration [8](#0-7) .

### Proof of Concept

The following Foundry-style test demonstrates the invariant break with a configured rebasing collateral and zero deposit/withdrawal fees:

```solidity
function test_RebasingCollateralYieldIsStuck() public {
    uint256 depositAmount = 100 ether;

    rebaseToken.mint(alice, depositAmount);

    vm.startPrank(alice);
    rebaseToken.approve(address(depositToken), depositAmount);
    depositToken.deposit(depositAmount, alice);
    vm.stopPrank();

    assertEq(depositToken.totalSupply(), depositAmount);
    assertEq(rebaseToken.balanceOf(address(treasury)), depositAmount);

    // Trigger the underlying token's ordinary positive rebase.
    rebaseToken.rebalanceAccount(address(treasury), depositAmount + 5 ether);

    assertEq(rebaseToken.balanceOf(address(treasury)), 105 ether);
    assertEq(depositToken.totalSupply(), 100 ether);

    vm.prank(alice);
    depositToken.withdraw(100 ether, alice);

    assertEq(depositToken.totalSupply(), 0);
    assertEq(aliceReceived, 100 ether);

    // No deposit-token supply remains, so Treasury.pull() can no longer be reached.
    assertEq(rebaseToken.balanceOf(address(treasury)), 5 ether);
}
```

The decisive assertion is that a full withdrawal of all `msdTOKEN` leaves the five-token rebase reward in `Treasury`, while `pull()` can only be invoked by a registered deposit token during a burn [3](#0-2) .
