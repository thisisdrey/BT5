## Title
Smart contract owner can front-run pending `TriggerSmartContract` calls by changing `consume_user_resource_percent` via `UpdateSettingContractActuator` - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java`)

### Summary
A deployed contract's `consume_user_resource_percent` field controls how much of the energy cost of a call is paid by the caller versus the contract's owner (via frozen energy). This value can be changed by the contract owner at any time through `UpdateSettingContractActuator`, with no timelock, cooldown, or restriction on frequency, and it is read live at transaction-execution time inside the block-producing loop rather than at broadcast time. This allows the owner to front-run a victim's already-broadcast `TriggerSmartContract` transaction (in the mempool) by raising `consume_user_resource_percent` to 100 immediately before it, shifting the entire energy cost onto the caller and causing them to pay/burn more TRX than they intended when they set their `fee_limit`.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` directly overwrites the `consume_user_resource_percent` field on the target `ContractCapsule` with no delay or restriction beyond an owner check: [1](#0-0) 

`validate()` only checks that the caller is the contract's `originAddress` and that the percent is within `[0,100]`; there is no rate limit or restriction preventing the owner from changing it in every block: [2](#0-1) 

At execution time (during block packing, not at broadcast/validation time of the victim's own transaction), `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio` fetches the *live* `consumeUserResourcePercent` from the `ContractCapsule` and computes how much energy the caller vs. the contract owner ("creator") must supply for the call: [3](#0-2) 

Because transactions are processed and packed by `Manager.generateBlock()` sequentially from the pending pool (ordered/sorted by fee), an attacker (the contract owner) who observes a pending `TriggerSmartContract` call to their contract can broadcast an `UpdateSettingContract` transaction with a higher `consume_user_resource_percent` (up to 100) and have it processed before the victim's transaction within the same block-production window: [4](#0-3) 

This mirrors the reported bug class exactly: a party controlling a shared resource (a market/marketplace owner setting fees; here, a contract owner setting the resource-split percentage) can race a victim's pending transaction and change economic parameters that the victim's transaction relies on, without the victim's consent, causing the victim to pay costs they did not agree to when signing/broadcasting their transaction.

### Impact Explanation
When `consume_user_resource_percent` is raised (e.g., to 100) right before a victim's `TriggerSmartContract` executes, the contract's frozen/origin energy no longer subsidizes the call; the caller must supply all the energy for the call from their own bandwidth/TRX. If the caller's `fee_limit` was set assuming a lower percentage (i.e., expecting substantial subsidy from the contract owner), the caller may unexpectedly burn significantly more TRX for the same call, or the call may run out of energy and revert while still consuming (burning) the TRX fee that was expended for the energy actually used. This is a direct, unconsented financial loss to the caller triggered purely by the contract owner's front-run action, analogous to the lender unexpectedly paying a higher marketplace fee in the referenced Teller report.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account that deploys a contract is, by definition, unprivileged with respect to the rest of the network (no committee/witness role required) and can call `UpdateSettingContract` at will. Observing pending transactions destined for one's own contract in the mempool and front-running with a resource-percent change requires no special access — it is directly reachable by any contract owner, matching the "unprivileged actor" scope required.

### Recommendation
Introduce a delay (e.g., effective only after N blocks, similar to how governance parameter proposals only take effect at the next maintenance cycle) before a `consume_user_resource_percent` (and `origin_energy_limit`, which has the analogous issue in `UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator`) change takes effect, so pending transactions that were broadcast prior to the change are executed against the previous value. Alternatively, snapshot/commit the percent value used for a transaction at the time the transaction enters the mempool rather than re-reading it live at execution/packing time.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker deploys `ContractX` with `consume_user_resource_percent = 10` and freezes enough energy so most calls are heavily subsidized.
2. Victim observes low resource cost for calling `ContractX`, sets `fee_limit` accordingly, signs and broadcasts a `TriggerSmartContract` transaction calling `ContractX`.
3. Attacker sees the victim's transaction in the mempool, immediately broadcasts an `UpdateSettingContract` transaction (as the contract's `originAddress`) setting `consume_user_resource_percent = 100`, using a higher priority/fee so it is packed first: [5](#0-4) 
4. When the block producer processes the victim's transaction afterward, `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio` now computes `creatorEnergyLimit = 0` (since `consumeUserResourcePercent >= ONE_HUNDRED` skips the creator-subsidy branch) — see the branch logic at [6](#0-5)  — forcing the victim to pay for all energy from their own `fee_limit`, resulting in unexpected TRX burn or an out-of-energy revert that still consumes the victim's fee.
