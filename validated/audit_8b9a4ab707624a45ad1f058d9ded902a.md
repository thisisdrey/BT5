### Title
Partial liquidation using the graduated liquidation formula can push a position back below `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL`, delaying required re-liquidation - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`)

### Summary
The `liquidate` function's graduated liquidation math computes the maximum repayable debt (`max-debt-usd`) strictly from *how far current LTV is past* `ltv-liq-partial`, but the actual collateral removed includes the liquidation penalty on top of the debt repaid. Because collateral is always removed at a higher proportional rate than debt is reduced, even a liquidation sized exactly at the formula's own allowed maximum for a position barely past the partial threshold drives the position's post-liquidation LTV below `ltv-liq-partial` — making the follow-up `liquidate` call fail with `ERR-HEALTHY` until the collateral price drops further. There is also no restriction stopping the borrower itself from calling `liquidate` against its own position, so this can be used as a cheap self-liquidation trick to reset the liquidation buffer.

### Finding Description
`liquidate` computes `current-ltv` and requires `current-ltv >= ltv-liq-partial` to proceed: [1](#0-0) 

The liquidation percentage/penalty and the resulting maximum debt eligible for repayment are derived purely from the *distance* between `current-ltv` and `ltv-liq-partial` (relative to `ltv-liq-full`): [2](#0-1) 

`max-debt-usd`, penalty, and the debt/collateral amounts actually moved are computed from this factor and applied: [3](#0-2) 

Because the collateral seized equals `debt_repaid × (1 + liq_penalty)` while the debt reduced equals only `debt_repaid`, collateral value always drops by a strictly larger USD amount than debt drops. For a position sitting only marginally above `ltv-liq-partial`, running the maximum allowed liquidation at that point (the formula intentionally produces only a tiny `liq-pct-scaled`/`max-debt-usd` there) removes a proportionally larger slice of collateral than debt, which mechanically drags LTV back under `ltv-liq-partial`. Concretely, with `ltv-liq-partial = 8500`, `ltv-liq-full = 9500`, `liq-penalty-min = 500` (5%), a position at collateral = $100,000 / debt = $85,010 (LTV = 8501 bps, i.e. just unsafe):
- `liq-pct-scaled` ≈ 10 bps (0.1%), `max-debt-usd` ≈ $85.01, `liq-penalty` ≈ 500 bps.
- Liquidating the max allowed $85.01 debt removes `85.01 × 1.05 ≈ $89.26` of collateral.
- New state: debt ≈ $84,924.99, collateral ≈ $99,910.74 → LTV ≈ 8499.7 bps, which is **below** `ltv-liq-partial` (8500).

The position is now reported "healthy" by the same check that gated the liquidation that was just performed, even though the liquidator only took the maximum amount the protocol's own formula permitted. No further liquidation is possible until the price drops enough to push LTV back above 8500 bps again — an artificial extra buffer created purely by the mechanics of the partial-liquidation math, not by any real improvement in collateralization intent.

`liquidate` does not restrict the caller from being the borrower itself (only same-block-borrow and pause/slippage checks are enforced): [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

This allows a borrower to self-trigger this minimal liquidation on their own position (paying only the small penalty on a sliver of debt) to reset the "unsafe" flag instead of legitimately adding collateral or repaying debt in full, exactly mirroring the referenced CDPVault self-liquidation griefing pattern.

### Impact Explanation
This delays legitimate liquidation of an under-collateralized position during continued price decline, extending the window where the position remains under-margined relative to what timely liquidation would have achieved. If the price continues to fall before the LTV climbs back above `ltv-liq-partial`, the eventual liquidation/bad-debt-socialization event (see `socialize-debt-asset` path triggered when `no-collateral-left`) hits with a larger shortfall than would have occurred with prompt liquidation, temporarily exposing lender funds to increased under-collateralization risk and delayed recovery — a temporary freezing/impairment of funds risk to depositors of the borrowed asset.

### Likelihood Explanation
This triggers deterministically any time a position's LTV sits only slightly above `ltv-liq-partial` (a routine, frequent state for borrowers near the liquidation boundary), and no privileged access or governance action is required — any liquidator (including the borrower itself) using amounts within the protocol's own computed `max-debt-usd` can reproduce it.

### Recommendation
Size the graduated liquidation so that the *post-liquidation* LTV is targeted to land at (or just above) `ltv-liq-partial` rather than bounding only pre-liquidation `max-debt-usd`/penalty by the pre-liquidation LTV distance — i.e., solve for `debt_repaid` such that `(debt_usd - debt_repaid) / (collateral_usd - debt_repaid*(1+penalty)) >= ltv-liq-partial` remains true (or accept it may cross to full liquidation), and/or re-validate whether the position is still liquidatable using the "before" state penalty-adjusted math rather than allowing a single call to silently produce a post-state below the trigger threshold.

### Proof of Concept
1. Configure an egroup with `LTV-LIQ-PARTIAL = 8500`, `LTV-LIQ-FULL = 9500`, `LIQ-PENALTY-MIN = 500`.
2. Borrower deposits collateral worth $100,000 and borrows debt worth $85,010 (LTV = 8501 bps, passes the `>= ltv-liq-partial` check in `liquidate`) — see health-check assertion at [6](#0-5) .
3. Anyone (including the borrower itself, since there is no borrower/liquidator address separation) calls `liquidate` with `debt-amount` set to the formula's own computed `max-debt-usd` (~$85.01 worth of debt tokens).
4. `calc-final-liquidation-amounts`/`scale-debt-for-liquidation` remove ~$89.26 of collateral for ~$85.01 of debt repaid, per [7](#0-6) .
5. Recompute LTV: now ≈ 8499.7 bps < 8500, so a subsequent `liquidate` call reverts with `ERR-HEALTHY` at the same check, even though the position's economic risk profile has barely changed.

;; Calculate liquidation factor: ((ltv-curr - ltv-liq-partial) * BPS) / (ltv-liq-full - ltv-liq-partial)
;; Capped at BPS (100%) to prevent over-liquidation
(define-private (calc-liq-factor (ltv-curr uint) (ltv-liq-partial uint) (ltv-liq-full uint))
  (min BPS (div-bps-down (- ltv-curr ltv-liq-partial) (- ltv-liq-full ltv-liq-partial))))

;; Apply curve exponent for graduated liquidation
;; liq-factor = liq-factor^alpha
(define-private (calc-liq-factor-exp (factor uint) (exp uint))
  (if (is-eq exp BPS) 
    factor
    (if (> exp BPS) 
        (/ (pow factor (/ exp BPS)) (pow BPS (- (/ exp BPS) u1)))
        (sqrti (* factor BPS))))) ;; assume factor^0.5

;; Scale penalty between min and max using liquidation factor
;; liq-penalty = liq-penalty-min + (liq-factor * (liq-penalty-max - liq-penalty-min) / BPS)
;; Capped at bound-max to handle cases where liq-factor > BPS
(define-private (calc-liq-factor-bound (liq-factor uint) (bound-min uint) (bound-max uint))
  (min bound-max (+ bound-min (mul-bps-down liq-factor (- bound-max bound-min)))))

;; Calculate debt to repay based on liquidation factor
;; debt-repay = liq-factor * debt / BPS
(define-private (calc-liq-debt-repay (debt uint) (liq-factor uint)) 
  (mul-bps-down liq-factor debt))

;; Calculate collateral to seize (includes liquidator bonus)
;; collateral-repay = debt-repay * (BPS + liq-penalty) / BPS
(define-private (calc-liq-collateral-repay (debt-repay uint) (liq-penalty uint)) 
  (mul-bps-down debt-repay (+ BPS liq-penalty)))

;; Calculate actual debt repayment when collateral is capped
;; debt-repay-real = (collateral-amount-usd * BPS) / (BPS + liq-penalty)
(define-private (calc-liq-debt-repay-real (collateral-amount-usd uint) (liq-penalty uint)) 
  (div-bps-down collateral-amount-usd (+ BPS liq-penalty)))
