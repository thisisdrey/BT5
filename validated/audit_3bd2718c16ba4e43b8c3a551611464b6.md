### Title
Dynamic Energy Factor uses asymmetric increase/decrease logic that lets any caller grief legitimate users of a contract with inflated energy costs - (File: `chainbase/src/main/java/org/tron/core/capsule/ContractStateCapsule.java`)

### Summary
The dynamic-energy-fee mechanism that scales a contract's per-cycle energy cost multiplier (`energyFactor`) is updated with the exact asymmetric pattern flagged in the external report: the factor is bumped up **immediately** (within the same cycle transition) once usage crosses a threshold, but is only reduced **gradually** over subsequent cycles. Because the trigger condition (`energyUsage > threshold`) is driven purely by aggregate TVM energy consumed against a contract in one cycle — something any unprivileged caller can generate by repeatedly invoking that contract — an attacker can spike the factor to `maxFactor` cheaply/quickly and keep every other caller of that contract paying inflated energy costs for many cycles while the value decays back down.

### Finding Description
`ContractStateCapsule.catchUpToCycle()` implements the update: [1](#0-0) 
increases `energyFactor` immediately by `increasePercent` (bounded by `maxFactor`) whenever `getEnergyUsage() > threshold` for the closed cycle, then: [2](#0-1) 
decays it only by a `decreasePercent` in the range `[75%, 100%]` per elapsed cycle — i.e. the factor can jump to its ceiling in one cycle but takes multiple cycles to return to baseline. This is structurally identical to the reported bug: `lastETWAPBaseSupply` (there) / `energyFactor` (here) is updated immediately in one direction and only relaxed slowly in the other, and the trigger (`energyUsage`) is fully attacker-controllable within a single cycle via ordinary, unprivileged transactions (repeated `TriggerSmartContractContract` calls against the target contract), analogous to the flash-loan mint/redeem round-trip used to spike `eTWAPBaseTokenSupply`.

The `energyUsage`/`energyFactor` state is read and updated on every VM invocation of a contract (`Program.java`, via `RepositoryImpl`), and the resulting factor is multiplied into the energy price charged to *all* callers of that contract for the following cycles, gated by governance-controlled parameters `getDynamicEnergyThreshold()`, `getDynamicEnergyIncreaseFactor()`, and `getDynamicEnergyMaxFactor()` in `DynamicPropertiesStore`. [3](#0-2) 

### Impact Explanation
Because the increase is immediate and driven by a threshold any unprivileged sender can exceed by issuing energy-heavy calls against a popular contract within a single cycle, an attacker can force `energyFactor` to `maxFactor`. Every legitimate caller of that contract then pays the inflated, factor-scaled energy price for as many cycles as it takes the exponential `decreasePercent` (`[75%,100%]` per cycle) to relax back to baseline — this can span many cycles depending on `DYNAMIC_ENERGY_DECREASE_DIVISION`/`increaseFactor` configuration, causing sustained over-charging / effective denial-of-service (failed/aborted calls for users whose energy budget no longer covers the inflated fee) against a specific, targetable contract. This mirrors the report's "several minutes of denied redemptions" impact, but here the effect is a network-reachable cost/DoS grief against any deployed contract, reachable purely by an unprivileged contract-calling transaction.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is Medium: the attacker needs no special privilege, only enough TRX/energy to push the target contract's cycle usage above `dynamicEnergyThreshold` — feasible for low/medium traffic contracts, and repeatable every cycle to keep the factor pinned near `maxFactor`. The dynamic energy feature must be enabled (`allowDynamicEnergy`), which is a chain-level toggle, not attacker-controlled, so this only applies on chains/mainnets where the feature is active.

### Recommendation
Apply symmetric update logic to `energyFactor` similar to the ETWAP fix recommendation: base the increase decision on a smoothed/windowed usage average (rather than a single cycle's raw usage), and/or bound how much of `maxFactor` a single cycle's spike can reach, so a short burst from a single attacker-controlled transaction stream cannot immediately saturate the multiplier while requiring many cycles to relax.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker selects a target contract with moderate energy usage and dynamic energy enabled.
2. Within one cycle, attacker submits (self-funded) `TriggerSmartContractContract` calls that push the contract's cumulative `getEnergyUsage()` above `dynamicEnergyThreshold` (see `ContractStateCapsule.catchUpToCycle`, lines 108-120).
3. At the next cycle boundary, `energyFactor` is bumped by `increasePercent`; attacker repeats every cycle to keep ratcheting the factor toward `dynamicEnergyMaxFactor`.
4. All subsequent legitimate callers of the contract are charged energy at the inflated `energyFactor` multiplier for multiple cycles afterward, since decrease is capped at `75%–100%` per cycle (lines 128-144), producing sustained increased cost / failed calls for legitimate users.
