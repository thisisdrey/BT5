## Analysis

The Zest v2 vault `deposit` functions mint shares and credit the internal `assets` accounting variable based on the caller-supplied `amount` parameter, without verifying that the actual underlying-token balance received by the vault matches that amount.

**Root cause** (e.g., `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar`):

`deposit` computes `inkind` (shares to mint) from `amount` via `convert-to-shares-preview`, then calls `receive-underlying`, mints `zft` shares equal to `inkind`, and unconditionally bumps `assets` by the full nominal `amount` — with no post-transfer balance check: [1](#0-0) 

`receive-underlying` performs a plain `transfer` call and trusts that the full `amount` arrived, with no reconciliation against the vault's actual token balance (`ubalance`): [2](#0-1) 

`total-assets`/`total-assets-preview`, which drive share pricing, `get-available-assets`, and withdrawal limits, are derived purely from the bookkeeping `assets` variable rather than the token's actual balance: [3](#0-2) 

Every other vault (`v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, `v0-vault-stx.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`, and their `local-testing/` mirrors) follows the identical pattern. [4](#0-3) 

### Title
Fee-on-transfer underlying tokens cause vault share/asset accounting insolvency - (File: `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar` and other `v0-vault-*.clar` files)

### Summary
Zest v2 vaults hardcode a specific `UNDERLYING` token contract per vault (e.g., `usdcx`, `ststx-token`, `ststxbtc-token`). `deposit` mints shares and increments the `assets` accounting variable using the nominal `amount` argument rather than the actual balance delta observed after `receive-underlying`'s transfer call.

### Finding Description
If the hardcoded underlying token ever applies a transfer fee — either because it already has one, or because it is upgraded later to add one (the exact USDT-style scenario called out in the referenced report) — a depositor sending `amount` will have only `amount - fee` actually land in the vault's balance, yet the vault will mint shares for the full `amount` and record `assets` as increased by the full `amount`. Because share pricing (`convert-to-shares-preview`/`convert-to-assets-preview`) and withdrawal availability (`get-available-assets`, `total-assets-preview`) are derived from this inflated `assets` bookkeeping value rather than a real balance check, the vault's tracked assets exceed its true underlying-token holdings. This mismatch persists and compounds with every subsequent fee-bearing deposit.

### Impact Explanation
The vault becomes under-collateralized relative to the shares it has issued. Since `redeem`/`send-underlying` pays out based on this same inflated accounting, later redeemers cannot fully be paid from actual vault holdings once the shortfall accumulates — resulting in permanent freezing of funds for some depositors and protocol insolvency for the affected vault, matching the in-scope "permanent freezing of funds" / "protocol insolvency" impact classes. This is reachable purely by an ordinary principal calling the public `deposit` entry point; no privileged action or DAO compromise is required.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood depends entirely on whether the specific underlying token contract used by a given vault has or later acquires a transfer fee. Since the underlying is hardcoded per vault and the code contains no defensive balance-delta check, this is a latent risk realized automatically the moment any covered token (or its future upgrade) charges a fee — with no way for the protocol to detect or reject it before insolvency starts accruing.

### Recommendation
In `receive-underlying`, measure the vault's underlying-token balance before and after the transfer and credit `assets` and mint shares based on the actual delta received, rather than trusting the caller-supplied `amount`. Apply the same before/after balance check symmetrically in `send-underlying` for withdrawals.

### Proof of Concept
1. Assume the `UNDERLYING` token for `v0-vault-usdc.clar` (`usdcx`) is upgraded to charge a 1% transfer fee (analogous to USDT's fee-switch capability).
2. User calls `deposit` with `amount = 1000`.
3. `receive-underlying` transfers `1000` from user; only `990` actually arrives at the vault due to the fee.
4. `deposit` still computes `inkind` from `amount = 1000` via `convert-to-shares-preview`, mints that many shares, and executes `(var-set assets (+ current-assets 1000))` — 10 units more than actually received. [5](#0-4) 
5. Repeat across many deposits; the gap between `var-get assets` and the vault's real `usdcx` balance grows, so eventually redeemers attempting to withdraw their proportional share via `send-underlying` cannot be fully paid out of actual vault funds.
