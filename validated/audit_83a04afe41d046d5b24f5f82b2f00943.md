### Title
Overpayment of attached NEAR deposit to `rlp_execute` is not refunded to the caller on success - (File: `runtime/near-wallet-contract/implementation/wallet-contract/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
The eth-implicit `WalletContract::rlp_execute` entry point accepts an arbitrary attached NEAR deposit from its caller (a relayer or any predecessor) and funds the resulting Near action from that deposit, but the amount actually needed for the action is determined independently from the signed Ethereum transaction's `value`/`yocto_near` fields. The contract only refunds the caller's attached deposit when the underlying cross-contract call **fails**; when it **succeeds**, any surplus between the attached deposit and the amount actually consumed by the action is silently retained by the wallet account instead of being returned to the payer.

### Finding Description
`rlp_execute` is `#[payable]` and reads `env::attached_deposit()` into `ExecutionContext::attached_deposit` [1](#0-0) . `CallerDeposit::new` captures the *entire* attached deposit for a non-self caller, purely so it can be refunded if the promise fails [2](#0-1) .

The actual value moved by the resulting Near action, however, is computed from the *signed Ethereum transaction's* `value`/`yocto_near` fields via `Action::try_into_near_action`, completely independent of how much the caller attached to the `rlp_execute` call [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) .

In `rlp_execute_callback`, the `CallerDeposit` is only ever used in the `PromiseResult::Failed` branch, where the whole attached amount is transferred back to `account_id` (the predecessor). In the `PromiseResult::Successful` branch there is no refund logic at all — any part of the attached deposit not consumed by the promise's own deposit is simply left as part of the wallet contract's own NEAR balance [5](#0-4) .

This mirrors the `InfinityExchange` pattern: the entry point accepts `msg.value`-equivalent (`attached_deposit`) up front, checks/consumes only what is minimally required for execution, and never computes/returns the difference to the payer on the success path — only the failure path has a refund. Existing tests confirm the asymmetry: `test_caller_refunds` explicitly checks that on failure `pre_balance - post_balance < deposit_amount` (i.e., partial refund happened) while on success `pre_balance - post_balance >= deposit_amount` (i.e., the entire attached amount, not just what was needed, is deducted with no refund) [6](#0-5) .

### Impact Explanation
Any predecessor (typically a relayer acting on behalf of an eth-implicit account owner, or the owner themselves calling through an intermediary) who attaches more NEAR to `rlp_execute` than the signed transaction's encoded `value` actually requires permanently loses the difference: it becomes part of the wallet contract account's own balance rather than being returned. Unlike a typical wallet/relayer flow where unspent value is refunded, here the surplus is captured by the target eth-implicit account with no on-chain mechanism to reclaim it. This is a real, irreversible transfer of value away from the payer that the protocol does not account for.

### Likelihood Explanation
The likelihood is lower than the original Dutch-auction scenario because the amount required by the action (`tx.value`) is a fixed value embedded in the user-signed Ethereum transaction rather than a time-varying, unpredictable price — a careful relayer can compute the exact deposit needed and would not normally overpay for pricing-uncertainty reasons. The bug is still reachable whenever a relayer implementation attaches a conservative/rounded-up deposit (e.g., to also cover the NEP-141 `storage_deposit` sub-case or out of caution), and there is no protocol-level or contract-level safeguard preventing or refunding such overpayment on the success path.

### Recommendation
Track the exact NEAR amount required to fund the derived Near action (post-`try_into_near_action`) and refund the caller (`predecessor_account_id`) for any portion of `attached_deposit` in excess of that amount, on both the success and failure branches of `rlp_execute_callback` (and its callback variants `address_check_callback` / `nep_141_storage_balance_callback`), rather than only refunding the full deposit on failure.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy the eth-implicit `WalletContract` for address `0xabc...` and give it the `AddressRegistrar`/target accounts used in `sanity.rs` tests.
2. As `caller` (a distinct account from the wallet), craft a signed Ethereum transaction whose ABI-encoded action is a `functionCall`/`transfer` with `yocto_near`/`value` corresponding to `X` yoctoNEAR.
3. Call `rlp_execute(target, tx_bytes_b64)` attaching a deposit of `X + Y` yoctoNEAR (`Y > 0`).
4. Observe (as in `test_caller_refunds`, success branch) that the call succeeds and `caller`'s balance decreases by at least `X + Y`, while `rlp_execute_callback`'s `PromiseResult::Successful` branch performs no refund of `Y` back to `caller` [7](#0-6) ; the surplus `Y` remains in the wallet contract account's balance.

impl CallerDeposit {
    pub fn new(context: &ExecutionContext) -> Option<Self> {
        // Only track for external (non-self) callers
        if context.current_account_id == context.predecessor_account_id {
            return None;
        }

        NonZeroU128::new(context.attached_deposit.as_yoctonear()).map(|yocto_near| Self {
            account_id: context.predecessor_account_id.clone(),
            yocto_near,
        })
    }
