### Title
Dust-transfer griefing via `MAX_TOKENS_PER_USER` blocks victim deposits and forces liquidation - (File: contracts/Pool.sol)

### Summary
`Pool` enforces a shared cap of `MAX_TOKENS_PER_USER = 30` across a user's deposit-token and debt-token lists, enforced by the `onlyIfAdditionWillNotReachMaxTokens` modifier in `addToDepositTokensOfAccount`/`addToDebtTokensOfAccount` (Pool.sol:143-148, 204-220). Because `DepositToken._transfer` registers any new token on the *recipient* when their balance was zero (DepositToken.sol:517-520), anyone can push entries onto a victim's list by sending dust amounts of every whitelisted deposit token. Once the victim's combined list reaches 30, every path that would add a new token to their account reverts: `deposit(onBehalfOf_)` for a collateral type they don't already hold (DepositToken.sol:234 → `_mint` → `addToDepositTokensOfAccount`), inbound `transfer`/`transferFrom` of a new msdTOKEN, and `seize` to a beneficiary that doesn't hold the token. The victim's account becomes unable to take on new collateral, analogously to the CVE's input-triggered denial of service.

### Finding Description
- `Pool.MAX_TOKENS_PER_USER` is 30 and the cap counts `debtTokensOfAccount + depositTokensOfAccount` (Pool.sol:79, 143-148).
- Registration is permissionless from the token side: `DepositToken.transfer`/`transferFrom` only check the *sender's* unlocked balance via `_revertIfLocked` (DepositToken.sol:348-376); `_transfer` then calls `pool.addToDepositTokensOfAccount(recipient_)` whenever the recipient's prior balance is zero (DepositToken.sol:518-520).
- `addToDepositTokensOfAccount` reverts with `UserReachedMaxTokens` when the account is at the cap (Pool.sol:216-219), reverting the entire transfer/mint/seize.
- Attack steps (all unprivileged EOA calls):
  1. Attacker deposits a tiny amount into each of the pool's deposit tokens (or acquires msdTOKENs) so they hold transferable, unlocked balances.
  2. Attacker calls `depositToken.transfer(victim, 1)` for each deposit token until the victim's `depositTokensOfAccount + debtTokensOfAccount` length is 30.
  3. Any subsequent `deposit(amount, victim)` for a collateral the victim doesn't already hold reverts; anyone transferring a new msdTOKEN to the victim reverts; liquidation that seizes into a beneficiary lacking the token reverts.

### Impact Explanation
An account at the cap cannot add any new collateral type. If the victim's position is approaching the liquidation threshold (`debtPositionOf` at Pool.sol:248-266), they are prevented from topping up collateral to restore health, guaranteeing the position becomes liquidatable — collateral is seized at a discount to the liquidator (`Pool.liquidate`/`DepositToken.seize`). This produces forced liquidation / temporary freezing of the ability to rescue funds, caused entirely by an unprivileged attacker. It also permanently blocks onboarding flows such as `deposit(..., onBehalfOf)` credit to the victim for new collateral types until the victim fully zeroes out an existing position.

### Likelihood Explanation
- Cost is bounded by 30 dust transfers plus dust deposits; each pool in `deployments/` whitelists multiple collateral types, and the cap is shared with debt tokens, so fewer than 30 deposit tokens may suffice if the victim already has debts.
- No privileged role, oracle manipulation, or governance action is required; `transfer` is a plain public ERC20 entry point guarded only by the sender's lock check.
- The griefing is not blocked by `whenNotPaused`, `nonReentrant`, or SynthContext checks — none of them apply to this path in a way that helps the victim.

### Recommendation
Make list registration tolerant instead of reverting: e.g., in `addToDepositTokensOfAccount`/`addToDebtTokensOfAccount`, return silently (or return a boolean) when at the cap rather than reverting, and have `depositOf`/`debtOf` iterate only over tracked tokens — noting that untracked dust would then be excluded from `debtPositionOf`/`unlockedBalanceOf`, so a safer alternative is to only register on deposit/mint paths (`deposit`, `issue`, `mint`) and treat plain `transfer`/`seize` receipts without registration (with `unlockedBalanceOf`/`depositOf` made resilient to untracked balances). Alternatively, enforce the cap on the *sender* side or require a minimum deposit/transfer amount that makes dust-listing economically non-viable.
