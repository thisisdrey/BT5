Confirmed: `MarketOrderCapsule` (chainbase/src/main/java/org/tron/core/capsule/MarketOrderCapsule.java) has no `fee` field at all — an order caches only `sellTokenId`, `sellTokenQuantity`, `buyTokenId`, `buyTokenQuantity`, `createTime`, `state`. No fee snapshot is stored with the order.

### Title
Market order cancellation fee is re-fetched at cancel time instead of being cached at order-placement time, permanently freezing user assets - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/MarketCancelOrderActuator.java)

### Summary
`MarketCancelOrderActuator` charges the *current* `MARKET_CANCEL_FEE` dynamic parameter when a resting order is cancelled, rather than the fee that was in effect (or reserved) when the order was originally placed. Because orders can rest in the order book indefinitely (up to `MAX_ACTIVE_ORDER_NUM = 100` per account, no expiry) and the fee is a committee-adjustable dynamic parameter, a fee increase between order placement and cancellation can leave the trader unable to afford cancellation, permanently trapping the assets they locked into the order.

### Finding Description
When a user places a sell order via `MarketSellAssetActuator`, their sell-side balance/asset is immediately moved out of their spendable balance and into the order book (`transferBalanceOrToken`, `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/MarketSellAssetActuator.java:134`), while `MARKET_SELL_FEE` is paid at that time. The `MarketOrderCapsule` created and persisted (`MarketOrderCapsule.java:35-49`) never records the fee that was applicable — it stores only token IDs/quantities/state.

Later, to reclaim the locked tokens, the user must send a `MarketCancelOrderContract`. `MarketCancelOrderActuator.calcFee()` fetches the fee *fresh* from the dynamic store: [1](#0-0) 

and this freshly-fetched fee is what both `validate()` and `execute()` check/deduct against the account's *current spendable* TRX balance: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

`MARKET_CANCEL_FEE` is a normal committee-governed dynamic parameter that can be changed at any time via a proposal, independent of any specific user's order: [4](#0-3) 

This is exactly the bug class described in the external report: the "service fee" (here, `MARKET_CANCEL_FEE`) is looked up at fulfillment time (cancellation) rather than cached/reserved at request time (order placement), so the amount the requester actually has available may no longer cover the newly current fee.

### Impact Explanation
Because the sell-side token quantity is already removed from the account's spendable balance/asset the moment the order is placed (`transferBalanceOrToken`), a trader who kept only a thin TRX buffer (just enough to cover the cancel fee at the time they placed the order) can find that buffer insufficient once `MARKET_CANCEL_FEE` is raised. `validate()` then rejects the cancellation with "No enough balance!" — the trader cannot cancel and cannot otherwise get their locked sell-side tokens back (there is no other actuator path to unlock an un-matched order's tokens). Their assets remain locked in the order book indefinitely, i.e., a permanent freezing of funds triggered purely by an ordinary, permitted change to a dynamic chain parameter, not by any wrongdoing of the trader.

### Likelihood Explanation
`MARKET_CANCEL_FEE` changes require a committee proposal to pass, so this is not attacker-triggerable at will by an arbitrary unprivileged account — it depends on normal governance activity. However, no malicious actor is required: an ordinary, legitimate fee increase (a routine, expected chain-parameter adjustment) combined with an ordinary trader who sized their reserved TRX to the fee at order-placement time is sufficient to strand funds. Given that orders have no expiry and can sit for arbitrarily long, and traders have no way to know a future fee, the likelihood of this occurring in normal operation over time is non-trivial.

### Recommendation
Cache the applicable cancel fee (or an escrowed fee reserve) in the `MarketOrder` at order-creation time, analogous to the reported fix pattern, so that cancellation always uses the fee that was in force when the order was placed — e.g., add a `fee`/`cancelFee` field to `MarketOrderCapsule`/`MarketOrder` proto set in `MarketSellAssetActuator`, and have `MarketCancelOrderActuator.calcFee()` read that cached value instead of `dynamicStore.getMarketCancelFee()` at cancel time. Alternatively, reserve/escrow the current cancel fee amount from the account balance at order-placement time so cancellation is guaranteed to succeed regardless of later fee changes.

### Proof of Concept
1. Committee sets `MARKET_CANCEL_FEE = 1 TRX`.
2. Trader A has 100 TRX. A places a sell order for 99 TRX worth of tokens via `MarketSellAssetContract`; `MarketSellAssetActuator.execute()` deducts `MARKET_SELL_FEE` and moves the sell quantity out of A's balance (`MarketSellAssetActuator.java:126-134`), leaving A with just over 1 TRX spendable — enough to cover the current 1 TRX cancel fee.
3. Committee later passes a proposal raising `MARKET_CANCEL_FEE` to 5 TRX (`ProposalService.java:232-235`, `DynamicPropertiesStore.saveMarketCancelFee`).
4. A submits `MarketCancelOrderContract` to cancel the still-unmatched order and recover the 99 TRX worth of locked tokens.
5. `MarketCancelOrderActuator.validate()` computes `fee = calcFee()` = 5 TRX (`MarketCancelOrderActuator.java:213,228`), sees `ownerAccount.getBalance() < fee`, and throws `ContractValidateException("No enough balance !")` (`MarketCancelOrderActuator.java:214-216`).
6. A has no other means to release the tokens locked in the order; the 99 TRX worth of assets are permanently frozen unless/until A acquires additional TRX from an external source to pay the new fee.
