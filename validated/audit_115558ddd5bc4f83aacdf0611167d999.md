### Title
sBTC collateral is valued using the raw BTC/USD Pyth feed instead of a peg-aware sBTC rate, enabling the same market-rate deposit/borrow arbitrage described in the stETH/ETH report - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`, `mainnet/contracts/proposals/mainnet/v0-init.clar`)

### Summary
The stETH/ETH report describes an oracle design flaw: valuing a liquid-staking derivative with a market-rate feed that is assumed to always equal a hard 1:1 exchange rate, which opens two-way deposit/borrow arbitrage whenever the derivative trades at a premium or discount to its reference asset. Zest reproduces this exact pattern for `sBTC`: the asset registry wires `sBTC` (and its vault share `zsBTC`) to the raw `BTC/USD` Pyth feed with no sBTC-specific transform, treating sBTC as if it always trades 1:1 with BTC.

### Finding Description
When assets are registered, `sBTC` is configured with `type: TYPE-PYTH, ident: BTC-FEED-ID, callcode: none`, i.e. its collateral/debt valuation is the plain BTC/USD market price: [1](#0-0) 

`zsBTC` (the vault share) is likewise priced off `BTC-FEED-ID` with only the liquidity-index callcode applied, again assuming sBTC == BTC: [2](#0-1) 

At runtime, `market.clar`'s oracle resolution pipeline (`price-resolve` / `resolve-callcode`) simply fetches this configured feed and applies only the registered callcode transform - there is no independent check of whether sBTC actually trades at parity with BTC: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

This price is exactly what feeds into the health checks in `collateral-add`, `collateral-remove`, and `borrow` via `get-assets` → `get-notional-evaluation`: [5](#0-4) 

The same "market-rate-as-exchange-rate" pattern is confirmed in the off-chain valuation helper, which explicitly comments "sBTC - Pyth oracle (BTC price)": [6](#0-5) 

This is architecturally identical to the reported ezETH/stETH issue: sBTC is a bridged/custody-backed representation of BTC (not BTC itself), and the protocol has no live redemption-rate or peg-deviation check comparable to the `get-ststx-ratio` mechanism used for stSTX (which pulls a dedicated on-chain ratio rather than reusing the base asset's market price). By contrast, sBTC reuses the underlying BTC market feed verbatim, so any premium/discount of sBTC vs BTC (bridge stress, custody concerns, liquidity crunch, thin secondary-market pricing) is invisible to the protocol's LTV math.

### Impact Explanation
If sBTC trades at a discount to BTC (analogous to the stETH depeg cited in the report), an attacker can:
1. Acquire discounted sBTC on the open market.
2. Deposit it as collateral via `collateral-add` / `supply-collateral-add`, where it is valued at the full, undiscounted BTC/USD price.
3. Borrow other vault assets (USDC, USDH, STX, etc.) up to the LTV-BORROW threshold of the applicable egroup, extracting more value than the collateral is actually worth on the open market.

If the discount persists or worsens, the position leaves the protocol holding under-collateralized debt once accounted at real market value - this is a form of protocol insolvency / theft of lender funds, since the borrowed assets came from the shared lending pools funded by other depositors. This lands in the Critical impact bucket (protocol insolvency / theft of funds at rest).

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood depends on sBTC ever trading away from a strict 1:1 peg with BTC (bridge/custody stress, secondary-market liquidity gaps), which is an external market condition, not something the attacker needs to cause on-chain. Once such a divergence exists, exploiting it requires only a normal `collateral-add` + `borrow` call from any unprivileged principal - no special privileges or DAO action needed, mirroring how the original stETH finding was rated Medium.

### Recommendation
Do not reuse the base asset's market-rate feed (`BTC-FEED-ID`) directly for a bridged/derivative asset like sBTC. Either:
- Source a dedicated sBTC/USD (or sBTC/BTC) market price feed and apply a callcode/haircut consistent with observed peg risk, similar to how `resolve-ststx` applies a distinct, protocol-verified ratio rather than reusing the STX price unmodified; or
- Apply a conservative discount/haircut in the LTV parameters for sBTC-collateralized egroups to bound the arbitrage window, and monitor peg deviation to pause borrowing against sBTC if it exceeds a threshold.

### Proof of Concept
1. Assume sBTC trades at a 3% discount to BTC on secondary markets (e.g., due to bridge/custody stress), while Pyth's `BTC-FEED-ID` still reports the full BTC/USD price.
2. Attacker buys discounted sBTC off-market.
3. Attacker calls `collateral-add` with the sBTC (or `zsBTC` after depositing to `vault-sbtc`) - `price-resolve` values it at full BTC/USD price because `callcode: none` (sBTC) / `CALLCODE-ZSBTC` (zsBTC) only apply liquidity-index scaling, not any peg-discount adjustment: [7](#0-6) 
4. Attacker calls `borrow` against this inflated collateral valuation up to `LTV-BORROW` of the resolved egroup, extracting borrowed assets worth more than the real market value of the sBTC pledged.
5. If sBTC's discount persists, the position is effectively insolvent relative to true market value, and the protocol/lenders bear the loss upon eventual liquidation or default.

(define-private (resolve-callcode (p uint) (callcode (optional (buff 1))))
  (let ((cc (unwrap! callcode (ok p))))
    (if (is-eq cc CALLCODE-STSTX) (resolve-ststx p)
