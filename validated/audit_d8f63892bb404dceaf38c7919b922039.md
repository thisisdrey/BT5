### Title
Frontrunning of `UpdateSettingContract` lets a contract owner change `consume_user_resource_percent` mid-flight to shift energy cost onto an already-broadcast caller transaction - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java)

### Summary
`UpdateSettingContract` lets the contract owner (deployer) change `consume_user_resource_percent` for a deployed smart contract at any time, with no check on transactions that are already broadcast/pending against that contract and no snapshotting of the percent used at submission time.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` unconditionally overwrites the contract's `consume_user_resource_percent` in the `ContractStore` as soon as the transaction is packed, with no validation of in-flight transactions targeting the same contract: [1](#0-0) 

The value is not bound to the caller's transaction at broadcast time. It is instead re-read live from the `ContractStore` twice during a `TriggerSmartContract` execution:

1. When computing the energy limit split available to the call, `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio` fetches the *current* percent from `ContractCapsule` at execution time: [2](#0-1) 

2. When settling the actual energy bill after execution, `TransactionTrace.pay()` re-reads the percent again from the store (not from any value captured when the caller's transaction was created/signed): [3](#0-2) 

3. `ReceiptCapsule.payEnergyBill` then splits the already-consumed energy between origin and caller using that freshly-read percent, directly debiting TRX/frozen energy from the caller's account for the origin's now-larger share: [4](#0-3) 

Because the percent is fetched fresh at both the energy-limit-computation stage and the fee-settlement stage - rather than being fixed to the value that existed when the caller's transaction was signed/broadcast - a contract owner can broadcast an `UpdateSettingContract` transaction that gets ordered ahead of a victim's pending `TriggerSmartContract` transaction in the same block (or simply race it), changing the percent (e.g. from a low value the caller relied on, up to 100) right before the victim's call executes. This is the direct analog of the reported bug class: a state-changing "update" operation that lacks any check for actions/positions already committed under the prior state, enabling frontrunning that causes the pre-existing action to be settled under different, attacker-controlled terms.

### Impact Explanation
A malicious or compromised contract owner can frontrun any pending call to their own contract to shift more of the energy burden onto callers, causing callers to pay unexpected TRX/energy fees they did not consent to when they signed their transaction (their frozen energy is consumed and/or TRX burned for the origin's share that was supposed to be paid by the origin). This is an unauthorized state change that produces an unintended monetary loss for the calling account, reachable purely via ordinary signed transactions (`UpdateSettingContract` by any contract deployer, `TriggerSmartContract` by any caller), matching the "unauthorized account operation / theft of funds" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any contract owner can broadcast `UpdateSettingContract` at will; there is no cooldown, timelock, or check against pending calls, and callers have no way to guarantee the percent used at signing time is the one applied at execution time. The attack requires only the owner to observe a pending call to their contract in the mempool and race an `UpdateSettingContract` transaction ahead of it (or into the same block before it), which is straightforward transaction ordering/frontrunning available to any full node/relay-capable actor.

### Recommendation
Bind the `consume_user_resource_percent` used for a `TriggerSmartContract` execution to a value that cannot be altered by a concurrently-mined `UpdateSettingContract` transaction affecting the same call - e.g., disallow percent changes from taking effect until a future block, or require the caller's expected percent to be validated against the on-chain value at broadcast time and reject execution if it has changed, rather than silently reapplying the freshly stored percent in both `VMActuator` and `TransactionTrace.pay()`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Owner deploys a contract with `consume_user_resource_percent = 0` (origin pays all).
2. A user broadcasts `TriggerSmartContract` expecting to pay nothing, per current settings.
3. Owner observes the pending transaction and broadcasts `UpdateSettingContract` setting `consume_user_resource_percent = 100`, getting it included in the same or an earlier position in the block (via `UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()`, which applies immediately: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java:41-49`).
4. The user's `TriggerSmartContract` then executes; `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio` (`actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/VMActuator.java:744-747`) and `TransactionTrace.pay()` (`chainbase/src/main/java/org/tron/core/db/TransactionTrace.java:239-253`) both read the new 100% value, so the entire energy bill is charged to the caller via `ReceiptCapsule.payEnergyBill` (`chainbase/src/main/java/org/tron/core/capsule/ReceiptCapsule.java:222-238`), despite the caller having signed under the assumption of 0%.
