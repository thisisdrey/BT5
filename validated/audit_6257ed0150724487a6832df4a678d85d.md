### Title
`accrue` can permanently freeze one leg's accrued interest when `index` and `lindex` round differently while `last-update` is bumped unconditionally - (File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-sbtc.clar)

### Summary
In `accrue`, `next-index` uses round-up (`mul-div-up`) on `rate` while `next-liquidity-index` uses round-down (`mul-div-down`) on a smaller derived `liquidity-rate` (via `calc-liquidity-rate`), so at small `time-delta` values the two multipliers can round to different outcomes (one changes, the other stays at `INDEX-PRECISION`). `accrue` updates `index`/`lindex` independently based on whether each actually differs from its current value, but advances `last-update` via a single `or` check covering both. This resets `time-delta` to zero for the *next* `accrue` call even though the unchanged side never captured its share of interest for that elapsed period, permanently losing it.

### Finding Description
`next-index` computes `time-delta = stacks-block-time - last-update` and `calc-multiplier-delta rate time-delta true` (round-up) [1](#0-0) . `next-liquidity-index` computes the same `time-delta` but applies `calc-multiplier-delta liquidity-rate time-delta false` (round-down) on `liquidity-rate = calc-liquidity-rate(rate, utilization, fee-reserve)`, which is always `<= rate` since it is `rate` scaled down twice by `util-pct` and `(1 - reserve-factor)` [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) .

Because `liquidity-rate <= rate`, and the two calls use opposite rounding directions, at small `time-delta` (1-2 seconds) the numerator `rate * time-delta * INDEX-PRECISION` can round up to a nonzero delta while the smaller `liquidity-rate * time-delta * INDEX-PRECISION` rounds down to zero (or vice versa with different fee-reserve/utilization combinations).

In `accrue`, each var is updated independently:
```
(if (not (is-eq idx next)) (var-set index next) false)
(if (not (is-eq lidx nliq)) (var-set lindex nliq) false)
...
(if (or (not (is-eq idx next)) (not (is-eq lidx nliq)))
    (var-set last-update stacks-block-time)
    false)
``` [4](#0-3) 

If only `index` changes (or only `lindex` changes), `last-update` still advances to `stacks-block-time` because of the `or`. On the *next* `accrue` invocation, `time-delta` is computed from the new `last-update`, so the elapsed period during which the frozen side did not accrue is gone — that side's `time-delta` basis has already been reset and can never be recovered.

`accrue` is invoked unconditionally from unprivileged, public entry points `deposit` and `redeem` [5](#0-4) , so any ordinary caller can trigger `accrue` at a block/time of their choosing (subject to normal transaction submission timing), without needing `system-borrow` access (`check-caller-auth`-gated) [6](#0-5) . An attacker only needs the vault to already have nonzero utilization (from any existing borrower) and to call `deposit`/`redeem` in two blocks separated by a small enough `time-delta` that the two multiplier computations round to opposite outcomes.

No existing check prevents this: there is no reconciliation logic that tracks "unaccrued remainder" per index; `time-delta` is derived solely from `last-update`, which is a single shared variable for both indices.

### Impact Explanation
This is a permanent freezing of unclaimed yield: whichever of `index`/`lindex` fails to update for a given interval never recovers that interval's interest, because the shared `last-update` advances regardless. Since `get-liquidity-index` (`lindex`) is exposed as a read-only getter consumed by other protocol components for pricing collateral/zTokens [7](#0-6) , an undercounted `lindex` relative to `index` creates a persistent accounting divergence between borrower debt growth and depositor-side liquidity growth. This matches the in-scope **High** category: theft/permanent freezing of unclaimed yield.

### Likelihood Explanation
- No privileged role required: `deposit`/`redeem` are public and unauthenticated beyond normal balance/slippage checks.
- Requires an existing nonzero-utilization state (any prior borrow, which need not be caused by the attacker) and the ability to trigger `accrue` at two blocks with a small `time-delta`.
- The exact rounding-divergence condition depends on `rate`, `utilization`, and `fee-reserve` at the time, so it is not deterministically triggerable on demand, but is a probabilistic, repeatable condition an attacker can wait for/engineer by adjusting deposits/redemptions (which shift `utilization` and thus `rate`/`liquidity-rate`) across low-time-delta blocks.
- Capital cost is limited to enough principal to move `utilization` into the desired rounding regime and gas/fees for repeated `deposit`/`redeem` calls.

### Recommendation
Track `time-delta` accrual per index independently, or only reset `last-update` to `stacks-block-time` once both `index` and `lindex` have actually advanced (i.e., require both conditions, not `or`), retaining the un-accrued remainder time-delta for the side that didn't change so it is included in the next computation. Alternatively, accumulate a persistent "carry" of un-rounded fractional interest for each index across calls instead of resetting the time basis whenever either index moves.

### Proof of Concept
Clarinet/vitest simnet plan:
1. Initialize vault, set `points-ir` so `interest-rate()` yields a `rate` and `fee-reserve` such that `calc-liquidity-rate` produces a materially smaller `liquidity-rate`.
2. Seed an existing borrowed position and deposited liquidity to establish nonzero `utilization`.
3. Advance the chain by 1-2 seconds and call `deposit` (or `redeem`) to trigger `accrue`; via `get-index`/`get-liquidity-index`/`get-last-update`, assert that only one of `index`/`lindex` changed while `last-update` advanced.
4. Advance again by the same small delta and call `accrue` a second time; assert that the previously frozen index's growth for the first interval is unrecoverable (i.e., total growth over both intervals is less than what continuous per-second accrual without the reset would have produced), confirming permanent loss of that period's yield.
