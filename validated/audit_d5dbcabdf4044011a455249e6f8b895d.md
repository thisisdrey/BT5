### Title
Contract owner can change `consume_user_resource_percent` (and asset issuer can change free bandwidth limits) at any time with no timelock, unfairly shifting energy/bandwidth costs onto users mid-interaction - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java`)

### Summary
Just as the OpenQ `BountyCore` owner could silently flip `kycRequired`/`invoiceRequired`/`supportingDocumentsRequired` at any time, a deployed TVM contract's origin owner can silently change `consume_user_resource_percent` via `UpdateSettingContractActuator`, and a TRC10 asset issuer can silently change `FreeAssetNetLimit`/`PublicFreeAssetNetLimit` via `UpdateAssetActuator`, at any time, with no cooldown, no notice, and no bound on how often the value can change. Both actuators only check that the caller is the recorded owner; they impose no rate limit or delay before the change takes effect.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` immediately persists a new `consume_user_resource_percent` for a deployed contract as soon as `validate()` confirms the caller is the contract's `originAddress`: [1](#0-0) 

This value directly controls how energy costs are split between the contract's own frozen energy and the calling user's energy/TRX in `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio`: [2](#0-1) 

Because there is no minimum interval between updates, the owner can advertise a low `consume_user_resource_percent` to attract users/dApp integrators, then raise it to 100 immediately before a high-value transaction is expected, shifting the entire energy bill onto the caller without warning. The unit test `twiceUpdateSettingContract` confirms two consecutive updates by the same owner both succeed with no restriction: [3](#0-2) 

The analogous pattern exists for TRC10 assets: `UpdateAssetActuator` lets the issuer change `FreeAssetNetLimit`/`PublicFreeAssetNetLimit` at any time, which is consumed by `BandwidthProcessor.useAssetAccountNet` to decide whether a holder's transfer is "free": [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation
Users interacting with a contract or holding/transferring a TRC10 asset make decisions (freeze TRX for energy, budget `fee_limit`, plan free-bandwidth usage) based on the currently published `consume_user_resource_percent` or free-asset limits. Because these can be changed unilaterally and instantaneously by the owner/issuer with no timelock, a malicious or opportunistic owner can retroactively shift resource costs onto unsuspecting callers right before they submit a transaction, causing the caller to unexpectedly burn TRX fees they did not budget for, or causing previously "free" asset transfers to suddenly fail/charge bandwidth. This is a fairness/trust-in-parameters issue directly analogous to the reported bug class, though it does not rise to direct fund theft from the contract itself — the impact is limited to unexpected fee/bandwidth cost shifted to counterparties.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account that deployed a contract, or any asset issuer, can trigger this at will via a normal signed transaction (`UpdateSettingContract`, `UpdateEnergyLimitContract`, `UpdateAssetContract`) with no special privilege beyond being the contract/asset owner, making this trivially reachable and repeatable at any time.

### Recommendation
Introduce a minimum interval / cooldown or a scheduled activation delay before `consume_user_resource_percent`, `origin_energy_limit`, or asset `FreeAssetNetLimit`/`PublicFreeAssetNetLimit` changes take effect, so callers have advance notice of parameter changes rather than instant, silent shifts of resource cost.

### Proof of Concept
1. Owner deploys a contract and sets `consume_user_resource_percent = 0` via `UpdateSettingContract`, so callers expect the contract to cover energy.
2. Users freeze minimal TRX and begin calling the contract relying on the advertised 0% rate, as shown to be immediately effective and repeatable in `UpdateSettingContractActuatorTest.twiceUpdateSettingContract`. [3](#0-2) 
3. Owner submits a second `UpdateSettingContract` transaction setting `consume_user_resource_percent = 100` right before a user's pending call executes.
4. `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio` now computes `creatorEnergyLimit = 0`, forcing the caller to cover 100% of energy from their own frozen energy/TRX balance, an unexpected cost shift enacted instantly without any cooldown. [6](#0-5)
