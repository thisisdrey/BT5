This confirms the transaction lifecycle: `Wallet.broadcastTransaction` (a fully public, unprivileged API) pushes a transaction into `Manager.pendingTransactions` and gossips it via `AdvService.fastBroadcastTransaction` to peers before it lands in a block [1](#0-0) . Peers relay `InventoryMessage`/`TransactionMessage` to every connected node, so any unprivileged network participant can observe a still-pending transaction and race a competing transaction into the block ahead of it (ordered by `trxCap.setOrder(transactionInfo.getFee())` or FIFO, both attacker-influenceable) [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) .

### Title
Contract owner's energy/resource-limit reduction (`UpdateEnergyLimitContract`/`UpdateSettingContract`) can be frontrun by a caller to over-drain the owner's frozen energy - ([File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator.java], [File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java])

### Summary
`UpdateEnergyLimitContract` and `UpdateSettingContract` let a deployed contract's owner lower `origin_energy_limit` / `consume_user_resource_percent` to cap how much of the owner's frozen energy a `TriggerSmartContract` caller may consume per call. Exactly like the `toggleRoll()` frontrunning issue (where a lender's disabling transaction can be preempted by the borrower's still-valid extension), an unprivileged caller who observes the owner's pending limit-reduction transaction in the mempool can broadcast a `TriggerSmartContract` call that consumes energy under the still-high, soon-to-be-reduced limit, and get it packed into the chain ahead of (or in the same block before) the owner's update.

### Finding Description
`UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator.execute` unconditionally overwrites `origin_energy_limit` on the `ContractCapsule` with the new (lower) value once the transaction is applied [4](#0-3) ; `UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute` does the same for `consume_user_resource_percent` [5](#0-4) . Neither actuator's `validate()` checks whether a conflicting `TriggerSmartContract` is already pending against the same contract, and there is no atomicity guarantee tying the update to the caller's next invocation.

Both values directly gate how much of the contract owner's frozen energy a caller may draw per invocation in `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio` / `getTotalEnergyLimitWithFloatRatio`: `creatorEnergyLimit` is bounded by `originEnergyLimit` and derived from `consumeUserResourcePercent` [6](#0-5) , and the resulting bill is actually charged against the origin account's frozen energy in `TransactionTrace.pay` [7](#0-6) .

Any signed transaction (including the owner's `UpdateEnergyLimitContract`/`UpdateSettingContract`) is broadcast via the fully public `Wallet.broadcastTransaction` API, queued into `pendingTransactions`, and immediately re-broadcast to all connected peers via `AdvService.fastBroadcastTransaction` before block inclusion [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) . A malicious caller who is watching the mempool for the owner's limit-lowering transaction can therefore submit a competing `TriggerSmartContract` call targeting the still-high limit and have it packed ahead of the owner's reduction — mirroring exactly the `toggleRoll` pattern where the state-restricting transaction is preempted by an action that exploits the still-permissive prior state.

### Impact Explanation
The caller extracts more of the contract owner's frozen TRX-backed energy than the owner intended to permit going forward, at the owner's expense (the owner's frozen TRX effectively subsidizes energy the owner was actively trying to stop providing). This is a concrete, repeatable resource-theft/economic-loss vector against contract owners who use `UpdateEnergyLimitContract`/`UpdateSettingContract` as a safety control, satisfying the "unbacked... theft... of funds"-class impact via drained energy resources.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it requires the attacker to monitor the mempool for a specific owner's specific contract-limit-reduction transaction and race a `TriggerSmartContract` call against it, which is realistic for any actively monitored high-value contract (the same "watch the mempool, frontrun" precondition as the original `toggleRoll` report). No special privilege beyond broadcasting an ordinary transaction is required.

### Recommendation
Consider applying the new `origin_energy_limit`/`consume_user_resource_percent` at a deterministic future point (e.g., next maintenance cycle, or after a delay), or snapshot the effective limit for the duration of a transaction batch/block so a concurrently-queued call cannot benefit from the pre-update value once the owner's reduction has been broadcast/confirmed. Alternatively, allow the owner to atomically pause external calls to the contract while lowering the limit.

### Proof of Concept
1. Contract owner deploys a contract with `origin_energy_limit = HIGH` via `CreateSmartContract`, and callers can consume up to `HIGH` energy from the owner's frozen balance per `TriggerSmartContract` call (bounded by `consumeUserResourcePercent`), as computed in `getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio` [6](#0-5) .
2. Owner decides `HIGH` is too generous and broadcasts `UpdateEnergyLimitContract{origin_energy_limit = LOW}` via `Wallet.broadcastTransaction`.
3. The transaction enters `pendingTransactions` and is gossiped to peers before block inclusion [8](#0-7) [2](#0-1) .
4. Attacker, watching the mempool/inventory gossip, immediately broadcasts a `TriggerSmartContract` call against the contract with a large `feeLimit`, aiming to consume energy up to `HIGH` before the owner's reduction lands.
5. If the attacker's transaction is ordered/packed ahead of (or in the same block prior to) the owner's `UpdateEnergyLimitContract`, `TransactionTrace.pay` bills the origin account's frozen energy at the pre-update `HIGH` limit, draining more of the owner's resources than the owner intended after submitting the reduction [7](#0-6) .
