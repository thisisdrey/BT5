### Title
Interest Accrual Timestamp Advances Even When Only One of the Two Indexes Rounds Up — Permanent Loss of Sub-Precision Yield to Suppliers - ([File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-stx.clar])

### Summary
Each vault (`v0-vault-stx`, `v0-vault-sbtc`, `v0-vault-ststx`, `v0-vault-usdc`, `v0-vault-usdh`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc`) tracks two independent compounding indexes — a borrow index (`index`) and a liquidity/supply index (`lindex`) — both derived from the same `last-update` timestamp and the same elapsed `time-delta`, but from two different rates (`borrow rate` vs. `liquidity rate = borrow-rate × utilization × (1 - fee-reserve)`), computed with a single shared `INDEX-PRECISION` of `u1000000000000` (1e12). Because the liquidity rate is always smaller than the borrow rate, it takes a larger `time-delta` for the liquidity index to accumulate one precision unit of interest than for the borrow index. The `accrue` function only re-arms `last-update` when *either* index changes, which silently discards the "banked" elapsed time the still-unchanged index needed to eventually round up — permanently losing that fractional interest.

### Finding Description
`next-index` and `next-liquidity-index` both compute their multiplier from `(- stacks-block-time (var-get last-update))`: [1](#0-0) 

The `accrue` routine then decides whether to persist `last-update` based on whether **either** index moved, not whether **both** moved: [2](#0-1) 

`INDEX-PRECISION` is fixed at `u1000000000000` (1e12) for every vault regardless of the underlying asset's decimals (e.g., sBTC with 8 decimals, USDC with 6): [3](#0-2) 

This is structurally the same root-cause class as the referenced Yieldoor M-8 report: an index/rate update whose granularity is insufficient relative to elapsed time and rate magnitude, so that fractional interest is rounded away rather than deferred. In Yieldoor, `totalBorrows` is recomputed by ratio every call and precision is lost outright when the accrued interest is below the rounding threshold. In Zest, the loss mechanism is subtly different but equally real: because `borrow rate > liquidity rate` at all utilization levels below 100%, the `index` (borrow) will frequently round up to a new value on time deltas that are still too small for `lindex` (liquidity) to round up. Since `idx != next` is true, the shared `last-update` is advanced anyway — clearing the accumulated `time-delta` that `lindex` needed. On the next accrual, `lindex`'s calculation restarts from `time-delta = 0` and has to re-accumulate elapsed time from scratch, permanently losing the interest that would have accrued during the discarded window. This can be repeated indefinitely: any ordinary user interacting with the vault frequently (deposit/borrow/repay/withdraw, or simply calling any state-changing entry point that triggers `accrue`) each block will keep re-arming `last-update` on borrow-index-only movement, continuously starving the liquidity index (and therefore supplier yield and the treasury LP mint, both of which derive from `lindex`/debt-delta) of interest that is mathematically owed but never captured.

### Impact Explanation
The liquidity index (`lindex`) directly determines the value suppliers redeem for their ztokens and drives the treasury reserve LP minting (`calc-treasury-lp-preview`/`accrue`), both computed from `debt-delta` derived off the borrow index while the supply-side index can lag due to the shared, prematurely-reset `last-update` clock. Suppliers permanently lose unclaimed yield that the interest-rate model computed as owed to them, and the DAO treasury correspondingly permanently loses reserve fee revenue. This matches the in-scope High-severity class: theft/permanent freezing of unclaimed yield.

### Likelihood Explanation
No privileged action or DAO misconfiguration is required. Any ordinary user (or the attacker themselves) triggering frequent vault interactions — which is normal usage, not an exotic attack — is sufficient to repeatedly re-arm `last-update` on sub-threshold time windows for low-utilization or low-decimal vaults (e.g., sBTC, stSTXbtc), where the liquidity rate is a small fraction of the borrow rate and 1e12 precision is comparatively coarse relative to 8-decimal collateral values. The effect compounds with every block that has any vault activity, similar to the Yieldoor PoC's "interact frequently" attack path.

### Recommendation
Track and persist `last-update` independently per index (separate `last-update-borrow` / `last-update-liquidity`), so an unchanged index retains its accumulated time-delta for the next accrual instead of being reset whenever the *other* index changes. Alternatively, increase `INDEX-PRECISION` (e.g., to 1e18 or higher, RAY-style) to reduce the frequency of rounding to zero, and/or accumulate the un-applied remainder rather than discarding it.

### Proof of Concept
1. Vault `v0-vault-sbtc` (8-decimal collateral) is at, say, 50% utilization; `borrow-rate` and `liquidity-rate` are computed such that `liquidity-rate ≪ borrow-rate` due to `fee-reserve` and utilization scaling.
2. A user calls any state-mutating entry point (e.g., `system-borrow`) every block; `accrue` runs each time.
3. Over several consecutive blocks, `time-delta` since `last-update` is small enough that `next-index` (borrow) rounds up to a new value (because the borrow rate is larger) while `next-liquidity-index` remains identical to `lindex` (because the liquidity rate is too small for that `time-delta` to move the 1e12-precision index).
4. Because `idx != next` is true, `accrue` sets `(var-set last-update stacks-block-time)` regardless of `lidx` being unchanged: [4](#0-3) .
5. The elapsed time that `lindex` needed to accumulate its own rounding unit is discarded; on the next call, `next-liquidity-index`'s `time-delta` restarts from zero, and the interest that should have accrued to `lindex` for that entire window is permanently lost, even though `borrow-rate`, `utilization`, and `fee-reserve` all correctly computed a non-zero owed value.
6. Repeated over many blocks of normal or attacker-driven activity, suppliers of that vault (and the DAO treasury's reserve share) permanently lose yield that the interest curve determined they were owed.

(define-private (next-index)
  (let ((states (var-get pause-states))
        (idx (var-get index)))
    (if (get accrue states)
        idx
        (let (
            (rate (interest-rate))
            (time-delta (- stacks-block-time (var-get last-update)))
            (multiplier (if (is-eq time-delta u0)
                          INDEX-PRECISION
                          (calc-multiplier-delta rate time-delta true))))
          (calc-index-next idx multiplier)))))

(define-private (next-liquidity-index)
  (let ((states (var-get pause-states))
        (lidx (var-get lindex)))
    (if (get accrue states)
        lidx
        (let (
            (rate (interest-rate))
            (liquidity-rate (calc-liquidity-rate rate (utilization) (var-get fee-reserve)))
            (time-delta (- stacks-block-time (var-get last-update)))
            (multiplier (if (is-eq time-delta u0)
                          INDEX-PRECISION
                          (calc-multiplier-delta liquidity-rate time-delta false))))
          (calc-index-next lidx multiplier)))))
