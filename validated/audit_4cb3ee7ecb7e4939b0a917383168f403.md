## Title
Rounding error in vault `system-repay` allows a borrower's tiny repayments to permanently avoid reducing aggregate `principal-scaled`/`total-borrowed`, corrupting vault debt accounting — (File: `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar` and equivalent per-asset vaults)

### Summary
The vault's `system-repay` function reduces the vault-wide aggregate scaled debt (`principal-scaled`) proportionally to the repaid amount using `mul-div-down`, exactly analogous to the `LibQuote.closeQuote` rounding bug in the referenced Sherlock report. When a borrower repays a small amount relative to the vault's total outstanding debt, the proportional reduction rounds down to zero, so the vault still records the same aggregate debt even though real underlying tokens were received and the borrower's individual per-account debt (tracked separately in `market-vault`) was correctly reduced. This creates a persistent, non-reconcilable divergence between the sum of individual account debts and the vault's own bookkeeping variables that back interest accrual and share pricing.

### Finding Description
`calc-principal-ratio-reduction` performs `mul-div-down(amount, scaled_principal, debt)` [1](#0-0)  and is used inside `system-repay`:

```
(capped-amount (if (> amount debt) debt amount))
(principal-reduction (calc-principal-ratio-reduction capped-amount scaled-principal debt))
(capped-reduction (if (> principal-reduction scaled-principal) scaled-principal principal-reduction))
(updated-scaled-principal (- scaled-principal capped-reduction))
(principal-repaid (mul-div-down capped-amount total-borrowed-amount debt))
(interest-paid (- capped-amount principal-repaid))
``` [2](#0-1) 

The only preconditions checked are pause state and `(> amount u0)` — there is no assertion that `principal-reduction`/`principal-repaid` is non-zero [3](#0-2) . `debt` here is the vault's aggregate outstanding debt across all borrowers (potentially very large for a well-utilized vault), while `capped-amount` is a single borrower's chosen repayment amount. When `capped-amount * scaled-principal < debt`, `principal-reduction` rounds to `0`, and `principal-repaid` similarly rounds to `0`, so:
- `principal-scaled` (aggregate scaled debt) is left unchanged.
- `total-borrowed` (aggregate raw principal) is left unchanged.
- The entire `capped-amount` received is credited as `interest-paid` and added to `assets` via `(var-set assets (+ (var-get assets) interest-paid))`.

Meanwhile, `market.clar`'s `repay` entry point independently and correctly reduces the caller's own per-account scaled debt in `market-vault`, guarded by `(asserts! (> repaid-scaled-debt u0) ERR-INSUFFICIENT-SCALED-DEBT)` [4](#0-3) . This per-account guard prevents a *zero* reduction at the account level, but it does not protect the vault's own aggregate state — the vault-level rounding is a function of `debt` (vault-wide) not of `account-scaled-debt`, so it triggers far more easily.

Any user with an active borrow position can call `market.clar`'s `repay` repeatedly with amounts just large enough to pass the account-level check but negligible relative to the vault's total outstanding debt. Each such repayment: (a) correctly reduces their own tracked debt in `market-vault`, but (b) fails to reduce the vault's `principal-scaled`/`total-borrowed`. This progressively decouples "sum of individually tracked borrower debts" from "vault's aggregate debt used for interest accrual and index growth."

### Impact Explanation
The vault's index (and hence `total-debt`/`total-assets`, which determines zToken share price via `convert-to-assets-preview`/`convert-to-shares-preview`) continues to compound interest on `principal-scaled` that no longer corresponds to real outstanding borrower obligations, because those obligations were already partially repaid without being deducted from the vault's aggregate ledger. This inflates the notional debt (and thus the implied backing) attributed to zToken holders beyond what borrowers can actually ever repay, since the true aggregate debt (sum of all individual accounts in `market-vault`) is lower than the vault's internally tracked `principal-scaled`. Over time and repeated small repayments, this phantom debt compounds, threatening the vault's ability to honor zToken redemptions at the implied share price — a protocol insolvency risk, which falls under the in-scope "vault share math and interest accrual" category.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires only an ordinary borrow position and calling the public `repay` function with a deliberately small amount, repeated multiple times — no privileged access, oracle manipulation, or flashloan is required. The larger a vault's total outstanding debt grows (which happens naturally as the protocol scales), the easier it becomes to find repayment amounts that pass the account-level guard while rounding to zero at the vault-aggregate level, making this increasingly likely to occur (even accidentally) as the protocol matures.

### Recommendation
In `system-repay` (all `v0-vault-*.clar` contracts), validate that `principal-reduction` (and `principal-repaid`) is non-zero whenever `capped-amount > 0`, mirroring the account-level guard already present in `market.clar`'s `repay`. Alternatively, restructure the accounting so the vault's aggregate scaled debt is derived by summing/removing exact per-account scaled-debt deltas passed up from `market-vault` (which are already rounding-safe) rather than being independently recomputed via a second `mul-div-down` against vault-wide aggregates.

### Proof of Concept
1. Vault has large aggregate `debt` (e.g., from many borrowers), with `principal-scaled` and `total-borrowed` correspondingly large.
2. Borrower B has an open position with scaled debt large enough that a very small `amount` still satisfies `market.clar`'s guard `(> repaid-scaled-debt u0)` (i.e., `amount-to-repay` computed via `mul-div-up` rounds to a valid positive `scaled-debt-repayment`).
3. B calls `market.clar` `repay` with this small `amount` repeatedly (e.g., in a loop across multiple transactions).
4. Each call: `market-vault`'s per-account scaled debt for B decreases correctly; but inside `vault.system-repay`, `calc-principal-ratio-reduction(capped-amount, scaled-principal, debt)` rounds to `0` because `capped-amount` is negligible relative to vault-wide `debt`, so `principal-scaled`/`total-borrowed` remain unchanged while `assets` increases by the full `capped-amount` as "interest."
5. Repeating this widens the gap between the true aggregate borrower debt (sum of `market-vault` per-account entries) and the vault's internally tracked `principal-scaled`, inflating index growth relative to real backing.
