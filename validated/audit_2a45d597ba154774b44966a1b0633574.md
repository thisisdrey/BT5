### Title
Sender can grief a relayer in meta transactions by forcing a failed inner-action balance refund back to themselves - (File: `docs/architecture/how/meta-tx.md`, `runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs`)

### Summary
The reported lottery bug lets a user act as both the payer and the beneficiary of a fee mechanism (referral) so that a cost meant to reduce another party's (LP depositors') take is instead diverted back to the user who "created" the cost. The closest reachable analog in nearcore is in NEP-366 meta transactions: the relayer prepays the deposit for the inner actions of a `DelegateAction`, but if the inner action fails on the receiver's shard, the deposit refund is sent to the `predecessor_id` of the failed receipt, which is the `sender_id` from the `DelegateAction` (i.e., the ordinary user, "Alice"), not the relayer who actually paid for it.

### Finding Description
When a `DelegateAction` is unwrapped in `apply_delegate_action`, a brand-new action receipt is created with `predecessor_id: sender_id.clone()` (Alice) and `receiver_id: delegate_action.receiver_id()` (Bob), while the relayer only appears as `signer_id`/`signer_public_key`: [1](#0-0) 

The relayer is the one who prepaid all gas and attached deposits for these inner actions, as explicitly commented in the code: "Relayer prepaid all fees and all things required by actions: attached deposits and attached gas... If something goes wrong, deposit is refunded to the predecessor, this is sender_id/Sender in DelegateAction." [2](#0-1) 

Deposit refunds in the runtime are generated for the failed receipt and sent to `receipt.balance_refund_receiver()` (the predecessor), unlike gas refunds which go to `signer_id`: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

This means a user (Alice) can construct a `DelegateAction` whose inner action attaches a large deposit to a call that is designed to fail once it reaches Bob's shard (e.g., an unknown method, insufficient gas budgeted for exec, or a contract that reverts). The relayer pays for and prepays that deposit up front on its own shard, but the balance refund for the failed inner receipt goes back to Alice as the `predecessor_id`, not to the relayer who actually funded it. This is a direct analog of the lottery bug: a user manufactures a scenario in which a fee/cost paid by one party (relayer / LP depositors) is refunded to themselves (Alice / self-referrer) rather than to the party that bore the cost.

This exact risk is explicitly documented as a known relayer hazard: "The relayer could waste NEAR gas without compensation if Alice somehow reduces her balance…", and more precisely in the "Balance refunds in meta transactions" section: "what if the transaction fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is Alice and therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached but will fail on Bob's shard." [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation
Relayers who support meta transactions and prepay attached deposits for user-submitted `DelegateAction`s can have deposits siphoned back to the requesting user instead of themselves whenever the inner action fails at the receiver. This is a direct financial loss to the relayer, analogous to the LP depositors receiving lower fees than expected in the original report — the party economically responsible for funding the operation is not the party who is refunded.

### Likelihood Explanation
The likelihood is high for any relayer service that accepts arbitrary user-supplied `DelegateAction`s with attached deposits without simulating/dry-running the inner action first. A user only needs to submit a `DelegateAction` whose inner action deliberately fails on the receiver's shard (e.g., calling a method that doesn't exist, or attaching insufficient gas for the receiver-side execution) while attaching a large deposit.

### Recommendation
This is treated by the protocol/docs as an inherent trust assumption of meta transactions rather than a bug to be "fixed" in the runtime consensus rules (changing predecessor-based deposit-refund semantics would be a protocol-wide change affecting all receipts, not just delegate actions). At minimum, relayer implementations must be documented and required to simulate/validate the inner actions of a `DelegateAction` before submitting them on-chain, and treat any deposit-refund-on-failure path as attacker-controlled when accepting arbitrary user `DelegateAction`s. If a protocol-level fix is desired, refund routing for a receipt generated from an unwrapped `DelegateAction` could be changed to send balance refunds to the relayer (the outer transaction's signer) rather than to the `DelegateAction.sender_id`, mirroring how gas refunds already go to the relayer.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice signs a `DelegateAction` with `sender_id = Alice`, `receiver_id = Bob`, containing a single inner `FunctionCall` action with a large `deposit` and a `method_name` that does not exist on `Bob`'s contract (or otherwise guaranteed to fail on execution).
2. The relayer, unaware the call will fail, wraps this in a transaction and submits it, prepaying the deposit and gas as described in `apply_delegate_action`. [1](#0-0) 
3. On Bob's shard, the inner action receipt (with `predecessor_id = Alice`) fails execution.
4. The runtime generates a deposit refund receipt sent to `receipt.balance_refund_receiver()`, i.e. `Alice`, rather than to the relayer. [6](#0-5) 
5. Alice ends up receiving back the deposit the relayer paid for, at the relayer's expense — the same "self-refer to capture a cost paid by someone else" pattern as the original lottery referral-fee bug.

```markdown
## Balance refunds in meta transactions

Unlike gas refunds, the protocol sends balance refunds to the predecessor
(a.k.a. sender) of the receipt. This makes sense, as we deposit the attached
balance to the receiver, who has to explicitly reattach a new balance to new
receipts they might spawn.

In the world of meta transactions, this assumption is also challenged. If an
inner action requires an attached balance (for example a transfer action) then
this balance is taken from the relayer.

The relayer can see what the cost will be before submitting the meta transaction
and agrees to pay for it, so nothing wrong so far. But what if the transaction
fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is `Alice` and
therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is
something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial
incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached
but will fail on Bob's shard.
```
