## Summary

The StHEU bug's core defect is: **an actor can move value directly into the balance that backs an exchange-rate calculation, without going through the accounting path that mints/burns proportional shares, and then extract value at the resulting skewed rate.** `pallet-asset-conversion` (the AMM used across the Polkadot SDK, including for transaction fee swaps) has the exact same class of primitive: its AMM "reserves" are not tracked in a dedicated storage counter that only advances via `add_liquidity`/`swap`/`remove_liquidity`; they are read live from the pool account's raw asset balance.

## Finding Description

`get_reserves` (used by `add_liquidity`, `remove_liquidity`, `swap`, and the `quote_price_*` runtime APIs) is defined in terms of the live balance of the pool account: [1](#0-0) 

`reserve1`/`reserve2` in `do_add_liquidity` are likewise taken straight from `Self::get_balance(&pool_account, ...)`: [2](#0-1) 

`do_remove_liquidity` recomputes `amount1`/`amount2` for a burn of LP tokens from those same live `get_reserves` values, i.e. the payout per LP token is `lp_redeem_amount * reserve / total_supply`: [3](#0-2) 

Because `reserve1`/`reserve2` are the pool account's *raw* asset balance rather than an internally accounted total, anyone can transfer tokens directly into the pool account (a plain `Assets::transfer`/`Balances::transfer`, no privileged call required) and instantly change what every subsequent `add_liquidity`, `swap`, `remove_liquidity`, or `quote_price_*` call sees as the pool's reserves — exactly the "donate to inflate the balance that the exchange rate is computed from" pattern from the report. The repo's own test `cannot_block_pool_creation` demonstrates unprivileged direct transfers into a not-yet-existing pool account are a recognized, reachable primitive: [4](#0-3) 

and a prior fix (`pr_12408`) already acknowledges that raw pool-account balances (rather than tracked reserves) drive pricing, and only patched the "reducible vs full balance" nuance — not the fact that unaccounted external transfers move the price/rate at all: [5](#0-4) 

This raw-balance reserve is also consumed as a price oracle by the on-chain fee-swap logic in `pallet-asset-conversion-tx-payment`, which calls `quote_price_tokens_for_exact_tokens` to decide exactly how much of a user's alternate asset gets withdrawn to cover a fixed native fee, and then executes `swap_tokens_for_exact_tokens` against the same live reserves: [6](#0-5) 

## Impact Explanation

An unprivileged attacker (no admin, governance, validator, collator, or leaked key required) can:
1. Donate assets directly into a pool account to shift `reserve1:reserve2` away from the true LP-backed ratio.
2. Immediately call `add_liquidity` to mint LP tokens at the now-skewed ratio (since `lp_token_amount` is derived from `mul_div(amount, total_supply, reserve)`), or call `swap_tokens_for_exact_tokens`/`swap_exact_tokens_for_tokens` to extract value from existing LPs at a price that does not reflect the pool's actual pre-donation liquidity depth.
3. In the fee-payment integration, donate to shift `quote_price_tokens_for_exact_tokens` just before dispatching a transaction so that less of the attacker's alternate asset is withdrawn to cover a fixed native fee than the pool's true depth would justify, siphoning value from the pool/LPs in the process — the on-chain analog of the owner claiming more HEU than deposited after inflating `totalHEU`.

This directly maps to the "Balances, assets... must conserve value and settle exactly once to the rightful beneficiary and amount" and "public underpriced work" pivots: value is extracted from liquidity providers without an equivalent contribution, and the misuse requires no privileged role.

## Likelihood Explanation

The attack requires only ordinary signed extrinsics (`transfer`, `add_liquidity`/`swap`) available to any account with funds, executed within a single block/transaction, so there is no dependency on a malicious relayer, validator, or governance actor. The existing `cannot_block_pool_creation` test already proves unprivileged accounts can freely alter a pool account's raw balances; nothing in `add_liquidity`, `remove_liquidity`, or `swap` re-derives reserves from an independent, donation-resistant counter, so the guard the report recommends for StHEU (restricting external balance injection to a controlled state) is absent here as well.

## Recommendation

Track pool reserves in dedicated pallet storage (updated only by `do_add_liquidity`, `do_swap`, and `do_remove_liquidity`) instead of reading the pool account's live asset balance, or reconcile/quarantine any balance delta not attributable to a tracked liquidity/swap operation before it is allowed to affect `get_reserves`, `quote_price_exact_tokens_for_tokens`, and `quote_price_tokens_for_exact_tokens`.

## Proof of Concept

1. Pool `(A, B)` exists with tracked reserves `reserve_A = 10_000`, `reserve_B = 10_000`, `total_supply(LP) = 10_000`.
2. Attacker (unprivileged) calls `Assets::transfer(A, attacker, pool_account, 90_000)` directly — bypassing `add_liquidity` entirely; this is the exact analog of `donate()`. Now `get_reserves` reports `reserve_A = 100_000`, `reserve_B = 10_000` while `total_supply` is unchanged.
3. Attacker calls `add_liquidity(A, B, amount1_desired=0, amount2_desired=10_000, ..., mint_to=attacker)`. Because `side1 = mul_div(amount1, total_supply, reserve1)` and `side2 = mul_div(amount2, total_supply, reserve2)` are computed from the inflated `reserve1`, `lp_token_amount = side1.min(side2)` is computed against a false reserve basis: [7](#0-6) 
4. Attacker then calls `remove_liquidity` for both their newly minted LP tokens and (if also an existing LP or via a subsequent swap) redeems at the corrected ratio once the donated `A` is priced into `amount1 = mul_div(lp_redeem_amount, reserve1, total_supply)`, extracting a share of `B` disproportionate to genuine capital contributed, at the expense of pre-existing LPs — mirroring the StHEU owner claiming more HEU than vested after inflating `totalHEU`.
