Asset issuer can front-run a TransferAssetContract to strip its free-bandwidth quota, forcing unexpected fees on the sender - (File: `chainbase/src/main/java/org/tron/core/db/BandwidthProcessor.java`)

### Summary
`TransferAssetContract` senders rely on the token issuer's currently configured `free_asset_net_limit` / `public_free_asset_net_limit` to get free bandwidth for token transfers. These limits are stored on the `AssetIssueContract` (the "pool-level" parameter, analogous to the Lavarage lending pool's interest rate) and can be changed by the asset issuer at any time via `UpdateAssetActuator`, with no cooldown or timelock. An issuer can watch the mempool for a pending `TransferAssetContract` from a holder and front-run it with an `UpdateAssetContract` transaction that drops the limits to `0`, causing the victim's already-signed and broadcast transaction to lose its expected free-bandwidth allowance and fall through to paying real bandwidth/TRX fees, or fail outright with `AccountResourceInsufficientException`.

### Finding Description
`UpdateAssetActuator.execute()` lets the asset owner set `free_asset_net_limit` and `public_free_asset_net_limit` on the `AssetIssueCapsule` instantly and unconditionally (only bounded by `< oneDayNetLimit`), with no delay before the change takes effect: [1](#0-0) 

These fields are consulted by `BandwidthProcessor.useAssetAccountNet()` at the moment any *other* account's `TransferAssetContract` is processed, to determine whether the sender/token pool gets free bandwidth: [2](#0-1) 

If the current usage exceeds the newly-lowered limit, `useAssetAccountNet` returns `false`, and `consume()` falls through the resource waterfall (`useAccountNet` → `useFreeNet` → `useTransactionFee`) or ultimately throws `AccountResourceInsufficientException` if none succeed: [3](#0-2) 

This is directly analogous to the reported bug class: a party who controls a rate/limit parameter stored at the "pool" level (the lender's interest rate / here, the asset issuer's free bandwidth limit) can change it and have that change apply immediately to a transaction that another, unrelated user has already signed and broadcast, before it is packed into a block — exactly the front-running window described in the report. There is no mechanism (comparable to the report's suggested "pass expected/max parameter" or "apply with a delay") protecting the sender of `TransferAssetContract` from this change.

### Impact Explanation
A malicious or economically motivated asset issuer can grief holders of its token by forcing their transfers to either:
- silently consume the sender's own bandwidth/TRX balance instead of the expected free quota (unexpected fee/resource loss), or
- fail entirely with `AccountResourceInsufficientException`, wasting the sender's effort and requiring resubmission.

This matches the "mild loss to users, headache/possible refunds for the protocol" characterization the original report's judge assigned Medium severity to — an unprivileged party (the asset issuer, who is not a validator/SR/committee member) can unilaterally and instantly change a parameter that other, unrelated users' pending transactions depend on for cost calculation.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account that has issued a TRC10 asset can call `UpdateAssetContract` at will. Observing a pending `TransferAssetContract` for its own token in the mempool and racing an `UpdateAssetContract` ahead of it (e.g., via higher fee ordering when `openTransactionSort` is enabled, or same-block ordering luck) is straightforward and requires no special privilege — it is reachable by any ordinary asset-issuing account, matching the "unprivileged... asset issuer" actor class explicitly permitted by scope.

### Recommendation
Introduce a delay/cooldown (e.g., effective at the next maintenance cycle, similar to witness brokerage-ratio updates) before changes to `free_asset_net_limit`/`public_free_asset_net_limit` take effect, so that transactions already in flight are evaluated against the limits that existed when they were broadcast, not limits changed in the same or an adjacent block.

### Proof of Concept
1. Account `A` issues asset `X` with `free_asset_net_limit = L` (nonzero).
2. Holder `B` broadcasts `TransferAssetContract` transferring `X`, expecting to use the free bandwidth quota (`bytes <= L - usage`).
3. Before `B`'s transaction is packed, `A` broadcasts `UpdateAssetContract` for asset `X` setting `new_limit = 0` (allowed instantly per `UpdateAssetActuator.validate()`/`execute()` shown above).
4. If `A`'s transaction lands first (same block or earlier block), `useAssetAccountNet()` in `BandwidthProcessor` now sees `freeAssetNetLimit = 0`, returns `false` for `B`'s pending transfer.
5. `B`'s transaction falls through to `useAccountNet`/`useFreeNet`/`useTransactionFee`, consuming `B`'s own resources/TRX unexpectedly, or fails with `AccountResourceInsufficientException` if `B` has none available — despite `B` having budgeted for free bandwidth at signing time.
