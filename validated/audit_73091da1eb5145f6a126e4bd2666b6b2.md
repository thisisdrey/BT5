### Title
Permanent liability oracle/integration failure blocks health checks and liquidation, freezing insolvent accounts - (File: `programs/marginfi/src/state/marginfi_account.rs`)

### Summary
`get_health_components` prices each active `Balance` by loading its bank's oracle (or, for integrated venues, an external reserve/spot-market account) via `OraclePriceFeedAdapter::try_from_bank` [1](#0-0) . Errors on the **asset** side are tolerated (price treated as zero, per `HealthCache.internal_err`/`ORACLE_OK` semantics) [2](#0-1) , but errors on the **liability** side are explicitly re-raised and propagated out of the whole health calculation in `calc_weighted_liab_value_standalone` [3](#0-2) . This mirrors the FundsAllocator pattern where a single failing external dependency (there: an adapter/protocol call; here: a bank's price/integration oracle) aborts an entire shared operation instead of being isolated/bypassed.

### Finding Description
Any instruction that must run the risk engine on a liability position (`lending_account_borrow`, `lending_account_withdraw`, and critically `check_pre_liquidation_condition_and_get_account_health` / `check_post_liquidation_condition_and_get_account_health` used by liquidation) calls `get_health_components`, which for a liability balance calls `calc_weighted_liab_value_standalone`. Unlike the asset path, this function does not swallow oracle/integration errors — it re-throws them, and `get_health_components` propagates via `?` [4](#0-3) .

For integrated venues (Kamino, Drift, Solend, Juplend), pricing a bank also requires loading and freshness-checking an external, third-party-controlled account (e.g. Kamino reserve, Drift spot market) [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) . If that external account becomes permanently stale/unrefreshable (protocol paused, hacked, or its refresh mechanism breaks) while a marginfi account has a liability in that bank, every future oracle read for that liability position fails deterministically.

Because liability-side errors are not tolerated, this deterministic failure:
- Blocks the account owner from ever passing an init health check to unwind other positions that also need the same remaining-accounts pass (though pure repay/deposit still work per README, since those bypass the risk engine).
- Blocks third parties from liquidating the account: `check_pre_liquidation_condition_and_get_account_health` and `check_post_liquidation_condition_and_get_account_health` both call `get_health_components` with the liability bank included, so a permanently-failing liability oracle makes the account **unliquidatable** [4](#0-3) [7](#0-6) .

If the account is or becomes underwater (e.g., market moves against the borrower while the price is unreadable), it can never be liquidated, directly producing unrecoverable bad debt for the bank/depositors — a permanent state that cannot be corrected without a governance-level intervention (the report's "emergency function to remove/bypass a bad adapter" analog does not exist in this codebase for the liability pricing path).

### Impact Explanation
This can lead to insolvency/bad debt: a bank's depositors bear the loss of an unliquidatable, underwater liability position, and the position itself is durably frozen from any risk-checked action. This satisfies "insolvency/bad debt" and "permanent lock/freeze" criteria in the unprivileged margin-account/liquidation/core-accounting path.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires a third-party integration's price/reserve account to become permanently stale or otherwise unloadable (e.g., protocol pause, hack, migration, or a data-account closure) while a marginfi account holds a liability in that bank. Third-party protocol outages/hacks are realistic external events outside marginfi's control, and the codebase intentionally supports many external integrations (Kamino, Drift, Solend, Juplend) whose refresh/freshness accounts are outside marginfi's governance. The likelihood is moderate — it is contingent on an external event, but once it occurs the DOS is deterministic and total for the affected account's liability side.

### Recommendation
Add a governance/emergency mechanism analogous to the fix cited in the report ("bypass failed adapter calls"): allow a privileged path (or the bankruptcy/liquidation flow specifically) to price a permanently-unreadable liability using a fallback/last-known-good/administrator-set price (similar to `OracleSetup::Fixed`) so that liquidation and bankruptcy resolution can proceed even when a third-party integration is irrecoverably broken, rather than having the liability-side `?` unconditionally abort the whole risk computation.

### Proof of Concept
1. Group admin creates a `KaminoPythPush` (or Drift/Solend) bank; a borrower takes a liability in that bank, with sufficient collateral elsewhere.
2. The underlying integration protocol (Kamino/Drift/Solend) is paused, hacked, or its reserve/spot-market account becomes permanently stale beyond `ensure_*_fresh` tolerance.
3. Market price of the collateral drops, making the account liquidatable.
4. A liquidator calls `lending_account_liquidate`; `check_pre_liquidation_condition_and_get_account_health` → `get_health_components` attempts to price the liability bank, hits `OraclePriceFeedAdapter::try_from_bank` failure (stale/unreadable), and `calc_weighted_liab_value_standalone` re-raises the error, aborting the whole liquidation transaction [3](#0-2) .
5. No other code path allows the liability to be repriced or forcibly liquidated/bankrupted, so the bad debt persists indefinitely, confirmed by the explicit "propagate the original oracle error" comment contrasted with the documented behavior for asset-side errors that are deliberately ignored [8](#0-7) .
