#No vulnerability found for this question.

The premise fails on both prongs. `wnear_id` is fixed once at contract initialization via `DefuseConfig` and stored in `Contract::state.wnear_id` [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 
— there is no unprivileged (or any) setter that lets an attacker change it post-deployment; it is only supplied by the deployer in `new`. An unprivileged attacker cannot make `self.wnear_id()` point at a contract they control.

NEAR cross-contract promises also cannot "partially succeed": `near_withdraw` on the wNEAR contract either completes atomically (burning the exact `U128` amount and transferring exactly that amount of NEAR to the predecessor) or fails atomically, and `promise_result_checked_void(0)` in `do_native_withdraw` checks the result of promise index 0 of that specific `.then()` chain — the very `near_withdraw` call scheduled for *this* `withdraw.amount* [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 
. The transferred amount in `Promise::new(withdraw.receiver_id).transfer(withdraw.amount)` is the same `withdraw.amount` value that was (a) already debited from the signer's own wNEAR balance via `internal_sub_balance`/`withdraw` before the promise was even scheduled, and (b) passed unmodified as the argument to `near_withdraw` [5](#0-4) 
. There is no code path where a different, larger amount is substituted, nor any aggregation of unrelated promise results across users/batches — each `NativeWithdraw` intent produces its own independent promise chain scoped to that single debit. The equality "NEAR transferred == NEAR unwrapped for this withdraw" therefore holds by construction, and the existing `require!(promise_result_checked_void(0).is_ok())` gate correctly enforces that the specific unwrap for this withdraw succeeded before forwarding funds.
