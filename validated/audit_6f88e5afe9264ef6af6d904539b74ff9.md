### Title
DoS of full liquidations via frontrunning `lending_account_liquidate` with an exact `asset_amount` - ([File: programs/marginfi/src/instructions/marginfi_account/liquidate.rs])

### Summary
`lending_account_liquidate` requires the liquidator to specify an exact, precomputed `asset_amount` of collateral to seize. The instruction enforces `pre_balance >= asset_amount` and reverts with `OverliquidationAttempt` if the liquidatee's current asset balance is smaller than what the liquidator specified. Because liquidation is permissionless and can be triggered by anyone (including the liquidatee's own secondary account or an unrelated griefer), an attacker can frontrun the liquidator's transaction with a small partial liquidation that shrinks the liquidatee's asset balance just below the liquidator's target amount, causing the liquidator's carefully sized transaction to revert.

### Finding Description
The liquidation instruction takes `asset_amount` as a fixed input from the liquidator: [1](#0-0) 

The doc comment explicitly acknowledges that `asset_amount` is bounded by the liquidatee's actual balance and that exceeding it fails the instruction: [2](#0-1) 

The enforcement is a hard check against the liquidatee's *current* pre-balance at execution time: [3](#0-2) 

Since `lending_account_liquidate` is permissionless (any account holding a valid, healthy marginfi account with compatible asset tags can call it, as seen in the fuzz harness and integration tests), a griefer — or the liquidatee routing through a second wallet they control — can submit a tiny liquidation (e.g. seizing a dust amount of the same collateral) that lands *before* the "real" liquidator's transaction. This reduces the liquidatee's on-chain `asset_shares`/balance for that bank so that, by the time the honest liquidator's transaction executes, `pre_balance < asset_amount`, and the check at line 314-317 reverts the whole transaction with `OverliquidationAttempt` — this is the direct analog of the underflow-based DoS described in the external DSCEngine report, except here it's an explicit bounds check rather than an arithmetic underflow, but the effect (a griefer can force a precisely-sized liquidation to always fail) is the same.

The codebase itself documents an operational workaround rather than a code-level fix: liquidators are told to conservatively undersize their liquidation amount (~70-80% of the negative health amount) to reduce, but not eliminate, this exposure: [4](#0-3) 

Fuzzing/test infrastructure also acknowledges there is no "liquidate-all" sentinel (`u64::MAX`) supported — callers must precompute an exact amount and are told to subtract a manual buffer to avoid exactly this problem: [5](#0-4) 

This confirms the protocol has no `type(uint256).max`-style "liquidate maximum available" parameter, which is exactly the mitigation recommended in the referenced report.

### Impact Explanation
This is a griefing/DoS vector rather than direct fund theft: a malicious actor (including the liquidatee themselves, using a secondary healthy account) can repeatedly frontrun exact-sized liquidations, forcing them to fail and be resubmitted with smaller, less efficient amounts, or forcing liquidators into a race of shrinking target sizes. This can delay full re-collateralization of unhealthy accounts, leave residual/dust debt on the books, and — under volatile market conditions — increase the risk of an account becoming insolvent (bad debt / bankruptcy) before it can be fully liquidated, since each failed liquidation attempt costs time and gives prices more opportunity to move further against the position. It does not directly cause fund loss on its own, but degrades the timeliness/effectiveness of the liquidation engine, which is a core solvency-protection mechanism.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is real but not trivial to exploit at scale: the attacker must open/maintain a marginfi account satisfying `validate_asset_tags` for both the asset and liability banks involved, must observe the liquidator's transaction in the mempool/priority queue (or race under leader scheduling on Solana), and must have enough of the relevant collateral/liability tokens to execute even a dust-sized liquidation on the target. This is easiest for the liquidatee to execute against themselves using a second wallet they control, or for a well-funded MEV searcher already active in this niche. The protocol's own documentation acknowledges liquidators must size targets conservatively specifically because of this class of failure, indicating the maintainers are aware the exact-amount interface is brittle to this kind of interference.

### Recommendation
Allow liquidators to signal "liquidate up to the liquidatee's full outstanding balance" (e.g., via a sentinel value such as `u64::MAX` for `asset_amount`) so that the actual amount used is clamped to the liquidatee's live `pre_balance` at execution time instead of failing when the precomputed value no longer matches. This mirrors the recommendation in the referenced report and removes the incentive/possibility for frontrunning-based griefing of exact-amount liquidations, while preserving the existing post-liquidation health-improvement checks.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual PoC (adapting the pattern from `programs/marginfi/tests/user_actions/liquidate.rs` and the fuzz harness in `trident-tests/fuzz_0/methods/core.rs`):
1. Liquidatee account becomes unhealthy (health check triggers `check_pre_liquidation_condition_and_get_account_health`).
2. Liquidator observes the account and computes `asset_amount = X` corresponding to the liquidatee's exact live collateral balance, and submits `lending_account_liquidate(asset_amount = X, ...)`.
3. Before that transaction lands, the liquidatee (via a second, healthy marginfi account they control, satisfying `validate_asset_tags`) calls `lending_account_liquidate` against themselves with a dust `asset_amount`, decrementing their own asset balance for that bank.
4. When the original liquidator's transaction executes, `pre_balance` (now `< X`) fails the check at [6](#0-5) , reverting with `OverliquidationAttempt` and forcing the liquidator to resubmit with a reduced/inefficient amount, repeating the cycle.
