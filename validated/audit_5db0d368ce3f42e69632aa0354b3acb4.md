## Title
Contract owners can front-run `TriggerSmartContract` calls via `UpdateSettingContract` to spike a caller's energy fee - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java`)

### Summary
`UpdateSettingContractActuator` lets a smart contract's deployer change `consume_user_resource_percent` at any time with no delay, timelock, or caller-side slippage protection. This mirrors the Sherlock finding where `setReferrerFee()` could be front-run to change a fee applied to another party's in-flight transaction — here the "fee" is the share of a `TriggerSmartContract` call's energy cost that the caller (vs. the contract owner) must pay.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` writes the new `consume_user_resource_percent` directly to the `ContractCapsule` as soon as the transaction is packed into a block, with no waiting period: [1](#0-0) 

Validation only checks that `msg.sender` is the contract's `origin_address` and that the percent is within `[0, 100]` — there is no restriction on how often or how quickly it can be changed: [2](#0-1) 

This value is read live at execution time in `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio()`, which determines how the total energy limit (and thus how much TRX-equivalent energy) is charged to the caller versus the contract owner for that specific call: [3](#0-2) 

The actual bill split at settlement time uses the same live percent value again in `ReceiptCapsule.payEnergyBill()`: [4](#0-3) 

Because a contract owner can observe a pending `TriggerSmartContract` transaction in the mempool and broadcast an `UpdateSettingContract` transaction with higher priority/fee to land first in the same or an earlier block, the owner can raise `consume_user_resource_percent` toward 100% right before the caller's transaction executes — shifting nearly the entire energy cost of that call onto the caller, then reverting it afterward. This is structurally identical to the referenced bug class: an actor with unilateral control over a fee parameter front-runs another party's transaction to temporarily spike the fee applied to it.

### Impact Explanation
A caller who priced in a low expected energy split can be forced, without any prior notice beyond their own `feeLimit`, to pay significantly more TRX for the same call than intended — up to their full `feeLimit`. This is a direct, repeatable fund-extraction vector for any deployed contract's owner against any user who calls it, with no slippage/cap mechanism protecting the caller from a manipulated split at execution time.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any contract owner can trigger this at will by simply broadcasting `UpdateSettingContract` transactions around target callers' `TriggerSmartContract` transactions; no special privileges beyond being the contract's `origin_address` are needed, and the mempool provides the visibility needed to front-run.

### Recommendation
Introduce a delay/cooldown before a `consume_user_resource_percent` change takes effect (e.g., applying only after N blocks), or allow the caller of `TriggerSmartContract` to specify an acceptable percentage bound so the transaction reverts if the live value is less favorable than expected at signing time.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker deploys/owns a contract `C` with `consume_user_resource_percent = 0` (owner pays all energy).
2. Victim signs and broadcasts `TriggerSmartContract` calling `C`, expecting to pay little/no energy fee, setting `feeLimit` high enough to tolerate normal execution.
3. Attacker observes the pending transaction and broadcasts `UpdateSettingContract` setting `consume_user_resource_percent = 100`, using a higher fee/earlier nonce ordering so it is included before the victim's transaction (`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute` at `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java:41-49`).
4. Victim's transaction executes under the new 100% split; `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio` and `ReceiptCapsule.payEnergyBill` now charge the victim the full energy cost instead of the expected owner-subsidized cost.
5. Attacker can then reset `consume_user_resource_percent` back to 0 for future calls, repeating the attack against subsequent victims.
