### No vulnerability found for this question.

**Rationale:**

The `deadline` field in `DefusePayload` is chosen entirely by the signer, and is enforced independently of the TonConnect `timestamp` field at the point of execution: `Engine::execute_signed_intent` unconditionally checks `deadline < Timestamp::now()` [1](#0-0)  using the real wall-clock time at execution, not anything derived from the signed `timestamp`. This check is correct and sufficient regardless of what `timestamp` value the signer embedded.

The `p.deadline < self.timestamp` check inside `TonConnectPayload::extract_defuse_payload` is a self-consistency sanity check (deadline must not precede the claimed signing time) explicitly documented as a deliberate relaxation from asserting `timestamp <= now()`, to avoid breaking `simulate_intents()` right after signing [2](#0-1) . It is not, and was never intended to be, the mechanism that bounds staleness — that job belongs solely to the `deadline < Timestamp::now()` check in the engine.

Setting `timestamp` and `deadline` both "far in the future" is not something an attacker can inject — it is a choice made by the signer when producing their own signature. A victim choosing to sign an intent valid for a very long window is equivalent to placing a long-lived limit order; this is standard, intended behavior for an intent-based system, not an exploit. For a `token_diff` intent specifically, the settled amounts are exactly the diff values embedded in the signed intent itself and matched via `TransferMatcher`/`finalize` — the contract does not re-price the diff at execution time based on any oracle, so "price changes benefiting the attacker" do not translate into the attacker extracting more value than the diff the victim explicitly signed. The nonce mechanism (`commit_nonce`) still guarantees the signed intent settles exactly once [3](#0-2) , matching the claimed binding exactly.

Since the number-of-settlements invariant holds, and the settled value is exactly what the signer authorized (not something manipulated by an attacker), there is no divergence in the equality the question poses, and no Critical/High impact per the rules — this is documented, intended behavior, as the question itself acknowledges.
