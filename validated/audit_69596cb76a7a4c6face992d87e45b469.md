### Title
Exchange creator can be sandwiched during ExchangeInject/ExchangeWithdraw due to missing slippage protection - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java, actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java)

### Summary
`ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` compute the counter-token amount from the Bancor-relay pool's current spot balances at execution time, with no caller-supplied expected/minimum bound, unlike `ExchangeTransactionContract` which enforces a `tokenExpected` slippage check. An unprivileged trader can broadcast an `ExchangeTransactionContract` immediately before, and a reversing one immediately after, an exchange creator's `inject`/`withdraw` transaction within the same block (or adjacent blocks under an SR's control of ordering), manipulating the pool ratio at the moment the inject/withdraw executes and then restoring it, extracting value from the creator's liquidity operation.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeInjectActuator.execute()` reads `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` from the `ExchangeCapsule` and derives `anotherTokenQuant` as a simple ratio of these balances against the injected `tokenQuant`, with no lower/upper bound parameter from the caller: [4](#0-3) . The `ExchangeInjectContract` protobuf definition confirms it only carries `owner_address`, `exchange_id`, `token_id`, and `quant` — no expected/slippage field. Likewise, `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()` derives `anotherTokenQuant` from the same live ratio with no bound: [5](#0-4) , and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` likewise lacks such a field.

By contrast, `ExchangeTransactionActuator` — the actuator meant for actual trading — explicitly requires and validates a caller-supplied `expected` minimum output: [6](#0-5) . Both `ExchangeInjectActuator` and `ExchangeWithdrawActuator` require `msg.sender` to equal the exchange's `CreatorAddress` (only the pool creator may inject or withdraw) [7](#0-6) , but any third-party account can still submit `ExchangeTransactionContract` trades against the same pool that shift `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` right up to the moment the creator's inject/withdraw is applied.

This mirrors the analog reported for `InfinityPool.pour()`: a liquidity/inject-style operation is priced off the pool's mutable, attacker-influenceable instantaneous state within the same block, and the state-shifting party (here, the trader who moves the ratio, rather than a "reset+unwind" loan) can reverse their own position immediately afterward at comparatively low cost (only the AMM's Bancor-curve round-trip slippage/fee, versus a direct free reversal in the original report), while the party who exercised the liquidity operation locks in value at the manipulated ratio.

### Impact Explanation
An exchange creator who injects or withdraws liquidity can have their `anotherTokenQuant` mis-priced by an amount proportional to how far an attacker shifts the pool's `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` ratio beforehand via `ExchangeTransactionContract`, and restores afterward. This causes the creator to receive/deposit tokens at an off-market rate, transferring value to the attacker — a concrete loss of funds for the exchange creator (medium impact, contained to whatever balance/precision the specific exchange's reserves allow, bounded by `dynamicStore.getExchangeBalanceLimit()`).

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate-to-high: broadcasting `ExchangeTransactionContract` transactions is unrestricted (any account, `calcFee()` returns 0 for this actuator per [8](#0-7) ), and an attacker only needs to observe a pending `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` in the mempool for a targeted exchange pool and race to sandwich it, or collude with block-producing ordering, within the transaction confirmation window.

### Recommendation
Add an `expected`/minimum (and/or maximum) bound field to `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract`, mirroring the check already present in `ExchangeTransactionActuator` (`tokenExpected`), and enforce it in `ExchangeInjectActuator.execute()`/`doValidate()` and `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()`/`doValidate()` before committing the new balances, so the exchange creator can reject execution if the counter-token amount is not within an acceptable range.

### Proof of Concept
1. Exchange creator broadcasts an `ExchangeInjectContract` (or `ExchangeWithdrawContract`) to add/remove liquidity from exchange `E` with `firstTokenBalance = A`, `secondTokenBalance = B`.
2. Before it is applied, an attacker broadcasts an `ExchangeTransactionContract` selling a large amount of token0 into `E`, shifting the ratio so `firstTokenBalance` increases and `secondTokenBalance` decreases sharply (per `ExchangeCapsule.transaction()` [9](#0-8) ).
3. The creator's inject/withdraw executes against this skewed ratio, computing `anotherTokenQuant` unfavorably to the creator (see `ExchangeInjectActuator.execute()` lines 71-83 / `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()` lines 77-89).
4. The attacker broadcasts a reversing `ExchangeTransactionContract` (selling token1 back) restoring the ratio close to its original state, minus the Bancor-curve round-trip cost, retaining the value extracted from the creator's mis-priced inject/withdraw.
