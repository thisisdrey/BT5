### Title
Rounding-dust TRX permanently lost when participating in an AssetIssue with a non-exact `trxNum`/`num` ratio - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ParticipateAssetIssueActuator.java`)

### Summary
`ParticipateAssetIssueActuator.execute()` debits the full TRX amount (`cost`) from the caller's balance but credits the caller with only `floorDiv(cost * num, trxNum)` units of the asset. When `trxNum` does not evenly divide `cost * num`, the fractional remainder of TRX paid buys strictly less than its fair share of the asset and is not returned to the payer nor recorded anywhere recoverable — it is permanently absorbed into the `toAddress` account's TRX balance with no corresponding decrease in that account's asset holdings, exactly mirroring the `AllocationVesting` pattern where the full `amount` is debited while only `amount / lockToTokenRatio` is credited.

### Finding Description
In `execute()`: [1](#0-0) 
the full `cost` is subtracted from `ownerAccount.getBalance()`, and `cost` (in full) is credited to `toAccount.getBalance()`. Meanwhile the asset amount transferred is computed as:
```
long exchangeAmount = multiplyExact(cost, assetIssueCapsule.getNum());
exchangeAmount = floorDiv(exchangeAmount, assetIssueCapsule.getTrxNum());
```
This is an integer floor division identical in structure to the reported `amount / lockToTokenRatio` truncation in `AllocationVesting`. Whenever `cost * num` is not an exact multiple of `trxNum`, the caller pays the full TRX `cost` but receives an asset amount rounded down, losing up to `(trxNum-1)/trxNum` of a token's worth of TRX value per participation, with no mechanism to reclaim the dust. The `validate()` path only checks `exchangeAmount <= 0` to reject degenerate trades; it does not reject or refund non-exact ratios the way `ExchangeWithdrawActuator` does (which explicitly rejects imprecise trades with "Not precise enough", see `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java:74-89` using `BigInteger` and requiring `longValueExact()`). [2](#0-1) 

### Impact Explanation
Any unprivileged account can call `ParticipateAssetIssueContract` (via `ParticipateAssetIssueActuator`) to buy a token-issuer's asset with TRX. Every such call where `cost * num % trxNum != 0` causes the buyer to overpay relative to what they receive; the dust is absorbed by the token issuer's account as unbacked TRX (received in full even though the corresponding asset amount owed was rounded down). Repeated/automated participation transactions can accumulate this dust systematically, representing a real, unrecoverable value loss for participants and an unearned gain for the issuer.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires no special privilege — any account with TRX and a target `AssetIssueContract` whose `trxNum`/`num` ratio is not an integer divisor of the chosen `cost` will trigger the truncation on every single participation call. Given many asset issuances use non-power-of-ten or otherwise non-divisor ratios, this is trivially and repeatedly reachable through the standard `ParticipateAssetIssueContract` transaction path.

### Recommendation
Mirror the approach already used in `ExchangeWithdrawActuator`: reject transactions where the ratio conversion is not exact (e.g., validate that `cost * num % trxNum == 0`, or refund/round the `cost` charged to the buyer down to the value that exactly corresponds to `exchangeAmount` before debiting/crediting balances), so that no TRX value is silently lost to rounding.

### Proof of Concept
1. Issuer creates an `AssetIssueContract` with `num = 3`, `trxNum = 7` (i.e., 3 tokens per 7 TRX).
2. Buyer calls `ParticipateAssetIssueContract` with `amount = 10` (TRX sun).
3. `exchangeAmount = floorDiv(10 * 3, 7) = floorDiv(30, 7) = 4`.
4. Buyer's TRX balance decreases by the full `10`; buyer receives `4` units of asset (worth `4*7/3 ≈ 9.33` TRX at the stated rate), losing `~0.67` TRX-equivalent to rounding — TRX that is fully credited to the issuer's `toAddress` balance without a corresponding proportional decrease in the issuer's asset holdings beyond `4` units.
5. Repeating this call accumulates unrecoverable dust loss for the buyer on every non-exact-ratio participation.
