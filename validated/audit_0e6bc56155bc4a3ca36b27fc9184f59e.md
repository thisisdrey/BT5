### Title
Front-runnable lump-sum emissions injection lets a whale steal accumulated depositor rewards - (File: `programs/marginfi/src/instructions/marginfi_group/configure_bank.rs`)

### Summary
`lending_pool_emissions_deposit` is a **permissionless** instruction that atomically injects `amount` of the bank's own mint into the liquidity vault and instantly raises `asset_share_value` for every current shareholder, pro-rata to `total_asset_shares` at that exact instant. Because the boost is applied as a single, discrete, mempool-visible event (rather than continuously accrued like interest), and because deposits/withdrawals have no minimum holding period or cooldown, any address can front-run the reward injection with a large deposit, capture a large share of the reward, and withdraw immediately afterward — exactly the MEV pattern described in the external report ("periodic/discrete distribution + proportionate rewards + predictable timing = front-runnable reward theft").

### Finding Description
`lending_pool_emissions_deposit` computes the new share value from the bank's *current* `total_asset_shares` read at call time: [1](#0-0) 

There is no snapshotting of "shares as of some earlier block" and no accrual/vesting period — the entire `amount` is distributed instantly to whoever holds shares in that same transaction/slot. The instruction is explicitly documented and marked as permissionless: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

`lending_account_deposit` and `lending_account_withdraw` have no time-lock, cooldown, or minimum-holding-period check — a user can deposit and then withdraw the same funds (plus any accrued gain) as soon as the next instruction/transaction is processed, subject only to a standard health check: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

This combination reproduces all three properties flagged in the external report:
1. **Periodic/discrete distribution** – the reward is delivered in a single lump sum at the moment `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` lands, rather than smoothly compounding like `accrue_interest`'s per-second APR model (which, by contrast, is immune to this class of attack since `time_delta` since `last_update` ties any gain to real elapsed holding time: [6](#0-5) ).
2. **Proportionate rewards regardless of duration** – the share-value bump is purely `amount / total_asset_shares`, with zero time-weighting, confirmed by the project's own test helper: [7](#0-6) 
3. **Predictable/observable timing** – because the instruction is permissionless and publicly callable (typically by the protocol's own reward-funding bot on a schedule), the pending transaction is visible in the mempool/public transaction flow, giving a searcher the exact window to front-run it.

### Impact Explanation
An MEV searcher who detects a pending `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` for a bank can, within the same or an adjacent slot:
1. Deposit a very large amount into that bank via `lending_account_deposit`.
2. Let the emissions-deposit transaction land, which raises `asset_share_value` for the whole pool, giving the searcher's newly-minted shares an immediate windfall proportional to their (momentary) share of `total_asset_shares`.
3. Withdraw immediately via `lending_account_withdraw`, realizing the gain and exiting.

This siphons value that should have accrued to genuine long-term depositors of that bank into the attacker's pocket — a concrete, unauthorized transfer of value (theft of accumulated reward) at the expense of unprivileged depositors, matching the "Accept" criteria of concrete theft/unauthorized transfer.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is **moderate**: it requires (a) an actor calling `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` with a non-trivial `amount` relative to the bank's TVL, and (b) the searcher/attacker being able to observe and race that transaction (feasible on Solana via priority fees/leader-slot targeting, similar to standard Solana MEV sandwich techniques). It does not require any privileged role — the deposit/withdraw functions used by the attacker are fully permissionless user actions. The main mitigating factor is that this instruction may in practice be used rarely/only for small "top-up" amounts, and large deposits/withdrawals will themselves need enough liquidity headroom (deposit caps) and gas/priority-fee cost to be profitable, so exploitability scales with the emitted amount and bank size.

### Recommendation
- Time-weight or vest `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` distributions instead of an instant lump-sum share-value bump — e.g., stream the injected amount into `asset_share_value` gradually over a window (similar to how `accrue_interest` ties gains to `time_delta`), or snapshot `total_asset_shares` from a prior, already-finalized slot/timestamp rather than the value read in the same transaction.
- Alternatively/additionally, enforce a minimum holding period (or a deposit cooldown before eligible withdrawal) for balances that would otherwise let a single-transaction deposit→reward→withdraw sequence extract a disproportionate share.
- Consider restricting `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` to admin/authorized callers if the permissionless design is not a hard product requirement, reducing the surface for adversarial timing.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual sequence (mirrors the external report's `test_YieldMEVAttack`), unverifiable without a running test harness but directly supported by the code paths cited above:
1. Bank `B` has depositor Alice with a long-standing position; `B.total_asset_shares = S`.
2. A reward-funding transaction calling `lending_pool_emissions_deposit(bank=B, amount=R)` is observed pending (permissionless, no access control beyond bank not being paused/reduce-only, see `LendingPoolEmissionsDeposit` accounts struct: [8](#0-7) ).
3. Attacker Bob submits `lending_account_deposit(bank=B, amount=W)` with `W >> S` in the same/adjacent slot, ahead of the emissions-deposit tx, so `total_asset_shares` becomes `S' = S + shares(W)`.
4. `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` executes: `asset_share_value` increases by `R / (S' * asset_share_value)` fraction, of which Bob's shares capture `shares(W) / S' ≈ W/(W+S_value)` of `R`.
5. Bob immediately calls `lending_account_withdraw(bank=B, withdraw_all=true)`, realizing `W` plus his captured slice of `R`, with negligible holding time, at Alice's expense.

Note: I was not able to execute this against the live bankrun/Anchor test suite to obtain exact numeric confirmation (no terminal/test execution available in this environment); the analysis is based on direct code-path review of `configure_bank.rs`, `deposit.rs`, and `withdraw.rs`. A background Devin session with test-harness access could adapt the existing `emissions_deposit.rs` tests to add a "front-run" scenario (deposit → emissions_deposit → immediate withdraw_all in one bankrun transaction batch) to empirically confirm the profit extraction.

  );

  );
