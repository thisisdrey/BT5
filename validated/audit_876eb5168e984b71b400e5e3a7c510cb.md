### Title
Permissionless `accrue()` griefing zeroes-out the DAO treasury's protocol fee share via rounding-down precision loss - (File: mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-stx.clar and its sibling vaults)

### Summary
`accrue()` in each vault (`v0-vault-stx.clar`, `v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, `v0-vault-usdc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`) is a public function with no caller-authorization check, unlike `system-borrow`/`system-repay` which require `check-caller-auth` [2](#0-1) . Any principal can call it repeatedly. Each call computes the treasury's protocol-fee cut of newly accrued interest as `reserve-inc = mul-div-down(debt-delta, fee-reserve, BPS)` and only mints treasury LP shares `if (> reserve-inc u0)` [3](#0-2) . Because `debt-delta` scales with the time elapsed since `last-update`, and `last-update` is reset to the current block time whenever the index changes at all [4](#0-3) , an attacker can call `accrue` frequently enough that each `debt-delta * fee-reserve` product falls below `BPS` (10000) and rounds down to zero — exactly the "funding fee rounds to zero" precision-loss class from the source report, applied here to the protocol's fee-reserve/treasury cut instead of a perp funding fee.

### Finding Description
The interest-accrual math is:
- `next-index` grows the borrow index by `rate * time-delta / SECONDS_PER_YEAR_BPS`, rounded up for debt but the derived `debt-delta` for fee purposes is computed via floor division twice in `accrue` (`old-debt`, `new-debt` both `mul-div-down`) [5](#0-4) .
- The DAO's cut of that interest is `reserve-inc = mul-div-down(debt-delta, fee-reserve, BPS)` — a second floor-division on top of the already-floored `debt-delta` [6](#0-5) .
- Crucially, `last-update` only advances when the index itself changes at all, so as soon as one call bumps the index by even 1 index-unit, the "clock" resets and the next call starts measuring `debt-delta` from a fresh, tiny `time-delta` again [7](#0-6) .
- Since `accrue` is a `define-public` function with zero access control (no `check-dao-auth` or `check-caller-auth`), and is also externally reachable and permissionless via the market router's `vault-accrue` [1](#0-0) , any principal can spam `accrue` (directly or through its own deployed contract) at a cadence fine enough that each call's `debt-delta` is small — making `reserve-inc` repeatedly floor to `0` and skip the treasury LP mint (`(if (> reserve-inc u0) (try! (ft-mint? zft treasury-lp .dao-treasury)) false)`), while the raw interest still fully accrues into `total-debt`/`total-assets` for the benefit of depositors [8](#0-7) . This is the same root-cause pattern as the referenced report: a percentage/utilization-style rate is derived through chained floor divisions over a small numerator, so the derived fee is a "fee-of-a-fee" that rounds to zero far more often than the underlying interest itself, permanently losing the protocol's share while the rest of the accounting proceeds normally.

Unlike the original report's natural low-liquidity scenario, here the trigger is deliberately controllable by an attacker: by choosing the calling cadence, the attacker forces `debt-delta` (and hence `reserve-inc`) to be systematically minimized every time, maximizing the number of times the treasury fee rounds to zero over the life of the vault.

### Impact Explanation
The DAO treasury's protocol fee (its unclaimed yield/royalty share of interest, represented as `treasury-lp`/`zft` shares minted to `.dao-treasury`) is permanently and repeatedly lost whenever `reserve-inc` floors to zero, since that portion of interest is never captured as treasury shares — it is not deferred or accumulated across calls, it is simply skipped for that increment forever. This matches the in-scope "High - theft of unclaimed yield or royalties, permanent freezing of unclaimed yield or royalties" impact class. Depositors are unaffected (they still receive the full underlying interest since `total-assets`/`total-debt` accounting is unaffected), but the protocol's fee revenue stream can be ground down toward zero by a griefing actor across all six vaults using this identical pattern.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is high: `accrue` requires no permission, no capital, and no privileged role — it is directly callable by any ordinary principal or their own deployed contract, and is cheap to call repeatedly (only gated by chain throughput/fees). The attack does not require any specific pool imbalance (unlike the original report which needed low reserves relative to interest); the attacker directly controls the call cadence and thus the size of `debt-delta` per call.

### Recommendation
Accumulate the reserve/treasury fee across calls (e.g., track a running remainder or accrue fee in higher-precision units and mint only when it crosses a whole-share threshold) rather than computing and discarding `reserve-inc` independently on every `accrue()` call. Alternatively, restrict how frequently `accrue()`'s state (specifically `last-update`) can be reset, or compute the treasury share using round-up arithmetic reconciled against a persistent fee-debt accumulator so that fractional amounts are never silently dropped.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker deploys no special contract; simply calls the vault's public `accrue()` (or `market::vault-accrue`) once per block for many consecutive blocks (or as fast as `stacks-block-time` grants distinct timestamps).
2. Each call: `debt-delta = new-debt - old-debt` is small because `time-delta` since the previous call's index-changing update is small; `reserve-inc = mul-div-down(debt-delta, fee-reserve, BPS)` evaluates to `0` because `debt-delta * fee-reserve < BPS`.
3. Because `reserve-inc == 0`, the `(if (> reserve-inc u0) (try! (ft-mint? zft treasury-lp .dao-treasury)) false)` branch is skipped every time [9](#0-8) , yet `index`/`lindex` still advance and `last-update` resets, so the loss is never recovered on a later, larger `debt-delta`.
4. Repeating over the life of the vault, the DAO treasury's cumulative protocol fee share is measurably smaller than it would be if `accrue()` were called at a natural, less frequent cadence (e.g., once per user action) — a direct, permanent reduction of unclaimed treasury yield attributable purely to the caller-controlled accrual cadence.
