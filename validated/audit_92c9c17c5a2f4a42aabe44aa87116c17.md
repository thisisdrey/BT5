### Title
Loan NFT seller can front-run a buyer by draining claimable cashflows before the NFT transfer - (File: tare-io__tare-contracts/contracts/LoansNFT.sol)

### Summary
The loan NFT is the on-chain title to a loan’s investor cashflows, but the contract set does not couple transfer of that title with payout of the claimable principal and interest held in `Loans.sol`. [1](#0-0)  `Loans.investorWithdraw` pays the current owner or unlocker without marking the NFT as claimed, and `LoansNFT._update` only enforces the ERC-5753 lock, so a seller can call `investorWithdraw` immediately before a `transferFrom` and leave the buyer with an NFT whose claimable balance is zero. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2)  This mirrors the lottery ticket front-run: claimable value is separable from the NFT transfer.  The protocol’s own `LoansExchange` is atomic and locks the NFT during an offer, so the attack does not apply to offers settled through that exchange; however, the NFT remains freely transferable outside the exchange. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

### Finding Description
- `Loans.investorWithdraw` authorizes the caller by reading `ownerAndUnlocker` from `LoansNFT`: if the token is unlocked, the owner (or an admin) can withdraw all net interest and principal payable. [2](#0-1) 
- The per-loan helper `_processInvestorWithdrawal` computes `_getNetInterestPayableToInvestor` and `_getNetPrincipalPayableToInvestor` and withdraws those amounts to the chosen recipient. [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) [9](#0-8) 
- Because the withdrawal only updates ledger balances and does not mutate or burn the NFT, the seller remains the owner and can transfer the token afterward. [10](#0-9) [11](#0-10) 
- `LoansNFT._update` checks only whether the token is locked and whether the caller is the unlocker; it never consults `Loans.getLoanValues` or prevents a transfer after the claimable cashflows have been drained. [3](#0-2) [1](#0-0) 
- `Loans.getLoanValues` exposes `investorPrincipalWithdrawable` and `investorInterestWithdrawable`, which a buyer would reasonably use to price the NFT, but these values can be zeroed by a front-running `investorWithdraw`. [1](#0-0) 
- The `LoansExchange` path mitigates the issue for its own offers because `createOffer` locks the NFT to the exchange and `acceptOffer` atomically transfers the NFT and payment; while locked, only the exchange can move the NFT, and the exchange does not call `investorWithdraw`. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation
A buyer who purchases a loan NFT based on its published claimable cashflows can lose those cashflows entirely if the seller front-runs the purchase with `investorWithdraw`. [2](#0-1) [7](#0-6)  The seller receives both the sale proceeds and the withdrawn USDC, while the buyer receives an NFT whose immediate claimable value has been drained. [10](#0-9) [3](#0-2)  This is a diversion of loan cashflows from an honest buyer and a corruption of the NFT’s economic value, falling under the Tare allowed impact of theft/diversion of USDC or loan cashflows from honest users. 

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires only that the seller is the current owner of a loan NFT with positive `investorPrincipalWithdrawable` or `investorInterestWithdrawable`. [1](#0-0)  The seller monitors the mempool for a buyer’s purchase transaction, submits `investorWithdraw` with higher priority, and then the buyer’s `transferFrom` executes. [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2)  No privileged role is needed; the seller uses the legitimate owner authorization of `investorWithdraw`. [12](#0-11)  The protocol does not require claims to be settled before transfer or burn claimed NFTs, so the front-run is always available for any direct NFT transfer. [3](#0-2) 

### Recommendation
Adopt the same mitigation class as the external report: either make `LoansNFT._update` enforce that all claimable cashflows are paid out to the seller before any owner-initiated transfer, or track a per-NFT claim nonce and disallow transfers once the NFT has been claimed while listed, or burn the loan NFT when it no longer represents claimable cashflows. [3](#0-2) [7](#0-6)  A minimal fix is to have `LoansNFT._update` call `Loans.investorWithdraw` (or verify `getLoanValues` returns zero claimable) before allowing a transfer by the owner, ensuring the buyer receives the cashflows priced into the NFT. [1](#0-0) [3](#0-2)  Alternatively, require all secondary-market transfers to flow through `LoansExchange`, which already locks the NFT and atomically settles cash and NFT movement. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5)
