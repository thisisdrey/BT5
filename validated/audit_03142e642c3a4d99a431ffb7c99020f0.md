Both `ExchangeInjectActuator` and `ExchangeWithdrawActuator` are creator-only (permissioned, analogous to the admin-only nature of `withdraw_protocol_liquidity`), and neither accepts any minimum-output/slippage-limit parameter, unlike `ExchangeTransactionActuator` which requires and enforces `expected` (a minimum-output check) via `tokenExpected <= 0` and `anotherTokenQuant < tokenExpected` validation. ### Title
Missing slippage/minimum-output protection in `ExchangeWithdrawActuator` and `ExchangeInjectActuator` enables frontrunning losses to exchange creators - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java`, `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java`)

### Summary
The bancor-style `Exchange` (TRC10 AMM) actuators `ExchangeWithdrawActuator` and `ExchangeInjectActuator` compute the counterpart token amount (`anotherTokenQuant`) purely from the exchange's live reserve ratio at execution time, with no user-supplied minimum/maximum bound. In contrast, the sibling `ExchangeTransactionActuator` (the trade/swap operation) requires an `expected` field and enforces `anotherTokenQuant >= tokenExpected` in `doValidate()`. Because `ExchangeWithdrawContract`/`ExchangeInjectContract` lack any equivalent field, a creator submitting a withdraw or inject transaction has no way to bound the resulting counterpart amount.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute` and its `doValidate` compute `anotherTokenQuant` proportionally from `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` read at execution time [4](#0-3) . The only validation on the resulting counterpart amount is that it must be non-zero and reasonably precise (`"Not precise enough"` check), not that it meets any minimum threshold requested by the caller [5](#0-4) .

Because `ExchangeTransactionContract` (a fully permissionless trade any account can broadcast) directly mutates `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` of the same `ExchangeCapsule` via `exchangeCapsule.transaction(...)` [6](#0-5) , any address can shift the exchange's price ratio immediately before a pending `ExchangeWithdrawContract` (or `ExchangeInjectContract`) is packed into a block, changing the `anotherTokenQuant` the creator will receive (or be forced to pay, for inject) with no way for the creator to cap the resulting loss. This mirrors the HydraDX finding: `remove_liquidity`-equivalent protection (`ExchangeTransactionActuator`'s `expected` check) exists for trades but is absent for the withdraw/inject operations that are also reserve-ratio-dependent.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker who observes a pending `ExchangeWithdrawContract` transaction in the mempool can submit an `ExchangeTransactionContract` (swap) beforehand to skew the pool ratio, causing the creator's withdrawal to redeem a smaller amount of the counterpart token than intended (or, for inject, to be debited a larger amount of the counterpart token). Unlike `ExchangeTransactionContract`, there is no `expected`/minimum bound the creator can set, so losses are not capped by any protocol-enforced slippage limit — only by the balance-limit and precision checks, which do not protect economic value.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation requires the exchange creator to submit an `ExchangeWithdrawContract` or `ExchangeInjectContract` while the exchange still has active trading (non-zero balances), and an attacker capable of observing the pending transaction and inserting their own `ExchangeTransactionContract` ahead of it in the same or an earlier block — feasible for any address given `ExchangeTransactionContract` is fully permissionless and has no cooldown or access restriction. This matches the judged Medium severity in the original report since it depends on operational timing (creator acting while trading is active) rather than being always exploitable.

### Recommendation
Add a caller-supplied bound (e.g., `expected`/minimum-out for withdraw, maximum-in for inject) to `ExchangeWithdrawContract` and `ExchangeInjectContract`, and enforce it in `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.doValidate`/`ExchangeInjectActuator.doValidate` analogous to the `tokenExpected` check already present in `ExchangeTransactionActuator.doValidate` [7](#0-6) .

### Proof of Concept
1. Exchange creator broadcasts `ExchangeWithdrawContract` for `exchangeId` X, withdrawing `tokenQuant` of `firstTokenId`, expecting to receive proportional `secondTokenId` based on current reserves.
2. Attacker observes this transaction and broadcasts `ExchangeTransactionContract` trading a large amount into the exchange, shifting `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` before the withdraw is included.
3. Attacker's transaction is ordered first (via fee/energy bidding or same-block ordering); `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute` then computes `anotherTokenQuant` from the now-skewed ratio, giving the creator materially less value than expected, with no `expected`/minimum parameter available to prevent this, as shown in `ExchangeWithdrawContract`'s field definition lacking any such bound [8](#0-7) .
