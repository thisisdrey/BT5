### Title
Balance Cache Inconsistency in `MarketSellAssetActuator` Self-Matching Leads to Fund Loss - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/MarketSellAssetActuator.java)

### Summary
`MarketSellAssetActuator.execute()` keeps a single in-memory `AccountCapsule` (`accountCapsule`) for the taker throughout order creation and matching, mutating it in place for the taker side of every match [1](#0-0) . When a maker order is matched, the actuator instead re-reads the maker's account fresh from `AccountStore`, updates it, and immediately persists it with `accountStore.put` inside the private `addTrxOrToken(orderCapsule, num)` / `returnSellTokenRemain(orderCapsule)` helpers [2](#0-1) . Finally, `execute()` unconditionally overwrites the account record with the stale in-memory taker `accountCapsule` [3](#0-2) .

### Finding Description
There is no validation anywhere in `MarketSellAssetActuator.validate()` or `matchOrder()`/`matchSingleOrder()` that rejects a new sell order matching against a pre-existing maker order placed by the **same** owner address [4](#0-3) . When the addresses coincide (self-match), two divergent views of the same `AccountCapsule` are updated independently:
1. The taker-side credit is applied only to the in-memory `accountCapsule` held for the whole `execute()` call (`addTrxOrToken(takerOrderCapsule, takerBuyTokenQuantityReceive, takerAccountCapsule)`) [5](#0-4) .
2. The maker-side credit is applied to a separately-fetched `AccountCapsule` read straight from `accountStore.get(...)` and persisted immediately via `accountStore.put(...)` in `addTrxOrToken(makerOrderCapsule, makerBuyTokenQuantityReceive)` [6](#0-5) .

Because both reads/writes target the same underlying account key when taker == maker, the second write's basis does not reflect the first write (still only in memory), and the final `accountStore.put(accountCapsule.createDbKey(), accountCapsule)` at the end of `execute()` clobbers whatever was persisted mid-match [7](#0-6) . This is exactly the caching pattern described in the report — mixing a cached in-memory balance object with directly persisted store writes for what turns out to be the same account — leading to lost or duplicated token/TRX balances depending on match ordering, match count, and whether `sellTokenQuantityRemain` returns are involved.

### Impact Explanation
A user can place orders that match their own resting orders in the order book (nothing prevents `ownerAddress == makerOrderCapsule.getOwnerAddress()`). Depending on interleaving of `addTrxOrToken`/`returnSellTokenRemain` direct-store writes vs. the final overwrite of the cached taker capsule, this can cause silent loss of the user's own TRX/TRC10 balance updates (funds effectively vanish from the ledger — a form of permanent freezing/loss of funds) or, in the reverse ordering (multiple maker matches followed by the final overwrite reapplying a stale taker-side credit), inflate the balance beyond what should be backed by the trade. Either outcome corrupts account balance invariants enforced only by careful update ordering, not by an explicit same-address barrier.

### Likelihood Explanation
Triggering this requires only a single unprivileged account: place a resting order (maker), then submit a second `MarketSellAssetContract` transaction with the complementary token pair/price so it self-matches. No special privileges, cooperating counterparties, or SR/witness capability are needed — any transaction broadcaster can attempt it repeatedly to probe for exploitable balance drift.

### Recommendation
In `MarketSellAssetActuator`, detect when `makerOrderCapsule.getOwnerAddress()` equals the taker's `ownerAddress` during `matchSingleOrder`/`addTrxOrToken`, and route the update through the same in-memory `accountCapsule` reference used for the taker (or otherwise serialize/merge the reads before persisting), analogous to the report's remediation of not caching/re-reading state for the recipient when sender and receiver are identical. Alternatively, disallow self-matching outright by comparing the new order's owner address against maker order owners before executing a match.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker account `A` places `MarketSellAssetContract` selling 100 TRC10 `X` for 100 TRC10 `Y` (maker order recorded in `MarketOrderStore`/`MarketPairPriceToOrderStore`).
2. The same account `A` submits a second `MarketSellAssetContract` selling 100 `Y` for 100 `X` at a matching price, causing `matchOrder()` to match against its own resting order from step 1.
3. During `execute()`, the taker-side credit (`Y`→`X` or vice versa) is applied to the cached `accountCapsule`, while the maker-side credit is fetched and persisted directly to `AccountStore` for the same address [8](#0-7) .
4. The final `accountStore.put(accountCapsule.createDbKey(), accountCapsule)` overwrites the store with the stale cached object, dropping the mid-match persisted update [3](#0-2) .
5. Inspect account `A`'s resulting `AssetV2Map`/`balance` after the transaction — it will not match the expected post-trade totals, demonstrating lost or duplicated token balance from the self-match caching inconsistency.

    AccountCapsule accountCapsule = accountStore
        .get(orderCapsule.getOwnerAddress().toByteArray());

    byte[] buyTokenId = orderCapsule.getBuyTokenId();
    if (Arrays.equals(buyTokenId, "_".getBytes())) {
