Audit Report

## Title
Incorrect minting in PSM: `mint()` credits internal stablecoin based on the requested transfer amount instead of the external asset actually received by the reserve - ([File: substrate/frame/psm/src/lib.rs])

## Summary
`Pallet::mint` in `substrate/frame/psm/src/lib.rs` transfers `effective_external` of `external_asset` to the PSM reserve account via `T::Fungibles::transfer`, then unconditionally mints `internal_to_user` + `fee` and increases `PsmDebt` based solely on the nominal `internal_equivalent`, without verifying that the reserve account's balance actually increased by the corresponding amount. [1](#0-0) . The generic `fungibles::Mutate::transfer` default implementation credits the destination with `BestEffort` precision while still returning `Ok(amount)` unconditionally, meaning a real shortfall on the deposit side would not be detected by `mint()`'s discarded return-value check. [2](#0-1) 

## Finding Description
`mint()` computes `internal_equivalent` and `effective_external` from the user-supplied `external_amount`, transfers `effective_external` to `psm_account`, and then mints `internal_to_user`/`fee` and inserts `new_debt` into `PsmDebt` — all derived from the nominal figures rather than any post-transfer balance check on `psm_account`. [3](#0-2)  The `transfer` call's `Result<Balance, DispatchError>` is only used via `?` to detect hard errors; it is never compared against the requested amount to detect a partial credit. [4](#0-3) 

The generic `Mutate::transfer` default implementation decreases the source's balance and then calls `Self::increase_balance(..., BestEffort)` on the destination, discarding its actual returned value (`let _ = ...`) and still returning `Ok(amount)` regardless of what was actually credited. [5](#0-4)  This confirms that, for asset backends where deposit-side crediting can legitimately differ from the requested amount (e.g., existential-deposit dust handling on the reserve account's first credit), `mint()` has no mechanism to detect the discrepancy.

By contrast, `redeem()` explicitly checks `Self::get_reserve(&internal_asset, &external_asset)` against `external_out` before paying out, and defensively errors via `Error::Unexpected` if the reserve is insufficient. [6](#0-5)  No analogous check exists on the `mint()` path for the deposit side.

## Impact Explanation
`PsmDebt` and internal-asset issuance are intended to be backed 1:1 by the reserve's external-asset holdings. If the reserve receives less than the nominal transferred amount under a `BestEffort`-credited backend, `mint()` still mints the full nominal `internal_to_user` + `fee` and records the full nominal `internal_equivalent` into `PsmDebt`, creating unbacked internal-asset supply — an "unbacked mint" condition matching the accepted impact gate for theft or unbacked mint.

## Likelihood Explanation
This is an unprivileged, externally reachable path: any signed account can call the public `mint()` extrinsic. [7](#0-6)  However, exploitability is conditioned entirely on the specific `T::Fungibles` implementation configured for a given runtime deployment actually exhibiting a deposit-side shortfall under `BestEffort` semantics (e.g., ED-related dust loss on the reserve account's first credit, or a non-standard asset backend). For the standard `pallet-assets`-backed `fungibles` implementations typically used in Polkadot SDK runtimes, `increase_balance` under normal conditions credits the full requested amount as long as the account is above the existential deposit or the transfer amount exceeds it; whether a genuine shortfall is reachable depends on runtime-specific configuration (asset existential deposits, freezing, or custom `Fungibles` adapters) that is outside the scope of the pallet code itself. The vulnerability is real as a defense-in-depth gap — `mint()` lacks the same reserve-balance verification that `redeem()` performs — but the report does not demonstrate a concrete, reproducible configuration within this repository's actual runtime wiring where the shortfall is triggered by an unprivileged caller without relying on unusual/edge asset-backend behavior.

## Recommendation
In `mint()`, read `psm_account`'s external-asset balance before and after the `T::Fungibles::transfer` call (or otherwise use the actually-credited delta) and use that balance difference — not the nominal `effective_external`/`internal_equivalent` — to determine `internal_to_user`, `fee`, and the `PsmDebt` increment, mirroring the `get_reserve` check already present in `redeem()`.

## Proof of Concept
1. Configure a PSM instance whose `external_asset` is backed by a `Fungibles` implementation where the reserve account (`psm_account`) is not yet in existence and the asset's existential-deposit/minimum-balance rules cause the first credit under `BestEffort` in `Mutate::transfer` (`substrate/frame/support/src/traits/tokens/fungibles/regular.rs:380-386`) to deliver less than the requested `amount`.
2. Call `Psm::mint(origin, internal_asset, external_asset, external_amount, max_fee)` with parameters landing in this edge case.
3. Observe `T::Fungibles::transfer` returns `Ok(effective_external)` while `psm_account`'s actual external-asset balance increased by less than `effective_external`.
4. Observe `mint_into` still credits the full nominal `internal_to_user` (+ `fee`) and `PsmDebt` is incremented by the full nominal `new_debt`, even though the reserve is under-collateralized relative to that debt — confirmed directly by the code at `substrate/frame/psm/src/lib.rs:743-756`.
