### Title
wNEAR debited with no refund on native_withdraw / storage_deposit / auth_call terminal-promise failure - (File: contracts/defuse/src/contract/tokens/nep141/native.rs, contracts/defuse/src/contract/tokens/nep141/storage_deposit.rs, contracts/defuse/src/contract/intents/auth_call.rs)

### Summary
`native_withdraw`, `storage_deposit`, and deposit-bearing `auth_call` all subtract wNEAR from the signer's internal balance via `Contract::withdraw` before unwrapping it and forwarding NEAR to an attacker-chosen `receiver_id`/`contract_id`. Unlike `nft_withdraw`, none of these three paths schedule a resolving callback that checks the outcome of the *final* promise (the native `transfer`, the `storage_deposit` call, or the `on_auth` call) and re-credits the signer on failure.

### Finding Description
The invariant that should hold is: `wNEAR_debited(signer) == NEAR_delivered(receiver) + NEAR_refunded(signer)`.

In `native_withdraw` (`contracts/defuse/src/contract/intents/state.rs:212-240`), the flow is:
1. `self.withdraw(...)` subtracts wNEAR from the signer immediately.
2. `near_withdraw` unwraps wNEAR into real NEAR held by the contract.
3. `do_native_withdraw` (`contracts/defuse/src/contract/tokens/nep141/native.rs:11-19`) checks only that step 2 succeeded (`require!(promise_result_checked_void(0).is_ok())`) and then issues `Promise::new(withdraw.receiver_id).transfer(withdraw.amount)` as the **final, unresolved** promise — there is no `*_resolve_native_withdraw` callback anywhere that inspects whether this final transfer succeeded and credits the signer back if it didn't.

The same pattern holds for `storage_deposit` (`contracts/defuse/src/contract/intents/state.rs:265-297`, `contracts/defuse/src/contract/tokens/nep141/storage_deposit.rs:14-26`): after `near_withdraw` succeeds, `do_storage_deposit` fires `ext_storage_management::ext(storage_deposit.contract_id).storage_deposit(...)` as the terminal promise with no resolver/refund step.

And for `auth_call` with non-zero `attached_deposit` (`contracts/defuse/src/contract/intents/state.rs:303-337`, `contracts/defuse/src/contract/intents/auth_call.rs:17-37`): after `near_withdraw` succeeds, `do_auth_call` fires `ext_auth_callee::ext_on(p).on_auth(signer_id, auth_call.msg)` fully attacker-controlled (`contract_id`, `state_init`, `msg`, `min_gas`) as the terminal promise, again with no resolver checking success/refunding.

If the terminal receipt in any of these three fails — e.g., `receiver_id`/`contract_id` is a non-existent account, or an attacker-deployed contract that intentionally panics or returns a malformed value causing a deserialization/gas failure on `on_auth`/`storage_deposit` — the NEAR attached to that failed receipt is refunded by the NEAR protocol back to the **predecessor of the failed receipt**, i.e., back to the Defuse contract's own account balance, not credited back to the signer's internal wNEAR ledger. The signer's wNEAR was already irreversibly subtracted in step 1 with no compensating logic. This contrasts directly with `nft_withdraw`'s `nft_resolve_withdraw` (`contracts/defuse/src/contract/tokens/nep171/withdraw.rs:160-195`), which explicitly checks the final promise result and calls `self.deposit(sender_id, ...)` to refund the token on failure — proving refund-on-failure is the intended pattern elsewhere in the codebase but is absent for these three paths.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker who is the signer of their own intent can point `NativeWithdraw.receiver_id`, `StorageDeposit.contract_id`, or `AuthCall.contract_id` at a non-existent account or a contract they control that reverts/misbehaves on the final call. The signer's own wNEAR balance is debited and the underlying NEAR is unwrapped and sent out, but on failure of the terminal receipt it is refunded to the Defuse contract's own account rather than the signer, permanently freezing that value from the signer's perspective (unrecoverable without a privileged `UnrestrictedWithdrawer`/DAO action). This matches the "Critical — user funds permanently frozen" category, repeatable per signer/per withdrawal attempt.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attacker only needs to sign an intent for their own account (no special privilege) specifying a target account/contract they fully control or a non-existent account, then call `execute_intents`. This requires no cooperation from other accounts and is straightforward to trigger. However, since this loses the attacker's own funds (self-inflicted unless targeting a victim's signed intent, which the attacker cannot forge), the realistic threat is either griefing one's own funds (not a real attacker-profit scenario) or, more importantly, applies when the flow is entered via any payload the attacker can induce another party or automated relayer/solver to sign against attacker-chosen receiver parameters — but per the rules, the signer/intent creator controls their own `receiver_id`/`contract_id`/`msg` fields, so this is self-targeted loss rather than value extracted from a victim without their authorization.

### Recommendation
Add resolver callbacks for `native_withdraw`'s final `transfer`, `storage_deposit`'s final `storage_deposit` call, and `auth_call`'s final `on_auth` call, mirroring `nft_resolve_withdraw`, that inspect `promise_result_checked_void`/`promise_result_checked_json` for the terminal promise and re-credit the wNEAR balance to the original signer when that terminal call fails.

### Proof of Concept
Not fully demonstrable from static analysis alone — a `near-workspaces` sandbox test would need to: (1) deposit wNEAR-backed balance for a test account, (2) submit a `NativeWithdraw`/`StorageDeposit`/`AuthCall` intent with `receiver_id`/`contract_id` pointing to a non-existent account or a deployed malicious contract that panics in `on_auth`/`storage_deposit`, (3) assert the signer's `balance_of` for the wNEAR `TokenId` is decremented, (4) assert no NEAR arrived at the target and the Defuse contract's own account balance increased by the refunded amount, and (5) assert the signer's balance is never restored — confirming `wNEAR_debited(signer) != NEAR_delivered(receiver) + NEAR_refunded(signer)`. I was not able to execute this test in this session; this assessment is based on static code tracing of the promise chains and the absence of resolver/refund logic, contrasted with the existing refund logic in `nft_resolve_withdraw`.
