### Title
Residual `DepositToken` ERC20 allowances remain valid after control of the holder account changes - ([File: contracts/DepositToken.sol])

### Summary
`DepositToken` implements standard ERC20-style `approve`/`transferFrom` with allowances keyed only by the token-holder address and spender address, not by the current controller/session/owner nonce. If collateral is held by a transferable smart-contract account, the previous controller can pre-approve themselves before handing the account to a new controller; after the handoff, `allowance[account][oldController]` still authorizes `transferFrom(account, ...)` for any unlocked balance. This is the Metronome analog of permissions surviving an ownership/control transfer because the authorization is not scoped to the new controller. [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

### Finding Description
`approve(spender_, amount_)` writes `allowance[_msgSender()][spender_]` with no epoch, owner nonce, or controller-binding. [2](#0-1)  `transferFrom(sender_, recipient_, amount_)` only checks `unlockedBalanceOf(sender_)` via `_revertIfLocked`, then consumes `allowance[sender_][_msgSender]` and moves the deposit tokens. [3](#0-2)  There is no hook that clears `allowance` when effective control of `sender_` changes, because the token holder is just an address and Metronome does not model account-controller rotation. The acquired msdTOKEN can be redeemed for underlying through `withdraw`, which calls `_withdraw` and pulls collateral from Treasury to a chosen recipient. [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

### Impact Explanation
Direct theft of user funds: the prior controller keeps a live spending right over a smart-contract account’s unlocked `DepositToken` balance after control transfers, can move the deposit tokens to themselves, and can withdraw the underlying collateral while the position is unlocked and the pool is not shutdown. The invariant broken is authorization continuity across account control transfer: `allowance` remains bound to the same holder address even though the entity controlling that address has changed. [3](#0-2) 

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires the victim to hold Metronome deposit tokens in a transferable contract account or equivalent custody arrangement and to take control without clearing prior ERC20 allowances. It does not require governor, keeper, oracle, bridge, or privileged-role assistance. The attack is limited to unlocked collateral; debt-backed balances are protected by `_revertIfLocked`/`unlockedBalanceOf`, and `withdraw` is blocked when the system is shutdown. [6](#0-5) [7](#0-6) 

### Recommendation
Scope spender authorization to an account-control epoch. For contract/custodied accounts, maintain an owner/controller nonce or salt for the holder and key allowances as `allowance[holder][epoch][spender]`; bump the epoch on supported custody-transfer/account-rotation hooks, or expose a permissioned “clear all my approvals” routine callable by the new controller before accepting custody. At minimum, document that smart-contract-account buyers must revoke all `DepositToken` allowances and provide a batched `decreaseAllowance`/revoke flow through `Operator.execute`. [8](#0-7) 

### Proof of Concept
Foundry/Hardhat fork outline:

1. Deploy or use existing `Pool`, `DepositToken` `msdX`, `Treasury`, and `Operator` from the target chain.
2. Create `SmartAccount` controlled by `attacker`.
3. From `attacker`, have `SmartAccount` deposit collateral and receive `msdX`; keep debt at `0` so `unlockedBalanceOf(SmartAccount) == balanceOf(SmartAccount)`. [9](#0-8) 
4. While still controlling `SmartAccount`, call `msdX.approve(attacker, type(uint256).max)`. [2](#0-1) 
5. Transfer control/ownership of `SmartAccount` to `victim` without changing `SmartAccount`’s address.
6. As `attacker`, call `msdX.transferFrom(SmartAccount, attacker, amount)`. Assert allowance was still valid despite the control change. [3](#0-2) 
7. As `attacker`, call `msdX.withdraw(amount, attacker)` and assert underlying collateral is pulled from Treasury to `attacker`. [5](#0-4) 

Expected result: step 6 succeeds from residual allowance, and step 7 redeems stolen deposit tokens for underlying collateral while not shutdown.
