### Title
Oracle price deviation allows over-issuance of synthetic debt that turns into bad debt after the oracle update - (File: contracts/Pool.sol, contracts/DebtToken.sol)

### Summary
Metronome's borrow-limit check has the same structure as the Morpho bug class: `DebtToken.issue()` caps issuance at the oracle-priced `_issuableInUsd` computed by `Pool.debtPositionOf()`/`Pool.depositOf()`, where collateral value is `masterOracle().quoteTokenToUsd(underlying, depositBalance).wadMul(collateralFactor)` [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) . Any oracle whose reported price lags the real market price (deviation-threshold push feeds, TWAPs, or pull oracles like the Redstone `pullPriceProvider` wired in deployments [3](#0-2) ) lets an unprivileged attacker front-run the price update, deposit collateral, and `issue()` the maximum synth at the stale high collateral price. After the update lands, the position is undercollateralized; the attacker extracts value via a second account liquidating the first (self-liquidation is blocked by `CanNotLiquidateOwnPosition` only for `liquidator == account` [4](#0-3) ), or simply defaults, leaving bad debt socialized onto the protocol and synth holders. The issue fee in `quoteIssueOut` is a fixed protocol parameter not required to exceed the oracle's max deviation, so it does not close the arbitrage [5](#0-4) .

### Finding Description
`debtPositionOf()` computes `_issuableLimitInUsd = Σ quoteTokenToUsd(collateral) * collateralFactor` and `issue()` enforces `amount_ <= quoteUsdToToken(synth, _issuableInUsd)` [6](#0-5) . Both the borrow leg and the liquidation leg (`quoteLiquidateIn`/`quoteLiquidateOut` via `masterOracle().quote` [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) ) consume the same spot oracle, so the Morpho condition applies: arbitrage is profitable when `price deviation > 1/(LIF − CF)` where LIF derives from `feeProvider.liquidationFees()` [9](#0-8) . With collateral factors near 1 (e.g., same-asset msUSD/msETH-style pairs) the deviation needed is small — easily within a single Chainlink threshold or a composed multi-feed deviation. A pull-oracle configuration makes it worse: the E2E tests show `Operator.execute` can batch `updatePrice` + protocol calls in one transaction [10](#0-9) , so the attacker can atomically act on a stale price immediately before a signed update is applied. `maxLiquidable = 50%` [11](#0-10)  and post-liquidation collateral locking only slow full extraction; they do not prevent the initial over-issuance, which is the value-stealing step.

### Impact Explanation
Direct protocol insolvency: the attacker mints synth worth more than the true post-update value of their collateral, sells/swaps the synth, and abandons the position. The residual debt exceeds seizable collateral, so the deficit is borne by the pool (bad debt socialization), identical in effect to the Morpho scenario.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires only a normal oracle deviation event (a price move at or beyond the feed's deviation threshold) plus front-running — no privileged role. Feasibility scales with high `collateralFactor` markets and multi-feed oracles; guaranteed-atomic access exists where pull oracles are used. Profitable whenever deviation exceeds `1/(LIF − CF)` after accounting for `issueFee`.

### Recommendation
Ensure the issue (mint) fee is always greater than the worst-case oracle deviation, or add a conservative haircut to collateral pricing used for `depositOf()` issuance limits (bid/ask or dual-oracle bounds), so that over-issuance relative to liquidation recovery is never profitable.

### Proof of Concept
Hardhat fork sketch (mirrors `test/Pool.test.ts` fixture):
1. Attacker EOA A deposits `X` of high-CF collateral via `DepositToken.deposit`.
2. On observing a pending oracle update that lowers collateral price by `d`, A front-runs it and calls `DebtToken.issue(quoteUsdToToken(synth, _issuableInUsd), A)` — minted at the stale high price.
3. Update lands: `debtPositionOf(A)._isHealthy == false`.
4. Attacker EOA B holds synth (transferred from A or bought) and calls `pool.liquidate(synth, A, repay, depositToken)` (repeat as `maxLiquidable`/locks allow), seizing collateral at the new price + `liquidatorIncentive` [12](#0-11) .
5. Assert `synthGained + collateralSeizedValue > collateralDepositedValue` whenever `d > 1/(LIF − CF)`; remaining unseizable debt in A's position is the protocol's loss.

Caveat: I could not read the full `Pool.liquidate` body within the iteration limit; the `liquidator != account` check and incentive math are confirmed via `quoteLiquidateIn/Out` and the test suite rather than the function body itself.
