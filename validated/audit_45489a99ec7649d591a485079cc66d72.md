## Title
Atomic all-or-nothing oracle price resolution in `get-assets` freezes a user's entire position when a single unrelated asset's price is stale/invalid — ([File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar])

### Summary
The reported bug class is: a batch/aggregate validation routine treats **any single invalid item** as fatal for the **entire batch**, instead of isolating/skipping the bad item, causing a broad denial-of-service for otherwise-valid operations. The Zest v2 market contract has a direct structural analog in its oracle price aggregation used for every collateral/debt health check.

### Finding Description
`get-assets` computes a user's "safe mask" (their collateral+debt assets intersected with the globally enabled bitmap) and resolves prices for **all of those assets in one shot** via `price-multi-resolve`, then unconditionally `unwrap-panic`s the result: [1](#0-0) 

`price-multi-resolve` folds over every oracle entry for the position and, through `iter-price-multi`, marks the **whole batch** `valid: false` the moment a single asset's `price-resolve` fails (stale timestamp, non-positive price, low confidence, oracle-type/callcode error, etc.), and then asserts on that aggregate flag, turning one bad asset into a hard error for the entire call: [2](#0-1) 

The per-asset validity check itself lives in `price-resolve`/`oracle-timestamp-fresh`, which reverts on stale timestamp, illegal price, or callcode failure (e.g. `resolve-ztoken`/`resolve-ststx` depending on external contract state): [3](#0-2) 

Because `get-assets` is called for the **entire position mask** (not just the asset being acted upon) from every state-changing entry point that needs a health check — `collateral-add`, `collateral-remove`, borrow, repay — a single unresolvable asset in a user's position blocks operations on **all other, unrelated, perfectly healthy assets** in that same position: [4](#0-3) 

This mirrors the report's root cause precisely: instead of skipping/excluding the problematic item (as Ethereum's `apply_deposit()` does for a bad-signature Deposit) the code (`price-multi-resolve` → `get-assets`) propagates one failure into a hard `unwrap-panic` that kills the entire aggregate operation, rather than isolating the fault to only the specific asset/action that actually depends on it.

### Impact Explanation
Any user whose position mask includes an asset whose price becomes momentarily unresolvable (feed not refreshed within its configured `max-staleness`, a callcode dependency like `call-ststx-ratio`/z-token index lookup failing, or an oracle call reverting) is unable to:
- repay debt (even to become healthier),
- remove or add collateral on any other, unaffected asset,
- perform any operation requiring `get-assets`,

for as long as that one asset's price stays stale/unresolvable — even though the user's actual requested action does not need that asset's price at all. This is a temporary freezing of user funds/positions, matching the in-scope "High - temporary freezing of funds" impact class.

### Likelihood Explanation
This does not require an attacker with special privileges: it is triggered by ordinary, expected conditions — a feed simply not being refreshed inside `max-staleness`, or a callcode dependency (`resolve-ztoken`, `resolve-ststx`) transiently failing. The more assets a user holds (and the more diverse egroups/oracles across those assets), the higher the chance that at least one is stale at any given block, making the freeze likelihood increase with normal portfolio diversification — the opposite of what a robust health-check design should produce.

### Recommendation
Do not aggregate unrelated assets into a single atomic price-resolution batch that fails outright on one bad entry. Either:
1. Resolve/require prices only for the specific asset(s) actually involved in the requested operation (e.g., only the asset being removed/added, plus assets contributing to the debt being checked), or
2. If a full-position health re-evaluation is required, allow per-asset failures to be surfaced as a typed, catchable error (not `unwrap-panic`) so callers can decide whether to exclude the stale asset from the health calculation (using its last-known-good state) rather than aborting the entire transaction, consistent with the referenced spec principle of skipping invalid/unresolvable items instead of hard-failing the whole batch.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice deposits sBTC and stSTX as collateral and borrows USDC against sBTC.
2. The DIA/Pyth feed backing stSTX (or its `resolve-ststx` ratio dependency) is not refreshed within `max-staleness`, so `price-resolve` for stSTX starts failing `oracle-timestamp-fresh`/`ERR-ORACLE-INVARIANT` at `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar#L387`.
3. Alice calls `repay` to clear her USDC debt, or `collateral-remove` on her sBTC (unaffected, healthy asset). Both paths call `get-assets(position-mask)`, which includes stSTX in the batch.
4. `iter-price-multi` catches the stSTX failure and sets `valid: false`; `price-multi-resolve` asserts and returns `ERR-ORACLE-MULTI`; `get-assets`'s `unwrap-panic` aborts the entire transaction.
5. Alice cannot repay her debt or withdraw her healthy sBTC collateral until the stSTX price becomes resolvable again — her whole position is frozen by an asset unrelated to the action she is trying to perform.

  (aids (list 64 uint)))
  (let ((init { output: (list), valid: true, aids: aids, idx: u0 })

    (asserts! (get valid response) ERR-ORACLE-MULTI)
