### Title
Exchange trading can be griefed/DoS'd by front-running the `EXCHANGE_BALANCE_LIMIT` check - ([File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeTransactionActuator.java])

### Summary
`ExchangeTransactionActuator` and `ExchangeInjectActuator` both enforce a global `EXCHANGE_BALANCE_LIMIT` on the token reserves of a bancor-style exchange pair. Because any unprivileged account can submit an `ExchangeTransactionContract` against any exchange (no creator/permission check), an attacker can front-run a victim's trade with a transaction that pushes one side of the pair's balance right up to the limit, causing the victim's legitimate trade to revert with `"token balance must less than " + balanceLimit"`. The attacker can then reverse their own trade to restore the balance, repeating the griefing at will — the same "front-run to hit the cap, then undo" pattern described in the original `maxContractBalance` report.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeTransactionActuator.doValidate()` computes the post-trade balance of the traded token and rejects the transaction if it would exceed `dynamicStore.getExchangeBalanceLimit()`: [1](#0-0) 

This check has no special-casing for who is submitting the trade — any account holding the traded asset/TRX can call it (only an account existence/balance check is performed, no creator restriction), unlike `ExchangeInjectActuator`/`ExchangeWithdrawActuator` which restrict the operation to `exchangeCapsule.getCreatorAddress()`: [2](#0-1) 

The limit itself is a single global dynamic property shared by every exchange pair: [3](#0-2) 

Because the check is `newBalance > balanceLimit` (strict inequality on the *resulting* balance rather than any per-account/per-tx quota), an attacker who observes a pending victim `ExchangeTransactionContract` in the mempool can submit their own trade in the same direction just before it, pushing the pool's token balance to just under the cap. The victim's transaction then computes a `tokenBalance` that exceeds `balanceLimit` and reverts in `doValidate()`. The attacker can subsequently submit the reverse trade (or a withdraw, if they are the creator) to bring the balance back down, repeating the denial at will and effectively gate-keeping who can trade on that exchange pair — the exact "deposit denial via limit front-running" pattern from the referenced report, but applied to `Exchange` trading instead of a treasury `deposit`.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker can reliably and repeatedly block other users' legitimate `ExchangeTransactionContract` calls on a given token pair by keeping the pool balance pinned near `EXCHANGE_BALANCE_LIMIT`, denying access to a core, unprivileged market-order-handling function of the chain and letting the attacker manipulate the exchange in their favor (e.g., preventing competing trades at a given price before executing their own).

### Likelihood Explanation
`EXCHANGE_BALANCE_LIMIT` is a static configuration value and exchange balances are public on-chain state, so any observer of the mempool/pending transactions can compute the exact quant needed to push a pool to the cap and time their front-running transaction accordingly. No special privileges are required — this is reachable by any signed transaction from any account holding the relevant asset/TRX.

### Recommendation
Do not use a hard global reserve cap that any transaction can push against; instead bound the *rate/size* of a single trade (e.g., max slippage or max fraction of pool per trade) rather than an absolute reserve ceiling, or make the cap per-exchange and configurable/removable similarly to the acknowledged mitigation in the referenced report (treat it purely as an emergency circuit breaker, not a routine trading constraint).

### Proof of Concept
1. Exchange `E` has `firstTokenBalance` close to but below `EXCHANGE_BALANCE_LIMIT`.
2. Victim broadcasts `ExchangeTransactionContract` selling token A for token B, which would raise `firstTokenBalance` to just below the limit (still valid).
3. Attacker observes this in the mempool and submits their own `ExchangeTransactionContract` selling a small extra amount of token A, ordered before the victim's transaction (front-run), pushing `firstTokenBalance` over `EXCHANGE_BALANCE_LIMIT`.
4. Victim's transaction is now evaluated with a higher starting `firstTokenBalance`; `tokenBalance = addExact(tokenBalance, tokenQuant)` exceeds `balanceLimit`, and `doValidate()` throws `"token balance must less than " + balanceLimit"`, causing the victim's trade to fail: [1](#0-0) 
5. Attacker submits a reverse trade to bring `firstTokenBalance` back down, and repeats the process to continuously deny the victim (or any competing trader) from trading on this pair.
