### Title
Discrepancy in `lastNav` accounting during `approveDeposit` and `approveRedemption` - (`File: tare-io__tare-contracts/contracts/PortfolioVault.sol`)

### Summary
The `PortfolioVault` contract suffers from an accounting discrepancy where `lastNav` is manually updated during `approveDeposit` and `approveRedemption` without correctly accounting for the assets already being tracked in the vault's internal balance-adjustment variables (`totalPendingDepositAssets` and `totalClaimableRedeemAssets`). This results in the "double-counting" or "lost-counting" of assets when the next `updateNav` cycle is triggered, leading to an incorrect share price calculation.

### Finding Description
In `PortfolioVault.sol`, the `updateNav` function calculates the vault's Net Asset Value (NAV) by taking the `assetToken.balanceOf(address(this))` and subtracting `totalPendingDepositAssets` and `totalClaimableRedeemAssets` [1](#0-0) .

However, in the `approveDeposit` function:
1. It decrements `totalPendingDepositAssets` by `assets` [2](#0-1) .
2. It then manually increments `lastNav` by `assets` [3](#0-2) .

Because `updateNav` already considers `assetToken.balanceOf(address(this)) - totalPendingDepositAssets` as the "actual" assets, when `totalPendingDepositAssets` is decremented, the result of that subtraction naturally increases. By also manually adding `assets` to `lastNav`, the protocol is accounting for the same increase twice relative to the internal ledger state used in `updateNav`.

Similarly, in `approveRedemption`:
1. It increments `totalClaimableRedeemAssets` by `assets` [4](#0-3) .
2. It then manually decrements `lastNav` by `assets` [5](#0-4) .

This logic mirrors the external report's root cause: a fee (or in this case, a state transition of assets) is reflected in the external balance/escrow but the internal accounting variable (`lastNav`) is adjusted in a way that creates a mismatch with the next automated reconciliation in `updateNav`.

### Impact Explanation
Material corruption of vault NAV and share pricing. Since `sharePrice()` depends on `lastNav` [6](#0-5) , an inflated or deflated `lastNav` causes unfair minting or redeeming of shares. Specifically, if `lastNav` is double-incremented, the share price appears higher than it should be, harming new depositors and unfairly benefiting existing shareholders.

### Likelihood Explanation
High. This occurs on every deposit and redemption approval. While `updateNav` eventually overwrites `lastNav` [1](#0-0) , the period between an approval and the finalization of the next `updateNav` cycle (which can be long due to paginated processing) leaves the vault in a corrupted state.

### Recommendation
Remove the manual increments/decrements to `lastNav` in `approveDeposit` and `approveRedemption`. The updates to `totalPendingDepositAssets` and `totalClaimableRedeemAssets` are sufficient to reflect the change in "available" assets for the next `updateNav` calculation. If `lastNav` must be updated immediately to allow for consecutive approvals in the same block, it should be done by only adjusting the "pending" counters and letting a unified `_calculateNav()` helper derive the value.

### Proof of Concept
1. Vault has 1000 USDC. `totalPendingDepositAssets` = 100. `lastNav` = 900.
2. `approveDeposit(100)` is called.
3. `totalPendingDepositAssets` becomes 0.
4. `lastNav` is manually set to 900 + 100 = 1000.
5. A user calls `updateNav`. The calculation is: `balanceOf(vault)` (1000) - `totalPendingDepositAssets` (0) = 1000.
6. The `lastNav` is now 1000.
7. If the logic intended `lastNav` to represent the value *before* the new assets were added (to keep share price stable during approval), the manual addition of `assets` to `lastNav` at line 349 is redundant because the reduction of the "pending" liability at line 346 already achieves this effect in the eyes of the `updateNav` logic.
