### Title
Failed `ProxyOFT._creditTo` mint leaves bridged synthetic tokens burned on source and permanently unclaimable on destination while the bridged-in cap is saturated — ([File: contracts/ProxyOFT.sol](contracts/ProxyOFT.sol))

### Summary
`ProxyOFT._debitFrom` burns the user's synthetic tokens on the source chain unconditionally, and `ProxyOFT._creditTo` mints them on the destination chain by calling `SyntheticToken.mint`. That mint reverts whenever `bridgedInSupply() > maxBridgedInSupply`, `totalSupply > maxTotalSupply`, or the synthetic token is inactive. Although the inherited `NonblockingLzAppUpgradeable` catches the revert and stores the payload in `failedMessages`, `retryMessage` simply re-executes the same reverting mint, so the credited tokens remain inaccessible for as long as the cap stays saturated. An unprivileged attacker can deliberately saturate and pin `bridgedInSupply` at the cap, turning every in-flight inbound bridge transfer into frozen funds: burned on the source chain, never minted on the destination.

### Finding Description
On the source chain, `ProxyOFT._debitFrom` burns the full `amount_` via `syntheticToken.burn(from_, amount_)` with no check that the destination chain will be able to credit it (contracts/ProxyOFT.sol:70-83). On the destination chain, `_sendAck` → `_creditTo` calls `syntheticToken.mint(toAddress_, amount_)` (contracts/ProxyOFT.sol:86-93). Inside `SyntheticToken._mint`, when the caller is the `proxyOFT`, `totalBridgedIn` is incremented and the mint reverts with `SurpassMaxBridgingSupply` if `bridgedInSupply() > maxBridgedInSupply` (contracts/SyntheticToken.sol:338-340); it also reverts with `SurpassMaxSynthSupply` when `totalSupply > maxTotalSupply` (line 347) and with `SyntheticIsInactive` via `onlyIfSyntheticTokenIsActive` (line 333).

The revert propagates through `nonblockingLzReceive`, which is invoked via `ExcessivelySafeCall` in `NonblockingLzAppUpgradeable._blockingLzReceive`; the failure is caught and stored in `failedMessages` (contracts/dependencies/@layerzerolabs/solidity-examples/contracts-upgradeable/lzApp/NonblockingLzAppUpgradeable.sol:29-39). The only recovery is `retryMessage` (lines 51-61), which re-runs the identical `_nonblockingLzReceive` → `mint` path and reverts again while the cap is saturated. There is no fallback crediting, no refund path back to the source chain, and no alternative receiver — the message hash is pinned to `(srcChainId, srcAddress, nonce)` and to the exact original payload, so the tokens can only ever be minted to the original `toAddress` under the same cap conditions.

The attacker path is fully unprivileged: `ProxyOFT.sendFrom` is callable by any EOA holding the synthetic token (contracts/ProxyOFT.sol:115-128). The attacker mints/obtains synths on chain A, calls `sendFrom` to bridge `maxBridgedInSupply - bridgedInSupply` worth of tokens to chain B, receives them there (raising `totalBridgedIn` to the cap), and simply holds them. Every subsequent victim bridge into chain B then fails in `_creditTo` and lands in `failedMessages`. Because `bridgedInSupply` is `totalBridgedIn - totalBridgedOut`, the attacker can keep it pinned indefinitely by not bridging out; it only releases if the attacker (or other users) bridge out of B or the governor raises `maxBridgedInSupply`.

### Impact Explanation
Victim funds are frozen: their synthetic tokens are burned on the source chain and cannot be minted on the destination chain while `bridgedInSupply` is at `maxBridgedInSupply`. `retryMessage` cannot succeed under the pinned state, and there is no admin-free alternative recovery path (failed messages can only be replayed with the identical payload). This satisfies the "temporary freezing of funds" criterion, with the freeze duration controlled by an attacker who paid only LayerZero fees and still retains full value on the destination chain. Additionally, even absent an attacker, honest users bridging in after organic cap saturation — or while `isActive` is false, or when `totalSupply` is at `maxTotalSupply` — hit the same stuck-funds state, matching the referenced bug class of unhandled cross-chain receive failure.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate-to-high for a griefing attacker: `maxBridgedInSupply` is a finite, publicly readable cap (e.g., the e2e suite documents `35,000,000 msUSD` and `4,500 msETH` caps on Base, `test/E2E.base.next.test.ts:493-504`), and saturating it requires only bridging the synth token, which the attacker keeps ownership of on the destination chain — cost is limited to LZ message fees and capital lockup, not loss of funds. The attack becomes cheaper the closer organic `bridgedInSupply` already is to the cap. No privileged role, oracle manipulation, or malicious LayerZero component is required; the destination-side mint revert is entirely a function of the deployed cap accounting.

### Recommendation
Make the receive path non-reverting for cap conditions, analogous to defensive `IXReceiver` design:

- In `ProxyOFT._creditTo`, wrap `syntheticToken.mint` in a try/catch. On failure, escrow the entitlement (e.g., record `amount_` per `(srcChainId, toAddress)` in a `pendingCredit` mapping) and emit an event, allowing the recipient to `claim()` the minted tokens later once headroom exists — rather than relying on payload-level `retryMessage`, which forces the full original payload to succeed atomically.
- Alternatively, on the source side in `_debitFrom`, enforce a conservative bound such that an in-flight message cannot push `bridgedInSupply` over `maxBridgedInSupply` on the destination (acknowledging that cross-chain state is eventually consistent, a source-side cap check against total outstanding in-flight volume is the robust fix).
- Keep `retryMessage` permissionless so anyone can unblock a victim's message once conditions change.

The same PoC pattern applies when `isActive` is toggled off or `totalSupply == maxTotalSupply` at delivery time: in all cases the burn on the source is irreversible and the destination mint silently fails into `failedMessages` with no permissionless recovery that does not depend on conditions the victim cannot control.
