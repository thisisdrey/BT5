## Title
Unbounded `origin_energy_limit` in `UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator` allows a contract owner to arbitrarily inflate their contract's energy allowance with no upper-bound sanity check - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator.java`)

### Summary
`UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator.validate()` is a privileged-but-permissionless actuator (any contract-deploying account can call it on contracts they own) that updates the `origin_energy_limit` field used to determine how much energy the contract creator subsidizes for callers. Like the reported `preallocate` bug, the validation only performs a lower-bound check and omits an upper-bound/cap check, letting the caller set the value to an arbitrary (attacker-chosen, up to `Long.MAX_VALUE`) number.

### Finding Description
The actuator's `execute()` directly persists the attacker-supplied value into the contract's protobuf field with no clamping: [1](#0-0) 

Its `validate()` method checks only that the new value is positive: [2](#0-1) 

There is no check against any upper bound (e.g. `getMaxFeeLimit()`, block energy limit, or `Long.MAX_VALUE` overflow-safety margin), unlike comparable checks elsewhere in the codebase for similar "resource limit" fields, e.g. `FreezeBalanceV2Actuator` bounding `frozenBalance` against the account balance, or `AssetIssueActuator` bounding several fields against `dynamicStore.getOneDayNetLimit()`: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

This value is later consumed by `ContractCapsule.getOriginEnergyLimit()` and used in `VMActuator` to compute the energy attributed to the contract creator/origin for TVM execution (`getAccountEnergyLimitWithFixRatio`), and is also fed into arithmetic in `TransactionTrace`: [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

Because I was not able to fully trace every downstream arithmetic use of `originEnergyLimit` in `TransactionTrace.java` within the available context (the index only surfaced 3 matches there, and I could not view their surrounding logic in this session), I cannot conclusively confirm whether an attacker-supplied `Long.MAX_VALUE` origin energy limit causes an arithmetic overflow/consensus-affecting miscalculation in energy receipt computation, or is otherwise safely clamped downstream (e.g., by `min()` against the transaction's actual energy usage or block energy limit). This is a real gap in my verification.

### Impact Explanation
If downstream code (e.g. `TransactionTrace`, `EnergyProcessor`, or receipt/fee computation) does unguarded arithmetic (`addExact`, multiplication for percent-based user/origin energy split) using this attacker-controlled unbounded value, the most likely consequences are:
- An `ArithmeticException` thrown mid-transaction-execution/receipt calculation, potentially causing inconsistent handling across nodes if not uniformly caught, or
- The contract owner being able to make their contract falsely appear to subsidize unlimited energy for callers, letting callers of that contract consume energy without paying (a form of resource/fee-accounting bypass), which is a medium-severity issue since it primarily benefits the contract owner's own callers rather than causing direct fund theft.

Given the missing sanity check mirrors the reported `preallocate` bug class precisely (a privileged setter with a lower-bound check but no upper-bound/cap check), this is a legitimate defect worth fixing regardless of the full downstream severity, which requires further investigation of `TransactionTrace.java` to fully confirm exploitability.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account that owns a deployed smart contract can invoke `UpdateEnergyLimitContract` at will (subject to `ReceiptCapsule.checkForEnergyLimit` being enabled, which is standard post-fork), making this trivially reachable via a single signed transaction with no special permissions. The lack of upper-bound validation is unconditional, so likelihood of triggering the missing check is high; likelihood of it causing consensus-impacting behavior downstream is unverified.

### Recommendation
Add an explicit upper-bound check in `UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator.validate()` for `newOriginEnergyLimit`, mirroring caps applied to other resource-limit fields in the codebase (e.g., bound it by a `dynamicStore`-configured maximum, or by `Constant.CREATOR_DEFAULT_ENERGY_LIMIT`/a documented maximum origin energy limit), and audit all downstream consumers (`VMActuator`, `TransactionTrace`) to ensure they use overflow-safe arithmetic (`Maths.addExact`/`multiplyExact`) whenever `getOriginEnergyLimit()` is used.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deploy a smart contract as account A (`CreateSmartContract`).
2. As account A, submit `UpdateEnergyLimitContract` with `origin_energy_limit = Long.MAX_VALUE`.
3. `UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator.validate()` only checks `newOriginEnergyLimit <= 0` and passes; `execute()` persists this value unchanged into `ContractStore`.
4. Trigger the contract via `TriggerSmartContract`; downstream energy computation in `VMActuator`/`TransactionTrace` consumes the unbounded `getOriginEnergyLimit()` value — full confirmation of the resulting behavior (exception vs. silent bypass) requires deeper tracing of `TransactionTrace.java`, which was not completed in this session.
