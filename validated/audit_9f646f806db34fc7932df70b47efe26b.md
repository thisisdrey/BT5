This confirms the mechanism: `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` at [1](#0-0)  computes `deposit_refund = total_deposit` when `result.result.is_err()`, and issues it via `Receipt::new_balance_refund(receiver_id, ...)` which routes to `receipt.balance_refund_receiver()`, defaulting to `predecessor_id()` [2](#0-1) . For a delegate-action inner receipt, `apply_delegate_action` sets `predecessor_id: sender_id.clone()` (Alice) even though the relayer funded the deposit [3](#0-2) , and this exact hazard is explicitly documented in-repo.

### Title
Relayer-funded deposits in meta-transactions are refunded to the malicious DelegateAction sender instead of the relayer on inner-receipt failure - (File: runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs, runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs)

### Summary
A malicious "Alice" (an unprivileged transaction signer using NEP-366 meta-transactions) can construct a `SignedDelegateAction` whose inner action carries an attached deposit and is guaranteed to fail on the receiver ("Bob's") shard. The relayer pays for and funds this deposit when submitting the outer transaction, but if the inner action's receipt fails, the protocol refunds the deposit to the `predecessor_id` of the failed receipt — which is Alice, not the relayer who actually paid.

### Finding Description
When a relayer submits a meta-transaction, `apply_delegate_action` unwraps the `DelegateAction` on Alice's shard and spawns a brand-new receipt whose `predecessor_id` is set to `sender_id` (Alice), not the relayer, even though the relayer funded the attached deposit/gas via the outer transaction: [3](#0-2) 

If the resulting receipt fails execution on the destination shard (e.g., Bob rejects the call, or Alice deliberately targets a receiver/method that always errors), `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` computes `deposit_refund = total_deposit` and generates a `Receipt::new_balance_refund` whose destination is `receipt.balance_refund_receiver()`: [4](#0-3) 

`balance_refund_receiver()` falls back to `predecessor_id()` when no explicit `refund_to` override is set: [2](#0-1) 

Since the inner receipt's `predecessor_id` is Alice (per `apply_delegate_action`), the deposit refund routes to Alice — the party who never funded the deposit — instead of back to the relayer who paid for it. This exact behavior is acknowledged directly in the codebase's own documentation and comments: [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

### Impact Explanation
This allows any ordinary NEAR account (Alice) using a relayer's meta-transaction service to extract funds it never deposited: Alice signs a `DelegateAction` whose inner action attaches a large deposit but is engineered to fail on the destination shard (e.g., a `Transfer`/`FunctionCall` to a receiver/method chosen so the receipt errors, such as insufficient storage-stake on the receiver, a nonexistent method, or exceeding a check). The relayer's transaction pays and burns the deposit balance from its own account when constructing/forwarding the receipt, but on failure the refund is credited to Alice, not the relayer, resulting in a concrete, repeatable transfer of relayer funds to Alice with no recourse — permanent loss of funds for the relayer and unearned gain for Alice. This mirrors the structure of the reported bug class: one leg of an asymmetric two-party flow is guaranteed to "succeed" for the attacker (the refund) while the corresponding payment obligation ("Bob" receiving/consuming the deposit) is never actually fulfilled.

### Likelihood Explanation
This requires no privileged access — it is fully reachable by an ordinary account using any general-purpose relayer that accepts and forwards a `SignedDelegateAction` with an attached deposit without independently validating that the inner action is virtually guaranteed to succeed on the destination shard. The behavior is deterministic and repeatable by the same malicious Alice account against the same or different relayers.

### Recommendation
This is a known, explicitly documented trust-boundary limitation of the current meta-transaction design rather than an undisclosed logic bug: the code comment in `total_deposit` and the `docs/architecture/how/meta-tx.md` "Balance refunds in meta transactions" section both call out that "relayer implementations must be aware of" this exact incentive for abuse, and instruct relayers to verify the `DelegateAction` before submitting it since "Relayer should verify DelegateAction before submitting it because it spends the attached deposit." No protocol-level fix is proposed in-repo; mitigations discussed are relayer-side (pre-validate that the inner action will very likely succeed, e.g., simulate execution, restrict deposit amounts, or require Alice to have sufficient balance/state ahead of time) or protocol changes such as routing failed-deposit refunds to the actual fee payer (signer of the outer transaction) rather than `predecessor_id`, which is out of scope of a minimal fix and would require a NEP-level protocol change.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice (attacker) creates an account with no meaningful balance and asks Relayer to submit meta-transactions on her behalf.
2. Alice crafts and signs a `DelegateAction` with `receiver_id = Bob`, containing an inner `Transfer` (or `FunctionCall`) action with a large `deposit`, chosen so that execution on Bob's shard is guaranteed to fail (e.g., `receiver_id` is an account that doesn't exist and deposit is below the amount required for implicit-account creation, or a `FunctionCall` to a method that always panics/errors, or the receiver's storage-staking check will fail per `runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs` step `LackBalanceForState`).
3. Relayer, without deep validation of the inner action's outcome, wraps the `SignedDelegateAction` in a transaction, pays gas + deposit (`total_deposit` includes the inner deposit per `runtime/runtime/src/config.rs:604-621`), and submits it.
4. On Alice's shard, `apply_delegate_action` spawns the inner receipt with `predecessor_id = Alice` and forwards it to Bob's shard: [7](#0-6) 
5. On Bob's shard, the inner receipt fails as designed. `refund_unspent_gas_and_deposits` issues `deposit_refund = total_deposit`, generating `Receipt::new_balance_refund(receiver_id = Alice, refund)` because `balance_refund_receiver()` resolves to `predecessor_id` = Alice.
6. Alice receives the entire attached deposit back, even though the relayer paid it and got nothing (no successful execution) in return. Repeating with different relayers scales the loss.

```markdown
## Balance refunds in meta transactions

Unlike gas refunds, the protocol sends balance refunds to the predecessor
(a.k.a. sender) of the receipt. This makes sense, as we deposit the attached
balance to the receiver, who has to explicitly reattach a new balance to new
receipts they might spawn.

In the world of meta transactions, this assumption is also challenged. If an
inner action requires an attached balance (for example a transfer action) then
this balance is taken from the relayer.

The relayer can see what the cost will be before submitting the meta transaction
and agrees to pay for it, so nothing wrong so far. But what if the transaction
fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is `Alice` and
therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is
something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial
incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached
but will fail on Bob's shard.
```
