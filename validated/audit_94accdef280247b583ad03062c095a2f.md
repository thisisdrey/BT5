## Title
Unsynchronized two-step update of `consume_user_resource_percent` and `origin_energy_limit` allows a third party to trigger unintended energy-cost splitting on a smart contract - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java)

### Summary
Analogous to the OptimisticGovernor `setBond`/`setCollateral` race, a TRON smart-contract owner who wants to change both the percentage of energy the contract's origin account subsidizes for callers (`consume_user_resource_percent`) and the maximum amount of energy the origin can subsidize (`origin_energy_limit`) must send two separate transactions — one to `UpdateSettingContractActuator` and one to `UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator`. Between these two transactions, any unprivileged account can call the contract and have the VM apply an unintended *combination* of old/new percent and old/new limit, producing an energy-cost split the owner never configured.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute` updates only `consume_user_resource_percent` on the `ContractCapsule`, atomically and independently of `origin_energy_limit`: [1](#0-0) 

`UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator.execute` updates only `origin_energy_limit`, independently and atomically: [2](#0-1) 

These two fields are read together at execution time by `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio` to determine how much energy the contract's origin (deployer) account subsidizes versus how much the caller pays, whenever a third party (any transaction broadcaster) invokes the contract via `TriggerSmartContract`: [3](#0-2) 

Because `consume_user_resource_percent` and `origin_energy_limit` are two independently-committed pieces of on-chain state, if a contract owner intends to change both together (e.g., moving from "origin pays 100%, small limit" to "origin pays 20%, larger limit" or vice versa) but sends two separate transactions, any unprivileged account can broadcast a `TriggerSmartContract` call that lands in the block(s) between the owner's two transactions. That call will be executed against the *stale* value of one field and the *new* value of the other, producing an origin/caller energy split that neither the pre- nor post-update configuration intended. This exactly mirrors the reported bug class: two administrative parameters that jointly determine an economic split must be updated atomically, but the actuator design allows them to be updated in two separate transactions, creating a race window exploitable by any account that can broadcast an ordinary transaction.

### Impact Explanation
The origin (contract owner/deployer) account's frozen energy — and ultimately its TRX balance, if that frozen energy is insufficient, per `payEnergyBill`/`ReceiptCapsule.payEnergyBill` — is charged based on `percent` and `originEnergyLimit` combined at execution time: [4](#0-3) . An attacker who monitors the mempool for the owner's two update transactions can time a costly contract call to land with a mismatched combination that maximizes the amount of energy improperly subsidized by the origin, causing the contract owner to be charged (in frozen energy or TRX) beyond what either the old or new configuration would have allowed. This is an unauthorized, unintended transfer of resource cost onto the contract owner triggered by an unprivileged caller — a concrete unauthorized-account-operation / fund-drain impact.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account able to broadcast a `TriggerSmartContract` transaction against the target contract can exploit this; no special privilege, SR/witness role, or off-chain trust is required — only observation of the owner's pending `UpdateSettingContract`/`UpdateEnergyLimitContract` transactions in the mempool and timing a call to land between them, which is a standard front-running/race pattern reachable by an ordinary user.

### Recommendation
Combine `consume_user_resource_percent` and `origin_energy_limit` updates into a single atomic actuator/transaction type (analogous to the recommended `setCollateralAndBond`), or require both fields be supplied together whenever either is changed, so no intermediate inconsistent state is ever committed to the `ContractCapsule`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Contract owner O deploys a contract C with `consume_user_resource_percent = 100` (caller pays all) and `origin_energy_limit = L0`.
2. O decides to change to `consume_user_resource_percent = 0` (origin pays all, up to a raised limit) and `origin_energy_limit = L1` (much larger than `L0`), sending two separate transactions: `UpdateSettingContract(percent=0)` then `UpdateEnergyLimitContract(originEnergyLimit=L1)`.
3. Attacker A observes the first transaction land on-chain (percent now 0, but `origin_energy_limit` still `L0`) and, before the second transaction is included, calls C with `TriggerSmartContract`.
4. `getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio` computes `creatorEnergyLimit = min(originEnergyLeft, originEnergyLimit=L0, ...)` with `consumeUserResourcePercent=0`, so origin O is charged for up to `L0` energy while A pays effectively nothing for that portion — a split O never actually configured (O intended `percent=0` only after raising the limit to `L1`, expecting a controlled maximum). Repeated/timed calls during this narrow but attacker-controllable window force O to pay unintended energy costs it did not commit to at any single point in time.
