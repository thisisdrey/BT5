### Title
Permanent loss of signer's wNEAR when `AuthCall.contract_id` lacks `on_auth` (MethodNotFound after irreversible withdraw+near_withdraw) - (File: contracts/defuse/src/contract/intents/state.rs)

### Summary
`State::auth_call` (`contracts/defuse/src/contract/intents/state.rs:303-337`) irreversibly debits the signer's wNEAR balance and unwraps it to NEAR via `near_withdraw` *before* it is known whether `on_auth` on the attacker-chosen `contract_id` can even be invoked. If `contract_id` has no `on_auth` export, `do_auth_call`'s (`contracts/defuse/src/contract/intents/auth_call.rs:33-37`) resulting `FunctionCall` receipt fails with `MethodNotFound`, and because this promise is the terminal, `.detach()`'d promise in the chain with no resolver callback, the NEAR is neither delivered to the intended callee nor recredited to the signer.

### Finding Description
The broken binding: `wNEAR_debited(signer)` should equal `NEAR_delivered(contract_id) + NEAR_recredited(signer)`. In the vulnerable path, `wNEAR_debited(signer) > 0` while `NEAR_delivered(contract_id) + NEAR_recredited(signer) == 0`.

Trace: `auth_call` first calls `self.withdraw(signer_id, [wNEAR, attached_deposit], ...)` (`state.rs:308-316`), which subtracts the signer's Verifier-internal wNEAR balance unconditionally and synchronously — this is not a Promise, it's an internal ledger mutation that commits immediately. It then schedules `ext_wnear::near_withdraw` followed by `.then(do_auth_call)` (`state.rs:318-332`), and the whole chain is `.detach()`'d (`state.rs:334`), meaning the contract does not await or inspect the final outcome. `do_auth_call` (`auth_call.rs:17-37`) checks only that the preceding `near_withdraw` succeeded (`promise_result_checked_void(0)`), then builds `Promise::new(auth_call.contract_id)` and issues `ext_auth_callee::ext_on(p).on_auth(...)` as the terminal action. Since `contract_id` is attacker-controlled and can be any deployed account without an `on_auth` method, this final `FunctionCall` action fails at the protocol level with `MethodNotFound`. There is no `.then()` resolver attached to this final promise (unlike, e.g., the NEP-141/NEP-245 withdraw flows in `contracts/defuse/src/contract/tokens/nep141/withdraw.rs` and `.../nep245/resolver.rs`, which do have `*_resolve_*` callbacks that re-credit on failure). The doc comment on `AuthCall::attached_deposit` even states explicitly: "NOTE: the `wNEAR` will not be refunded in case of fail" (`contracts/defuse/core/src/intents/auth.rs:30`), confirming this is a known, intentional gap rather than an oversight that's guarded elsewhere.

Attacker's exact payload: sign or have the victim sign (or, per the threat model, the attacker acts as their own signer, so this simply describes any signer using `AuthCall` normally) an `Intent::AuthCall` with `contract_id` = attacker's deployed account (any contract without `AuthCallee::on_auth`, e.g., a bare `near-sdk` contract or even a plain non-contract account with insufficient gas/wrong ABI), `attached_deposit` > 0, and `msg` arbitrary. Submitting via `execute_intents` executes `auth_call`, debiting wNEAR and unwrapping to NEAR, then failing on the final receipt. No guard (`MultiPayload::verify`, nonce checks, `assert_one_yocto`, `#[private]`) inspects whether `on_auth` exists before the debit and withdrawal are committed, and none of them provide a refund path after the fact.

### Impact Explanation
The signer's wNEAR balance is permanently reduced by `attached_deposit` with no corresponding NEAR delivered to any account and no balance re-credited — value leaves the Verifier's internal ledger without ever reaching an authorized destination, and is not returned to the signer. This matches "user funds permanently frozen" / lost under the Critical impact category. It is repeatable per intent execution and does not require any elevated privileges — only that the signer (which could be the attacker deceiving a victim, or, more directly relevant to this unprivileged-attacker scope, any user who is induced to target a bad `contract_id`) has sufficient wNEAR balance.

Note: the practical severity nuance is that this requires the *signer themself* to name a bad `contract_id` — an attacker cannot force another user's signed intent to target a different `contract_id`, since `contract_id` is part of the signed intent. So the realistic attack requires either (a) tricking a victim into signing an `AuthCall` intent pointed at the attacker's broken contract (social-engineering-adjacent, arguably out of the strict "unprivileged attacker acting alone" repeatable-exploit model), or (b) the signer accidentally targeting their own misconfigured/incomplete contract. This is a real fund-loss bug but its trigger condition depends on the signer's own choice of `contract_id`, not a manipulation the attacker can impose on a victim's already-signed, otherwise-correct payload.

### Likelihood Explanation
Preconditions are simple and cheap to reach: any account with wNEAR balance in the Verifier ≥ desired `attached_deposit`, and any deployed contract lacking `on_auth`. No role/permission is needed. However, exploitation against a *third-party victim* requires that victim to sign an intent naming the attacker's broken contract, which is a decision the signer makes, not one the attacker can force onto an existing valid payload.

### Recommendation
Add a `#[private]` resolver callback attached after `do_auth_call`'s promise (or restructure `do_auth_call` itself to chain a `.then()` resolver) that inspects the `on_auth` promise result and, on failure (including `MethodNotFound`), re-credits the signer's Verifier-internal wNEAR balance (mirroring the pattern used in `contracts/defuse/src/contract/tokens/nep141/withdraw.rs` and `contracts/defuse/src/contract/tokens/nep245/resolver.rs`).

    #[private]
    pub fn do_auth_call(signer_id: AccountId, auth_call: AuthCall) -> Promise {
        if !auth_call.attached_deposit.is_zero() {
            require!(
                promise_result_checked_void(0).is_ok(),
                "near_withdraw failed",
            );
        }

        let min_gas = auth_call.min_gas();
        let mut p = Promise::new(auth_call.contract_id);

        if let Some(state_init) = auth_call.state_init {
            p = p.state_init(state_init, NearToken::ZERO);
        }

        ext_auth_callee::ext_on(p)
            .with_attached_deposit(auth_call.attached_deposit)
            .with_static_gas(min_gas)
            .on_auth(signer_id, auth_call.msg)
    }
