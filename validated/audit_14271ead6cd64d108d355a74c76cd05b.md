## Analysis

The strongest reachable analog in java-tron to the Maia `BoostAggregator` fee-manipulation bug is the smart‑contract `consume_user_resource_percent` setting, controlled unilaterally by a contract's deployer/owner via `UpdateSettingContract`, and applied instantly — with no per-caller snapshot or timelock — to every subsequent `TriggerSmartContract` call made by any unprivileged caller.

### Title
Contract owner can instantly raise `consume_user_resource_percent` to 100% and shift the entire energy bill onto unsuspecting callers - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java)

### Summary
`UpdateSettingContractActuator` lets any smart-contract's owner change `consume_user_resource_percent` (0–100) for that contract at any time, with the change taking effect on the very next transaction. This percentage determines how much of a call's energy bill is charged to the caller versus the contract owner. A malicious/careless contract owner can set it to `100`, causing every subsequent unprivileged caller to pay 100% of the energy cost (in TRX, if their frozen energy is insufficient) instead of the previously advertised split, exactly mirroring the `BoostAggregator.protocolFee` bug where the owner unilaterally re-prices a shared resource that other users are already relying on.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` writes the new percent directly into the `ContractCapsule` with no delay, no versioning, and no per-caller opt-in: [1](#0-0) 

`validate()` only bounds the value to `[0, 100]` and checks that the sender is the contract's origin address — there is no cooldown, no maximum-change-rate, and no lock protecting callers who already built transactions around the previous value: [2](#0-1) 

This percentage is read live, at execution time of each `TriggerSmartContract`, both when pre-computing the total allowed energy limit: [3](#0-2) 

and when the actual bill is split between origin (contract owner) and caller in `TransactionTrace.pay()`: [4](#0-3) 

`ReceiptCapsule.payEnergyBill()` then charges the caller's portion; if the caller's frozen energy is insufficient, TRX is deducted directly from the caller's balance for the shortfall: [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

Note the contrast with the `UpdateBrokerageActuator`/`DelegationStore` fee, which was explicitly hardened (per-cycle snapshotting via `setBrokerage(cycle, ...)` applied only at the next maintenance boundary) to prevent exactly this class of retroactive fee-change abuse: [7](#0-6) [8](#0-7) 

`UpdateSettingContract` has no equivalent protection — the change is live immediately, matching the exact root cause the Maia report flagged in `BoostAggregator.setProtocolFee`.

### Impact Explanation
Any unprivileged account calling a smart contract can have their expected energy-cost split rewritten by the contract owner between the time they observe the contract's setting and the time their transaction is packed into a block. Setting `consume_user_resource_percent` to 100 forces the caller to pay for 100% of the contract's energy consumption, which — if the caller's frozen energy is insufficient — is billed directly from the caller's TRX balance. This is an unauthorized transfer of cost/value from the caller to the contract owner (who now pays 0% instead of whatever was previously communicated), which can be repeated on every call, draining callers who interact with the contract in good faith.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any contract owner (an unprivileged, non-SR, non-witness "contract deployer" role explicitly in-scope) by broadcasting a single `UpdateSettingContract` transaction. No special privilege beyond having deployed the contract is required, and the change applies to the very next `TriggerSmartContract` transaction from any caller, including ones already pending in mempool. This mirrors the original finding's mechanics precisely (owner unilaterally sets a fee/percentage parameter that retroactively hits users' pending/ongoing interactions).

### Recommendation
Adopt the same mitigation pattern already used for witness brokerage in `DelegationStore`/`MaintenanceManager`: snapshot the effective `consume_user_resource_percent` per caller at the time of their call (e.g., cache the value seen when the caller's transaction was constructed/simulated, or introduce a maintenance-cycle-style delay before an `UpdateSettingContract` change takes effect) so an owner cannot retroactively/instantly alter the cost-sharing ratio for calls that were already in flight.

### Proof of Concept
1. Contract owner deploys a contract with `consume_user_resource_percent = 0` (owner pays all energy) via `CreateSmartContract`.
2. Callers observe this value and begin interacting with the contract expecting free/low-cost calls.
3. Owner submits `UpdateSettingContract` setting `consume_user_resource_percent = 100` — accepted immediately per `UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` ( [1](#0-0) ).
4. The next `TriggerSmartContract` from any caller is billed with `percent = 0` for the origin at `TransactionTrace.pay()` ( [9](#0-8) ), so the caller pays 100% of the energy, deducted from the caller's TRX balance if frozen energy is insufficient ( [6](#0-5) ).
