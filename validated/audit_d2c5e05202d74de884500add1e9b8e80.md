### Title
Meta-transaction (`DelegateAction`) balance refunds are routed to the delegator, letting an unprivileged `sender_id` front-run/grief the relayer and steal the attached deposit paid on their behalf - (File: `runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs`, `runtime/runtime/src/lib.rs`)

### Summary
In NEP-366 meta-transactions, a relayer pays all gas and attached deposits for actions that a delegator (`sender_id`) asked to have executed. The protocol computes the receipt's required cost and forwards it exactly like the original bug's `_calculateFillOrderInfo`: it trusts values on the delegator's side (the delegator's account state / inner action) at the moment the relayer signed/submitted, but the delegator can change that state before the inner receipt is actually applied on their shard, and on failure the deposit refund is paid to the delegator (predecessor), not the relayer. This mirrors the `limitPrice_e36` front-run: an untrusted party can move the on-chain state between "commit" and "execute" so that the party who fronted the assets (the executor/relayer) ends up bearing the loss while the untrusted party keeps or receives the benefit.

### Finding Description
`apply_delegate_action` unwraps a `SignedDelegateAction` on the delegator's (`sender_id`'s) shard and spawns a brand-new receipt whose actions and required deposit were fixed by the relayer at submission time: [1](#0-0) 

The code comment itself documents the asymmetric trust/loss model: the relayer prepays all fees and deposits, but "if something goes wrong, deposit is refunded to the predecessor, this is `sender_id`/Sender in DelegateAction" while "gas is refunded to the signer, this is Relayer." The comment explicitly places the burden of pre-verification entirely on the relayer: [2](#0-1) 

The project's own architecture docs confirm this is a live, exploitable timing gap, not merely a theoretical concern: a delegator such as "Alice" can reduce her own balance in the receiving contract "in just the right moment" between the relayer forwarding the transaction and the inner action actually executing on Bob's shard, wasting the relayer's already-burnt NEAR gas with no compensation: [3](#0-2) 

And separately, if the inner action fails on the receiver shard, the attached token balance the relayer fronted is refunded to Alice (the predecessor), not the relayer, creating a direct financial incentive for Alice to front-run/manipulate her own state so that inner actions fail after the relayer has already paid, redirecting the refund to herself: [4](#0-3) 

This is structurally identical to the `fillOrder` bug class: an unprivileged actor (`sender_id`/order creator) can alter state that the value-transfer calculation depends on, between the moment an executor (relayer) commits/pays and the moment execution actually occurs, causing the executor to lose the pre-paid assets.

### Impact Explanation
The relayer's prepaid deposit for the inner delegated actions can be redirected to the delegator instead of returned to the relayer whenever the delegator manipulates their own account/contract state (e.g., causing the inner action to fail) between the time the relayer signs/submits the outer transaction and the time the delegate action executes on the delegator's shard. This is concrete loss of funds (the relayer's fronted NEAR/attached deposit) transferred to an unprivileged account (the delegator), matching the "theft of funds" bar for this analog.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any ordinary account can act as a delegator (`sender_id`) in a `DelegateAction`/`DelegateV2` and has full read/write control of their own account and contracts. The relayer's decision to submit is necessarily based on a state snapshot; there is no protocol-level atomicity or reservation of the delegator's state between submission and execution, so a malicious delegator can reliably trigger the unfavorable refund path on demand. The docs already acknowledge this is a known behavior requiring "trust between the relayer and its user," confirming the path is realistically reachable by an ordinary client without any privileged role.

### Recommendation
- Route failed-inner-action deposit refunds for `Delegate`/`DelegateV2` actions back to the relayer (the outer transaction's signer/original payer) rather than to `sender_id`, at least for the portion of the deposit the relayer supplied, mirroring how gas refunds already go to the relayer.
- Alternatively, require relayers to specify a maximum acceptable state/nonce condition (already partly covered by `max_block_height` and nonce) that also captures balance/contract-state preconditions, so a delegate action can be safely rejected on-chain if delegator state changed in a way that would harm the relayer.
- At minimum, elevate the current documentation-only warning into an explicit protocol safeguard (e.g., a relayer-specified "refund beneficiary override" for `DelegateAction` inner deposits) instead of relying purely on off-chain trust between relayer and delegator.

### Proof of Concept
1. Alice creates account `FT` balance and signs a `DelegateAction` with an inner `ft_transfer`-style action plus a "pay relayer" action, matching the flow described in `docs/architecture/how/meta-tx.md`.
2. Relayer checks Alice's current `$FT` balance, deems it sufficient, wraps the `SignedDelegateAction` in an outer transaction, and submits it, prepaying all gas and attached deposit as shown in `apply_delegate_action` (`runtime/runtime/src/actions.rs:483-516`).
3. Before the delegate action's inner receipt executes on Alice's/FT's shard, Alice submits a separate transaction that drains her `$FT` balance (or otherwise makes the inner action fail).
4. The inner action fails; per the documented and implemented refund routing, the attached deposit is refunded to `sender_id` (Alice), not the relayer (`docs/architecture/how/meta-tx.md:225-242`), while the relayer has already burnt gas and lost the deposit it fronted. [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5)

pub(crate) fn apply_delegate_action(
    state_update: &mut TrieUpdate,
    apply_state: &ApplyState,
    action_receipt: &VersionedActionReceipt,
    sender_id: &AccountId,
    signed_delegate_action: VersionedSignedDelegateActionRef<'_>,
    result: &mut ActionResult,
) -> Result<(), RuntimeError> {
    // The inner delegate signature is verified below, here on the receiver shard.
    // Meter its verification compute against this shard's `compute_limit`; the gas
    // for it was already burnt at tx conversion on the signer shard. Without the
    // fix the compute is instead mis-charged on the signer shard (which never runs
    // this verify), letting the work escape the receiver shard's budget. See
    // `signature_verification_cost`.
    if apply_state.config.wasm_config.fix_ml_dsa_cost_charging {
        let verify_compute = delegate_signature_verification_compute(
            &apply_state.config.fees,
            signed_delegate_action.delegate_action().public_key(),
        );
        result.compute_usage = safe_add_compute(result.compute_usage, verify_compute)?;
    }
    if !signed_delegate_action.verify() {
        result.result = Err(ActionErrorKind::DelegateActionInvalidSignature.into());
        return Ok(());
    }
    let delegate_action = signed_delegate_action.delegate_action();
    if apply_state.block_height > delegate_action.max_block_height() {
        result.result = Err(ActionErrorKind::DelegateActionExpired.into());
        return Ok(());
    }
    if delegate_action.sender_id().as_str() != sender_id.as_str() {
        result.result = Err(ActionErrorKind::DelegateActionSenderDoesNotMatchTxReceiver {
            sender_id: delegate_action.sender_id().clone(),
            receiver_id: sender_id.clone(),
        }
        .into());
        return Ok(());
    }

    validate_delegate_action_key(state_update, apply_state, delegate_action, result)?;
    if result.result.is_err() {
        // Validation failed. Need to return Ok() because this is not a runtime error.
        // "result.result" will be return to the User as the action execution result.
        return Ok(());
    }

    // Generate a new receipt from DelegateAction.
    let new_receipt = Receipt::V0(ReceiptV0 {
        predecessor_id: sender_id.clone(),
        receiver_id: delegate_action.receiver_id().clone(),
        receipt_id: CryptoHash::default(),

        receipt: ReceiptEnum::Action(ActionReceipt {
            signer_id: action_receipt.signer_id().clone(),
            signer_public_key: action_receipt.signer_public_key().clone(),
            gas_price: action_receipt.gas_price(),
            output_data_receivers: vec![],
            input_data_ids: vec![],
            actions: delegate_action.get_actions(),
        }),
    });

    // Note, Relayer prepaid all fees and all things required by actions: attached deposits and attached gas.
    // If something goes wrong, deposit is refunded to the predecessor, this is sender_id/Sender in DelegateAction.
    // Gas is refunded to the signer, this is Relayer.
    // Some contracts refund the deposit. Usually they refund the deposit to the predecessor and this is sender_id/Sender from DelegateAction.
    // Therefore Relayer should verify DelegateAction before submitting it because it spends the attached deposit.

    let prepaid_send_fees = total_prepaid_send_fees(&apply_state.config, action_receipt.actions())?;
    let required_cost = receipt_required_cost(apply_state, &new_receipt)?;
    // This gas will be burnt by the receiver of the created receipt.
    // Compute costs of that are not relevant at this point, the "used" gas is
    // only reserved for execution later, potentially on a different shard.
    result.gas_used = result.gas_used.checked_add_result(required_cost.gas)?;
    // This gas was prepaid on Relayer shard. Need to burn it because the receipt is going to be sent.
    // gas_used is incremented because otherwise the gas will be refunded. Refund function checks only gas_used.
    result.gas_used = result.gas_used.checked_add_result(prepaid_send_fees.gas)?;
    result.gas_burnt = result.gas_burnt.checked_add_result(prepaid_send_fees.gas)?;
    result.compute_usage = safe_add_compute(result.compute_usage, prepaid_send_fees.compute)?;
    result.new_receipts.push(new_receipt);

    Ok(())
```

## Relayer

Meta transactions only work with a relayer. This is an application layer
concept, implemented off-chain. Think of it as a server that accepts a
`SignedDelegateAction`, does some checks on them and eventually forwards it
inside a transaction to the blockchain network.

A relayer may choose to offer their service for free but that's not going to be
financially viable long-term. But they could easily have the user pay using
other means, outside of Near blockchain. And with some tricks, it can even be
paid using fungible tokens on Near.

In the example visualized above, the payment is done using \$FT. Together with
the transfer to John, Alice also adds an action to pay 0.1 \$FT to the relayer.
The relayer checks the content of the `SignedDelegateAction` and only processes
it if this payment is included as the first action. In this way, the relayer
will be paid in the same transaction as John.

Note that the payment to the relayer is still not guaranteed. It could be that
Alice does not have sufficient $FT and the transfer fails. To mitigate, the
relayer should check the $FT balance of Alice first.

Unfortunately, this still does not guarantee that the balance will be high
enough once the meta transaction executes. The relayer could waste NEAR gas
without compensation if Alice somehow reduces her \$FT balance in just the right
moment. Some level of trust between the relayer and its user is therefore
required.

## Balance refunds in meta transactions

Unlike gas refunds, the protocol sends balance refunds to the predecessor
(a.k.a. sender) of the receipt. This makes sense, as we deposit the attached
balance to the receiver, who has to explicitly reattach a new balance to new
receipts they might spawn.

In the world of meta transactions, this assumption is also challenged. If an
inner action requires an attached balance (for example a transfer action) then
this balance is taken from the relayer.

The relayer can see what the cost will be before submitting the meta transaction
and agrees to pay for it, so nothing wrong so far. But what if the transaction
fails execution on Bob's shard? At this point, the predecessor is `Alice` and
therefore she receives the token balance refunded, not the relayer. This is
something relayer implementations must be aware of since there is a financial
incentive for Alice to submit meta transactions that have high balances attached
but will fail on Bob's shard.
```
