### Title
Oracle price resolution failures can permanently block borrow, collateral-add, collateral-remove, and liquidate - (File: mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar)

### Summary
### Finding Description
The external report describes a class of bug where a core AMM operation (`swap`) makes a mandatory external call to a secondary contract (`AlgebraVirtualPool`), and if that external call reverts, the core operation is entirely blocked until the secondary contract is fixed or unlinked. The analog in Zest's market contract is the oracle price-resolution pipeline that every collateral-adding, borrowing, collateral-removing, and liquidating action must pass through.

`price-resolve` in `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar` calls `resolve-price-feed`, which for Pyth-typed assets calls `call-pyth`, an unconditional external `contract-call?` to the third-party `pyth-storage-v4` contract: [1](#0-0) 

and for DIA-typed assets calls `call-dia`, an external `contract-call?` to the third-party `dia-oracle` contract: [2](#0-1) 

Both of these are wrapped in `unwrap!`, so any revert or unexpected response from the external oracle contract causes `try!`/`unwrap!` to bubble up and abort the entire caller transaction: `ERR-ORACLE-PYTH` / `ERR-ORACLE-DIA`.

`price-resolve` and `price-multi-resolve` are invoked from `get-assets` (used inside `borrow`, `collateral-add`, `collateral-remove`, and `liquidate`) and directly inside `collateral-remove` for disabled-collateral pricing: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

`liquidate` itself also unconditionally resolves prices for the borrower's full position before it can compute health/liquidation parameters: [5](#0-4) 

If either the Pyth or DIA contract becomes unavailable/misbehaving for a given asset's price identifier (e.g., feed removed, contract paused/upgraded incompatibly, out-of-gas due to internal state bloat, or any other revert condition inside the oracle contract, not caused by Zest), every market entry point that must resolve that asset's price — `borrow`, `collateral-add` (when adding new collateral with existing debt), `collateral-remove` (with debt, or disabled-collateral flow), and `liquidate` — will unconditionally revert. There is no fallback, staleness bypass, or alternate price source; the `try!`/`unwrap!` pattern turns any oracle-contract-side failure into a hard failure of the entire market flow for every asset combination that includes the affected asset, exactly mirroring the `activeIncentive`/`AlgebraVirtualPool` push-failure pattern in the referenced report.

### Impact Explanation
This lands in the "temporary freezing of funds" bucket (High impact): while the affected oracle contract is broken/reverting, ordinary users cannot borrow against, deposit new, or withdraw (with debt) any asset priced by the failing feed, and — critically — liquidators cannot liquidate positions holding the affected collateral or debt asset. Since `liquidate` also resolves the full position (not just the assets being seized/repaid) via `get-assets mask` over the borrower's entire mask, a single problematic asset in a borrower's position can block liquidation of that position, indirectly threatening protocol solvency (bad debt can accrue unbounded while liquidation is blocked) if the outage persists across a market downturn.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is moderate: it depends on an external, non-Zest-controlled oracle contract (Pyth storage or DIA oracle) reverting or behaving unexpectedly. Such events are plausible (contract upgrades, price feed deprecation/removal, provider bugs, network-level disruptions on the oracle's own dependencies) and, unlike a DAO misconfiguration, are outside Zest's control and not addressed by any of the excluded "DAO registry" or "egroup invariant" carve-outs. There is no circuit breaker or fallback price mechanism in `price-resolve`/`resolve-price-feed` to allow the market to continue operating in a degraded mode for unaffected functionality (e.g., liquidations of positions not involving the broken asset, or borrow of unrelated assets that still get bundled into `get-assets` for the whole mask).

### Recommendation
Short term: wrap the external oracle calls (`call-pyth`, `call-dia`) so failures are handled gracefully per-asset rather than reverting the entire transaction — e.g., allow `liquidate` to proceed using the last-known-good cached price (subject to `max-staleness`) or a governance-supplied emergency price when the live oracle call reverts, particularly for liquidation and collateral-removal paths where blocking has systemic risk. Long term: decouple "get all prices for the position mask" from "get prices strictly required for the operation" so a stuck asset doesn't block operations unrelated to it, and add an oracle-outage circuit breaker/pause + admin fallback path distinct from full market pause.

### Proof of Concept
1. Assume the Pyth `pyth-storage-v4` contract (or the DIA oracle) reverts or returns malformed data for a specific `ident` (e.g., due to an upstream bug, feed removal, or the oracle contract's own logic reverting) — a condition entirely outside Zest's control, analogous to `AlgebraVirtualPool` reverting in the referenced report.
2. Any account holding a position whose collateral/debt mask includes the asset tied to that feed calls `borrow`, `collateral-add` (with existing debt), or `collateral-remove` (with debt or disabled collateral): `get-assets`/`price-resolve` calls `call-pyth`/`call-dia`, which reverts via `unwrap!` → `ERR-ORACLE-PYTH`/`ERR-ORACLE-DIA`, aborting the whole transaction.
3. A liquidator attempts `liquidate` on an unhealthy position that includes the same asset among the borrower's (enabled) collateral/debt set: `get-assets mask` in `liquidate` triggers the same failure, so the position cannot be liquidated at all while the oracle keeps failing.
4. Until the oracle contract is fixed, or Zest's DAO reconfigures/disables the asset (an out-of-band remediation, not a code-level mitigation), all of borrow/collateral-add/collateral-remove/liquidate for positions touching that asset are frozen — funds (including unclaimed yield/interest continuing to accrue as bad debt) are temporarily locked and the protocol accumulates unliquidatable risk.

(define-private (call-pyth (ident (buff 32)))
  (let ((res (unwrap! (contract-call? 'SP1CGXWEAMG6P6FT04W66NVGJ7PQWMDAC19R7PJ0Y.pyth-storage-v4 get-price ident) ERR-ORACLE-PYTH)))
    (ok res)))

(define-private (resolve-pyth (ident (buff 32)))
  (let ((response (try! (call-pyth ident)))
        (price (get price response))
        (expo (get expo response))
        (conf (get conf response))
        (final-price (normalize-pyth price expo))
        (timestamp (get publish-time response)))
    (try! (check-confidence price conf))
    (ok { value: final-price, timestamp: timestamp })))

(define-private (resolve-dia (ident (buff 32)))
