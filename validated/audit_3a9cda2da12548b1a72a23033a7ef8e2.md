### Title
Old Operator's Pending Commission Shares Incorrectly Charged Commission After `switch_operator` — (`aptos-move/framework/aptos-framework/sources/staking_contract.move`)

---

### Summary

After `switch_operator` is called, the `distribution_pool` retains shares belonging to `old_operator` representing their pending (not yet withdrawable) commission. When `update_distribution_pool` is subsequently called with `new_operator` as the `operator` parameter, the exemption check `if (shareholder != operator)` no longer protects `old_operator`'s shares. Those shares are treated as a regular (non-operator) shareholder and charged commission, with the deducted shares transferred to `new_operator`. This causes `old_operator` to receive less than their legitimately earned commission.

---

### Finding Description

**Step 1 — `switch_operator` leaves old_operator's shares in the distribution_pool.**

`switch_operator` calls `request_commission_internal(old_operator, &mut staking_contract)` before reassigning the contract to `new_operator`: [1](#0-0) 

`request_commission_internal` calls `add_distribution(old_operator, staking_contract, old_operator, commission_amount)`, which calls `pool_u64::buy_in` to add `old_operator` as a shareholder in `distribution_pool` with shares worth `commission_amount`. It then calls `stake::unlock_with_cap(commission_amount, ...)` to move that amount to `pending_inactive`: [2](#0-1) 

The staking contract (including the `distribution_pool` with `old_operator`'s shares) is then moved to the `new_operator` key: [3](#0-2) 

**Step 2 — `update_distribution_pool` is called with `new_operator` as the operator.**

When `request_commission` or `distribute` is subsequently called for `new_operator`, `update_distribution_pool` is invoked. The exemption check uses the current `operator` parameter: [4](#0-3) 

Because `old_operator != new_operator`, `old_operator`'s shares are not exempt. The function computes `unpaid_commission` on the apparent increase in `old_operator`'s share value and transfers those shares to `new_operator`.

**Step 3 — Concrete trigger path.**

The short-circuit at line 1008 (`if (distribution_pool.total_coins() == updated_total_coins) { return }`) prevents the double-charge during the immediate `request_commission(new_operator)` call (because `pending_inactive` hasn't changed yet at that point in `add_distribution`). However, once the lockup expires and `distribute(staker, new_operator)` is called, `distribute_internal` passes `inactive + pending_inactive` as `updated_total_coins`: [5](#0-4) 

At that point `updated_total_coins > distribution_pool.total_coins()`, the short-circuit does not fire, and `old_operator`'s shares are charged commission with the proceeds going to `new_operator`.

---

### Impact Explanation

`old_operator` loses a portion of their legitimately earned commission. Specifically, the loss is approximately:

```
old_operator_loss ≈ (old_commission / (old_commission + new_commission))
                    × new_commission × commission_percentage / 100
```

These funds are transferred to `new_operator` without authorization. This constitutes direct theft of staking balances from `old_operator`.

---

### Likelihood Explanation

The trigger requires only:
1. A staker calling `switch_operator` while `old_operator` has pending (not yet withdrawable) commission — a normal operational scenario.
2. A subsequent `request_commission` or `distribute` call for `new_operator` after the lockup expires — also routine.

No privileged access is required. A staker colluding with `new_operator` can deliberately exploit this, or it can occur accidentally in normal operation.

---

### Recommendation

In `update_distribution_pool`, the operator exemption must account for the possibility that a prior operator's shares exist in the pool. One approach: when `switch_operator` is called, record the `old_operator`'s address in the `StakingContract` as a "legacy operator" and exempt it from commission charging in `update_distribution_pool` until their shares are fully distributed. Alternatively, immediately redeem `old_operator`'s shares from the distribution pool at the time of the switch (recording the owed amount separately) so no shares belonging to a former operator remain in the pool when `update_distribution_pool` is called with a different operator address.

---

### Proof of Concept

```move
// Pseudocode integration test
#[test]
fun test_switch_operator_double_commission() {
    // Setup: staker creates contract with old_operator, 10% commission
    // Pool earns 100 APT rewards → old_operator's pending commission = 10 APT
    // (request_commission NOT called yet, so commission is still in active stake)

    // Staker switches to new_operator (also 10% commission)
    // switch_operator internally calls request_commission_internal(old_operator)
    // → old_operator gets 10 APT shares in distribution_pool
    // → 10 APT moved to pending_inactive

    // Time passes, pool earns another 100 APT rewards
    // request_commission(new_operator) is called
    // → new_operator gets 10 APT shares in distribution_pool
    // → 10 APT more moved to pending_inactive

    // Lockup expires; distribute(staker, new_operator) is called
    // update_distribution_pool is called with updated_total_coins = 20 APT, operator = new_operator
    // old_operator's shares (worth 10 APT) appear to "gain" 10 APT * (10/20) = 5 APT
    // unpaid_commission = 5 APT * 10% = 0.5 APT transferred from old_operator to new_operator

    // Assert: old_operator receives 9.5 APT instead of 10 APT
    // Assert: new_operator receives 10.5 APT instead of 10 APT
    assert!(old_operator_received == 10_APT, 0); // FAILS: receives 9.5 APT
}
```
