### Title
No timelock/ragequit period for `UpdateSettingContractActuator` allows front-run cost-shifting on contract callers - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java`)

### Summary
`UpdateSettingContractActuator` lets a smart-contract owner change `consume_user_resource_percent` — the ratio that determines how much of a call's energy cost is charged to the contract owner's frozen energy versus the calling account's own resources/TRX balance — and the new value takes effect on the very next transaction, with no delay, notice period, or ragequit window. This mirrors the reported UniV3OracleImpl issue, where critical, price/cost-affecting parameters (`$defaultFee`, `$defaultPeriod`, `$defaultScaledOfferFactor`, `$_pairOverrides`) can be changed and take immediate effect, enabling front-running against unsuspecting counterparties.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` directly overwrites the deployed contract's `consumeUserResourcePercent` field in the `ContractStore` as soon as the transaction is included in a block, with no staging/queue mechanism: [1](#0-0) 

The only requirement to call it is that the caller is the contract's `originAddress` (the deployer/owner) — an ordinary, unprivileged account, not a committee/witness role: [2](#0-1) 

This percentage is read live, at execution time of every subsequent contract call, by `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio()` to compute how much energy the caller must front versus how much comes from the contract owner's frozen energy: [3](#0-2) 

It is used again later, in `TransactionTrace.pay()`, to split the actual energy bill between the caller and the origin/owner account: [4](#0-3) 

Finally, `ReceiptCapsule.payEnergyBill()` charges the caller TRX out of pocket for any energy beyond their free/frozen energy allotment, and reverts with `BalanceInsufficientException` if the caller can't cover it: [5](#0-4) 

Because there is no delay between when `UpdateSettingContractActuator` changes the percent and when it takes effect on the chain, a contract owner can observe a pending call transaction in the mempool and front-run it by raising `consume_user_resource_percent` toward 100%, shifting the entire energy cost of that call onto the caller instead of the owner's frozen energy — exactly the "no ragequit period" pattern described in the external report, just applied to an on-chain resource-fee-sharing parameter instead of an oracle quote parameter.

### Impact Explanation
An unsuspecting caller who triggers a smart contract expecting the owner to subsidize most of the energy cost (as historically configured) can be forced, via front-running, to pay the full TRX-denominated energy fee for that call, or have their transaction fail with `BalanceInsufficientException` if their balance is insufficient. This is a direct, unauthorized shift of the energy-fee financial burden onto an unprivileged transaction broadcaster without warning, i.e., unexpected loss of TRX funds for the caller triggered purely by the timing of an adversarial parameter change.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account that deployed a smart contract can perform this at will and cheaply (`calcFee()` returns 0): [6](#0-5) 

Since mempool transactions are visible before confirmation, front-running the `UpdateSettingContract` transaction ahead of a targeted caller's `TriggerSmartContract` transaction is straightforward for any contract owner motivated to shift costs onto specific callers (e.g., in adversarial or dispute scenarios), making this readily reachable and repeatable.

### Recommendation
Introduce a delay/ragequit window for `consume_user_resource_percent` changes — e.g., queue the new value and only apply it after N blocks/maintenance cycles — so callers have time to observe and react to the change (similar to how other sensitive dynamic parameters are gated by proposal/maintenance-cycle mechanisms elsewhere in the protocol, as seen in `ProposalService`/`ProposalUtil`).

### Proof of Concept
1. Contract owner deploys a contract with `consume_user_resource_percent = 0` (owner pays all energy) and advertises this to attract callers.
2. A caller broadcasts a `TriggerSmartContract` transaction expecting near-zero TRX cost.
3. Before that transaction is packed into a block, the owner broadcasts and gets included a `UpdateSettingContract` transaction setting `consume_user_resource_percent = 100`, exploiting `UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()`'s immediate-effect write.
4. When the caller's transaction executes afterward, `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio()` and `TransactionTrace.pay()`/`ReceiptCapsule.payEnergyBill()` now attribute 100% of the energy usage to the caller, charging their balance in TRX or causing a `BalanceInsufficientException` revert — a result the caller had no way to anticipate or avoid.
