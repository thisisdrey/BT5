### Title
Contract owner can instantly change `consume_user_resource_percent` / `origin_energy_limit` with no timelock, silently rug-pulling callers who trusted the contract's fee-sharing configuration - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java`, `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator.java`)

### Summary
`UpdateSettingContractActuator` and `UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator` let a deployed contract's `origin_address` owner change, at any time and with immediate effect, the percentage of energy a *caller* must pay for (`consume_user_resource_percent`) and the maximum energy the *contract owner* is willing to subsidize (`origin_energy_limit`). There is no delay, no notice, and no way for a caller who already broadcast a transaction relying on the old configuration to protect themselves.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` directly overwrites `ContractCapsule`'s `ConsumeUserResourcePercent` field for the target contract address as soon as the transaction is applied, with the only validation being that the caller is the contract's `originAddress` and the percent is between 0 and 100: [1](#0-0) 
Similarly, `UpdateEnergyLimitContractActuator.execute()` overwrites `OriginEnergyLimit` unconditionally upon validation success: [2](#0-1) 
Validation for both only checks that the address is a valid account and that it matches the deployed contract's stored `originAddress`, i.e., the person who deployed the smart contract: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

There is no time-lock, no cooldown, and no on-chain warning period before the change takes effect. This is structurally the same bug class as H-02: a role (`manager` in the Karak report, the contract deployer/`originAddress` here) can unilaterally and instantly change a critical operating parameter that other unprivileged parties (vault depositors in the report, contract callers/transaction broadcasters here) rely on when deciding to interact, with no opportunity to exit or adjust beforehand. `consume_user_resource_percent` and `origin_energy_limit` are exactly the parameters that determine how the energy cost of a call is split between the calling account (which pays TRX/frozen energy) and the contract owner (who may subsidize energy up to `origin_energy_limit`), as consumed in the VM fee-charging logic in `VMActuator.java`.

### Impact Explanation
A caller (an unprivileged transaction broadcaster) typically inspects a contract's current `consume_user_resource_percent`/`origin_energy_limit` before interacting with it, expecting the owner to subsidize most of the energy cost. The contract owner can broadcast an `UpdateSettingContract`/`UpdateEnergyLimitContract` transaction that raises `consume_user_resource_percent` to 100 (or drops `origin_energy_limit` to a minimal value) immediately before, or even within the same block as, a victim's call. Because the change takes effect the instant the transaction is applied and there is no time-lock, a caller who signed and broadcast a transaction under the old economic assumptions can be forced to burn far more of their own TRX/energy than they consented to when they built and signed their transaction — an unauthorized extraction of the caller's resources with no warning or recourse, directly analogous to the "rogue vault" scenario in H-02 where trusting users get rug-pulled by an unannounced parameter change.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any contract deployer already possesses the privilege to call these actuators (only an `originAddress` check is required), and the change is a single, cheap, zero-fee (`calcFee()` returns 0) transaction that any deployer can send at will. No special conditions, forks, or governance approval are needed, and the change is fully deterministic and repeatable each time a deployer wants to alter caller economics ("twiceUpdateSettingContract"/"twiceUpdateEnergyLimitContract" tests confirm updates can be applied repeatedly without restriction).

### Recommendation
Introduce a request-and-finalize (time-locked) process for `UpdateSettingContract` and `UpdateEnergyLimitContract`, similar to the recommendation in the source report: require the owner to propose a new `consume_user_resource_percent`/`origin_energy_limit`, hold it for a minimum number of blocks/time before it becomes active, and emit an on-chain event so callers can observe pending changes and avoid interacting with the contract (or adjust their `fee_limit`) before the change takes effect.

### Proof of Concept
1. Deployer publishes a contract with `origin_energy_limit` high and `consume_user_resource_percent` low (e.g., 10), advertising cheap calls.
2. Victim inspects the contract, builds and signs a transaction calling it, expecting to pay only ~10% of energy cost.
3. Before the victim's transaction is included (or in the same block, ordered first), the deployer broadcasts `UpdateSettingContract` setting `consume_user_resource_percent` to 100 via `UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute()` [5](#0-4) .
4. The victim's already-signed transaction is now processed under the new 100% setting, so the victim pays for 100% of the energy consumed instead of the expected 10%, draining more TRX/energy than they agreed to when signing.

```java
    try {
      UpdateSettingContract usContract = any.unpack(UpdateSettingContract.class);
      long newPercent = usContract.getConsumeUserResourcePercent();
      byte[] contractAddress = usContract.getContractAddress().toByteArray();
      ContractCapsule deployedContract = contractStore.get(contractAddress);

      contractStore.put(contractAddress, new ContractCapsule(
          deployedContract.getInstance().toBuilder().setConsumeUserResourcePercent(newPercent)
              .build()));
      RepositoryImpl.removeLruCache(contractAddress);

      ret.setStatus(fee, code.SUCESS);
    } catch (InvalidProtocolBufferException e) {
      logger.debug(e.getMessage(), e);
      ret.setStatus(fee, code.FAILED);
      throw new ContractExeException(e.getMessage());
    }
    return true;
```
