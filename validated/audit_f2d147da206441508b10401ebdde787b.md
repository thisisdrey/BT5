### Title
Stale NAV in `PortfolioVault` allows front-running loan status changes to manipulate share price - (`tare-io__tare-contracts/contracts/PortfolioVault.sol`)

### Summary
The `PortfolioVault` relies on a cached `lastNav` for share-price-sensitive operations like `approveDeposit` and `approveRedemption`. While the protocol has guards against stale NAV based on time and NFT ownership changes, it does not account for material changes in the underlying loan ledger state (e.g., a loan becoming late or defaulting) that occur between NAV updates. This allows malicious actors to front-run a NAV update after a negative loan event to exit the vault at an inflated share price.

### Finding Description
In the Tare protocol, the `PortfolioVault` calculates its Net Asset Value (NAV) by iterating over its loan portfolio and querying an external `NavCalculator`. This process is paginated and triggered manually by a `PORTFOLIO_MANAGER` [1](#0-0) .

The vault's share price is derived from `lastNav`. To prevent the use of stale prices, `_requireFreshNav` checks:
1. If a computation is in progress [2](#0-1) .
2. If the `loansNFT.ownershipNonce` has changed (meaning an NFT was added or removed) [3](#0-2) .
3. If the time since the last update exceeds `maxNavAge` [4](#0-3) .

However, the loan's internal state (status, next due date, etc.) in `Loans.sol` can change without triggering any of these guards. For example, a `Servicer` can call `updateLoanData` to mark a loan as `ChargedOff` or update a `nextDueDate` that indicates a missed payment [5](#0-4) . These changes significantly impact the loan's valuation but do not bump the `ownershipNonce` or invalidate the vault's NAV automatically.

An attacker (or a sophisticated investor) can monitor the `Loans` contract for such state changes and immediately call `requestRedeem` (or `requestDeposit` if the change is positive) to lock in a request that will be processed using the currently cached (but now inaccurate) NAV once the manager calls `approveRedemption`.

### Impact Explanation
Malicious or front-running users can exploit the lag between a loan's financial deterioration and the vault's NAV reflection. This results in:
- **Theft of Value**: Reducers exit at a higher NAV than the actual value of the underlying assets, diluting remaining honest shareholders.
- **Unfair Arbitrage**: Depositors can enter the vault at a discount if they front-run a positive update (e.g., a large unexpected recovery payment).
- **Material Corruption of NAV**: The vault's accounting fails to preserve fair share pricing during periods of loan volatility.

### Likelihood Explanation
The likelihood is high for institutional-grade credit protocols where loan events (defaults, late payments) are public on-chain or easily observable. Since NAV updates are manual and paginated, there is a guaranteed window of opportunity between a `Loans.sol` state change and the finalization of a new `PortfolioVault.updateNav` cycle.

### Recommendation
Implement a mechanism to invalidate the cached NAV when material loan state changes occur. This could include:
1. Adding a `stateNonce` in the `Loans` contract that increments on any ledger or status change.
2. Updating `PortfolioVault._requireFreshNav` to check this `stateNonce` against a cached value, similar to how `ownershipNonce` is handled.
3. Alternatively, allow the `Loans` contract to call `vault.invalidateNav()` when `updateLoanData` or `applyWaterfall` is called for a loan owned by a vault.

### Proof of Concept
1. `PortfolioVault` has a `lastNav` of 1,000,000 USDC based on 10 active loans. `maxNavAge` is 24 hours.
2. 1 hour after the last `updateNav`, a `Servicer` calls `Loans.updateLoanData(loanId, LoanStatus.ChargedOff, ...)` for one of the vault's loans, effectively wiping out 100,000 USDC of value.
3. The `ownershipNonce` does not change because the NFT is still owned by the vault.
4. An attacker sees the `ChargedOff` event and immediately calls `PortfolioVault.requestRedeem`.
5. The `PORTFOLIO_MANAGER` calls `approveRedemption` later that hour. `_requireFreshNav` passes because the timestamp is within `maxNavAge` and the nonce is the same.
6. The attacker's shares are redeemed at the old NAV (1,000,000 USDC valuation) instead of the true NAV (900,000 USDC).
7. The 10,000 USDC loss is borne entirely by the remaining shareholders. [6](#0-5) [5](#0-4)
