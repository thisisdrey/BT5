### Title
Permissionless `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` allows Just-In-Time (JIT) capital to capture a disproportionate, risk-free share of reward top-ups at the expense of long-term depositors - (File: `programs/marginfi/src/instructions/marginfi_group/configure_bank.rs`)

### Summary
The SushiSwap Trident bug describes a bot that mints a large, tightly-ranged liquidity position immediately before a reward-triggering event, claims the reward, then burns the position, repeating this to farm incentives with effectively no market exposure. Marginfi's `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` instruction (permissionless) increases `bank.asset_share_value` proportionally across *all current shareholders* the instant it executes [1](#0-0) . Since the payout is split purely by each depositor's share-of-pool at the moment the instruction lands, an attacker can deposit a large amount of capital immediately before this call (or before an emissions/`super_admin_deposit` top-up) and withdraw immediately after, capturing a share of the reward proportional to their inflated stake with no holding period, no lock-up, and (for a same-mint deposit) essentially zero price risk.

### Finding Description
`lending_pool_emissions_deposit` computes the new `asset_share_value` as `(total_assets + amount) / total_asset_shares`, where `total_asset_shares` is read at call time [2](#0-1) . Every current shareholder — regardless of how long they have held shares — gets the same proportional boost. Deposit and withdraw instructions have no minimum holding period or time-weighting: `lending_account_deposit` and `lending_account_withdraw` simply accrue interest and mint/burn shares immediately [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) . Nothing in these code paths inspects how recently the balance was opened.

This mirrors the JIT liquidity pattern in the report: a large, short-lived stake, opened immediately before a value-injecting event and closed immediately after, capturing reward flow meant for standing participants. The docs even acknowledge the same-bank emissions mechanism is intentionally "pro-rata in real time" [5](#0-4) , which is precisely the condition that allows a front-runner with no time-in-pool to claim a full pro-rata slice.

`super_admin_deposit` has the identical share-value-boosting mechanic (admin-gated, but same math) [6](#0-5) , and is a secondary place the same class of front-run applies if its execution slot/timing is predictable or if the admin transaction is publicly visible/simulatable before landing.

### Impact Explanation
An attacker who can predict or front-run an emissions-deposit (or admin) top-up transaction can:
1. Deposit a large amount into the target bank just before the top-up lands (or in the same transaction bundle, since deposit has no cooldown).
2. Let the top-up execute, immediately raising `asset_share_value` for their inflated share balance.
3. Withdraw immediately afterward.

This siphons value intended for genuine, longer-term depositors to a purely opportunistic, momentary depositor, with only negligible interest-rate exposure (no price/IL risk since deposit and reward are the same underlying mint). Repeated indefinitely, this could materially degrade the yield genuine depositors receive from emissions campaigns — an unauthorized/unfair value transfer within the accounting layer, though it does not create insolvency or bad debt (funds are conserved; only the *split* of a reward is skewed).

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is constrained by:
- `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` and `super_admin_deposit` calls are not typically dispatched with a hidden/atomic simultaneity — an attacker would need to observe the top-up transaction in the mempool/validator queue and land a deposit beforehand, which is plausible on Solana but requires active monitoring/bots.
- The economic gain is bounded by pool size and top-up size; larger pools dilute the attacker's captured share, and gas/opportunity cost of capital must be weighed against the reward captured.
- No protocol-level defense (lock-up period, time-weighted rewards, deposit cooldown) currently exists to prevent this in the reviewed code paths.

### Recommendation
- Introduce a minimum holding period or time-weighted share accounting (e.g., snapshotting shares over a window rather than instantaneously) before applying `lending_pool_emissions_deposit`/`super_admin_deposit` boosts.
- Alternatively, restrict/rate-limit permissionless emissions top-ups to admin-controlled or randomized timing, and/or require deposits to be locked for a minimum duration before they qualify for a same-block/same-slot reward top-up.
- Consider requiring `lending_pool_emissions_deposit` calls to be bundled atomically with a check that excludes shares deposited within the same instruction/transaction from the reward split.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual sequence (based on `programs/marginfi/tests/misc/emissions_deposit.rs`, e.g. `emissions_same_bank_deposit_updates_asset_share_value`):
1. Attacker observes a pending `lending_pool_emissions_deposit(amount)` transaction targeting Bank X (permissionless, so its parameters/timing may be predictable, e.g. scheduled weekly top-ups per `guides/USER/EMISSIONS.md`).
2. Attacker calls `lending_account_deposit` on Bank X with a large `amount`, immediately before the emissions-deposit transaction lands, increasing their `asset_shares` and the bank's `total_asset_shares`.
3. The emissions-deposit transaction executes: `bank.asset_share_value` is recalculated as `(total_assets + emissions_amount) / total_asset_shares` [7](#0-6) , boosting every depositor's share value including the attacker's freshly-minted large stake.
4. Attacker immediately calls `lending_account_withdraw` (or `withdraw_all`) to realize the gain, exiting with a disproportionate cut of the reward relative to time held in the pool.

I could not find any code enforcing a minimum deposit-holding duration or excluding newly-minted shares from a same-block reward distribution, which is what would be needed to fully rule out this analog; a Devin session with full repo/test access could confirm whether any off-chain scheduling/anti-bot safeguards exist outside the on-chain program.

```rust
/// Permissionlessly deposit same-mint emissions directly into the bank liquidity vault,
/// increasing depositor value through asset share value.
pub fn lending_pool_emissions_deposit(
    ctx: Context<LendingPoolEmissionsDeposit>,
    amount: u64,
) -> MarginfiResult {
    if amount == 0 {
        return Ok(());
    }

    let clock = Clock::get()?;
    let mut bank = ctx.accounts.bank.load_mut()?;
    let group = ctx.accounts.group.load()?;

    utils::validate_bank_state(&bank, utils::InstructionKind::FailsIfPausedOrReduceState)?;

    // Reject mints with non-zero transfer fees or active transfer hooks.
    let mint_ai = ctx.accounts.mint.to_account_info();
    check!(
        !utils::nonzero_fee(mint_ai.clone(), clock.epoch)?,
        MarginfiError::InvalidTransfer
    );
    check!(
        !utils::has_transfer_hook(mint_ai)?,
        MarginfiError::InvalidTransfer
    );

    let total_asset_shares = I80F48::from(bank.total_asset_shares);
    check!(
        total_asset_shares > I80F48::ZERO,
        MarginfiError::EmissionsUpdateError
    );

    bank.accrue_interest(
        clock.unix_timestamp,
        &group,
        #[cfg(not(feature = "client"))]
        ctx.accounts.bank.key(),
    )?;

    transfer_checked(
        CpiContext::new(
            ctx.accounts.token_program.key(),
            TransferChecked {
                from: ctx.accounts.emissions_funding_account.to_account_info(),
                to: ctx.accounts.liquidity_vault.to_account_info(),
                authority: ctx.accounts.depositor.to_account_info(),
                mint: ctx.accounts.mint.to_account_info(),
            },
        ),
        amount,
        ctx.accounts.mint.decimals,
    )?;

    let total_assets = bank.get_asset_amount(total_asset_shares)?;
    let updated_total_assets = total_assets
        .checked_add(I80F48::from_num(amount))
        .ok_or_else(math_error!())?;

    bank.asset_share_value = updated_total_assets
        .checked_div(total_asset_shares)
        .ok_or_else(math_error!())?
        .into();
```

```markdown
## Summary

Some banks, including banks from other venues, might offer a token incentive to depositors, or even
to borrowers. We call a set of incentives over time a "Campaign".

For example, a Campaign might distribute 7 tokens of A to lenders per week (one per day). Each
lender's share is determined on a pro-rata basis in real time. If there are two lenders, each
depositing the same amount, then each will be 3.5 tokens per week.

Now let's say there are two users, the first one has \$1 in deposits. User 2 deposits \$1 on
Thursday, and \$5 more on Saturday. This means User 1 and 2 both get 0.5 tokens/day on Thursday and
Friday. On Saturday and beyond, User 1 gets $1/(1+6)= 0.143$ tokens, and User 2 gets $6/(1+6)=0.857$
tokens/day.
```
