### Title
Liquidation accepts any asset as the repaid-debt asset without verifying it is `debt`-enabled - (File: `mainnet/contracts/market/v0-4-market.clar`)

### Summary
The report's bug class is that `LlamaCore._castApproval`/`_castDisapproval` verify a policyholder *has some role* but never verify that role is the specific `approvalRole` the action requires, letting an unrelated role's weight count toward approval. In Zest's `liquidate`, the analogous defect is that the function derives `debt-aid`/`coll-aid` purely from whatever `<ft-trait>` contracts the liquidator passes in and processes them as debt/collateral without asserting that the resolved asset actually carries the `debt: true` / `collateral: true` role flag from the asset registry — the same "any role accepted, not just the required one" pattern.

### Finding Description
In `borrow`, the market explicitly checks the specific required role before proceeding: [1](#0-0) 
```
(asserts! (get debt asset) ERR-BORROW-DISABLED)
```

`liquidate`, however, resolves `coll-asset`/`debt-asset` from caller-supplied trait references and immediately extracts `coll-aid`/`debt-aid` with no equivalent role assertion: [2](#0-1) 

It then proceeds straight into `process-debt-asset`/`process-collateral-asset` and the repay/collateral-remove flow using those unchecked IDs: [3](#0-2) [4](#0-3) 

Compounding this, `get-position` in `market-vault.clar` computes the returned `debt` list using `MAX-U128` as the enabled-mask argument (rather than the caller-passed `enabled-mask` used for collateral), meaning debt entries are never filtered by the registry's currently-enabled-debt bitmap: [5](#0-4) 

So even if the DAO disables an asset for `debt` via `assets.disable`, a borrower's stale debt entry for that asset remains fully liquidatable, and `liquidate` never re-validates the role at the point of use — mirroring the report's core flaw of "checks that *a* role/bit is set, not that it is the specific required one."

### Impact Explanation
This does not directly enable theft beyond the protocol's designed liquidation mechanics (repay debt, receive discounted collateral) since `debt-to-repay`/`coll-final` amounts are still bounded by `process-debt-asset`/`scale-debt-for-liquidation` against the borrower's actual scaled debt and collateral balance — so a liquidator cannot manufacture debt/collateral for an asset the borrower doesn't actually hold. The primary risk is that liquidations can proceed against `debt`-disabled assets that the DAO intended to freeze (e.g., during an incident response), undermining the `assets.disable` "borrow-disabled" safety control's completeness — a subset of the "deliberate safety design decision" exclusion for disabled-collateral liquidation, but here affecting the *debt* side, which is not explicitly excluded.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires a borrower who already holds debt in an asset later disabled by the DAO for debt purposes, and a liquidator invoking `liquidate` naming that debt asset — a narrow but plausible operational sequence, not requiring any privileged access beyond ordinary liquidator/borrower interaction.

### Recommendation
In `liquidate`, add explicit role assertions mirroring `borrow`'s check — e.g. `(asserts! (get debt debt-asset) ERR-...)` is not appropriate (debt could be legitimately disabled but still liquidatable by design), but at minimum align `get-position`'s debt-mask filtering with the same enabled-bitmap semantics used for collateral, or document/confirm this divergence as intentional (since disabled-collateral liquidation is explicitly called out as intended design, the debt side may be similarly intentional and would need protocol-team confirmation).

### Proof of Concept
1. DAO calls `assets.disable(debt-asset, false)` to disable an asset for new debt origination.
2. A borrower who already had debt in that asset (borrowed prior to disable) remains undercollateralized.
3. A liquidator calls `liquidate` passing that asset as `debt-ft`; `get-asset`, `get-position` (using `MAX-U128` for debt lookup), and `process-debt-asset` all proceed without any role check against the current `debt` flag, allowing the liquidation to execute exactly as if the asset were still debt-enabled.
