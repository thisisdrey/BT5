### Title
Front-runnable dust repayment can force `lending_account_repay` and `lending_account_liquidate` to revert via `OperationRepayOnly` - ([File: programs/marginfi/src/state/marginfi_account.rs])

### Summary
The `RepayOnly` code path inside `increase_balance_internal` reverts whenever the amount being repaid exceeds the target position's outstanding liability by more than a small dust tolerance. Because `lending_account_repay` (non-`repay_all` mode) and `lending_account_liquidate` both compute a fixed repayment amount ahead of time and feed it into this same strict path, an unprivileged attacker can front-run either instruction with a tiny partial repayment that shrinks the victim's liability just enough to make the pre-computed repay amount "overshoot," causing the legitimate repay or liquidation transaction to revert. This mirrors the Revert Lend `V3Vault` bug class (M-16) where repay/liquidate calls fail if the specified share amount exceeds outstanding debt shares.

### Finding Description
`BankAccountWrapper::repay` calls `increase_balance_internal` with `BalanceIncreaseType::RepayOnly`: [1](#0-0) 

Inside `increase_balance_internal`, the amount to repay is split into `liability_amount_decrease` (capped at the current liability) and any excess `asset_amount_increase`. For `RepayOnly`, if that excess is larger than `ZERO_AMOUNT_THRESHOLD`, the call fails with `MarginfiError::OperationRepayOnly` instead of clamping the repayment and returning the surplus: [2](#0-1) 

This strict-overshoot check is exercised by two unprivileged, user-reachable instructions with a fixed, pre-computed repay amount:

1. `lending_account_repay` (when `repay_all` is `false`) passes the caller-specified `amount` directly into `bank_account.repay(...)`: [3](#0-2) 
(There is a `repay_all` bypass in `repay_all()` that avoids this exact-amount problem for full closes, but any exact-amount, non-`repay_all` repay is subject to this failure mode.)

2. `lending_account_liquidate` computes `liab_amount_final` from the liquidator-supplied `asset_amount` and current oracle prices at the start of the instruction, then applies it to the liquidatee's liability via `repay`: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

There is no dynamic re-check or capping of `liab_amount_final` against the liquidatee's liability at the moment of the `repay` call — it is computed once, up front, from the pre-transaction state (`check_pre_liquidation_condition_and_get_account_health` and the price fetches happen before this point). If any other transaction (including one submitted by the liquidatee themselves, or a bystander repaying on their behalf) partially repays the liquidatee's liability in between/before, the `liab_amount_final` value used inside `liquidate` can now exceed the shrunken outstanding liability by more than `ZERO_AMOUNT_THRESHOLD`, causing the entire liquidation transaction to abort with `OperationRepayOnly`.

### Impact Explanation
An unprivileged actor (the borrower themselves, or any third party) can repeatedly submit minuscule repayments (e.g., 1 native unit) against a target position immediately before:
- A legitimate exact-amount `lending_account_repay` transaction from the borrower attempting to reduce debt and improve health — this can be forced to fail exactly when the position is near the liquidation threshold, preventing the borrower from averting liquidation.
- A liquidator's `lending_account_liquidate` transaction — this can be forced to fail, delaying/blocking timely liquidation of an unhealthy account. Repeated griefing can keep an insolvent account unliquidated for extended periods, increasing the risk of bad debt for the protocol (since asset/liability prices continue to move against the position while liquidation is blocked).

This is a denial-of-service on core accounting paths (repay/liquidate) that can be weaponized to protect an unhealthy position from timely liquidation or block a borrower's own risk-reducing repayment, with downstream insolvency/bad-debt risk to the bank.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires only: (1) knowledge of a pending repay/liquidate transaction (observable via mempool/MEV or by monitoring on-chain state), and (2) the ability to submit a transaction that lands before the victim's transaction (front-running). No privileged role is needed — any account holder can call `lending_account_repay` against any position's liability bank as long as they hold the liability asset, since repay does not require ownership of the account being repaid to reduce its liability shares (it repays a specified marginfi account's balance). This mirrors the exact MEV precondition the original report's judge (`ronnyx2017`) noted for the analogous Revert Lend finding, i.e., moderate-to-real likelihood in adversarial/competitive liquidation environments, and higher after such an issue class becomes publicly known as a known DoS technique.

### Recommendation
- For `lending_account_repay`, when not using `repay_all`, clamp the repay amount to the account's outstanding liability instead of reverting on overshoot, and refund/short-transfer the excess token amount back to the payer (as recommended in the referenced report).
- For `lending_account_liquidate`, recompute or cap `liab_amount_final` (and the corresponding `insurance_fund_fee`/`liab_amount_liquidator`) against the liquidatee's live liability balance immediately before calling `repay`, so a partial pre-repayment cannot force the whole liquidation to revert; alternatively, allow the liquidatee-side repay call to clamp to the current liability and adjust the liquidator's compensation/asset seizure proportionally.

### Proof of Concept
Conceptual reproduction, consistent with the referenced report's PoC pattern, mapped onto marginfi instructions:
1. User A borrows against a bank and has an outstanding liability of `X` liability shares/amount.
2. Attacker (or bystander) calls `lending_account_repay(bank, amount=1, repay_all=false)` targeting User A's position, reducing the liability by a dust amount `d` (where `d > ZERO_AMOUNT_THRESHOLD`).
3. User A (or a liquidator) had already prepared/submitted a `lending_account_repay(bank, amount=X, repay_all=false)` (or `lending_account_liquidate` with `asset_amount` sized to fully repay `X`).
4. Because the liability is now `X - d`, `increase_balance_internal` computes `asset_amount_increase = amount - (X - d) = d > ZERO_AMOUNT_THRESHOLD`, triggering the `RepayOnly` check and returning `MarginfiError::OperationRepayOnly`, reverting the victim's transaction.
5. The attacker can repeat this before every retried victim transaction, indefinitely blocking the borrower's repayment or the liquidator's liquidation of the unhealthy account. [6](#0-5)
