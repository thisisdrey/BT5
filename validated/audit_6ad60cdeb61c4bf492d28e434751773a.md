**Reasoning:** The premise doesn't hold given how resharding actually treats these columns. `BufferedReceiptIndices` and `BufferedReceipt` are *not* split per-shard-id during a shard split — they're copied atomically to exactly one child (the "left" child) as whole trie columns, never partially: [1](#0-0) 

This means the indices map (`BufferedReceiptIndices.shard_buffers`, a `BTreeMap<ShardId, TrieQueueIndices>`) and every corresponding `BufferedReceipt{index, receiving_shard}` row travel together to the left child, or neither travels to the right child — there is no possibility of "indices reset while rows persist" because both live under the same copy-or-not decision: [2](#0-1) [3](#0-2) 

The right child's lack of buffered receipts is explicitly enforced with an assertion tying congestion-info accounting to the actual absence of buffered rows: [4](#0-3) 

`get_buffered_receipt_indices`'s default-on-missing behavior is therefore consistent with the physical absence of rows in the right child, not a mismatch: [5](#0-4) 

Additionally, child shard ids from a split are freshly allocated (not reused from any prior shard), per the dynamic-resharding pipeline (`derive_v3` "allocating two new shard ids"), so there is no plausible shard-id collision between an old buffered-receipt row and a newly created shard's fresh, empty index namespace. The attacker (an unprivileged contract flooding cross-shard calls to saturate one shard's outgoing buffer) has no way to force indices and rows to diverge, since both are governed by the same atomic column-copy decision in `retain_split_shard`/`boundary_account_to_intervals`, and forwarding logic (`forward_from_buffer_to_shard`) correctly re-resolves receiver shard via the *current* shard layout when draining parent-shard buffers, preventing double-delivery or loss.
