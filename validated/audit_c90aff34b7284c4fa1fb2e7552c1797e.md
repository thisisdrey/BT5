### Title
`Operator.execute` retains the victim's identity while calling arbitrary targets — any contract reached during a batch can impersonate the victim against all SynthContext contracts ("reverse tabnabbing" via `window.opener`-style persistent sender context) - ([File: contracts/Operator.sol])

### Summary
The Passbolt tabnabbing bug exists because a page opened via `window.open` retains a live `window.opener` handle to the trusted origin, letting the malicious page act "as" the opener. `Operator.execute` is the on-chain equivalent: it stores the initiating EOA in the transient `MSG_SENDER` slot for the *entire duration of the batch* (`setMsgSender` modifier), then performs arbitrary `functionCallWithValue` to attacker-reachable targets. While any sub-call is executing, every `SynthContext`-derived contract (`Pool`, `DepositToken`, `DebtToken`, `SyntheticToken`, `ProxyOFT`, `VesperGateway`, `NativeTokenGateway`, `SmartFarmingManager`, `RecurringAirdrop`) resolves `_msgSender()` to the stored victim — not to the contract actually calling. A malicious contract invoked anywhere in the victim's batch is therefore a "new tab with an opener handle": it can call back into the protocol and act as the victim.

### Finding Description
`Operator.execute` writes `MSG_SENDER_STORAGE = msg.sender`, iterates `calls_`, and only clears the slot after all calls finish (`contracts/Operator.sol:20-55`). `SynthContext._msgSender()` returns `operator.getActualMsgSender()` whenever `msg.sender == operator` (`contracts/utils/SynthContext.sol:14-24`). During a sub-call `target.functionCallWithValue(...)`, the target contract (or anything it calls) can invoke `DepositToken.withdraw`, `DepositToken.transfer`, `Pool.swap`, `DebtToken` mint paths, `SmartFarmingManager.flashRepay`, `RecurringAirdrop.claim`, etc., and every `_msgSender()`/modifier check (`onlyIfCanMint`, `onlyIfSmartFarmingManager` gates aside) sees the victim. `nonReentrant` only protects `Operator` itself — it does not block reentry into `Pool`/`DepositToken` because `ReentrancyGuardTransient` uses per-contract slots, and the reentry is a fresh call while no Pool-level guard is held. The identity invariant (caller == account acted upon) is broken for the full batch window.

### Impact Explanation
The malicious callee can drain the victim's unlocked collateral (`DepositToken.withdraw`), steal share tokens (`transfer` of unlocked balances), open/mint debt and swap proceeds away, or claim airdrops — direct theft of user funds, matching the report's impact of acting "on the user behalf" through the trusted opener reference.

### Likelihood Explanation
Requires the victim's `execute` batch to reach an attacker-controlled contract (e.g., a malicious swap router/token embedded in a batched zap), the same UI:R precondition as the original advisory. All mechanics are on-chain and unprivileged.

### Recommendation
Clear `MSG_SENDER` around each untrusted sub-call (tstore zero before `functionCallWithValue`, restore after), or restrict `Call.target` to a registry-allowlisted set of Metronome contracts.

### Proof of Concept
Fork test: victim calls `Operator.execute([Call(evil,0,...)])`; inside `evil.fallback`, call `DepositToken.withdraw(victimBalance)` — succeeds with `_msgSender() == victim` and funds are sent to attacker-chosen recipient.
