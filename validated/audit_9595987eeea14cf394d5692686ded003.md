### Title
Attached NEAR deposit is permanently absorbed by the eth-implicit wallet contract when an intermediate cross-contract call fails before the transfer is executed - (File: runtime/near-wallet-contract/implementation/wallet-contract/src/lib.rs)

### Summary
The near-wallet-contract (the eth-implicit account's Ethereum-emulation contract) accepts an attached NEAR deposit on `rlp_execute` and merges it into the value of the outgoing action (transfer/`ft_transfer`) that will be sent once a preliminary lookup succeeds. If that preliminary lookup (`address_registrar.lookup` or `storage_balance_of`) fails, the contract returns a failure response without ever issuing a refund of the caller's deposit, even though the tracked `CallerDeposit` refund mechanism exists and is wired only into the *final* callback of the promise chain. The result is that a caller's (relayer's) attached deposit is credited to the account balance (as happens automatically for any payable receipt in NEAR) but never sent onward and never refunded — a silent, permanent loss of the caller's funds analogous to the Tadle bug where a counterparty's deposit becomes non-refundably captured before the corresponding obligation is fulfilled.

### Finding Description
`rlp_execute` is `#[payable]`; any deposit attached by the calling relayer is credited to the wallet contract's own account balance as soon as the receipt is processed by the runtime, independent of what the contract logic later does [1](#0-0) .

The deposit is tracked as a `CallerDeposit` purely for later refund bookkeeping, and only for external (non-self) callers [2](#0-1) . The same deposit amount is also folded directly into the value of the outgoing `Transfer`/`FunctionCall` action via `try_into_near_action(additional_value)` [3](#0-2) .

For two transaction kinds — `EOABaseTokenTransfer` targeting another eth-implicit account, and `ERC20Transfer` — the contract first issues a *preliminary* cross-contract call (`address_registrar.lookup` or `token.storage_balance_of`) before it ever spawns the promise that actually moves value [4](#0-3) .

The callbacks for these preliminary calls (`address_check_callback` and `nep_141_storage_balance_callback`) each match on `PromiseResult::Failed` and return an `ExecuteResponse{success:false,...}` immediately — without touching `caller_deposit` at all: [5](#0-4) [6](#0-5) 

The *only* place that refunds `caller_deposit` on failure is `rlp_execute_callback`, which is invoked solely after the final value-moving promise (the actual `Transfer`/`ft_transfer`), i.e., only after the preliminary lookup has already succeeded: [7](#0-6) 

Consequently, if the *first* promise in the chain (registrar lookup or `storage_balance_of`) fails for any reason (contract not deployed, insufficient gas allotted — only `REGISTRAR_LOOKUP_GAS`/`NEP_141_STORAGE_BALANCE_OF_GAS` = 5 TGas — a bug in the counterpart contract, or transient failure), the contract:
1. never executes the value-transferring action (so the intended recipient gets nothing), and
2. never refunds the caller's deposit (so the relayer/caller gets nothing back),
while the deposit itself was already unconditionally credited to the wallet contract's (i.e., the eth-implicit account owner's) balance the moment `rlp_execute` was processed.

This is structurally the same bug class as the Tadle report: value is credited/settled to one party immediately upon a triggering action, while the counterparty's "settlement obligation" (in Tadle: seller delivering tokens; here: the contract completing the transfer or refunding on failure) can be skipped through a code path that the fund-holder does not control, permanently depriving the payer of both the good and their money.

### Impact Explanation
Any relayer submitting an `rlp_execute` transaction with a non-zero deposit for an `EOABaseTokenTransfer` to another eth-implicit account, or an `ERC20Transfer` to an unregistered NEP-141 receiver, can have their attached NEAR permanently absorbed into the wallet-contract account's balance with no recourse, if the preliminary lookup call fails. This is a genuine, unprivileged-reachable loss/freezing of funds for the caller — the eth-implicit account (and its owner) receives a windfall it did not pay gas or NEAR for, while the relayer/caller who funded the transaction gets neither the intended transfer nor a refund.

### Likelihood Explanation
This does not require a malicious actor to trigger — it can occur from ordinary failure modes: the hardcoded 5 TGas budgeted for `address_registrar.lookup`/`storage_balance_of` can be exhausted, the registrar or token contract account could be temporarily unavailable, or the callback could simply receive an unexpected response format (which is also handled by the same non-refunding branch, e.g. the `Err(_)` deserialization branches at lines 149-158 and 211-220 of `lib.rs`, though those specifically are noted in comments as intentional non-refund cases). The `PromiseResult::Failed` branches specifically have no such justification and simply omit the refund that exists everywhere else in the file, making this a plausible, moderate-likelihood defect for any relayer who is unlucky enough to hit an intermediate-call failure.

### Recommendation
In `address_check_callback` and `nep_141_storage_balance_callback`, on `PromiseResult::Failed`, issue the same caller-deposit refund logic used in `rlp_execute_callback` (create a `promise_batch_create` to `caller_deposit.account_id` and transfer `caller_deposit.yocto_near`) before returning the failure `ExecuteResponse`. Consider factoring the refund-on-failure logic into a shared helper to prevent this class of omission from recurring as new intermediate-callback paths are added.

### Proof of Concept
Not independently executable from the indexed code alone (no local reproduction environment available), but the flaw is demonstrable by code inspection:
1. A relayer calls `rlp_execute` with a non-zero attached deposit for a transaction parsed as `TransactionKind::EthEmulation(EthEmulationKind::EOABaseTokenTransfer { address_check: Some(address), .. })` (target is another eth-implicit account) — see dispatch at [8](#0-7) .
2. NEAR runtime credits the deposit to the wallet contract's balance immediately as part of receipt processing (standard payable-method semantics); `CallerDeposit` is recorded for refund purposes.
3. The chained promise to `address_registrar.lookup(...)` fails (e.g., registrar account missing, out of gas, or any contract-side error).
4. `address_check_callback` executes the `PromiseResult::Failed` arm at [5](#0-4) , returning `ExecuteResponse{success:false,...}` — no `Transfer` action is ever spawned and `caller_deposit` is never used to create a refund promise.
5. The deposit remains permanently in the wallet contract's account balance; the relayer receives neither the intended transfer execution nor their deposit back.

**Uncertainty note:** I was unable to fully trace whether some outer layer (e.g., a relayer-facing RPC wrapper or the address-registrar's own contract) independently guarantees availability/success of the lookup call, which would reduce practical likelihood. This would require a background Devin session with full repository/test access (including `runtime/near-wallet-contract/implementation/wallet-contract/src/internal.rs` and `address-registrar/src/lib.rs` in full) to confirm there is no compensating safeguard elsewhere.
