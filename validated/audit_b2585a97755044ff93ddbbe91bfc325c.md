### Title
Bad debt is socialized to depositors before uncollected insurance fees are swept into the insurance vault - ([File: programs/marginfi/src/instructions/marginfi_group/handle_bankruptcy.rs])

### Summary
`lending_pool_handle_bankruptcy` computes the insurance fund available to cover bad debt strictly from the physical `insurance_vault.amount` [1](#0-0) , ignoring `bank.collected_insurance_fees_outstanding` — fee amounts that have already accrued economically to the insurance fund but still physically sit in the `liquidity_vault` until `lending_pool_collect_bank_fees` is run [2](#0-1) . If bankruptcy is executed before fees are swept, the true, already-earned insurance backing is treated as unavailable, and any shortfall is socialized to depositors via `bank.socialize_loss()` instead [3](#0-2) . This mirrors the Ajna finding: funds that economically belong to the "reserve"/insurance pool but haven't yet been formally moved/claimed are wrongly excluded from bad-debt coverage, causing depositors to eat losses ahead of their proper place in the loss-absorption order.

### Finding Description
The protocol's own documentation acknowledges this exact ordering dependency: "Run `collect_bank_fees` before beginning the next step so the insurance fund is fully capitalized" [4](#0-3) . This confirms that `collected_insurance_fees_outstanding` is understood to be part of the insurance fund's true capacity, yet the `handle_bankruptcy` code does not enforce or perform this collection itself — it relies purely on `insurance_vault.amount` at call time [5](#0-4) .

Critically, `lending_pool_handle_bankruptcy` is **permissionless** for any bank with `PERMISSIONLESS_BAD_DEBT_SETTLEMENT_FLAG` set [6](#0-5) . Any unprivileged user can call it the moment an account becomes bankrupt, without first calling `collect_bank_fees` (also permissionless, but a separate transaction) [7](#0-6) . Doing so locks in a socialized loss computed against an artificially depleted insurance fund, even though `collected_insurance_fees_outstanding` tokens sitting in the `liquidity_vault` are economically already earmarked for insurance coverage.

### Impact Explanation
The impact is a real, on-chain, irreversible transfer of loss from the insurance fund/protocol fee mechanism onto ordinary depositors. `handle_bankruptcy` permanently zeroes and disables the bankrupt account [8](#0-7) , and `socialize_loss` reduces `asset_share_value` for all remaining depositors in the bank, which is also irreversible — confirmed by the fuzz-invariant comments noting `asset_share_value`/`collected_insurance_fees_outstanding` are only allowed to decrease specifically via a successful bankruptcy call [9](#0-8) . Once bad debt is settled and the account is closed, the accrued fee amount later gets swept to the insurance vault as normal (for future use), but it can never retroactively be applied to the loss that was already socialized to depositors in this event. This is a concrete instance of "insolvency socialized to depositors ahead of an available (if not-yet-collected) protocol reserve," directly analogous to Ajna's M-12.

### Likelihood Explanation
Likelihood is low-to-moderate: it requires (1) a bank with `PERMISSIONLESS_BAD_DEBT_SETTLEMENT_FLAG` enabled, (2) a bankrupt account with outstanding uncollected insurance fees at the time of the call, and (3) `collect_bank_fees` not having been run beforehand — either by omission in normal operational flow or by a user deliberately front-running the sweep to force excess socialization. Since bankruptcy itself is described in the docs as "rare" and never yet executed on the main pool [10](#0-9) , real-world occurrence is uncommon, but the ordering is not enforced at the protocol level, so it is trivially triggerable whenever the precondition (bad debt + permissionless flag + uncollected fees) coincides.

### Recommendation
Before computing `available_insurance_fund` in `lending_pool_handle_bankruptcy`, either (a) require/CPI a `collect_bank_fees` sweep as part of the same instruction so `collected_insurance_fees_outstanding` is moved into `insurance_vault` first, or (b) include `min(collected_insurance_fees_outstanding, available_liquidity_vault_balance)` in the `available_insurance_fund` computation directly, mirroring the Ajna recommendation to not treat not-yet-claimed/collected reserve funds as unavailable liabilities when computing bad-debt coverage.

### Proof of Concept
1. Bank `B` has `PERMISSIONLESS_BAD_DEBT_SETTLEMENT_FLAG` set.
2. Interest accrues on `B`, increasing `collected_insurance_fees_outstanding` to a non-trivial amount `F`, which still sits physically in `liquidity_vault` (not yet in `insurance_vault`).
3. A borrower's collateral crashes; after liquidation their account has bad debt `D` with `D > insurance_vault.amount` but `D <= insurance_vault.amount + F`.
4. An unprivileged user calls `lending_pool_handle_bankruptcy` directly (without calling `lending_pool_collect_bank_fees` first).
5. `available_insurance_fund = insurance_vault.amount` (excluding `F`) [1](#0-0) , so `socialized_loss = D - insurance_vault.amount > 0` is charged to all remaining depositors via `bank.socialize_loss()` [11](#0-10) , even though `F` (already-accrued insurance-earmarked funds) could have covered the remaining `D - insurance_vault.amount` if `collect_bank_fees` had been run moments earlier.
6. The loss to depositors is now locked in; a subsequent `collect_bank_fees` call simply moves `F` into `insurance_vault` for future (different) bad debt, not this already-finalized shortfall.

**File:** guides/RISK_AND_LIQUIDATORS/BANKRUPTCY.md (L34-46)
```markdown
Run `collect_bank_fees` before beginning the next step so the insurance fund is fully capitalized.

Next, the group administrator runs `handle_bankruptcy` on the user. For banks where
`PERMISSIONLESS_BAD_DEBT_SETTLEMENT_FLAG` is enabled, anyone can do this. This will perform the
following logic:

* If bank's insurance fund > liabilities, then the insurance fund is used to repay the user's liability.
* If the bank's insurance fund is not sufficient, the remainder will be covered by taking liquidity
  out of the bank, reducing the asset share value. This socializes the loss to all remaining
  depositors.
* If the bank's insurance fund and liquidity are not sufficient (super-bankruptcy), the bank is
  killed. The asset share value is set to zero, wiping out all holdings for all other depositors.
  This state is irrecoverable, and the bank is permanently disabled.
```
