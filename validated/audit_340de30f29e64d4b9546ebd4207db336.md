### Title
Contract owner can front-run `TriggerSmartContract` calls via `UpdateSettingContract` to shift energy fee burden onto callers - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/UpdateSettingContractActuator.java)

### Summary
An unprivileged smart-contract deployer can front-run any pending call to their own contract by broadcasting an `UpdateSettingContract` transaction that raises `consume_user_resource_percent` to 100 right before the victim's `TriggerSmartContract` transaction executes, shifting energy cost from the contract owner onto the unaware caller — the same "referrer front-runs fee" pattern from the external report, applied to java-tron's energy-fee split instead of a bond-purchase referrer fee.

### Finding Description
`UpdateSettingContract` lets a contract's `origin_address` owner set `consume_user_resource_percent`, the percentage of energy consumption charged to the calling account versus the contract owner. `UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute` writes this value directly into the `ContractCapsule` with no delay, cooldown, or cycle boundary: [1](#0-0) 
The `twiceUpdateSettingContract` test confirms the change is effective immediately for the very next transaction that touches the contract: [2](#0-1) 

When a user later sends `TriggerSmartContract`, the current (post-front-run) `consumeUserResourcePercent` is read live from storage and used to compute how much of the total energy is billed to the caller versus the contract owner: [3](#0-2) 
The actual billing split at execution/settlement time again re-reads the same live value: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

Because a caller signs and broadcasts `TriggerSmartContract` without any way to pin the percentage they are agreeing to, a malicious contract owner can watch the mempool, see an incoming call to their contract, and submit `UpdateSettingContract` (percent → 100) with higher priority/fee so it lands in an earlier position in the same or a preceding block. The victim's call then executes with 100% of energy usage billed to the caller instead of the percentage they expected when computing their `fee_limit`, potentially exhausting the caller's frozen energy/bandwidth or draining their TRX balance for the energy fee, or even causing the call to fail with insufficient balance/energy if `fee_limit` was sized for the previous, lower percentage.

### Impact Explanation
This directly causes unbacked/unexpected loss of funds for a legitimate transaction sender: TRX (used to pay energy fee) that the caller did not expect to spend is drawn from their balance, or their transaction fails after consuming resources, purely because a third party (the contract owner) unilaterally and immediately altered a fee-split parameter that the caller relied on when constructing their transaction. This matches the "Medium" impact class from the source report — unexpected fee extraction from an unaware transaction sender by a party who controls a mutable percentage.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any contract owner can perform this at will and at negligible cost (`calcFee()` for `UpdateSettingContract` — verify sender need only be the contract's `origin_address`, which is a normal unprivileged account). Any caller who queries the contract's current percent before signing (e.g. via `getContract`/`GetSmartContractRequest`) and prices their `fee_limit` accordingly is vulnerable to this front-run, since java-tron provides no mechanism (delay, cycle-boundary application, or immutability) analogous to what other subsystems like `UpdateBrokerageContract` provide (`UpdateBrokerageContract` values are staged and only take effect at the next maintenance cycle, per `MaintenanceManager.doMaintenance`) — `UpdateSettingContract` has no such protection.

### Recommendation
Introduce a delay for `consume_user_resource_percent` changes analogous to the brokerage mechanism already used elsewhere in java-tron (e.g., stage the new percent and only apply it after N blocks or at the next maintenance cycle), or require the caller-visible percent to be pinned at transaction-broadcast time (e.g., include an expected-percent field in `TriggerSmartContract` that the actuator validates against the live value and rejects on mismatch).

### Proof of Concept
1. Contract owner deploys a contract with `consume_user_resource_percent = 0` (owner pays 100% of energy) and advertises this to attract callers.
2. A caller queries the contract, sees percent = 0, and signs `TriggerSmartContract` with a `fee_limit` sized only for bandwidth/minor caller-side costs.
3. Contract owner detects the pending call in the mempool and submits `UpdateSettingContract` with `consume_user_resource_percent = 100` and a higher energy/bandwidth priority so it is included first (see `UpdateSettingContractActuator.execute`, [1](#0-0) ).
4. The victim's `TriggerSmartContract` is then processed with `consumeUserResourcePercent = 100`; `VMActuator.getTotalEnergyLimitWithFixRatio` allocates 0 energy from the creator and the caller must cover 100% of energy usage ( [6](#0-5) ), and `TransactionTrace.pay`/`ReceiptCapsule.payEnergyBill` bill the caller for the full amount, either draining unexpected TRX from the caller's balance or causing an out-of-resource failure that still consumes the caller's bandwidth/fee.
