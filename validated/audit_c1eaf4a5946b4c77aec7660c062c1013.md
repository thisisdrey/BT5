### Title
Missing minimum-received-amount check in `ExchangeWithdrawContract` allows front-running of pool withdrawals - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java`)

### Summary
`ExchangeWithdrawActuator` lets an owner withdraw a chosen quantity of one token from a bancor-style `Exchange` pool, and the actuator computes the paired ("another") token amount from the *current* pool ratio at execution time, without letting the caller specify a minimum acceptable amount for that paired token, unlike the sibling `ExchangeTransactionContract` which has an explicit `expected` field enforced in validation.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeWithdrawContract` only carries `owner_address`, `exchange_id`, `token_id`, and `quant` — there is no `expected`/minimum field: [1](#0-0) 

In `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()`, the paired token quantity `anotherTokenQuant` is derived purely from the pool's current balances at the moment of execution (`bigSecondTokenBalance.multiply(bigTokenQuant).divide(bigFirstTokenBalance)` or the symmetric case), and this value is what actually gets credited to the withdrawer's account: [2](#0-1) 

`doValidate()` re-derives the same ratio-based `anotherTokenQuant` and only checks internal consistency/precision (`"Not precise enough"`), never comparing it against any caller-supplied floor: [3](#0-2) 

This is structurally identical to the reported `AeraVaultV1.returnFunds()`/`finalize()` bug class: a withdrawal from an AMM-like pool that pays out based on the pool's spot ratio at execution time, with no caller-enforced minimum. By contrast, `ExchangeTransactionActuator` (the swap path) *does* have this protection — it carries a `token_expected` field and enforces it: [4](#0-3) [5](#0-4) 

An attacker who observes a pending `ExchangeWithdrawContract` transaction in the mempool can broadcast their own `ExchangeTransactionContract` swap transaction with a higher energy price/bandwidth priority to get included first in the same block, shifting `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` before the withdraw executes. Because Tron block producers order transactions largely by arrival/fee within a block (no batch-auction protection), and the withdraw actuator recomputes `anotherTokenQuant` from whatever the pool balances are *at execution time*, the victim's withdrawal is filled at the attacker-manipulated ratio instead of the ratio they observed when submitting.

### Impact Explanation
The victim (the exchange creator, since `ExchangeWithdrawContract` requires `accountCapsule.getAddress().equals(exchangeCapsule.getCreatorAddress())`) can be forced to receive materially fewer of the paired token than expected for the same `quant` withdrawn of the chosen token, resulting in unbacked/unfavorable value extraction analogous to the Aera Vault `finalize()` front-running issue. Since this only affects the balance split between the two tokens in the pool at withdrawal (not an ability to steal funds outside the pool or corrupt other accounts' balances), it is a bounded, medium-severity value-extraction issue rather than unbounded theft.

### Likelihood Explanation
Any account can create an `Exchange` and become its creator (the only actor who can call `ExchangeWithdrawContract`), and any account can submit `ExchangeTransactionContract` swaps against that same exchange to shift its balances immediately beforehand. Both are ordinary, unprivileged broadcastable transactions processed by their respective actuators, and reordering/insertion within the same block by fee/priority is a normal capability of any transaction broadcaster — no special privilege, witness collusion, or network-level manipulation is required, only correctly-timed transaction submission.

### Recommendation
Add a caller-specified minimum acceptable `anotherTokenQuant` (mirroring `ExchangeTransactionContract.expected`) to `ExchangeWithdrawContract`, and enforce it in `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.doValidate()`/`execute()`, reverting if the pool-ratio-derived paired amount falls below the caller's specified floor.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker observes a pending `ExchangeWithdrawContract` transaction from the exchange creator for `exchangeId` withdrawing `tokenQuant` of `firstTokenID`.
2. Attacker submits an `ExchangeTransactionContract` swap against the same `exchangeId` (selling `secondTokenID` for `firstTokenID`, or vice versa) with sufficient fee/bandwidth to be ordered before the victim's transaction in the same block, shifting `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance`.
3. When `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()` runs for the victim's transaction, it recomputes `anotherTokenQuant` from the now-skewed balances (`ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java` lines 77-90), crediting the victim with a materially worse ratio than what they observed when they built and signed their transaction — with no on-chain mechanism to reject the unfavorable outcome, since the contract carries no minimum-amount field to check against.
