### Title
Missing slippage protection in `ExchangeInjectActuator` and `ExchangeWithdrawActuator` allows users to deposit/withdraw Bancor-pool liquidity at a manipulated ratio - (File: `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java`, `actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeWithdrawActuator.java`)

### Summary
The `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` protobuf messages, unlike `ExchangeTransactionContract`, do not carry a user-supplied minimum/expected-amount field. As a result, `ExchangeInjectActuator` and `ExchangeWithdrawActuator` compute the counterpart token quantity purely from the exchange pool's current `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` ratio at execution time, with no way for the caller to bound the acceptable output. This is directly analogous to the reported bug class: deposit/withdrawal calls lacking slippage control and relying on the pool's instantaneous balance ratio rather than a caller-protected price.

### Finding Description
`ExchangeTransactionContract` includes an `expected` field [1](#0-0)  that `ExchangeTransactionActuator.doValidate()` uses to reject trades whose output falls short of the caller's minimum: `anotherTokenQuant < tokenExpected` throws `ContractValidateException` [2](#0-1) .

In contrast, `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` only carry `owner_address`, `exchange_id`, `token_id`, and `quant` — no min-output/expected parameter at all [3](#0-2) .

`ExchangeInjectActuator.execute()` derives `anotherTokenQuant` solely from the pool's current balances at execution time and immediately commits it, with no caller-supplied bound: `anotherTokenQuant = floorDiv(multiplyExact(secondTokenBalance, tokenQuant), firstTokenBalance)` [4](#0-3) . Its validation path performs the identical unconstrained computation and only checks `anotherTokenQuant <= 0` and a global `balanceLimit`, never a user-provided minimum [5](#0-4) .

Similarly, `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()` computes the withdrawn counterpart amount from the live pool ratio with no expected/minimum guard: `anotherTokenQuant = bigSecondTokenBalance.multiply(bigTokenQuant).divide(bigFirstTokenBalance).longValueExact()` [6](#0-5) , and its `doValidate()` only checks that the exchange has enough balance and that rounding "remainder" precision is within an internal tolerance — never that the result meets a caller-specified expectation [7](#0-6) .

Because `ExchangeInjectContract`/`ExchangeWithdrawContract` transactions are ordinary signed transactions broadcast to the network, any address holding tokens in a TRON on-chain Bancor-style `Exchange` pool is exposed: another transaction (a large `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade) can be placed in the same block/mempool window immediately before the victim's inject/withdraw transaction to shift `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance`, after which the victim's inject/withdraw executes at the skewed ratio with no ability to bound the loss.

### Impact Explanation
A user calling `ExchangeInjectContract` (adding liquidity) or `ExchangeWithdrawContract` (removing liquidity) can be sandwiched by a preceding trade against the same `Exchange`, causing them to inject assets at an unfavorable ratio (donating value to the pool/attacker) or withdraw fewer counterpart tokens than the pool's fair-value ratio would imply. This is a direct loss of user funds for any account issuing these normal, permissionless contract calls — matching the report's "High" impact classification of suboptimal-share/asset loss during deposit/withdrawal.

### Likelihood Explanation
Both `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` are ordinary user-callable transaction types processed by the corresponding actuators; no privileged role is required. An attacker only needs to submit an `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade against the same exchange ID immediately before the victim's inject/withdraw transaction (a standard front-running/sandwich pattern), which is straightforward given TRON's public mempool/block-producer ordering. The lack of any expected/minimum field in the two contract messages means there is no way for honest users to protect themselves at the protocol level.

### Recommendation
Add an `expected`/minimum-output field to `ExchangeInjectContract` and `ExchangeWithdrawContract` (mirroring `ExchangeTransactionContract.expected`), and enforce it in `ExchangeInjectActuator.doValidate()`/`execute()` and `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.doValidate()`/`execute()` by rejecting the transaction when the computed `anotherTokenQuant` is less than the caller-specified minimum, consistent with the existing pattern in `ExchangeTransactionActuator`.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker and victim both hold balances in the same `Exchange` (identified by `exchange_id`) with `firstTokenBalance`/`secondTokenBalance` X/Y.
2. Victim broadcasts an `ExchangeWithdrawContract` for `quant` of `firstToken`, expecting roughly `Y*quant/X` of `secondToken` back, per the ratio computed at [8](#0-7) .
3. Attacker submits a large `ExchangeTransactionContract` trade against the pool just before the victim's transaction is packed into a block, shifting the balances to X'/Y' such that `Y'*quant/X'` is significantly smaller than `Y*quant/X`.
4. The victim's `ExchangeWithdrawActuator.execute()` runs against the post-trade balances with no minimum-output enforcement, so it succeeds and returns the reduced `anotherTokenQuant`, realizing an unbounded loss for the victim, since no `expected` parameter exists in `ExchangeWithdrawContract` to abort the transaction [9](#0-8) .
