### Title
Unbounded multiplicative `index`/`lindex` accrual will overflow Clarity's checked `uint` arithmetic in `accrue()`, permanently freezing all vault operations - (File: `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-stx.clar`, and identically in `v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`)

### Summary
Every Zest vault maintains two monotonically-growing multiplicative accumulators, `index` and `lindex`, that compound on every `accrue()` call for the lifetime of the contract, with no cap, no reset, and no `unchecked`-style arithmetic escape hatch (Clarity's `uint` math traps/aborts on overflow, just like the Nibbl `Twav.sol` cumulative price accumulator in the referenced report). Because `accrue()` is invoked from every state-mutating entry point (`deposit`, `redeem`, `system-borrow`, `system-repay`, `socialize-debt`), a single overflowing multiplication permanently bricks the vault.

### Finding Description
`index` and `lindex` start at `INDEX-PRECISION` and are advanced every accrual via: [1](#0-0) [2](#0-1) 

Each step multiplies the current index by a `multiplier` computed from the accrued interest rate and elapsed time, using unchecked-looking but actually *checked* helper math: [3](#0-2) 

`mul-div-down`/`mul-div-up` compute the full product `x * y` before dividing by `z`. In Clarity, `uint` is a 128-bit unsigned integer and arithmetic operations abort the transaction (trap) on overflow instead of wrapping, exactly the "checked" behavior that made the referenced Solidity 0.8+ `Twav.sol` accumulator brick the Nibbl contract once `cumulativeValuation` overflowed. Here, `index` is multiplied by `multiplier` (itself proportional to `INDEX-PRECISION`) on every accrual: [4](#0-3) 

Because `index` only ever grows (there is no decrement path — `calc-index-next` always compounds upward while `multiplier >= INDEX-PRECISION`), repeated accrual under sustained non-zero utilization will eventually cause the intermediate product `index * multiplier` inside `mul-div-down`/`mul-div-up` to exceed `MAX-U128` (`u340282366920938463463374607431768211455`), at which point Clarity's checked arithmetic aborts the transaction.

`accrue()` is called unconditionally from every user-facing lending/vault operation: [5](#0-4) 

and from `system-borrow`/`system-repay`: [6](#0-5) 

as well as from the market's per-block cache routine that every collateral/debt operation depends on: [7](#0-6) 

Once the trap condition is hit, `accrue()` (and therefore `deposit`, `redeem`, `system-borrow`, `system-repay`, `socialize-debt`, and `market.accrue-and-cache` for that asset) reverts on every subsequent call, forever — there is no `unchecked` fallback path and no way to "reset" or split the accumulator once it exceeds the safe multiplication bound, mirroring exactly the root cause and irreversible outcome described in the referenced report.

### Impact Explanation
Once the compounded `index`/`lindex` value crosses the point where `index * multiplier` (or any other checked multiplication that consumes it, e.g. `calc-cumulative-debt`, `total-debt`, `debt-preview`) exceeds `u340282366920938463463374607431768211455`, the vault permanently traps on `accrue()`. Because every deposit, redemption, borrow, and repayment path calls `accrue()` first, this causes a permanent freeze of all principal and unclaimed yield held by that vault — matching the in-scope "permanent freezing of funds" / "permanent freezing of unclaimed yield" impact class. Every vault in the protocol (`v0-vault-stx`, `v0-vault-sbtc`, `v0-vault-ststx`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc`, `v0-vault-usdc`, `v0-vault-usdh`) shares the identical accrual math, so the same class of failure applies to each.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reaching the exact overflow point through purely organic interest compounding requires an extended, unrealistic timeframe under normal interest-rate curves — the same reason the original Nibbl finding was downgraded to Medium by the judge for lack of a concrete PoC of the trigger being reached in practice. However, the risk is compounded because `mul-div-down`/`mul-div-up` compute the full `x*y` product before dividing, meaning the intermediate product overflows far earlier than the final mathematically-meaningful result would require — i.e., the vault can trap well before `index` itself is anywhere near `MAX-U128`, purely because of the unreduced intermediate multiplication. Sustained high-utilization periods driving the interpolated interest rate to its curve maximum for extended periods accelerate compounding significantly and shrink the time-to-overflow, making this more reachable than a naive "wait for uint128 to fill up" analysis suggests. No privileged action or DAO compromise is required — only sustained normal usage (borrowing/utilization) of a vault, matching an ordinary-principal-reachable path in the allowed "vault share math and interest accrual" surface.

### Recommendation
- Bound the growth of `index`/`lindex` explicitly and revert with a controlled error (rather than a raw arithmetic trap) once `index` approaches a safe ceiling well below `MAX-U128`, and provide a governance path to migrate/re-baseline the index (e.g., a "index reset" epoch mechanism, similar to rebasing) before that ceiling is reached.
- Avoid `mul-div-down`/`mul-div-up` patterns whose intermediate product is far larger than the final result; use wider intermediate representations or reorder operations (e.g., divide before multiplying where precision loss is acceptable) to prevent premature overflow traps.
- Emit a monitoring event/alert as `index` approaches a defined safety threshold so the DAO can act well ahead of an unrecoverable freeze.

### Proof of Concept
1. A vault (e.g. `v0-vault-usdc.clar`) sustains high utilization such that `interest-rate` remains near the top of its configured curve for an extended period (via normal borrow/repay activity, no special privilege needed).
2. Each `accrue()` call executes `next-index` → `calc-multiplier-delta` → `calc-index-next`, compounding `index` further:
`(define-private (calc-index-next (index-curr uint) (multiplier uint)) (mul-div-down index-curr multiplier INDEX-PRECISION))` — see [4](#0-3) .
3. As `index` compounds upward indefinitely, the intermediate product `index-curr * multiplier` inside `mul-div-down` eventually exceeds `MAX-U128`.
4. The next call to `accrue()` (from `deposit`, `redeem`, `system-borrow`, `system-repay`, or `market.accrue-and-cache`) traps/aborts due to Clarity's checked `uint` multiplication.
5. Every subsequent transaction touching that vault (by any ordinary principal) fails identically, permanently freezing all supplied principal and unclaimed yield in the vault with no on-chain recovery path.
