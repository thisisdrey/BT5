### Title
Repeated public `accrue()` calls with minimal time-deltas zero out treasury reserve fee via chained rounding-down divisions - (File: `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-usdc.clar` and equivalent vault contracts)

### Summary
The `accrue()` function, which is public and callable by any unprivileged principal, computes the DAO treasury's reserve fee (`treasury-lp`) through a chain of four sequential `mul-div-down` divisions that each round down. Because `accrue()` also resets the `last-update` timestamp used as the time-delta base, an attacker can force `accrue()` to run on every block with a minimal time-delta, causing each division in the chain to independently truncate to zero, while the borrower-facing `index` still compounds. This permanently denies the treasury its reserve-fee share of interest that borrowers are still paying, mirroring the "multiple divisions" rounding class from the External Report (River `_onEarnings`), but here landing on `dao-treasury` share minting instead of operator rewards.

`old-debt` and `new-debt` are each computed with an independent `mul-div-down` (rounds down), `debt-delta` is their difference, `reserve-inc` is another `mul-div-down` (rounds down) of `debt-delta * fee-reserve / BPS`, and `treasury-lp` is a final `mul-div-down` (rounds down) of `reserve-inc * total-supply / (ta-preview - reserve-inc)`. Every step in this chain rounds in favor of "not minting" — i.e., away from the treasury — exactly the pattern the External Report flags: instead of collapsing the pipeline into a single division (numerator/denominator) delegated once at the end, the code performs multiple independent divisions, each capable of truncating a small delta to zero.

Critically, `next-index()` bases its `time-delta` on `last-update`, and `accrue()` resets `last-update` to `stacks-block-time` on every call where `index` or `lindex` changes: [4](#0-3) 

`accrue()` has no auth check and is reachable by any principal directly, or indirectly through `deposit`/`redeem`/`transfer`/`system-borrow`, all of which call `try! (accrue)` first: [5](#0-4) 

Because the borrower-side multiplier is rounded *up* (`calc-multiplier-delta ... true`), `index` (and thus outstanding borrower debt) keeps compounding correctly (or even slightly in the protocol's favor) across many tiny calls. But the treasury-side computation is a chain of round-down divisions computed from `scaled-principal` (a large, roughly constant value) and a *tiny* per-call `idx` delta. When `debt-delta` per call is small (e.g., 0 or 1 unit, which is virtually guaranteed if `accrue()` is invoked every block instead of at natural, longer-spaced user-driven intervals), `reserve-inc = debt-delta * fee-reserve / BPS` truncates to `0` far more often than if the same total interest had accrued over one longer interval and been divided once. Over time this causes `dao-treasury` to permanently lose most or all of its reserve-fee yield, even though the underlying interest is genuinely being paid by borrowers into `index`.

### Impact Explanation
This is a rounding-driven, permanent loss of the protocol treasury's unclaimed interest-fee yield (`dao-treasury`'s share of vault shares minted via `ft-mint? zft treasury-lp .dao-treasury`), triggerable by any unprivileged principal repeatedly calling the public `accrue()` entrypoint (or by naturally frequent user activity) on every vault instance (`v0-vault-usdc.clar`, `v0-vault-sbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-stx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststx.clar`, `v0-vault-ststxbtc.clar`, `v0-vault-usdh.clar`). It maps to "theft/permanent freezing of unclaimed yield" for the treasury: the debt/interest still accrues and is paid by borrowers into the vault's `index`, but the reserve-fee share that should compound into `dao-treasury` shares is truncated away by the chained divisions before it is ever minted, and can never be recovered because there is no compensating carry-forward of the truncated remainder between calls.

### Likelihood Explanation
The attack requires only a normal wallet calling a public, unauthenticated function (`accrue`) with no cooldown or minimum-interval guard; each call is cheap (a handful of reads/writes) and can be issued every block. No DAO or oracle compromise, no flashloan, and no privileged registry state is needed — it is purely a consequence of the arithmetic chain and the fact that `last-update` is reset on every successful accrual.

### Recommendation
Follow the same fix pattern recommended in the External Report: reduce the reserve-fee calculation to a single division. Compute one combined numerator (`debt-delta_num * fee-reserve * total-supply`) and one combined denominator (`BPS * (ta-preview_denominator - reserve-inc-equivalent)`) and perform the division once inside a dedicated helper, so a single small residual can't compound zero-truncation across four separate `mul-div-down` calls. Additionally, consider tracking a running "undistributed fee remainder" across `accrue()` calls (carry the truncated remainder forward instead of discarding it) or bounding how frequently `accrue()`'s time-delta/timestamp state can be reset by a caller, so repeated small-interval calls cannot structurally erase the treasury's fee share.

### Proof of Concept
1. Attacker (unprivileged) observes vault `v0-vault-usdc.clar` with nonzero `fee-reserve` and outstanding `principal-scaled` (borrowed debt).
2. Attacker calls `accrue()` in every block (or immediately after any other user's tx that itself calls `accrue()` and resets `last-update`).
3. Each call: `next-index()` computes a `time-delta` of ~1 block; `calc-multiplier-delta` rounds the borrower-side multiplier up, so `index` still advances (debt is charged normally over time).
4. In the same call, `old-debt`/`new-debt` (via `mul-div-down`) differ by a very small `debt-delta` because the interval is tiny; `reserve-inc = mul-div-down(debt-delta, fee-reserve, BPS)` truncates to `0` whenever `debt-delta * fee-reserve < BPS`.
5. Because `reserve-inc` is `0`, the `(if (> treasury-lp u0) (try! (ft-mint? zft treasury-lp .dao-treasury)) false)` branch never mints anything for that interval.
6. Repeating this every block over the vault's lifetime, `dao-treasury` accumulates near-zero reserve-fee shares despite borrowers continuously paying interest reflected in the growing `index`, versus the materially larger `treasury-lp` that would have been minted had `accrue()` only been invoked at natural, longer-spaced intervals (a single division over the full elapsed interest).
