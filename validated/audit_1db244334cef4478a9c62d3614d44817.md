Audit Report

## Title
Permissionless-asset freezer can permanently lock unrelated liquidity in `pallet-asset-conversion` pools - (File: `substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/lib.rs`)

## Summary
`pallet-assets::create` lets any account permitted by `T::CreateOrigin` become the `issuer`/`admin`/`freezer` of a new asset class, retaining unilateral freeze rights. `pallet-asset-conversion::do_create_pool`/`do_add_liquidity` accept arbitrary `AssetKind` pairs with no check on the counterpart asset's freezer/admin trustworthiness, pooling other users' funds together with the attacker-controlled asset into one `pool_account`. `do_remove_liquidity` burns the LP token and then performs two sequential `T::Assets::transfer` calls out of `pool_account`; since dispatch is transactional, a `Frozen` failure on the malicious asset's leg reverts the entire extrinsic, including the leg for the unrelated, non-malicious asset, permanently locking all LPs' funds in that pool.

## Finding Description
`create` in `substrate/frame/assets/src/lib.rs` (L843-889) sets `issuer = admin = freezer = admin` for the caller-designated admin. `freeze`/`freeze_asset` (L1192-1217, L1266-1280) let that freezer lock a specific account or the whole asset class at will, gated only by `origin == d.freezer` — no counterparty consent required. `do_create_pool`/`do_add_liquidity` in `substrate/frame/asset-conversion/src/lib.rs` (L729-892) never inspect who the freezer/admin of either `AssetKind` is; they transfer both assets into a computed `pool_account` (L855-856, L709-710). `do_remove_liquidity` (L895-966) burns LP tokens first (L942-949) and only then performs two sequential `T::Assets::transfer` calls out of `pool_account` (L951-952). `can_decrease`/`reducible_balance` in `substrate/frame/assets/src/functions.rs` (L190-192, L203-205, L256) return `Frozen` if the account or asset class is frozen. Because FRAME dispatch executes inside a storage transaction that rolls back entirely on `Err`, a `Frozen` failure on the second (malicious asset) leg of `do_remove_liquidity` reverts the whole extrinsic — including the already-computed LP-burn and the first (unrelated asset) transfer.

## Impact Explanation
This matches the "permanent user-fund lock" impact category: liquidity providers' funds in the non-malicious leg of a pool become permanently unwithdrawable once the paired asset is frozen by its (attacker-controlled) freezer, because both legs are bundled into a single atomic dispatchable with no partial-withdrawal or force-thaw escape hatch in `pallet-asset-conversion`.

## Likelihood Explanation
Exploitability depends entirely on whether the deployed runtime's `pallet-asset-conversion::Config` permits pairing pools with permissionlessly-creatable/freezable assets (i.e., whether `T::AssetKind` and any pool-creation origin filter allow untrusted asset classes to be pooled against trusted ones). I was not able to verify, within the available tool budget, the actual `AssetKind`/origin configuration used by production runtimes such as Asset Hub in this repository, so I cannot confirm whether this path is reachable end-to-end in a live, in-scope deployment or is precluded by runtime-level asset trust restrictions.

## Recommendation
`pallet-asset-conversion` should either (a) restrict poolable `AssetKind`s to trusted/system-registered assets whose freezer/admin is governance-controlled, or (b) make `remove_liquidity`/swap resilient to a frozen leg — e.g., support partial/best-effort withdrawal of the non-frozen asset instead of reverting the whole atomic operation, or provide an escape hatch allowing LPs to redeem the non-frozen asset independently of the frozen one.

## Proof of Concept
1. Attacker calls `pallet_assets::create(origin, id=X, admin=attacker, min_balance=1)`, becoming issuer/admin/freezer of `X` (subject to runtime's `CreateOrigin` permitting this).
2. Attacker mints `X` to self and calls `pallet_asset_conversion::create_pool(origin, Native, X)`.
3. Victim calls `add_liquidity(origin, Native, X, amount1, amount2, ..., victim)`, depositing native tokens and `X` into `pool_account`, receiving LP tokens.
4. Attacker calls `pallet_assets::freeze(origin, id=X, who=pool_account)` (or `freeze_asset`).
5. Victim calls `remove_liquidity(...)`: the LP-token burn (`T::PoolAssets::burn_from`) and the first `T::Assets::transfer` (native leg) execute, but the second `T::Assets::transfer` for `X` fails with `Token::Frozen` per `can_decrease` — the whole extrinsic reverts transactionally, leaving the victim unable to redeem any of their native-token liquidity while `X` stays frozen.
