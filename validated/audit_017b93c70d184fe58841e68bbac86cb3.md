### Title
Small deposits can round down to zero shares while assets are still accepted, permanently losing depositor funds - (File: `mainnet/contracts/vault/v0-vault-stx.clar` and equivalent `v0-vault-*.clar` vaults)

### Summary
`deposit()` mints shares based on `convert-to-shares-preview`, which floors via `mul-div-down`. When `total-assets-preview` exceeds `total-supply-preview` (share price > 1, i.e., after interest has accrued), a sufficiently small deposit `amount` yields `inkind = 0`. The function proceeds to accept the underlying tokens and increase `assets` regardless, but mints zero zft shares to the depositor.

### Finding Description
`convert-to-shares-preview` is defined identically in every vault: [1](#0-0) 
It returns `(mul-div-down amount ts ta)` when both `ta` and `ts` are non-zero, which rounds toward zero and can produce `0` for small `amount` whenever `ta > ts` (i.e., once the vault has accrued any interest, since share price > 1).

`deposit()` computes `inkind` this way, checks only `amount > 0` and `min-out` slippage, but never asserts `inkind > 0` before transferring in the underlying and minting: [2](#0-1) 
Since the default `min-out` can be `0`, the slippage check `(asserts! (>= inkind min-out) ERR-SLIPPAGE)` passes trivially when `inkind` is `0`. The function then calls `receive-underlying`, pulling the depositor's tokens into the vault and increasing `assets`, while `ft-mint? zft inkind recipient` mints `0` shares.

By contrast, `redeem()` in the same contract explicitly guards against this exact rounding case: [3](#0-2) 
demonstrating that the developers were aware zero-output rounding is a real risk requiring a guard — but the guard is missing on the mint side.

### Impact Explanation
A depositor who submits a small enough `amount` (relative to the current share-to-asset ratio) has their underlying tokens absorbed into the vault's `assets` total with zero corresponding zft shares minted. Those tokens are non-recoverable by that depositor (they hold no shares entitling them to withdraw anything), i.e., permanent freezing/loss of the deposited funds for that user, while other shareholders benefit from the inflated `assets` backing. This maps to the "Temporary/permanent freezing of funds" impact class analogous to the reported VotingEscrow issue, and is reachable by any ordinary principal simply calling `deposit`.

### Likelihood Explanation
Reachable by any user without special privileges via the standard `deposit` entry point. Likelihood scales with the vault's share price: once any interest has accrued (`ta > ts`), a deposit amount smaller than `ta/ts` in underlying units zeroes out `inkind`. For higher-decimal or higher-priced underlying assets (e.g., sBTC, stSTX) this dust threshold is easily hit by routine small deposits or an attacker deliberately depositing dust to grief/test.

### Recommendation
Add `(asserts! (> inkind u0) ERR-OUTPUT-ZERO)` in `deposit()` for all six `v0-vault-*.clar` contracts, mirroring the existing guard already present in `redeem()`, so that deposits which would mint zero shares revert instead of silently absorbing the depositor's underlying tokens.

### Proof of Concept
1. Let a vault accrue interest so that `total-assets-preview() > total-supply-preview()` (share price `p = ta/ts > 1`).
2. As a fresh depositor, call `deposit(amount, 0, recipient)` with `amount` chosen such that `amount * ts / ta == 0` under integer division (e.g., `amount < ta/ts`).
3. Observe: `receive-underlying` transfers `amount` of the depositor's tokens into the vault, `var-set assets (+ current-assets amount)` increases tracked assets, but `ft-mint? zft 0 recipient` mints zero shares — the depositor now holds a balance of `0` zft and has no way to redeem the deposited `amount`. (define-private (convert-to-shares-preview (amount uint))
  (let ((ta (total-assets-preview))
        (ts (total-supply-preview)))
    (if (is-eq ts u0)
        amount
        (if (is-eq ta u0)
            u0
            (mul-div-down amount ts ta)))))
