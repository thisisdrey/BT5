### Title
Rounding Down in `ExchangeInjectActuator` Lets the Exchange Creator Under-Contribute Liquidity, Skewing the AMM Pool Ratio - (File: actuator/src/main/java/org/tron/core/actuator/ExchangeInjectActuator.java)

### Summary
`ExchangeInjectActuator` computes the counterpart token amount (`anotherTokenQuant`) that must be pulled from the caller's account when injecting liquidity into a Bancor-style TRON `Exchange` pool, using floor (round-down) integer division. Because this quantity determines how much value the injecting account actually contributes to preserve the pool's price ratio, rounding it down systematically lets the caller contribute slightly less than the mathematically required proportional amount — the same rounding-direction defect described in the external ERC4626 report, just applied to a deposit/inject flow instead of a withdraw flow.

### Finding Description
In `execute()`, when a caller injects `tokenQuant` of one token, the actuator computes the required amount of the other token with `floorDiv`: [1](#0-0) 

The `validate()` path performs the identical floor-division calculation (`BigInteger.divide()`, which truncates toward zero) to authorize the operation and check balances: [2](#0-1) 

Both balances are then updated by adding `tokenQuant` and the rounded-down `anotherTokenQuant` to the pool: [3](#0-2) 

To preserve the invariant `firstTokenBalance / secondTokenBalance` (the pool's implied exchange rate) after an injection, the counterpart amount owed by the depositor should be rounded up — analogous to ERC4626's requirement that `convertToShares`/`previewDeposit`-style calculations round in favor of the pool, not the depositor. Rounding down here means the injecting account contributes marginally less of the second token than proportionally required, shifting the pool ratio in the depositor's favor at the expense of the implied value held by the pool (which subsequently affects `ExchangeWithdrawActuator` and `ExchangeTransactionActuator` participants who trade against that ratio).

Only the exchange's creator may call `ExchangeInject` (`doValidate()` enforces `accountCapsule.getAddress().equals(exchangeCapsule.getCreatorAddress())`), but the creator is still an ordinary, unprivileged, single-signature account (not an SR/witness/committee role), matching the "asset issuer" persona explicitly in scope. By choosing `tokenQuant` values that maximize the truncated remainder (e.g. values where `secondTokenBalance * tokenQuant` is just under a multiple of `firstTokenBalance`), the creator can repeatedly inject liquidity while systematically under-paying the counterpart side, cumulatively skewing the exchange's rate to their advantage across multiple injections.

### Impact Explanation
Repeated exploitation lets the exchange creator extract value from the AMM pool's price/ratio over many injections, distorting the exchange rate seen by later `ExchangeTransactionActuator` traders and `ExchangeWithdrawActuator` withdrawers, and understating the counterpart contribution required to keep the constant-ratio invariant intact — a concrete unbacked-value/asset-imbalance condition consistent with a Medium-severity token/AMM accounting bug.

### Likelihood Explanation
Exploitation only requires broadcasting a normal, self-signed `ExchangeInjectContract` transaction as the exchange's creator with a crafted `quant`, with no special privileges beyond exchange ownership, and no interaction with other actors needed to trigger the rounding-down behavior on every injection.

### Recommendation
Change the `anotherTokenQuant` calculation in both `execute()` and `doValidate()` of `ExchangeInjectActuator` to round up (ceiling division) instead of `floorDiv`/`BigInteger.divide()`, ensuring the depositor always contributes at least the proportionally required amount of the counterpart token, consistent with the "round in favor of the pool" ERC4626 pattern.

### Proof of Concept
1. Create (or use) an exchange with `firstTokenBalance = F`, `secondTokenBalance = S` where `S` and `F` are not exact multiples of each other.
2. As the exchange creator, call `ExchangeInjectContract` with `tokenID = firstTokenID` and a `tokenQuant` chosen such that `S * tokenQuant` is not evenly divisible by `F` (maximizing the fractional remainder discarded by `floorDiv`).
3. Observe that `anotherTokenQuant = floorDiv(S * tokenQuant, F)` is one unit less than the true proportional value (`ceil(S*tokenQuant/F)`), so the creator pays less second-token than required to preserve the pool ratio.
4. Repeat across many injections; the pool ratio drifts in the creator's favor each time, extractable via subsequent `ExchangeTransactionActuator` trades or `ExchangeWithdrawActuator` withdrawals at the skewed rate.
